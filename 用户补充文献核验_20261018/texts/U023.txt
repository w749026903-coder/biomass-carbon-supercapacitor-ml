1Abstract
2Gel polymer electrolytes are important components of quasi-solid-state supercapacitors 
3because they reduce electrolyte leakage and improve device safety. However, the PVA polymer 
4network can restrict ion mobility and increase internal resistance. In this work, TiO 2 
5nanoparticle-modified PVA-NaOH gel electrolytes were developed for quasi-solid-state 
6symmetric supercapacitors (QSSCs). The TiO 2 content was varied from 0.10 to 0.30 wt%, and 
7the electrochemical properties were evaluated using cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic 
8charge-discharge (GCD), and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). XRD analysis 
9confirmed that TiO 2 consisted of mixed anatase and rutile phases, while FE-SEM images 
10revealed nanosized particles with partial agglomeration. Among the investigated compositions, 
11the gel electrolyte containing 0.15 wt% TiO 2 exhibited the best electrochemical performance, 
12delivering a specific capacitance of 177.50 F g -1 at 1 A g-1, compared with 105.60 F g-1 for the 
13TiO 2-free PVA-NaOH gel. This composition also exhibited the lowest series resistance (Rs) and 
14charge-transfer resistance (R ct), with values of 0.549 and 0.426 Ω, respectively. The device 
15retained 79.25% of its initial capacitance at 10 A g -1 and showed approximately 100% 
16capacitance retention after 5,000 charge-discharge cycles at 10 A g -1. Furthermore, an energy 
17density of 24.65 Wh kg -1 was achieved at a power density of 963.94 W kg-1. These results 
18indicate that an appropriate TiO 2 content can reduce internal resistance and improve charge-
19storage capability, rate performance, and long-term cycling stability. The findings support the 
20potential of PVA-NaOH/TiO 2 gel electrolytes for further development of quasi-solid-state 
21symmetric supercapacitors.

21. Introduction
3The rapid development of portable and wearable electronic devices has increased the 
4demand for energy-storage systems that are safe, flexible, and capable of rapid charge-discharge 
5operation. Supercapacitors are attractive for these applications because of their high power 
6density, rapid electrochemical response, and good cycling stability. However, device 
7performance is not determined by the electrode materials alone. The electrolyte also plays an 
8important role in ion transport, electrode-electrolyte interfacial behavior, and electrochemical 
9stability. Although aqueous electrolytes generally provide high ionic conductivity, electrolyte 
10leakage and limited structural integrity restrict their use in quasi-solid-state and flexible devices. 
11Gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) have therefore attracted considerable attention because they 
12combine liquid-like ion transport with the dimensional stability of a polymer network and can 
13function simultaneously as both electrolyte and separator [1-4].
14Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) is widely used as a host polymer for GPEs because of its 
15hydrophilic nature, good film-forming ability, flexibility, and ability to retain water and ions 
16within the polymer network. However, ordered polymer regions and strong hydrogen bonding 
17between hydroxyl groups can restrict polymer-chain mobility and ion transport, resulting in 
18higher resistance than that of liquid electrolytes. Previous studies have also shown that both the 
19polymer matrix and the alkali-metal cation can influence charge-storage behavior and the 
20response at the electrode-electrolyte interface [5]. Therefore, modifying the local environment of 
21the PVA network to facilitate ion mobility while maintaining the quasi-solid-state structure and 
22long-term stability remains an important challenge in the development of GPEs for 
23supercapacitors [6-9].
24One approach to improve the properties of PVA-based GPEs is the incorporation of 
25nanoscale inorganic fillers. TiO 2 is particularly attractive because of its chemical stability and its 
26ability to interact with the PVA matrix. Previous studies have reported that ceramic fillers, 
27including TiO 2, can modify the polymer environment, increase amorphous regions, and improve 
28ionic transport in PVA-based systems [10-12]. Beenarani and Sugumaran further demonstrated 
29that the amount of TiO 2 strongly influences the electrochemical performance of PVA-TiO2 
30composite electrolytes and that an optimized filler content provides better performance than

6Although PVA-TiO 2 systems and alkaline PVA-based gel electrolytes have been 
7investigated in several configurations, the use of TiO 2 to modify PVA-NaOH gel electrolytes for 
8quasi-solid-state symmetric supercapacitors remains comparatively less explored. In particular, 
9the influence of TiO2 content on the electrochemical performance and internal resistance of 
10PVA-NaOH-based devices has not been systematically clarified. In this work, PVA-NaOH gel 
11electrolytes containing different amounts of TiO 2 were developed and evaluated by cyclic 
12voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD), and electrochemical impedance 
13spectroscopy (EIS). The resulting devices were compared with those using aqueous NaOH and 
14TiO 2-free PVA-NaOH electrolytes to identify the optimal TiO2 content and to clarify the 
15relationship between TiO 2 loading, cell resistance, and charge-storage performance in quasi-
16solid-state symmetric supercapacitors. 
172. Experimental
182.1 Materials
19Commercial titanium dioxide nanoparticles (P25 TiO 2, 99.5%), polyvinylidene fluoride 
20(PVDF, Mw ≈ 534,000), and N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, 99%) were purchased from Sigma-
21Aldrich. Sodium hydroxide (NaOH, 99%) was obtained from RCI Labscan. Polyvinyl alcohol 
22(PVA, Mw ≈ 115,000) was supplied by Loba Chemie. Carbon black (C, 99%) was purchased 
23from Alfa Aesar, while commercial activated carbon (AC) was obtained from Nanjing XFNANO 
24Materials Tech Co., Ltd. (XFNANO).

29and 220 rpm for 2 h until a clear and homogeneous solution was obtained. After stirring, the

72.3 Preparation of 3 M NaOH solution
8A 3 M NaOH solution was prepared by adding approximately 70 mL of deionized (DI) 
9water to a beaker, followed by the gradual addition of 12 g of NaOH under continuous stirring at 
10room temperature for 1 h. Because the dissolution of NaOH is exothermic, NaOH was added 
11slowly to avoid excessive temperature increase. After complete dissolution, DI water was added 
12to adjust the final solution volume to 100 mL, and the solution was stirred for an additional 1 h. 
13The resulting NaOH solution was stored in a tightly sealed alkali-resistant container to minimize 
14the uptake of moisture and carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. 
152.4 Preparation of NaOH/TiO 2 Suspension for Gel Electrolytes
16A TiO 2 stock suspension was prepared by adding 100 mg of TiO2 powder to 10 mL of 3 
17M NaOH solution, corresponding to a TiO 2 concentration of 10 mg mL-1. The suspension was 
18stirred at room temperature at 220 rpm for 15 min, followed by ultrasonication for 15 min and 
19further stirring for another 15 min to promote uniform dispersion of the TiO 2 nanoparticles 
20before use. For the preparation of gel electrolytes with different TiO 2 contents, 0.40, 0.60, 0.80, 
21and 1.20 mL of the TiO 2 stock suspension were used, corresponding to TiO2 amounts of 4, 6, 8, 
22and 12 mg, respectively. Additional 3 M NaOH solution was then added in volumes of 16.267, 
2316.067, 15.867, and 15.467 mL, respectively, to maintain a constant total NaOH solution volume 
24of 16.667 mL for all compositions. Thus, the NaOH content was kept constant, while only the 
25amount of TiO 2 was varied. Each NaOH/TiO2 suspension was stirred at room temperature for 15 
26min, ultrasonicated for 15 min, and then stirred for an additional 15 min. The resulting 
27suspensions were immediately used for the preparation of PVA–NaOH/TiO 2 gel electrolytes to 
28minimize sedimentation and agglomeration of TiO 2 nanoparticles prior to mixing with the PVA 
29solution.

32.5 Preparation of PVA–NaOH/TiO₂ Gel Electrolytes
4For each composition, 22 g of the PVA stock solution was heated and stirred at 85 °C and 
5220 rpm for 15 min. The NaOH/TiO 2 suspension was then gradually added to the PVA solution 
6over 5 min under the same conditions to promote the distribution of NaOH and TiO 2 within the 
7PVA solution. After complete addition, the mixture was continuously stirred at 85 °C for 2 h 
8until a homogeneous mixture was obtained. The mixture was then cooled to room temperature, 
9ultrasonicated for 15 min, and stirred for an additional 15 min at room temperature to reduce 
10TiO 2 agglomeration and air bubbles generated during mixing. 
11For each composition, 9 g of the gel mixture was poured into a plastic mold to obtain a 
12gel sheet with an average thickness of approximately 0.45 mm. The gel was allowed to set at 
13room temperature for 2 h and was subsequently frozen overnight. After complete gel formation, 
14the gel sheets were cut into approximately 1.5 × 1.5 cm 2 pieces and used as both the electrolyte 
15and separator for supercapacitor assembly. All gel electrolyte compositions contained 2 g of 
16PVA, 2 g of NaOH, 20 g of DI water from the PVA stock solution, and a total volume of 16.667 
17mL of 3 M NaOH solution. The only variable among the compositions was the amount of TiO 2 
18added to the system. The resulting samples were denoted as T0.10, T0.15, T0.20, and T0.30 
19according to the TiO 2 content used in the gel electrolyte preparation.
202.6 Electrode Preparation and Assembly of Quasi-Solid-State Symmetric 
21Supercapacitors 
22The electrodes for the quasi-solid-state symmetric supercapacitors were prepared by 
23mixing activated carbon (AC), polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF), and carbon black in a weight 
24ratio of 8:1:1, respectively. The components were mixed with 320 µL of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone 
25(NMP) and stirred until a homogeneous slurry was obtained. The resulting slurry was coated 
26onto nickel foam with a coating area of approximately 1 × 1 cm 2. The coated electrodes were 
27then dried at 80 °C for 24 h to remove the NMP solvent. The prepared electrodes were used as 
28both the positive and negative electrodes for the symmetric supercapacitor, with an electrode 
29mass in the range of 3.00 - 3.50 mg.

53. Results and discussion
6To evaluate the effect of replacing the aqueous NaOH electrolyte with a polymer gel 
7electrolyte, the electrochemical properties of symmetric supercapacitors using 3 M NaOH 
8aqueous electrolyte and PVA-NaOH gel electrolyte were compared by CV, GCD, and EIS, as 
9shown in Figure 1. Figure 1(a) presents the CV curves recorded at a scan rate of 2 mV s -1. Both 
10systems exhibit nearly rectangular and symmetric profiles, which are consistent with electric 
11double-layer capacitance (EDLC) behavior of activated carbon electrodes. However, the PVA-
12NaOH system shows a smaller enclosed CV area than the 3 M NaOH system, indicating a lower 
13charge-storage capability. This result is consistent with the GCD curves measured at a current 
14density of 1 A g -1 in Figure 1(b). Although both systems exhibit nearly triangular charge-
15discharge profiles, the PVA-NaOH system shows a shorter discharge time, resulting in a 
16decrease in specific capacitance from 120.11 F g -1 for 3 M NaOH to 105.60 F g-1 for PVA-
17NaOH.
18This difference is further supported by the EIS results in Figure 1(c). The 3 M NaOH 
19system exhibits series resistance (R s) and charge-transfer resistance (Rct) values of 0.594 and 
200.092 Ω, respectively, whereas the corresponding values for the PVA-NaOH system increase to 
210.722 and 0.923 Ω. In particular, the marked increase in R ct reflects a higher resistance at the 
22electrode-electrolyte interface in the gel-based system. The increased resistance may be 
23associated with more restricted transport of Na + and OH- within the PVA network compared with 
24that in the aqueous NaOH electrolyte. Previous studies have reported that highly ordered 
25polymer regions and strong interchain interactions can reduce the free volume and segmental 
26motion of polymer chains, thereby limiting ion transport and interfacial contact [12, 17, 18]. 
27These results indicate that reducing the resistance associated with the PVA-NaOH gel is 
28important for improving its electrochemical performance. TiO 2 was therefore introduced as an

4To reduce the limitations associated with ion transport and resistance in the PVA-NaOH 
5gel electrolyte, TiO 2 nanoparticles were incorporated at contents ranging from 0.10 to 0.30 wt%, 
6and their effects on the electrochemical properties of quasi-solid-state symmetric supercapacitors 
7(QSSCs) were investigated by CV, GCD, and EIS, as shown in Figure 3. The CV curves 
8recorded at a scan rate of 2 mV s -1 in Figure 3(a) retain nearly rectangular and symmetric profiles 
9without distinct redox peaks, indicating that charge storage remains predominantly governed by 
10electric double-layer capacitance (EDLC) at the activated carbon electrodes [10, 13]. However, 
11the enclosed CV area varies noticeably with TiO 2 content. The area increases from T0.10 and 
12reaches a maximum for T0.15, followed by a decrease at TiO 2 contents of 0.20 and 0.30 wt%, 
13indicating that the amount of TiO 2 strongly influences the charge-storage capability of the 
14system. 
15A similar trend is observed in the GCD curves measured at a current density of 1 A g -1, as 
16shown in Figure 3(b). All samples exhibit nearly triangular and symmetric charge-discharge 
17profiles, consistent with capacitive behavior and good reversibility. Among the investigated 
18compositions, T0.15 shows the longest discharge time and delivers the highest specific 
19capacitance of 177.50 F g -1, whereas T0.10, T0.20, and T0.30 exhibit values of 149.00, 141.34, 
20and 119.76 F g -1, respectively. Compared with the TiO2-free PVA-NaOH gel, which delivers 
21105.60 F g -1, the incorporation of 0.15 wt% TiO2 increases the specific capacitance by 
22approximately 68%. The value obtained for T0.15 is also higher than that of the 3 M NaOH 
23aqueous electrolyte (120.11 F g -1). These results demonstrate that an appropriate TiO2 content 
24can markedly improve the charge-storage performance of the PVA-NaOH gel-based system.
25The EIS results in Figure 3(c) further support the trends observed from the CV and GCD 
26measurements. The Nyquist plots show resistance-related responses in the high-frequency region 
27and mass-transport-related behavior at lower frequencies. The experimental data were analyzed 
28using the equivalent circuit shown in Figure 3(d), consisting of a series resistance (R s), charge-
29transfer resistance (R ct), constant phase element (CPE), and Warburg element (W) [24-26]. The 
30fitted R s values are 0.604, 0.549, 0.662, and 0.871 Ω for T0.10, T0.15, T0.20, and T0.30,

184. Conclusion
19This study developed TiO 2-modified PVA-NaOH gel electrolytes for quasi-solid-state 
20symmetric supercapacitors. The TiO 2 used in this work consisted of mixed anatase and rutile 
21phases with nanosized particles. Among the investigated TiO 2 contents of 0.10 - 0.30 wt%, the 
220.15 wt% composition showed the best electrochemical performance under the investigated 
23conditions. It delivered a specific capacitance of 177.50 F g -1 at 1 A g-1, compared with 105.60 F 
24g -1 for the TiO2-free PVA-NaOH gel. This composition also exhibited the lowest Rs and Rct 
25values among the PVA-NaOH/TiO 2 samples, at 0.549 and 0.426 Ω, respectively, consistent with 
26reduced overall cell resistance and lower resistance at the electrode-electrolyte interface. The

28approximately 100% capacitance retention after 5,000 charge-discharge cycles at 10 A g -1. In
