ABSTRACT: Biomass-derived porous carbon offers an eco-
friendly solution for energy storage, but challenges persist in
optimizing the pore structure and electrochemical stability. In this
study, we present a novel approach for fabricating nitrogen−
oxygen-codoped porous carbon (NOPC) electrodes derived from
sustainable longan shells, designed for electrical double-layer
capacitors (EDLCs). The N
3
OPC-3 electrode, optimized through
high-temperature activation and surface doping with urea and nitric
acid, exhibits outstanding electrochemical properties, achieving a
specific capacitance of 463 F·g
−1
at a current density of 0.5 A·g
−1
in
a 6 M KOH electrolyte during a three-electrode test. Furthermore,
the symmetric supercapacitor based on N
3
OPC-3 exhibits a high
energy density of 31.9 W h·kg
−1
at a power density of 700 W·kg
−1
, as well as an exceptional cycling stability, retaining 89% of its
initial capacitance after 10,000 cycles. These superior performance characteristics can be attributed to the well-developed 3D
hierarchical porous structure, which enhances ion diffusion, and the synergistic effects of nitrogen and oxygen doping, which
promote pseudocapacitive behavior and improve electronic conductivity. The results highlight the potential of biomass-derived
materials as sustainable, high-performance electrode materials for energy storage applications, offering an eco-friendly alternative to
traditional carbon sources while addressing the growing need for efficient and scalable energy storage systems.
KEYWORDS: longan shell-derived porous carbon, nitrogen and oxygen codoping, hydrothermal method, supercapacitor electrode,
sustainable energy storage
1. INTRODUCTION
In light of the escalating global energy demands and constraints,
the scientific community has intensified efforts to harness
renewable energy sources as sustainable alternatives to fossil
fuels. However, conventional renewable energy sources such as
solar, wind, hydropower, and biomass exhibit intermittent
production patterns, leading to challenges in providing a stable
and continuous energy supply year-round.
1−3
As a result, the
introduction of an energy-efficient storage device is required.
Supercapacitors (SCs) have emerged as a promising class of
energy storage devices, effectively bridging the gap between
traditional dielectric capacitors and electrochemical batteries
and enabling efficient energy management due to their unique
ability to deliver rapid charge and discharge, combined with
relatively high energy density and exceptional cycle stability.
4−6
Carbon-based capacitors, specifically electrical double-layer
capacitors (EDLCs), dominate the commercial landscape,
owing to their simplicity, high power density, and better cycle
life.
7,8
Their superior performance is primarily influenced by the
design of the electrode materials, with a focus on the
development of porous channels that facilitate rapid ion
migration while enhancing material conductivity.
9,10
The
operating principle of EDLCs relies on the reversible spatial
separation of electrons and ions at the interface between the
electrode material and the electrolyte, occurring at nanometer-
scale distances under the influence of an applied electric
field.
11,12
In this context, increasing the specific surface area
(SSA) of the electrode material is a critical factor in optimizing
the energy storage and charge−discharge performance of
EDLCs.
Porous carbon has become an ideal electrode material for
EDLCs due to its high SSA, excellent conductivity, and
structural adjustability, which are crucial for enhancing energy
storage capabilities.
13,14
Among the various carbon sources,
biomass-derived carbon materials, such as those obtained from
peels,
15,16
shells,
17,18
timber waste
19,20
and other agricultural
residues, offer a sustainable, cost-effective alternative to
traditional carbon sources while delivering exceptional electro-
chemical performance. Numerous studies have shown that

2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Preparation of Spongy Longan Shell-Derived Porous
Carbon (PC). The PC was prepared through one-step carbonization
and activation of potassium citrate using long-span shell powder. First,
the longan shell was washed with water and ethanol to eliminate
impurities and then dried and finely powdered. Subsequently, a mixture
of 0.5 g of longan shell powder and 1.5 g of potassium citrate was
thoroughly pulverized and transferred to a corundum crucible, followed
by heating in a tube furnace at 800 °C for 2 h under a pure N
2
atmosphere. The final black powdery products were washed with dilute
HCl and deionized water and then dried to obtain a series of PC-x (1, 2,
3, 4) materials, where x denotes the mass ratio of potassium citrate to
longan shell powder.
2.2. Preparation of NOPC by the Hydrothermal Method.
Initially, 1 g of PC was ultrasonically dispersed in 40 mL of 1 M HNO
3
and stirred for 2 h. The suspension was then transferred to a PTFE-lined
hydrothermal kettle and preoxidized at 130 °C for 3 h. The samples
were rinsed with distilled water and dried, yielding a black powder,
designated as OPC-3. Then, 1.5 g of urea and 0.5 g of OPC-3 were
ultrasonically dispersed in 40 mL of distilled water and heat-treated at
180 °C for 10 h with the same operation. The resulting sample was
labeled as N
y
OPC-3, where y denotes the mass ratio of urea to that of
OPC-3. Additionally, nitrogen-doped longan shell-derived porous
carbon without HNO
3
preoxidation treatment, named N
3
PC-3, was
prepared using the same procedure as a control experiment. The
preparation process of the NOPC composites is described in Scheme 1.
Material characterization and electrochemical measurements were
performed as detailed in the Supporting Information.
3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Morphological and Structural Characterizations.
Figure 1 presents the microscopic morphology and pore
structure of PC-x, OPC-3, and N
3
OPC-3, as observed through
scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Longan shell powder
after activation with potassium citrate shows an irregular blocky
structure with a pronounced spongy-porous appearance. As an
organic weak base salt, potassium citrate has an activation
mechanism similar to that of potassium hydroxide, while offering
the added advantages of being nontoxic and having a low
environmental impact during synthesis and processing.
28,29
This
makes it a safer and more sustainable choice for various
applications. The alkali metal component etches both the
surface and interior of the carbon material at elevated
temperatures, creating additional pores. At the same time, the
gases released during pyrolysis, including CO
2
and H
2
O, help to
further enhance the pore formation within the carbon matrix.
Additionally, the organic fraction of potassium citrate
[(C
6
H
5
O
7
)
3−
] acts as an additional carbon source, assisting in
the pyrolysis of the longan shell powder and promoting the
development of a more defined porous structure. The degree of
porosity and the resultant structure formed in the longan shell
powder varied significantly with different mass ratios of
potassium citrate to the precursor. When small amounts of
potassium citrate (1:1 and 2:1) were applied, the resulting
carbon materials exhibited relatively low porosity, with the
surface appearing less developed in terms of pore formation
(Figure 1a,b). In contrast, when the potassium citrate to
precursor mass ratio was increased to 3:1, the resulting carbon
materials exhibited an optimal porous sponge-like morphology
(Figure 1c), which features a unique multiscale porous structure
with a high SSA, facilitating enhanced electrolyte storage,
shortening ion diffusion paths, and promoting electrochemical
processes.
30,31
However, as shown in Figure 1d, an excess of
potassium citrate leads to the formation of a looser and less
structured carbon material resembling stacked carbon nano-
sheets. This structural change is likely due to the excessive
amount of potassium citrate [(C
6
H
5
O
7
)
3−
], which alters the
pyrolysis process, potentially causing the material to undergo
excessive dissolution and reassembly, to the detriment of energy
storage applications. Further, SEM images of the samples
following nitric acid (HNO

doping (Figure 1e,f) reveal that these processes do not
significantly alter the microstructural features of the carbon
materials. Despite these modifications, the overall morphology
of the carbon materials remains largely intact, suggesting that the
structural integrity of the carbon remains well preserved
throughout these post-treatment processes. Additionally,
carbon, nitrogen, and oxygen elements are uniformly distributed
within the N
3
OPC-3 structural framework (Figure 1g−i).

pivotal role in determining capacitive behavior as it allows
electrolyte ions to pass freely and form double electric layers at
the pore walls, thereby significantly augmenting charge storage
capability.

value for N
3

surface- and diffusion-controlled mechanisms contribute to the

dominant role in the electrochemical response of the electrode.
In addition, the quantitative contributions of surface and
insertion capacitances are presented in Figure 4h,i. As the scan

, the proportion of
surface capacitance rises significantly from 58.2% to 88.1%. This
trend can be attributed to the fact that at higher scan rates, ion
diffusion within the electrode material becomes increasingly
restricted, thereby enhancing the dominance of the surface
electrochemical activity.

electrode samples was evaluated through EIS testing with a

4. CONCLUSION
In summary, NOPC has been successfully fabricated, derived
from sustainable longan shells as an electrode material for SCs.
The optimized N
3
OPC-3, synthesized using a combination of
high-temperature activation and hydrothermal doping with urea
and nitric acid, possesses a 3D hierarchical porous structure with
micropore-dominated pore size and nitrogen- and oxygen-rich
functional groups, facilitating efficient ion diffusion and
enhanced Faradaic redox reactions, significantly improving
both energy and power densities. Consequently, N
3
OPC-3
exhibited a high specific capacitance of 463 F·g
−1
at 0.5 A·g
−1
and a long cycling stability of 93.5% (after 10,000 cycles at 200
mV·s
−1
). The EDLC based on N
3
OPC-3 delivered an ultrahigh
capacity of 134 F·g
−1
at 1 A·g
−1
and maintained a high capacity
of 94 F·g
−1
at 10 A·g
−1
. Moreover, the device exhibited a
maximum energy density of 31.9 W h·kg
−1
at a power density of
700 W·kg
−1
and an extremely long cycling performance (89%
capacitance retention after 10,000 cycles at 20 A·g
−1
). These
findings have a certain reference significance for different
biomass precursors used as energy storage materials and provide
a feasible idea for the production of green energy storage
devices.
