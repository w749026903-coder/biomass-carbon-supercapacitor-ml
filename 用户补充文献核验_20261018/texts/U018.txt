 Abstract: Introduction: Supercapacitors have shown substantial promise in electrochemical energy 
storage devices, where porous carbon materials demonstrate exceptional potential applications in 
their electrodes owing to their large specific surface area, high electrical conductivity, and rationally 
tunable pore architectures. 
Methods: Sodium lignosulfonate and graphene oxide-based porous carbon materials (LC/rGO) were 
prepared and characterized. The electrochemical performance of the samples was investigated with 
three-electrode configurations. 
Results: LC/rGO demonstrated mesoporous architecture and excellent electrochemical performance. 
The kinetic analysis on the electrochemical properties of the materials revealed an electric double-
layer capacitance dominated energy storage mechanism. 
Discussion: XRD and Raman analysis on the structures of the as-prepared carbon materials suggest-
ed a relatively high degree of defects and disorder. Investigations on the morphology, the pore size 
distributions and the surface chemistry of the samples demonstrated that the materials had a high 
specific surface area, mesporous structures and multi-atomic doping of nitrogen and oxygen func-
tional groups. All these features could be taken into account for the high electrochemical perfor-
mance of carbon. 
Conclusion: LC/rGO as an electrode material demonstrated a high specific capacitance of 296 F g
-1 
at 0.1 A g -1 and outstanding cycling stability with 97% of the initial capacitance after 10,000 cycles 
at 5 A g -1 in a 6 M KOH electrolyte. The assembl ed symmetric supercapacitor using the as-
synthesized materials exhibited energy density of 10.6 Wh kg -1 at 300 W kg-1 and cycling stability of 
95% capacitance after 10,000 charge-discharge cycles, promising for supercapacitor applications.

 Graphene oxide (GO) tends to stack, signifi-
cantly reducing its specific surface area. Introduc-
ing templates or supporting materials such as car-
bon nanofibers and carbon nanohorns can con-
struct three-dimensional porous structures for the 
diffusion channels of electrolyte ions, improving 
their electrochemical performance [22-24]. Re-
cently, a few studies combining reduced GO with 
lignin to obtain porous carbon materials were re-
ported. With a two-step process involving hydro-
thermal reaction and high-temperature carboniza-
tion, Jiang et al . fabricated graphene-templated 
lignin-derived porous carbon materials for high-
performance supercapacitors. It showed a specific 
capacitance of 330 F g -1 at 1 A g -1, an energy den-
sity of 11.3 Wh kg -1 at a power density of 254 W 
kg-1, and cycling stability of 100% after 10,000 
cycles [25]. 
 In this study, we used sodium lignosulfonate, 
melamine, and graphene oxide to prepare porous 
carbon materials with potassium hydroxide activa-
tion. The carbons as an electrode demonstrated a 
specific capacitance of 296 F g
-1 at 0.1 A g -1 and 
cycling stability with 97% of the initial capaci-
tance after 10,000 cycles at 5 A g
-1 in a 6 M KOH 
electrolyte. The assembled symmetric supercapaci-
tor using the prepared carbon materials exhibited 
an energy density of 10.6 Wh kg
-1 at 300 W kg -1 
and cycling stability with 95% capacitance after 
10,000 charge-discharge cycles.  
2. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
 Sodium lignosulfonate and melamine (Alad-
din), potassium hydroxide (Chengdu Ding Sheng 
Times Technology Co., Ltd.), hydrochloric acid 
and N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (Chengdu Kelong 
Chemicals Co., Ltd.), acetylene black and polyvi-
nylidene fluoride (Taiyuan LiZhiyuan Technology 
Co., Ltd.) were used for the preparation of sam-
ples. All chemicals were of analytical grade and 
used without further purification. 
 Graphene oxide (GO) was prepared according 
to the improved Hummers method. GO was dis-
persed ultrasonically in water (2 mg/mL) for use. 
Five grams of sodium lignosulfonate was added to 
80 mL of deionized water, followed by the addi-
tion of GO. The mixture was stirred for 0.5 hours 
to produce a homogeneous mixture. A total of 3g 
of a dried mixture of sodium lignosulfonate and 
GO was then mixed with melamine and KOH in a 
mass ratio of 1:0.75:0.5. It was then heated in a 
furnace at 800°C for 1 hour under a nitrogen at-
mosphere. The product was soaked in a 2M HCl 
aqueous solution for 12 hours, followed by wash-
ing with water and ethanol. The samples were ob-
tained after drying and named as LC/rGO.  
 The structures of samples were studied by X-
ray diffraction (XRD) with a radiation of the cop-
per target Cu kα (Empyrean) and Raman spectros-
copy (Horiba Scientific LabRAM HR Evolution). 
The morphologies of the samples were analyzed 
by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Hitachi 
S-4800). The pore size distributions of the samples 
were characterized by nitrogen adsorption and de-
sorption at 77 K using a gas sorption analyzer (mi-
cromeritics ASAP 2460).

teristics of the materials as electrodes in aqueous

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
 The XRD pattern of the samples is shown in 
Fig. ( 1). It exhibited a broad diffraction peak at 
23.6° and a weak diffraction peak at 44°, corre-
sponding to the (002) and (100) planes of carbon 
materials, respectively. Raman spectrum of the 
representative samples, as shown in Fig. ( 2), had 
the D and G bands at 1336 cm
-1 and 1590 cm -1, 
respectively, representing the degree of disorder 
and graphitization in the material [26]. The I
D/IG 
value of LC/rGO samples was 1.04, suggesting 
that the carbon materials had a relatively high de-
gree of defects and disorder. The addition of mel-
amine during the activation process was beneficial 
to increase the defect density [27].  
 The morphology and the pore size distributions 
of the samples were studied with FE-SEM and ni-
trogen adsorption and desorption at 77 K, respec-
tively, as shown in Figs.  (3 and 4). The porous 
structures of the carbon materials were observed. 
The activation and gases generated during the de-
composition of melamine at high temperatures 
could create nano-pores, enriching the pore struc-
tures of the carbon materials [28]. The materials 
had a specific surface area of 2011 m
2g-1, and their 
pore size distribution (PSD) indicated mainly 
mesopores of 2-5 nm width. It was worth mention-
ing that the mesoporous structures would be bene-
ficial for ion transport and facilitate the rapid reali-
zation of kinetic reactions [29]. The elemental 
mapping images of LC/rGO samples demonstrated 
that the C, O, N, and S elements were uniformly 
distributed (not shown). Analyzed with XPS, the 
surface chemistry of LC/rGO materials showed 
diffraction peaks at 284.8 eV (C 1s), 531.5 eV (O), 
and 400.1 eV (N), respectively, suggesting the 
doping of O and N elements in the carbon frame-
work, consistent with the elemental mapping. The 
high-resolution N 1s spectrum of LC/rGO revealed 
various nitrogen-containing functional groups, in-
cluding pyridinic nitrogen (N-6, 398.4 eV), pyra-
zolic nitrogen (N-5, 400.1 eV), quaternary nitro-
gen (N-Q, 401.2 eV), and nitrogen oxides (N-x, 
402.9 eV). The high-resolution S 2p spectrum of 
LC/rGO exhibited three fitted peaks corresponding 
to C-S-C (2p
3/2) at 164.0 eV, C-S-C (2p 1/2) at 
165.4 eV, and C-SOx at 168.5 eV. The surface 
composition of LC/rGO was found to be 85.3 at.% 
C, 6.5 at.% O, 7.9 at.% N and 0.3 at.% S. 
 As shown in Fig. ( 5), cyclic voltammetry (CV) 
investigations were performed on the samples, 
with the voltage window set between -1 V and 0 
V, and the scan rate increased from 1 mV s
-1 to 
100 mV s-1. The CV curves of all materials exhib-
ited rectangular shapes, indicating that the capaci-
tance was mainly dominated by the double-layer 
charge storage mechanism [30]. As the scan rate 
increased, the CV curves maintained their shapes, 
suggesting that the materials had excellent ion 
transport capacity and reversibility. This was be-
cause their mesopores removed the restrictions of 
ion storage and diffusion [22, 30, 31]. Additional-
ly, slight humps were observed at -0.8V and  
-0.2V, indicating redox reactions during charge-
discharge processes, which were attributed to the 
pseudo-capacitance caused by heteroatom doping 
[32, 33]. The LC/rGO electrode materials were 
further assembled into an aqueous symmetric su-
percapacitor, using 6 M KOH as the electrolyte, 
with two LC/rGO electrode sheets of the same 
mass serving as the positive and negative elec-
trodes. The galvanostatic charge/discharge (GCD) 
investigation confirmed that the working voltage 
of LC/rGO reached 1.4 V in the symmetric super-
capacitor, which had a specific capacitance of 52.9 
F g
-1 at a current density of 0.5 A g -1. A cyclic 
voltammetry (CV) study with a scan rate of 50 mV 
s
-1 indicated the device had maximum energy den-
sity of 10.6 Wh kg -1 and a corresponding power 
density of 300 W kg -1. When the energy density 
was 7.7 Wh kg -1, the power density reached 5,995 
W kg-1.

STUDY LIMITATIONS 
 Despite the high electrochemical performance 
of the as-prepared materials demonstrated by the 
EDLC symmetric device, the relatively low energy 
density remains one of the main areas of study for 
their practical applications [34-36]. 
CONCLUSION 
 In summary, sodium lignosulfonate and gra-
phene oxide-based porous carbon were prepared 
using a facile method. The materials had a high 
specific surface area and featured mesoporous 
structures and multi-atomic doping of nitrogen and 
oxygen functional groups. Benefiting from their 
mesopores, LC/rGO composites exhibited high 
performance as electrode materials for supercapac-
itors. It demonstrated a specific capacitance of 296 
F g
-1 at a current density of 0.1 A g -1 and showed 
outstanding cycling stability, losing only 3% of its 
initial capacitance after 10,000 cycles. A symmet-
ric supercapacitor with two electrodes of the same 
mass of LC/rGO exhibited an energy density of 
10.6 Wh kg -1 at a power density of 300 W kg -1. 
The excellent electrochemical properties of these 
materials highlight their significant potential for 
applications in sustainable energy resources.
