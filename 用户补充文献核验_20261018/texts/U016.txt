Preparation of Carbon Electrode Material with a Large
Speciﬁc Surface Area and Multiscale Pore Structure from
Biowaste Kochia for Symmetrical Supercapacitor
Kai Fan, Xiping Lei,* Junzhan Zhang,* Ting Yu, Haonan Chen, and Jing Liu
1. Introduction
Recently, the excessive consumption of fossil fuel energy and the
deterioration of the living environment have attracted strong
attention, prompting the scientiﬁc community to turn to research
on renewable green energy such as wind energy, solar energy,
and hydropower, but they all have a common shortcoming that
is intermittent and dif ﬁcult to satisfy today ’s world with increas-
ing demand for energy. [1–3] And with the continuous expansion
of the electric vehicle market, people have increased the demand
for environmentally friendly, renewable resources with the high
power density and high energy density. [4,5] Therefore, to solve
this problem, it is necessary to study sustainable energy produc-
tion and storage technology.
Among various energy storage devices, supercapacitors, as a
very ideal energy storage device have a higher power density than
batteries,[6] a higher energy density than
traditional capacitors, [7] fast charging
speed,[8] and excellent cycle performance, [9]
have been studied. Among them, electrode
materials play a vital role in the capacitance
performance of supercapacitors. Electrode
materials that are currently being studied
extensively include carbon materials, con-
ductive polymers, and metal oxides. [10–12]
Carbon-based materials with electric
double-layer capacitance behavior, such
as activated carbon, have a rich pore struc-
ture, large speci ﬁc surface, and good cycle
stability.[13–15] They have the conditions for
the preparation of excellent electrode mate-
rials and have attracted attention. However,
traditional activated carbon is produced
from coal and its derivatives, which is
non-renewable and its high cost limits its
development. In contrast, to comply with
the global advocacy of the circular economy
and the effective reuse of resources, waste
biomass can be used as the main raw mate-
rial for the preparation of activated carbon. [2] Among them,
plants with high carbon content, speci ﬁc structure and composi-
tion, and renewable nature can be the preferred biomass carbon
source. Through the high-temperature carbonization process,
the lignocellulose in the original plant skeleton is removed to
form a carbon skeleton and a porous structure.[16] However, most
plant-based carbon materials have the disadvantages of low spe-
ciﬁc capacitance, inconspicuous pore structure, and small spe-
ciﬁc surface area (SSA) through simple high-temperature
pyrolysis.[17] Activators such as potassium hydroxide, [18] sodium
hydroxide,[19] and phosphoric acid [20] have been studied to opti-
mize them. The use of chemical activators can ameliorate the
porous structure of different morphologies and optimize the
SSA.[21–23] The improved pore structure will signi ﬁcantly accel-
erate the transport rate of electrolyte ions and have more active
sites. Some studies have developed activated carbon for superca-
pacitor electrode materials by directly carbonizing waste plants.
Yifu Zhang prepared biomass activated carbon by directly car-
bonizing kelp, bamboo leaves, and waste rice husks, not only
achieved waste recycling and reuse to prepare new energy carbon
materials with excellent pore structure but also used carbon
materials with excellent pore structure as a substrate to prepare
composite materials with higher speciﬁc capacitance.[24–27] Other
studies have used a combination of carbonization and KOH acti-
vation, such as Zhanglin Liu used lignocellulose-rich waste

Kochia that is used as Chinese herbal medicine is always abandoned as a
biowaste after extracting useful components. However, the richness of ligno-
cellulose and the natural tubular structure make it a potential source of biomass
carbon. After high-temperature pyrolysis at 800 °C and further etching by proper
activation conditions, a graded porous structured carbon-based supercapacitor
electrode material (KAC-5) can be obtained. The electrode material is prepared in
a 1:5 carbon-to-base ratio, possessing a speci ﬁc surface area of 1441.30 m 2 g/C0 1,
and a graded macro-meso-micro structure that is rich in micropores. The
capacitance retention retains at 90.31% even after 10 000 cycles in 6 M KOH
electrolyte and exhibits outstanding capacitance performance with a speci ﬁc
capacitance of 284 F g /C0 1 at a current density of 1 A g /C0 1. The symmetrical
supercapacitor is assembled with KAC-5 and also has good cycling stability in 6 M
KOH aqueous electrolyte, reaching 88.83% after 10 000 cycles with an energy
density of 12.3 Wh kg /C0 1 at a power density of 300 W kg /C0 1. Based on the above,
sustainable preparation of energy storage material from waste biomass for high
capacitance and stable biomass-based supercapacitors has been achieved.

This article uses the biomass waste collected from the tradi-
tional Chinese herbal medicine Kochia as the raw material. A
large number of Kochia is directly discarded, which leads to a
waste of resources. The lignin, cellulose, and hemicellulose in
the plant are pyrolyzed under high temperatures to retain the
pore structure and the volatile components are discharged to
make the natural tube bundle structure in the original plant more
obvious and stable. Use different proportions of KOH for chem-
ical activation to further optimize the pore structure. The micro-
pore and mesopore structure are fully etched on the original
tube-like macroporous wall to obtain a biomass-activated carbon
electrode material with a hierarchical pore structure, promoting
the storage and rapid transportation of a large number of elec-
trolyte ions, and the increased SSA provides more active sites.
So far, there are only a few reports of using Kochia as electrode
material for supercapacitors. This study explored the in ﬂuence
on the electrochemical performance of electrode materials by
changing the ratio of activators. The electrode material samples
exhibit excellent electrochemical performance, high surface area,
and effective ion diffusion characteristics. We have also explored
the assembly and application of electrode material with electro-
lytes, which is expected to go further in the application of
supercapacitors.
2. Results and Discussion
Figure 1 is a schematic representation of the entire experimental
process including the activation mechanism. The Kochia was car-
bonized at 800 °C making the lignocellulose pyrolysis open mac-
ropores to obtain KC-800. Then chemically etched with different
proportions of KOH and KAC-n was obtained. Usually, KOH is
completely decomposed at 600 °C. During this period, potassium
compounds as a chemical activator with carbon undergo redox
reactions to etch the carbon skeleton. [23,33] The process had
H2O and CO 2 generated for further physical activation.
During the activation process, the potassium ions are effectively
embedded in the lattice of the carbon matrix causing lattice
expansion, which cannot be recovered and therefore results in
a large SSA and high porosity. [34] Finally, a symmetric superca-
pacitor was assembled using KAC-5 as the electrode material
with optimal performance. The experimental section shows
the detailed preparation process.
According to the FESEM image of Kochia in Figure 2 a, the
powder was found to be composed of ﬁbrous lignocellulose
and had a very rough surface with no visible pore structure.
In contrast to it, Figure 2b,c shows the optimal sample that
was ﬁrst carbonized and then activated by KOH because of
the pyrolytic volatilization of lignocellulose, the originally blocked
plant tube structure is exposed, the tubes with macropores at
both ends are completely opened with pore size distribution
in the range of 5-10 μm, and opening tube structure increases
the SSA of the entire material. The exposed macropores can
be used as channels for rapid electrolyte transfer [35] and facilitate
KOH etching of the internal duct walls to form more micropores
and mesopores, thus providing a large number of active sites. [36]
To further investigate the etching of the KAC-5 surface after
KOH treatment, TEM analysis was carried out, and the results
could be seen in Figure 2d. The red circles show some of the
micropores and the mesopores formed by the interconnection
of the etched micropores, and there are a large number of more
evenly distributed micropores and some mesopores on the sur-
face, this is the result of etching the surface of the material by
KOH activation and etching through the macropores into the
tube structure. Figure 2e,f shows the C and O elemental
mapping images of KOH-5, respectively. After the activation
treatment, the surface is composed of only C and O elements,
which indicates that KAC-5 can only be used as an electrode
material for a carbon-based double-layer supercapacitor, but
the presence of some oxygen increases the surface wettability
of the electrode material and provides some pseudo capacitance,
which facilitates the higher electrochemical performance of
supercapacitors in aqueous electrolytes. [31]

To explore the composition of Kochia, the FT-IR spectrum of
Kochia is shown in Figure 2g. The absorption peaks of lignin,
cellulose, and hemicellulose are obvious. The characteristic
absorption peak of lignin occurring at 830 cm /C0 1 is ascribed to
C-H out-of-plane stretching. The peaks at 1624 cm /C0 1 indicate
aromatic skeletal vibrations. Moreover, the peaks centered at
2926 and 3357 cm /C0 1 can be attributed to the C ─H and O ─H
stretching vibration, respectively. [37] The absorption peaks of cel-
lulose can be found at 895, 1038, 1157, and 1375 cm /C0 1, which
correspond to ring vibration, C ─O stretching vibration of gluco-
sidic bond, C ─O asymmetric stretching vibration, and C ─H
bending vibration, respectively. [38] As for hemicelluloses, the
peaks at 1240 and 1733 cm /C0 1 indicate the C ─O asymmetric
vibrations and C =O stretching vibrations of acetyl. [39,40] The
results show that Kochia is composed of lignocellulose. Lignin
in most plant cells acts as a binder to wrap around cellulose
as a skeleton, and hemicellulose acts as a ﬁller to form a stable
structure similar to reinforced concrete in cells. [41,42] Therefore,
lignocellulose can be decomposed to form porous carbon
through high-temperature pyrolysis, and chemical activation
can greatly change the structure of this biomass. The above
conditions indicate that rationally optimizing the components
of Kochia has certain signi ﬁcance for the preparation of target
carbon materials.
To select a suitable carbonization temperature, the TGA curve
of the above sample is observed in Figure 2h. It is mainly divided
into three stages: ﬁrst, continuous decomposition of free water in
the temperature range of 100 °C with about 6.7% of weight loss;
the most obvious decline is the second stage of lignocellulose
decomposition process with 71.9% of weight loss: hemicellulose
is ﬁrst decomposed in the range of 220 to 315 °C, followed by
cellulose in the range of 315 to 400 °C and lignin is completely
decomposed at 550 °C. [43] In addition, the third stage was rela-
tively stable with a weight loss of 21.4% from 550 to 900 °C.
To determine the carbonation temperature, galvanostatic
charge/discharge tests at a current density of 1 A g /C0 1 were car-
ried out on KC-600, KC-700, KC-800, and KC-900, and the high-
est speci ﬁc capacitance of 184 F g /C0 1 with a current density of
1Ag /C0 1 was obtained at the temperature of 800 °C, as shown
in Figure S1, Supporting Information. Therefore, 800 °C was
selected as the carbonization temperature.

adsorption/desorption isotherms were used to test KAC-5.

relative pressure P/P 0 < 0.1, indicating that KOH activation
forms a large number of micropores; [25,44] the existence of hys-
teresis loops and good overlap of the isotherms prove that the
pore size distribution of mesopores is very narrow. The inset

As shown in Figure 3 a from XRD determination, two broad
diffraction peaks at a 2 θ value of approximately 15° and 23° were
observed and ascribed to the diffraction peaks of cellulose and
hemicellulose in Kochia. [46] Diffraction peaks of around 23°
and 43° can be seen in the curves of KC, KAC-4, KAC-5, and
KAC-6, corresponding to the (002) and (100) crystal planes of gra-
phitic carbon, respectively. [47] Compared to KC, the (002) crystal-
line plane of the activated samples is all shifted to the left.
Previous studies have demonstrated the redox reaction between
KOH and carbon during activation to form potassium-containing
compounds as well as the production of gas such as CO 2 further
physical activation affects the extent of defects and produces
more active sites, while the metal potassium ions expand the
space of the atomic carbon layer, inﬂuencing the degree of graph-
itization.[23] Accordingly, further structural information can also
be obtained from the Raman spectra measured in Figure 3b, in
which the independent peaks at 1340 cm /C0 1 (D-band) and
1580 cm/C0 1 (G-band) corresponded to the disordered form of car-
bon materials and graphitic lattice vibrations, respectively. [48]
Assessment of the degree of disorder in carbon materials by
the intensity ratio of the D-band to the G-band. The ID/IG values
for KC, KAC-4, KAC-5, and KAC-6 were 0.96, 0.98, 0.99, and
1.00, indicating an increase in defect density after activation.
The abundant disordered and defective carbon in KAC-n was
beneﬁcial to the improvement of surface area and the number
of activated sites for reactions and promoted ion diffusion and
transportation, and these were all positive for the capacitive per-
formance of carbon materials. [45]
XPS analysis was performed to determine the surface elemen-
tal composition and content of KAC-5. The presence of C and O
can be obtained from Figure 3c. Element C accounted for 91.75%
of the total and the element O accounted for 8.25%. The C 1 s
spectrum of KAC-5 can be ﬁtted into four peaks in Figure 3d,
corresponding to the C ─C (284.8 eV), C ─O (286.4 eV), C = O
(287.9 eV), and O ─C = O (288.9 eV). [49,50] Similarly, in
Figure 3e, the O 1 s spectrum could be ﬁtted with three major
peaks at 530.8, 532.1, and 533.3 eV, representing C = O, C ─O,
and O─C─O, respectively.[27,32,51] Surface wettability of electrode
materials can be improved by the presence of these oxygen-
containing functional groups, as also evidenced by the contact
angle test shown in Figure 3f. A drop of water was transiently
absorbed within 1 s of contacting the porous carbon material,
and the measured contact angle was 58.25°, indicating that
KAC-5 has good hydrophilicity, which is conducive to full contact
between electrolyte and electrode material as a way to generate
more electrochemical reactions. [49]
Due to the porous structure and high SSA of the prepared
samples, performances of KC, KAC-4, KAC-5 and KAC-6 were
ﬁrst evaluated using 6.0 M KOH as the electrolyte in a three-
electrode system. Figure 4a compares the CV curves of this acti-
vated carbon at a scan rate of 50 mV s /C0 1. These curves are almost
always rectangular in shape and are typical of double-layer capaci-
tors.[2,44,52] After activation by KOH, compared to KC, the CV
curves of the others have bulges in the lower potential region
around /C0 0.5 V, which is caused by the oxygen content on the
surface leading to the formation of pseudo-capacitance. The
effect of KOH activation is re ﬂected, KAC-4, KAC-5, and KAC-
6 are accompanied by redox peaks due to the pseudo-capacitance
provided by the presence of oxygen after activation, which makes
the area surrounded by CV curve larger and the electrochemical
performance is better than KC. Compared to the other three
materials, KAC-5 has the largest integral area of the CV curve,
indicating its highest speci ﬁc capacitance. [44,47,53] This result is
also reﬂected in the charge/discharge curves in Figure 4b, where
KAC-5 has the longest discharge time at a current density of
1Ag /C0 1. The symmetrical GCD curves demonstrate the excellent
capacitive performance and high coulomb ef ﬁciency of all four
materials,[10,19] and this suggests that the appropriate carbon-
base ratio is also particularly important for electrochemical per-
formance. According to Equation (1), the speci ﬁc capacitances of
KC, KAC-4, KAC-5, and KAC-6 are 184, 256, 284, and 239 F g /C0 1,
respectively. KAC-5 exhibits the highest speci ﬁc capacitance and
higher than previously reported biomass carbon electrode mate-
rials as shown in Table S1, Supporting Information. Compared
with KC, the surface of activated KAC-5 contains certain oxygen
elements and is more abundant in microporous structure and
mesoporous structure, which will increase the contact area
between the electrode material and the electrolyte, so that the
active site increases, which are particularly important for porous
carbon materials, and re ﬂect a difference of 100 F g /C0 1 before and
after activation in terms of speci ﬁc capacitance. A comparative
multiplier performance graph is made to test the performance
of the material at high current densities, as shown in
Figure S3, Supporting Information. The speci ﬁc capacitance
was further calculated from Equation (1) for current densities
from 0.5 to 10 A g /C0 1. The maximum speci ﬁc capacitance of
the KAC-5 reaches 288 F g /C0 1 (0.5 A g/C0 1), even at high current
densities of 10 A g /C0 1, and KAC-5 still maintains a speci ﬁc capac-
itance of 227 F g /C0 1 with a capacitance retention rate of 78.8%.

to easily light tricolor LED bulbs. These above results demon-
strated that the prepared biomass activated carbon KAC-5 could
be used as excellent electrode materials for supercapacitors.
3. Conclusions
Kochia was ﬁrst used as a precursor in the preparation of
activated carbon material for supercapacitors in this work. The
results showed that the SSA of the electrode material was
increased by opening the natural tube structure originally con-
tained in the plant through the carbonation process, and then
further modulating the pore size distribution to create a multi-
scale pore structure by KOH activation to increase the active sites
and SSA. Its electrochemical performance as an electrode mate-
rial for supercapacitors was superior to that of other previously
reported biomass carbon materials. By adjusting the appropriate
carbon-to-base ratio, KAC-5 has a rich microporous structure
occupying 82.28% of the pore volume, with a SSA of
1441.30 m2 g/C0 1. In the three-electrode system, the maximum
speciﬁc capacitance of KAC-5 reaches 284 F g/C0 1 when the current
density is 1 A g/C0 1, and the capacitance retention for 10 000 cycles
is 90.31%. The energy density of the symmetrical supercapacitor
assembled with KAC-5 as the active material can reach
12.3 Wh kg/C0 1 when the power density is 300 W kg /C0 1. All these
excellent properties are derived from its natural tubular structure
and its rich lignocellulose. This study provides a sustainable
method for the preparation of supercapacitor electrode material
from biomass waste.
4. Experimental Section
Materials: The following materials, Kochia (Deyang, Sichuan Province,
China). potassium hydroxide (KOH, Macklin, AR), hydrochloric acid (HCl,
AR), acetylene black (AB, ECP600JD, LION Ketjenblack), polyvinylidene
ﬂuoride (PVDF, Arkema), and ethanol (C 2H6O, AR), were purchased.
Deionized water was employed directly without further treatment.
Preparation of Activated Carbon : The purchased Kochia was ultrasoni-
cated with absolute ethanol ﬁrst to remove surface impurities, then rinsed
with deionized water, and then placed in an oven for thorough drying.
These dried sticks were crushed and grounded through a 35-mesh sieve.
Then the Kochia powder was kept in a tube furnace at 800 °C for 2 h at a
heating rate of 5 °C min/C0 1,N 2 was used as the protective gas. The samples
were thoroughly washed with deionized water and dried in an oven,
recorded as Kochia carbon (KC). KC and KOH were mixed according to
a mass ratio of 1:5 and a suspension is prepared, and dried it in a
75 °C oven for 12 h. The mixture after drying was activated in a tube fur-
nace at 600 °C under an N 2 atmosphere for 2 h at a heating rate of
5 °C min/C0 1. The product ’s excess KOH and soluble salts were removed
with 1M hydrochloric acid and then washed with deionized water for
neutrality. The resulting carbon material was recorded as Kochia activated
carbon (KAC). For comparison, three different carbon-to-base ratios
(1:4, 1:5, 1:6) were prepared, respectively, as KAC-4, KAC-5, and KAC-6.
Characterization: The powder X-ray diffractometer was performed with
an X-ray diffraction instrument to characterize the crystal structure of sam-
ples (XRD, Bruker D8). Raman spectrometer was used to characterize the
degree of defects and graphitization. (LabRAM HR Evolution). The micro-
scopic morphology of samples was carried out by ﬁeld-emission scanning
electron microscope (FESEM, GeminiSEM500). The distribution of ele-
ments in the samples was determined through energy-dispersive spectros-
copy (EDS, GeminiSEM500). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM,
Talos F200X) was performed to observe the microstructures of the
obtained products. The SSA and pore size distribution of the analyzed
samples were tested by using a nitrogen adsorption meter (BET,
Micromeritics APSP 2460). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy was per-
formed to analyze chemical composition (XPS, ESCALAB 250 electron
spectrometer). The Fourier transform infrared was used to characterize
the type of functional group of the raw material (FTIR, ISQ 7000
Nicolet iS50 TGA/DSC3).
Electrochemical Performance : The prepared carbon material, polyvinyli-
dene ﬂuoride, and acetylene black were thoroughly mixed in a mass ratio of
8:1:1 to form a slurry, the slurry was coated on the foamed nickel with a
size of 1 cm /C2 1 cm and dried at 75 °C after 1 h, and they were pressed at
10 MPa for 1 min. The three-electrode system was assembled with pressed
electrode sheets as working electrodes, and the Hg/HgO electrode was
used as the reference electrode and the platinum sheet as the counter
electrode. The single-electrode sheet active material load of the three-
electrode system was 3 mg cm /C0 2, with 6 M KOH as the electrolyte.
CHI605E electrochemical workstation for cyclic voltammetry (CV) tests
was recorded from /C0 1.0 to 0 V at a scan rate of 5, 10, 20, 50,
100 mV s/C0 1, and galvanostatic charge –discharge (GCD) tests were
recorded from /C0 1.0 to 0 V at a current density of 1,2,3,5,10 A g /C0 1.
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests in the frequency
range 0.01 HZ –100 kHz with an amplitude of 5 mV were performed on
a CHI660E electrochemical workstation.
