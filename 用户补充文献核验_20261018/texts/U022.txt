Abstract
An appealing choice for energy storage system that merges the advantages of
zinc-ion battery with supercapacitor is the zinc-ion hybrid supercapacitor
(ZHS). Carbonaceous materials have recently received significant attention as
the viable cathode material for ZHS. Carbon-based materials having large sur-
face area and suitable pore structure exhibit fast kinetics and good charge stor-
age behavior in ZHS. Carbonization temperature plays a vital role in
optimizing surface area and developing the pore structure of the carbon. Acti-
vated carbon is successfully prepared from teak wood at different carboniza-
tion temperatures of /C24 600
/C14 C, 750 /C14 C, and 900 /C14 C, followed by KOH activation.
The teak wood-derived material carbonized at /C24 750/C14 C (ATC2) shows the high-
est specific surface area of /C24 898.16 m2 g/C0 1. Fabricated ZHS with ATC2 as cath-
ode delivers the highest specific capacitance of /C24 116 F g /C0 1 at 0.5 A g /C0 1 and
exhibits outstanding capacitance retention of /C24 102.8% after 10 000 continuous
galvanostatic charge-discharge cycles recorded at 10 A g /C0 1. In addition, the
device shows /C24 42 W h kg /C0 1 energy density at /C24 408 W kg /C0 1 power density.
The fabricated ZHS can power an LED and a digital clock, suggesting its
potential application as energy storage device.

1 | INTRODUCTION
Demand for renewable energy has been increasing expo-
nentially to mitigate the issues of global warming, rapid
depletion of fossil fuel reservoir, and continuous require-
ment of power supply for our daily needs. Solar, wind,
biomass, tidal, and so on are the different sources of
renewable energy but practical applications still impose
several challenges due to the lack of suitable technologi-
cal intervention. Electrochemical energy storage devices
show remarkable impotency to store renewable energy.
Different configurations of energy storage devices show
different advantages and disadvantages. Among these,
supercapacitor is considered as the compatible energy
storage device due to quick charging-discharging, excel-
lent cyclic life, and wide temperature tolerance capabil-
ity. However, this device suffers from lower energy
density compared to the batteries. Batteries show higher
energy density but lower power density.
1-4 The major dif-
ference between batteries and supercapacitors arises due
to the different energy storage mechanisms. Diffusion-
controlled redox reactions in batteries help to store large
amounts of energy with long discharge time but, hinder
in achieving high power density. In contrast, supercapa-
citors store energy through EDLC mechanism. Therefore,
battery and capacitor-like electrodes are ensemble to

16-18 Nowadays, multivalent

lent metal-ion hybrid capacitors. These multivalent metal
ions are less reactive, naturally abundant, highly safe, cost-
effective, and exhibit high volumetric energy density.
Manufacturing of these metal ion-based HSCs has become
easier due to these advantages.

Among these, Zn metal ion supercapacitors (ZHS) have
attracted more attention compared to the other metal
electrodes due to the high volumetric capacity of

/C0 0.76 V (Zn/Zn 2+) (vs SHE), long cycle stability, safety,
economy, and environmental acceptance. 24-26 Al a r g e
number of cathode materials are available for ZHS such as
carbon-based materials (activated Carbon, graphene, CNT,
etc), conducting polymers, Mxenes, and many other metal
oxides, nitrides, and so on.
27-31 A greater contribution of
cathode materials is an essential aspect of ZHS. More
investigation is being pursued to improve the performance
of cathode materials. Appropriate adsorption/desorption of
electrolyte ions prefer a high specific surface area and suit-
able pore structure of the electrode material, which in turn
raises the EDLC performance and cycling performance of
the material. Many efforts are being taken for tuning mor-
phology and structure of the cathode materials. Porous
activated carbons (ACs) are extensively used as cathode
materials for ZHS due to larger surface area, controllable
porosity, various morphology, and low resistivity of elec-
tricity. Wang et al. fabricated a device employing coconut
cell-derived AC as the cathode material with Zinc foil
anode. The device delivered /C24 170 F g
/C0 1 capacitance at
0.1 A g /C0 1 current density with considerable energy den-
sity (52.7 W h kg /C0 1) and /C24 91% capacitance retention
after 20 000 GCD cycles at 2 A g /C0 1 current density in
organic electrolyte. 32 Dong et al. used 2 M ZnSO 4 electro-
lyte in AC cathode-based ZHS. Fabricated device showed
/C24 121 mAh g /C0 1 capacity with 0.2-1.8 V voltage window. 33
Chen et al. fabricated flexible solid-state ZHS by using
carbon spheres as cathode and zinc-deposited carbon
cloth as anode with polyacrylamide hydrogel electrolyte.
The flexible solid-state ZHS showed the highest capacity
of /C24 86.8 mA h g
/C0 1 and /C24 98% capacity retention beyond
15 000 cycles at a current density of 1.0 A g /C0 1. The maxi-
mum energy density was /C24 59.7 W h kg /C0 1 with the power
density of /C24 447.8 W kg /C0 1.34 Performance of ZHS can be
enhanced by controlling pore structure, surface area, and
morphology of AC. The carbonization temperature for
producing porous activated carbon can affect the electro-
chemical activity of the materials. Usually, carbonization
and activation are the two common steps in the prepara-
tion of activated carbon. In the carbonization process,
pyrolysis of carbonaceous organic compound takes place
and pores are generated due to the escape of non-carbon
elements in the form of volatiles. So, the carbonization
temperature is related directly to the pore structure.
Therefore, optimizing the carbonization temperature tai-
lors the pore structure and surface area of porous car-
bon.
35,36 Many researchers reported the preparation of
activated carbon from rice husk, almonds, cocoa pods,
Prosopis juliflora , peanut shells, coffee beans, and many
biomass sources. 37-39 But, there is still a need to find and
investigate the alternative sources that can be easily regu-
lated structurally during the synthesis process. Herein,
teak wood was employed as the carbon source and KOH
as an activating agent to prepare activated carbon. Teak
wood is native to India, Myanmar, and Thailand; it is used
in many areas, such as shipbuilding, fine furniture, door
and window, flooring, paneling, Venetian blinds, and so
on. So, teak wood waste is an abundant material from the
wood industry; thus, it is the low-cost bulk material. Teak
wood-derived activated carbon (ATC) carbonized at differ-
ent temperatures showed different surface area and pore
structure which directly impacted the materials' electro-
chemical activity. The fabricated ZHS device delivered
/C24 116 F g
/C0 1 capacitance at 0.5 A g /C0 1 current density with
potential window of 0.2-1.8 V. Further, ZHS displayed
/C24 42 W h kg /C0 1 of energy density and /C24 408 W kg/C0 1 of
power density at 0.5 A g /C0 1 current density. The energy
density decreased only by half of its initial value even at
10-fold increase in power density. The ZHS exhibited
/C24 102.8% capacity retention after 10 000 GCD cycles at
10 A g /C0 1 current density. ATC2 ZHS could power LED
and digital clock demonstrating its practical applications.

2 | EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1 | Synthesis of activated carbon from
teak wood
Teak tree branches were collected from our Institute's
Garden. The gathered branches were chopped into little
pieces before washing and drying for 2 days at /C24 60/C14 C.
Small pieces were hammered and grinded into a fine
powder using mixer grinder. The obtained powder was
carbonized at different temperatures of 600 /C14 C, 750 /C14 C,
and 900 /C14 C inside tube furnace under N 2 atmosphere at a
heating rate of 5 /C14 C min/C0 1. Carbonized powder was mixed
with aqueous KOH solution (Mass ratio of carbonized
powder: KOH /C24 1:4) and stirred overnight, followed by
washing. The dried powder was heated at /C24 850/C14 C inside
a tube furnace under N 2 atmosphere at 5 /C14 C min /C0 1 heat-
ing rate for 120 min. Finally, the black powder was thor-
oughly washed with dilute HCl and DI water through
centrifugation until pH became neutral for removing
metallic potassium (Supporting Information,
S1). After
washing, it was filtered and dried at 60 /C14 C. The nomencla-
ture of the AC powders is shown in Table 1. The sche-
matic of the procedure for the preparation of ATC is
shown in Figure
1. Characterization details are described
in Supporting Information (Sections S5 and S6).

4 | CONCLUSIONS
In brief, ATC was prepared via two steps: carbonization
and activation process of teak wood derived carbon. Vari-
ations in carbonization temperature directly affected the
surface area and pore structure. Mesoporous ATC2 exhib-
ited highest specific surface area of /C24 898.168 m
2 g/C0 1 and
delivered better electrochemical performance compared
to ATC1 and ATC3. Accessible mesopores with high sur-
face area facilitated effective charge storage and fast ion
transport. ATC2 ZHS delivered specific capacitance of
/C24 116 F g
/C0 1 at 0.5 A g /C0 1 current density and showed out-
standing cyclic stability of /C24 102.8% of its initial capaci-
tance retained even after 10 000 GCD cycles at 10 A g /C0 1
current density. This work showed the role of carboniza-
tion temperature for the preparation of activated carbon
from teak wood source for suitable cathode material of
ZHS as energy storage device and confirmed its use as a
potential electrode material for renewable energy storage
devices.
