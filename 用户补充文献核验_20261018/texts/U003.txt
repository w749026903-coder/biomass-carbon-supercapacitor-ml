ABSTRACT: High-surface-area porous carbon electrodes that
feature a well-balanced pore distribution and facilitate rapid ion
transport are crucial for enhancing electric double-layer capacitors.
Conventional phenolic resin-derived carbons are usually dominated
by micropores and show limited pore connectivity, which restricts
electrolyte infiltration and rate performance. Herein, we report a
cation-dependent salt-templating strategy by introducing equimolar
ZnCl
2
, MgCl
2
, and MnCl
2
into a resorcinol−hexamethylenetetr-
amine hydrothermal system. The three divalent cations direct
markedly different carbon-framework evolution. Zn
2+
mainly
promotes micropore formation and smaller carbon spheres, Mg
2+
favors particle fusion and larger pores, whereas Mn
2+
converts discrete microsphere assemblies into a continuous three-dimensional
hierarchical porous network. Among the resulting samples, PC-Mn exhibits a specific surface area of 1459 m
2
g
−1
, a total pore
volume of 1.12 cm
3
g
−1
, and a micropore surface area measuring 1105 m
2
g
−1
, along with well-developed mesoporous channels
ranging from 2 to 4 nm. When tested in 6 M KOH, PC-Mn reaches a capacitance of 270.15 F g
−1
and maintains 182.49 F g
−1
at a
current density of 20 A g
−1
, achieving a capacitance retention of 95.6% following 10,000 cycles. Furthermore, the PC-Mn symmetric
supercapacitor exhibits a specific capacitance of 252.04 F g
−1
and an energy density of 21.24 Wh kg
−1
at a power density of 20,123 W
kg
−1
. These results demonstrate that regulating the divalent cation species in salt-templated phenolic resin systems provides an
effective approach for tailoring hierarchical pore structures and improving the electrochemical performance of porous carbon
electrodes.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid development of renewable energy systems, portable
electronics, and advanced energy-storage technologies has
increased the demand for electrochemical energy-storage
devices with high power density, fast charge−discharge
capability, and long cycle life.
1−4
Electric double-layer
capacitors (EDLCs) store charge primarily through the
reversible adsorption and desorption of electrolyte ions at
the electrode−electrolyte interface. Their high power capa-
bility, rapid response, and excellent cycling stability make them
attractive for applications requiring fast energy delivery.
However, their energy-storage performance is strongly
influenced by the surface area, pore architecture, and ion-
transport characteristics of the electrode materials.
1,2,5,6
Porous
carbons are among the most widely investigated electrode
materials for EDLCs because of their high chemical stability,
tunable pore structures, good electrical conductivity, and
relatively low cost. An effective porous carbon electrode should
combine abundant micropores for ion adsorption with
mesopores and macropores that facilitate electrolyte infiltration
and ion transport.
2,5,7
Therefore, rational control of the pore
architecture is essential for achieving both high charge-storage
capacity and rapid electrochemical kinetics.
Among the different carbon precursors, phenolic resins are
attractive for porous carbon fabrication due to their control-
lable cross-linked structure, favorable carbon yield, excellent
thermal stability, and customizable morphology.
8−11
Previous
studies have shown that resorcinol−formaldehyde or phenolic
resin-derived carbons can form hierarchical porous structures
through polymer-network design, template assistance, or
activation treatment, leading to promising electrochemical
performance in supercapacitors. Nevertheless, conventional
phenolic resin-derived carbons often contain a dense frame-
work dominated by micropores. Their insufficient pore
connectivity restricts ion diffusion at high current densities
and causes rate performance degradation.
7,10
To address this
issue, KOH activation, hard templating, and soft templating
have been widely adopted.
2,12−15
Yet, strong alkali activation
usually involves corrosive reagents, complicated post-treat-

These limitations motivate the development
of simpler and more controllable pore-engineering strategies
for phenolic resin-derived porous carbons.
Salt templating has emerged as an effective strategy for
tuning the pore structure of porous carbons. Compared with
conventional hard templates such as silica, inorganic salts are
generally low-cost, thermally stable, removable, and potentially
recyclable. During carbonization, they can act as space-
confined templates, molten media, or local activating agents
to guide carbon framework evolution.
19−21
Previous studies
have shown that single-salt, molten-salt, and multicomponent-
salt systems can generate porous carbons with high surface
areas and hierarchical pore structures by regulating the
interactions between salt phases and carbon precur-
sors.
12−14,17−19
These studies demonstrate that salt templating
can serve not only as a pore-forming approach but also as a
means of regulating carbon-framework evolution and mass-
transport characteristics.
Despite these advances, the role of individual metal cations
in regulating the evolution of phenolic resin-derived carbon
remains insufficiently understood. Most previous studies have
focused on salt dosage, salt composition, carbon precursor, or
carbonization conditions, with relatively limited attention to
how the identity of the metal cation affects precursor
polymerization, phase separation, carbon-framework forma-
tion, and pore reconstruction.
19,21
Moreover, the use of
multicomponent salt systems or combined hard-template/
salt-template strategies can make it difficult to distinguish the
contribution of an individual cation. Therefore, systematic
comparisons of different divalent metal cations under
otherwise comparable phenolic resin hydrothermal conditions
are particularly valuable.
12,13,22,23
In this study, ZnCl
2
, MgCl
2
, and MnCl
2
were selected as
representative divalent metal chlorides for three consider-
ations. First, the three salts possess the same chloride anion
and metal cations with the same +2 valence state, which
enables a more controlled comparison while minimizing the
influence of anion type and cation valence. Second, Zn
2+
, Mg
2+
,
and Mn
2+
exhibit distinct coordination characteristics,
hydration behavior, Lewis acidity, and thermal behavior,
which may lead to different interactions with the resorcinol-
HMTA polymerization system and subsequent carbon-frame-
work evolution. Third, these chlorides are readily available and
can be uniformly introduced into the aqueous precursor
system. Therefore, an equimolar comparison of these three
salts provides a suitable model system for investigating cation-
dependent regulation of phenolic resin-derived porous
carbons. These characteristics make the three cations a suitable
model system for investigating cation-dependent regulation of
carbon-framework evolution and pore formation.
23,24
Accordingly, equimolar ZnCl
2
, MgCl
2

2
were
separately introduced into a resorcinol−hexamethylenetetr-

smaller carbon spheres and micropores, Mg
2+
favors particle

nected porous framework. The resulting PC-Mn exhibits a

Synthesis of Salt-Templated Phenolic Eesin-Derived
Porous Carbons
Figure 1a illustrates the synthetic procedure of the samples. Briefly,
resorcinol (1.515 g) and hexamethylenetetramine (1.92 g) were
dissolved in 50 mL of deionized water under continuous stirring at
room temperature to form a homogeneous solution. Subsequently,
0.06 mol of ZnCl
2
, MgCl
2
·6H
2
O, or MnCl
2
·4H
2
O was slowly added
to the solution under an ice bath, followed by sufficient stirring to
minimize excessive heat release and premature polymerization. An
equimolar amount of each metal chloride was used to enable a
controlled comparison of the effects of the different divalent cations.
After extensive mixing, the solution was transferred to a 100 mL
stainless-steel autoclave lined with Teflon and heated to 150 °C for 12
h. Once it cooled to room temperature on its own, the resulting
polymer was separated by centrifugation, washed three times with
ethanol and deionized water, and then dried in a vacuum oven at 90
°C overnight. The carbonization of the dried polymer precursor was
subsequently carried out in a tubular furnace with a continuous flow
of N
2
. The temperature was raised to 800 °C at a rate of 5 °C min
−1
and kept constant for 2 h. Following the carbonization process, the
resulting powder was treated with 3 M HNO
3
for 24 h to eliminate
any remaining inorganic substances. Ultimately, the final product was
gathered through filtration, carefully rinsed with deionized water until
achieving neutrality, and then dried under vacuum to yield the desired
porous carbon.
According to the metal salts introduced during the hydrothermal
process, the resulting porous carbon samples were denoted as PC-Zn,
PC-Mg, and PC-Mn, respectively. For comparison, a blank sample
was prepared following the same hydrothermal and carbonization
procedures without the addition of any metal chloride. To ensure a
consistent post-treatment protocol, the carbonized blank sample was
also treated with 3 M HNO
3
for 24 h, washed thoroughly with
deionized water until neutral, and vacuum-dried under the same
conditions. The resulting sample was denoted as PC.
Characterizations
Characterization employed a field-emission scanning electron micro-
scope (FE-SEM) made by ZEISS, namely the Sigma500 model.
Imaging via transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was conducted
using a Talos F200X microscope, which functioned at an acceleration
voltage of 100 kV. The nitrogen (N
2
) adsorption and desorption
isotherms were assessed at 77 K with a Micromeritics 3Flex surface
area analyzer. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded
utilizing a SmartLab diffractometer that operated with graphite-
monochromated Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.54178 Å). For the X-ray
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis, data were collected with a
Thermo Scientific ESCALAB 250Xi spectrometer that employed Al
Kα excitation (hν = 1486.6 eV). The collection of Raman spectra was
performed using a Thermo Scientific DXR2 Raman microscope,
which was equipped with a 532 nm laser as the source of excitation.
The residual metal contents in the acid-washed carbon samples were
quantitatively determined by inductively coupled plasma mass
spectrometry (ICP-MS, Agilent 7900, Agilent Technologies).
Electrochemical Measurements
Electrochemical measurements were carried out using a workstation
from ChenHua Instruments. To prepare the working electrodes, the
active carbon was mixed with PTFE binder and acetylene black at a
mass ratio of 80:10:10 and thoroughly ground to obtain a
homogeneous slurry. The slurry was subsequently coated onto nickel
foam current collectors with a geometric area of 1 cm

10 MPa for 3 min. The mass loading of active material was
approximately 3−5 mg per electrode. In the three-electrode
configuration, a platinum sheet and Hg/HgO electrode were used
as the counter and reference electrodes, respectively. For the two-

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
SEM images were first used to examine the morphological
evolution of the carbon materials. As shown in Figure 1b,c, the
salt-free PC consists predominantly of relatively well-dispersed
microspheres with smooth surfaces, suggesting that the
phenolic resin precursor undergoes a conventional nuclea-
tion-and-growth process under the hydrothermal conditions.
27
In contrast, obvious morphological differences appeared after
the introduction of different divalent metal chlorides.
After introducing ZnCl
2
, the carbon spheres become smaller
and exhibit rougher surfaces, accompanied by partial particle
aggregation (Figure 1d,1e). This morphological change
suggests that Zn
2+
modifies the polymerization and phase-
separation behavior of the precursor, thereby suppressing
excessive sphere growth.
27,28
In contrast, PC-Mg retains a
spherical morphology but exhibits a broader particle-size
distribution and pronounced interparticle fusion (Figure
1f,1g), suggesting that Mg
2+
favors particle connection,
coalescence, and secondary growth.
A markedly different morphology is observed for PC-Mn.
Rather than forming discrete spherical aggregates, the
precursor evolves into a continuous and interconnected
three-dimensional framework after MnCl
2
regulation (Figure
1h,1i). Such a continuous framework is expected to provide
interconnected pathways for electrolyte penetration and ion
transport.
Overall, these observations demonstrate that the identity of
the divalent cation strongly influences the morphological
evolution of the phenolic resin-derived carbon. Zn
2+
mainly
induces sphere refinement and surface roughening, Mg
2+

To further clarify how metal cations regulate the micro-
structure of phenolic resin-derived carbon, the crystal structure
and defect characteristics of the samples were first examined.
Figure 2a illustrates that all analyzed samples exhibit two
distinct diffraction peaks. These peaks are broad and of
relatively low intensity, occurring at approximately 2θ = 23.8°
and 43.6°. The (002) and (100) planes of disordered carbon
are represented by these particular angles, which signifies that
this material is present in the samples. This indicates that the
introduction of different metal salts does not change the overall
amorphous nature of the phenolic resin-derived carbon.
Raman spectra provide further evidence for this structural
feature. As shown in Figure 2b, each sample exhibits distinct D
and G bands around 1350 and 1580 cm
−1

to areas of disordered carbon and structural defects, whereas
the G band is linked to sections containing sp

carbon. These results suggest that the salt-regulated carbons
are still dominated by disordered carbon frameworks, while
retaining a certain degree of local sp
2
carbon structure. Such
defect-rich frameworks can increase the surface activity of the
electrodes and provide more accessible interfaces for electro-
lyte ion adsorption and transport.

CONCLUSION
In this work, a cation-dependent salt-templating strategy was
developed to regulate the structural evolution of phenolic
resin-derived porous carbons using equimolar ZnCl
2
, MgCl
2
,
and MnCl
2
. The results demonstrate that the identity of the
divalent cation strongly influences the morphology and pore
architecture of the resulting carbons. Zn
2+
favors the formation
of smaller carbon spheres and micropores, Mg
2+
promotes
particle fusion and larger-pore development, whereas Mn
2+
facilitates the formation of a continuous three-dimensional
interconnected porous framework.
Among the resulting samples, PC-Mn exhibits the most
favorable combination of microporous charge-storage sites and
interconnected mesoporous transport pathways, leading to
enhanced charge-storage kinetics and rate capability. In 6 M
KOH, PC-Mn delivers a specific capacitance of 270.15 F g
−1
at
0.5 A g
−1
and retains 182.49 F g
−1
at 20 A g
−1
. The Dunn
analysis further indicates a predominantly capacitive charge-
storage behavior at high scan rates. In the symmetric two-
electrode configuration, PC-Mn also exhibits favorable
energy−power characteristics. Overall, this work demonstrates
that controlling the identity of the divalent cation provides an
effective route for tailoring the pore architecture and
electrochemical kinetics of phenolic resin-derived porous
carbons.
