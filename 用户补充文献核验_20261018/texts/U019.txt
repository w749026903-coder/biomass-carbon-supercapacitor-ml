Sustainable Development of Interconnected Hierarchical 
Porous Carbon From Rice Panicle Waste: Synergistic 
KOH/CO2 Dual- Activation Strategy for Advanced 
Symmetric Supercapacitors
Awitdrus Awitdrus  |  Alifia Andra Sakinah  |  Rakhmawati Farma  |  Iwantono Iwantono  |   
Agustino Agustino

Keywords: CO2 activation | KOH activation | porous activated carbon | rice panicles | symmetric supercapacitor
ABSTRACT
Herein, agricultural waste of rice panicles was adopted as a raw precursor to prepare PAC using KOH activation at different 
concentrations (0, 0.25, 0.50, and 0.75 M) coupled with CO 2 activation at 850°C and its implementation as symmetric su -
percapacitor electrodes. RPPAC-  0.25-  850 exhibited high S BET, hierarchical porosity, and a high degree of graphitization. At 
1 A g−1, RPPAC-  0.25-  850 attained a gravimetric capacitance of 280 F g−1, Coulombic efficiency of 86.53%, and an impressive 
energy density of 38.95 Wh kg−1 at a power density of 413.5 W kg−1. Likewise, the RPPAC-  0.25-  850-  based symmetric SC de -
vice exhibited high GCD cycling stability, retaining 88% capacitance at 10 A g−1 after 6000 GCD cycles, suggesting superior 
capacitance efficiency. The results suggest that agricultural waste can be a sustainable energy storage solution, with optimal 
KOH activation conditions for exceptional SC performance, and also offers a viable potential route for greener, more efficient 
energy solutions.
1   |   Introduction
Regarding the enormous amount of fossil fuel usage and the 
consequent negative impacts on the environment, renewable 
energy is expected to initiate a transformational transition in en-
ergy fabrication and consumption [ 1–3]. To solve these pressing 
challenges, renewable energy sources such as geothermal, tidal, 
solar, and wind have appeared as viable, inexpensive, and eco -
logically benign alternatives [ 4, 5]. Nevertheless, since these re -
newable sources are intermittent, with insufficient consistency 
or seasonal changes, the development of effective energy storage 
devices is required to maintain a balanced energy supply and 
demand [6, 7]. Conventional energy storage technologies have 
various drawbacks, including expensive costs, limited lifespans, 
and environmental problems, highlighting the need for modern 
and sustainable energy storage alternatives [ 8]. In this regard, 
supercapacitors (SCs) are recognized as attractive energy stor -
age choices owing to their capacity to store and release energy 
quickly, rendering them ideal for use in transportation (electric 
vehicles) [9], consumer electronics (smartphones, laptops, and 
wearables) [10], power backup systems [11, 12], grid stabilization 
[13], and energy storage [9].
To address the rising future energy system demands, such 
as portable devices and hybrid electric cars, considerable 
advances in SC technology are required. These include con -
structing contact between electrode/electrolyte interfaces, 
optimizing working conditions, and investigating various 
electrolyte concentrations and types. High-  performance elec -
trode materials with excellent rate performance, balanced

Conventional electrode materials, such as ceramics [ 15], poly-

shown promising results; however, their prevalent practical 
usage is hampered by issues such as nonbiodegradability, bi -
ological incompatibility, high cost, extraction challenges, tox -
icity, high-  temperature resistance restrictions, and durability 
issues [19].
Carbonaceous materials, like activated carbon [ 20–22 ], 
graphene [ 23–25], carbon nanotubes [ 26, 27 ], and hydrogel-  
derived nanoporous carbon [ 28], are promising candidates for 
SC electrode materials owing to their high specific surface 
area (SSA), electrical conductivity, and tailored applications. 
Carbonaceous materials made from renewable resources 
align with sustainability concerns and reduce environmen -
tal effects and are gaining popularity for their potential [ 29]. 
Among carbon-  based materials, porous activated carbons 
(PAC) are particularly promising because of their high acces -
sibility, the potential for developing their SSA, and their large 
feedstock base [ 11].
The energy storage performance of PAC electrodes in SCs is 
mainly driven by an electric double-  layer capacitance (EDLC) 
mechanism, in which charge is stored via reversible phy -
sisorption of electrolyte ions at the carbon/electrolyte inter -
face rather than through Faradaic processes, thereby enabling 
rapid charge–discharge kinetics and exceptional cycling sta -
bility [ 11, 30 ]. Because this mechanism depends critically on 
ion accessibility to the internal surface, the pore-  size distribu -
tion and overall pore architecture are as decisive for capacitive 
performance as the SSA. Micropores (< 2 nm) contribute the 
most to the surface area and their dimensions approach those 
of the desolvated electrolyte ions, substantially enhancing the 
capacitance; however, an exclusively microporous framework 
often impedes ion diffusion and constrains rate capability 
[31]. In contrast, meso-  and macropores act as ion-  buffering 
reservoirs and transport channels that shorten diffusion 
pathways to the microporous surfaces, thus improving rate 
performance and power density despite contributing less ca -
pacitance per unit area [ 32]. Consequently, an interconnected 
hierarchical pore architecture that integrates abundant micro -
porosity with a complementary meso–/macroporous network 
is commonly recognized as the optimal structural motif for 
high- performance SC electrodes, as it balances the competing 
requirements of high specific capacitance, rapid ion transport, 
and mechanical stability [ 32, 33].
Biomass- derived porous activated carbons (BPAC) are among 
the most promising materials for SCs because they are gener -
ated from abundant, varied, and inexpensive renewable green 
resources. The conventional technique to generate BPAC in -
volves precarbonization with a physical or chemical activa -
tion procedure [ 34]. Various BPAC materials from agricultural 
residues have been studied as SC electrode materials, such as 
Prunus persica  seed stones [ 20], rosehip wood [ 35], pecan nut -
shells [36], Zanthoxylum armatum  seed [ 37], Persea americana  
leaf [38], Hevea brasiliensis  seed shell [39], Strychnos potatorum  
seeds [40], cabbage [41], rice husks [42–44], jackfruit waste [45], 
and so on. These investigations revealed that biomass waste can 
be converted into high- value carbon materials for energy storage 
systems.
In this work, rice panicles (RP) as postharvest waste products 
were chosen as carbon sources for the fabrication of PAC materi-
als and their implementation as electrode materials for symmet-
ric SCs. The preparation process involved base activation using 
varying KOH concentrations (0, 0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 M) followed 
by CO 2 activation at elevated temperatures using the single- 
route approach. This single-  route activation technique offers 
several advantages, such as (i) it transforms agricultural waste 
into valuable energy storage materials, (ii) it yields activated 
carbons with unique morphologies and large SSA, and (iii) it 
employs a straightforward preparation method. Unlike the pro -
tocols commonly used to prepare BPAC, in which carbonization, 
chemical activation, and physical activation are carried out as 
separate steps, the KOH and CO2 activations in the present work 
were combined into a single continuous thermal route. This syn-
ergistic dual- activation design shortened the overall processing 
time and chemical consumption relative to stepwise routes with 
sequential chemical- then- physical activation. Furthermore, this 
strategy valorized RP, an abundant postharvest waste product, 
unlike rice husk and other commonly reported biomass pre -
cursors, which remain underexplored as SC electrodes. The re -
sulting rice panicle-  derived porous activated carbon (RPPAC) 
demonstrated a high SSA, an optimized pore structure, and 
favorable electrochemical properties. Systematic studies of the 
electrochemical parameters of the prepared RPPAC in a two- 
electrode configuration provide insights into its performance, 
thereby establishing a clean, effective, and sustainable approach 
for producing carbon materials from renewable agricultural 
waste sources.
2   |   Experimental Parts
2.1   |   Chemicals and Reagents
The initial stage involves the collection of RPs biomass from 
Kenagarian Ladang Laweh, Banuhampu District, Agam 
Regency, West Sumatra, Indonesia. Potassium hydroxide (KOH, 
Smart- Lab Indonesia), sulfuric acid (H 2SO4, Sigma −Aldrich, 
USA) were purchase from the CV. Pulo Batu, Pekanbaru 
Indonesia. The pure N2 and CO2 gas were supplied from CV. Ajb 
Gastan Gas, Pekanbaru, Indonesia.
2.2   |   Preparation of RPPACs
The process of fabricating PACs from RP waste involves sev -
eral critical stages that contribute to the overall effectiveness 
and efficiency of the final product. Initially, the RPs were cut 
into smaller pieces, approximately 2–3 cm in size. This size re -
duction is essential for facilitating uniform drying and subse -
quent processing. The cut RPs were then sun-  dried for 4 days 
to reduce their moisture content. After drying, the panicle 
underwent precarbonization at 250°C for 2 h under vacuum 
conditions. This step initiates the breakdown of organic mate -
rials and begins the transformation into carbon-  rich material, 
which is essential for producing high-  quality PAC materials. 
Once precarbonization was complete, the material was ground

The activation process involved treating the powder with po -
tassium hydroxide (KOH) at varying concentrations: 0.25, 0.5, 
and 0.75 M. KOH acts as a chemical agent that improves the 
SSA and porosity of the PACs, which is critical for SC perfor -
mance. KOH activation was performed on a hot plate while 
stirring with a magnetic stirrer at 80°C for 2 h and a spin rate 
of 350 rpm. The resulting PACs were converted into monolithic 
coins using a hydraulic machine at a pressure of 7 metric tons 
and then subjected to a heating process in a furnace through 
the single-  route procedure technique. Initially, they were 
heated under nitrogen gas protection at 600°C, which further 
enhanced the carbon structure. This temperature was selected 
to promote the reaction between KOH and the carbon matrix, 
yielding in the creation of new pores and the enlargement of 
existing pores through potassium-  mediated reactions and gas 
evolution. The inert nitrogen atmosphere prevented unwanted 
combustion during this treatment. Once this temperature was 
reached, the N 2 gas was replaced with CO 2 for physical activa -
tion at 850°C for 2.5 h. At this higher temperature, controlled 
carbon–CO 2 gasification further opened and widened the 
pores formed during KOH activation, thereby enhancing pore 
development and connectivity of the PAC materials, which is 
essential for their application in energy storage devices, es -
pecially for SC devices [ 46, 47 ]. Hence, the combined KOH/
CO2 activation route was employed to achieve more effective 
pore formation than either activation step alone. After activa -
tion, the samples were rinsed repeatedly with distilled H 2O 
until a neutral pH was reached, followed by drying overnight 
at 105°C. This rinsing step is crucial for removing residual 
chemicals and impurities that could affect the performance 
of the PAC materials in SCs. Finally, the samples were des -
ignated as RPPACx-  y, where x and y denote the KOH dosage 
used (0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 M) and CO 2 activation temperature, 
respectively. As a control sample, to investigate the impact 
of combining KOH and CO 2 activation, the precarbonized 
RP was heated without KOH activation (only CO 2 activation) 
using the same technique (heat treatment and washing) as 
mentioned above. The resultant sample was denominated as 
RPPAC- 0- 850 according to the prepared without KOH activa -
tion or only CO 2 activation. The schematic representation of 
preparing RPPACs is presented in Figure  1.

2.4   |   Electrode Fabrication and Electrochemical 
Performance Evaluation
All the RPPAC electrodes were polished to obtain a thickness 
and diameter of approximately 0.015–0.02 cm and 0.7–0.8 cm, 
respectively [48]. The 1 M H2SO4 is employed as the electrolyte. 
The two identical electrodes were positioned vertically, crowded, 
and separated by Whatman filter paper as a separator material 
and flanked by two pairs of stainless sheet type 316 as current 
collectors, then clamped using a cell body from acrylic, as we 
have previously reported [ 49]. In the symmetric two-  electrode 
setup, cyclic voltammetry (CV) assessments were attempted at 
voltage ranges between 0–1 V using varying sweep rates from 1 
to 20 mV s−1, and galvanostatic charging/discharging (GCD) as -
sessments were executed at current densities varying from 1 to 
10 A g−1 in the same voltage range. Further, the GCD curve was 
used to investigate cyclic stability for 6000 cycles at 10 A g−1. The 
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were gath -
ered between the frequency range of 10 −2–102 kHz.

The morphological structure changes of the RPPAC samples as 
observed in the SEM micrograph (Figure  3a–h) demonstrate the 
significant effect of KOH concentration during activation paired 
with CO2 activation on the pore architecture and surface texture of 
the resulting porous carbon materials. The control sample, RPC- 
0- 850 (Figure  3a,b), which only received CO 2 activation, had a 
solid and compact framework without any obvious porosity. The 
surface morphology of RPC-  0- 850 was uneven and rough, with 
only a few tiny holes. This suggests that CO 2 activation alone at 
850°C is inadequate to yield a highly porous architecture. This 
discovery aligns with previous observations that CO 2 activation 
primarily develops micropores but is less successful in build -
ing a well-  connected pore framework without KOH activation. 
Upon introducing 0.25 M KOH (RPPAC-  0.25- 850, Figure  3c,d), 
a significant change in morphology was observed. The surface 
developed drastically more porous, with a declared advancement 
of interconnected pores and a sponge- like 3- dimensional frame-
work. The pore walls were thinner, and the overall framework 
was more open than in RPC- 0- 850. This improvement in porosity 
was caused by the chemical reactions between carbon and KOH 
at high temperatures, producing numerous potassium com -
pounds and gases (e.g., H2, CO, and CO2), which lead to etching 
and enlargement of pores [62, 63]. The synergistic impact of KOH 
and CO2 activation enhances the surface area and optimizes PSD, 
providing effective charge storage and ion transport. Particularly, 
the RPPAC- 0.25- 850 sample, with its well- developed hierarchical 
porosity, is associated with the superior electrochemical behavior 
of all samples, as the interconnected pores offer numerous active 
sites and quick ion diffusion routes, which are crucial for high 
capacitance and rate capability in SCs [ 64]. Raising the KOH 
concentration to 0.5 M RPPAC- 0.5- 850, (Figure  3e,f) generated 
further morphological changes. The SEM micrograph showed 
a structure that was significantly more porous, with bigger and 
randomly formed pores. However, certain pore walls appeared 
to have partially fused or collapsed, confirming that excessive 
KOH activation could disrupt the mechanical integrity of the 
carbon matrix. Over- etching can cause micropores to merge into 
mesopores or macropores, possibly limiting the available surface 
area for electrochemical reactions. At the highest KOH concen -

The elemental composition of the RPPAC samples was con -
firmed by energy-  dispersive X- ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis 
(Figure  4). The elemental composition differences detected in 
the RPPAC samples were mainly caused by the interaction of 
KOH/CO2 activation processes. When activated only by CO 2 
activation or without KOH activation, the RPPAC-  0- 850 mainly 
compose of carbon (C), oxygen (O), and silicon (Si) elements. 
The silica element originates from the RP, along with organic 
carbon and some oxygen- containing groups. At a low KOH con-
centration (0.25 M), the RPPAC- 0.25- 850 is also composed of C, 
O, and Si elements. In this state, the chemical activation with 
KOH is relatively mild. KOH acts as a chemical etchant, reacting 
with carbon to develop porosity and improve the SSA. At higher 
KOH concentrations (0.5 and 0.75 M), the RPPAC-  0.50- 850 and 
RPPAC- 0.75- 850 exhibit the presence of C, O, Si, and K ele -
ments. The detected K in RPPAC- 0.50- 850 and RPPAC- 0.75- 850 
is attributed to residual potassium-  containing species associ -
ated with the KOH activation process, whereas Si is associated 
with the inherent silica/mineral fraction of RP.
The XRD patterns of all RPPACs are shown in Figure  5. A broad 
and weak characteristic peak was observed at approximately 
22° and 44°, associated with the (002) and (100) of the amor -
phous carbon and graphite planes, respectively, confirming the 
existence of a graphitic carbon framework structure with a sig -
nificant number of defects [ 65, 66]. RPPAC-  0.25- 850 exhibited 
a higher (002) diffraction peak intensity than the other three 
RPPAC samples, indicating the continuous development of sp 2 
carbon planes in RPPAC-  0.25- 850. This substantially promoted 
the graphitization degree of the material [ 66]. The presence of 
both amorphous and graphitic carbon improves the material's 
hydrophilicity and electrical conductivity. Moreover, the inten -
sity of the RPPAC- 0.25- 850 at the low- scattering angle peaks sig-
nificantly increased, indicating the presence of many micropores

4   |   Conclusion
In summary, we have successfully prepared RPPACs by KOH 
activation coupled with CO 2 activation and its implemen -
tation as symmetric SC electrodes. The KOH activation was 
performed at various concentrations, that is, 0, 0.25, 0.50, 
and 0.75 M and the CO 2 activation was carried out at 850°C 
for 2.5 h. The synergistic effect and mechanism of KOH and 
CO2 activation were systematically studied. The combina -
tion of KOH and CO 2 activation by employing different KOH 
concentrations had an obvious effect on the pore framework 
formation of the PAC materials. The RPPAC-  0.25-  850 exhibits 
high S BET, hierarchical porous architecture, and a high degree 
of graphitization. The RPPAC-  0.25-  850 electrode attained a 
gravimetric capacitance of 280 F g−1 at 1 A g −1 and also dis -
played CE of 86.53%. The Ragone plot revealed the impres -
sive energy and power densities of 38.95 and 413.5 W kg−1, 
respectively. Furthermore, the RPPAC-  0.25-  850-  based sym -
metric SC device has good GCD cycling stability, maintaining 
about 88% capacitance at 10 A g−1 after 6000 cycles, indicating 
higher capacitance efficiency. The research findings indicate 
that agricultural waste can be a sustainable energy storage 
solution, offering a renewable and abundant carbon source. 
It also highlights the necessity of tailoring KOH activation 
conditions, such as KOH concentration, for obtaining remark -
able SC performance, as well as opening the route for novel 
advances in electrochemical energy storage and conversion, 
encouraging greener and more effective energy solutions.

formance, this valorization strategy supports circular-  economy

translation of these laboratory-  scale findings toward commer -
cially viable and sustainable energy-  storage technologies.
