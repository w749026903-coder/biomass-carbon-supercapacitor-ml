ABSTRACT
This study presents a machine learning (ML)-driven framework for optimizing 
the design of polyaniline (PANI)-based supercapacitors, a promising material 
for energy storage. The framework integrates experimental data and advanced 
optimization techniques to systematically investigate the effects of key fabrication 
parameters such as mesh size, deposition cycles, voltage window, and operating 
potential on performance metrics, including specific capacitance, energy density, 
and internal resistance (IR) drop. Two ML models, gradient boosting regressor 
(GBR) and random forest regressor (RFR), were trained on 145 experimental data-
sets, achieving high predictive accuracy  (R2 > 0.95). Bayesian optimization (BO) 
was used to identify the global optimum fabrication conditions, resulting in sig-
nificant reductions in mean absolute percentage errors 15% for energy density, 
20% for capacitance, and 7% for IR drop. Feature importance analysis, includ-
ing SHAP and partial dependence plots, revealed that mesh size and operating 
potential were the most influential parameters, aligning with electrochemical 
theories on ion transport and polymer growth. This integrated approach not 
only accelerates the design and optimization of PANI-based supercapacitors but 
also minimizes material waste and provides a scalable, interpretable pathway for 
ML-driven optimization in the development of high-performance, cost-effective 
energy storage devices.

GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT
Introduction
Supercapacitors play a pivotal role in the advancement 
of smart energy storage technologies. The intermittent 
availability of energy required for routine activities 
necessitates the development of efficient and reliable 
storage systems across various applications, including 
those in optics. As a result, there is a growing impe-
tus to investigate alternative energy storage solutions, 
such as rechargeable batteries [ 1], fuel cells [ 2], and 
supercapacitors [3]. Supercapacitors have gained sig-
nificant attention due to their favorable characteristics, 
notably high power density and substantial specific 
capacity [4–6]. The overall performance and efficiency 
of supercapacitors are critically influenced by the 
properties and design of the electrode materials used. 
Polyaniline (PANI) is a prominent conductive poly-
mer for electrode materials due to its high theoretical 
capacitance, adjustable conductivity, environmental 
stability, and cost-effectiveness. It has been extensively 
studied for supercapacitor applications, especially in 
asymmetric and hybrid configurations [7, 8].
One promising strategy involves electrodepositing 
PANI onto conductive substrates such as stainless-
steel mesh, which provides a robust, flexible, and 
conductive platform [ 9]. Nevertheless, optimizing 
the electrodeposition conditions to maximize capaci-
tance and energy density while minimizing the IR 
drop remains a challenging task. The IR drop, a sud-
den voltage loss occurring at the onset of discharge, 
is one of the major performance-limiting factors in 
PANI-based supercapacitors. It reflects the internal 
resistance of the device, influenced by several inter-
dependent variables, including the morphology and 
conductivity of the electrode, interface contact, elec-
trolyte composition, and electrode design [10]. Exper-
imentally, researchers have sought to mitigate this 
issue through extensive parametric tuning, adjusting 
electrodeposition techniques (e.g., cyclic voltammetry 
(CV) vs. galvanostatic methods), scan rates, electrolyte 
concentrations, operating potential window (OPW), 
and cycle numbers [11]. However, the sheer number 
of combinatorial possibilities introduces complex-
ity, cost, and time delays to the experimental design 
process.
In this context, machine learning (ML) presents 
a transformative approach to materials optimiza-
tion, particularly for electrochemical systems where 
parameter interactions are non-linear and data rich. 
By training ML algorithms on experimentally acquired 
datasets, predictive models can be built to identify 
patterns and recommend optimized conditions faster 
and more accurately than traditional trial-and-error 
methods. In recent years, ML has shown promise in 
predicting battery cycle life [12], optimizing catalysts 
[13], and guiding material synthesis pathways [14]. 
However, its application in supercapacitor fabrica-
tion, particularly for optimizing electrodeposition 
parameters to address IR drop and enhance energy 
performance, remains underexplored. Although ML 
models have shown significant promise in predicting 
the specific capacitance and energy density of super-
capacitors, they encounter several limitations that can 
affect their accuracy and generalizability [15– 17]. A 
primary concern is their reliance on the quality and 
quantity of available data; limited or noisy datasets 
can lead to overfitting, where models perform well 
on training data but poorly on unseen data [18]. This

issue is particularly prevalent in materials science 
because obtaining extensive experimental data can be 
a resource-intensive process.
Additionally, many ML models function as “black 
boxes”, providing predictions without clear explana-
tions. This lack of transparency hinders understand-
ing the underlying physical phenomena and limits the 
ability to draw actionable insights for material design. 
Furthermore, traditional ML models may not inher-
ently incorporate the governing physical laws of elec-
trochemical systems, potentially leading to physically 
inconsistent predictions. Integrating physics-informed 
neural networks (PINNs) has been proposed as a solu-
tion to address this challenge. PINNs embed physical 
laws into learning to improve prediction accuracy and 
consistency [19]. However, developing such models 
requires a delicate balance between data-driven learn-
ing and adherence to physical principles, and chal-
lenges remain in effectively training these networks. 
Therefore, while ML offers valuable tools for predict-
ing supercapacitor performance metrics, carefully 
considering data quality, model interpretability, and 
incorporating physical laws is essential to overcome 
these limitations [20–22].
As part of the ongoing improvement of supercapac-
itor performance, our group fabricated electrodepos-
ited PANI on steel meshes and utilized them as elec-
trode materials for high-performance supercapacitor 
fabrication. The electrodeposited PANI-based superca-
pacitor exhibited a high energy density, a property that 
has already been reported [23, 24]. However, there is a 
need to develop an optimal condition for attaining this 
energy density, which is complex. Electrodeposited 
PANI on metal meshes still struggles with inconsistent 
capacitance, energy density, and a significant IR drop 
caused by non-uniform films and limited transport 
pathways. The project aims to pair controlled experi-
ments with supervised ML and Bayesian optimization 
to model how fabrication parameters influence elec-
trochemical performance. This provides a data-driven 
way to reduce IR drop and improve overall energy 
storage without relying on trial-and-error. This led to 
the development of ML models that can predict opti-
mal condition using features such as mesh sizes, depo-
sition voltage, deposition cycles, and OPWs. There-
fore, the cost, time, and resources can be minimized 
using available models without the stress of attaining 
the optimal condition. ML models like random forest 
regressor (RFR) [25] and gradient boosting regressor 
(GBR) [26] can help scientists save time by predicting 
optimal conditions for improving energy density and 
capacitance in supercapacitors suitable for flexible and 
non-flexible uses. This study introduces a data-driven 
framework to optimize key electrochemical parame-
ters for PANI-based supercapacitor fabrication using 
ML. The research systematically investigates the effect 
of various experimentally controllable parameters 
such as mesh size of stainless-steel substrates (20, 50, 
100, and 200 pores per inch), electrodeposition voltage 
window (0.0–1.0 V, 0.0–1.1 V, and 0.0–1.3 V), number 
of CV deposition cycles (5, 15, 25, 35) and OPW (0–0.4, 
0–0.6 and 0–0.8 V) on the resultant capacitance, energy 
density, and IR drop. A fixed electrolyte composition 
(0.5 M aniline in 0.5 M  H2SO4) and scan rate (50 mV/s) 
were maintained for electrodeposition. The datasets 
derived from these experiments were then fed into 
ML models, which were trained and tested to predict 
supercapacitor performance under varying conditions. 
The datasets were proportioned into 70% training and 
30% testing sets for the reported ML models, followed 
by validation and optimization using Bayesian optimi-
zation (BO). GBR performed best for capacitance and 
energy density, achieving root mean squared errors 
(RMSE) of 48.04 and 183.55, respectively, while RFR 
excelled for IR-drop with an RMSE of 0.0427. Before 
making predictions, we cleaned the dataset to ensure 
compatibility with Python 3.13.3, engineered features, 
and tuned hyperparameters. For GBR, key settings 
influencing predictions were the number of estima-
tors, learning rate, maximum depth, and minimum 
samples per split. For RFR, important parameters 
included the minimum sample leaf, the maximum 
depth, the maximum features, and the number of 
trees. These adjustments optimized the models’ ability 
to predict efficiently. Feature importance scores (from 
scikit-learn’s feature_importances_) highlighted critical 
inputs like mesh size and OPWs. BO further refined 
these settings, boosting model accuracy.

material properties and electrochemical performance, 
ML models can efficiently forecast these metrics, 
guiding the design and optimization of energy stor-
age devices. For example, one study employed arti-

Materials, methodology, 
and electrochemical measurements
Materials
In this study, woven stainless steel meshes with four 
different sizes, 20, 50, 100, and 200 pores per inch, were 
sourced from a local supplier in Dammam, Eastern 
Province, Saudi Arabia. These variations were selected 
to assess their systematic impact on the electrochemi-
cal behavior of supercapacitors. High-purity sulfuric 
acid (~ 95%) and ethanol, essential for both electro 
polymerization and electrode cleaning processes, were 
procured from Fisher Chemical Company. Aniline, 
used in the synthesis of PANI for electrode fabrica-
tion, was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich with a purity 
of 99.05%, compliant with ACS standards. High-grade 
reagents were critical to ensure reproducibility and 
reliability in supercapacitor device fabrication.
Preparation of woven steel mesh substrate
Woven steel meshes with mesh sizes of 20, 50, 100, 
and 200 per inch were cut into small electrodes with a 
1  cm2 working electrode area. The samples were first 
sonicated in ethanol for 15 min to remove surface con-
taminants, such as oil and dirt. They were then rinsed 
with deionized water and sonicated again in water 
for an additional 15 min to ensure thorough cleaning. 
Finally, the meshes were dried in an oven at 50 °C for 
one hour to eliminate residual moisture, preparing 
them for the subsequent electrodeposition process.
Electropolymerization process
Electropolymerization of PANI onto the woven steel 
mesh substrates was performed using a CHI 760E 
Potentiostat in a standard three-electrode electro -
chemical setup. The prepared steel mesh served as 
the working electrode, while a platinum wire and an 
Ag/AgCl electrode functioned as the counter and ref-
erence electrodes, respectively. The electrodeposition 
solution consisted of 0.5 M aniline in 0.5 M sulfuric 
acid  (H2SO4). Electrodeposition was carried out via 
CV at a fixed scan rate of 50 mV/s. To examine the 
influence of electrochemical parameters on deposition 
quality and supercapacitor performance, three volt-
age windows were explored: 0.0–1.0 V, 0.0–1.1 V, and 
0.0–1.3 V. Within each voltage range, the number of 
deposition cycles was varied (5, 15, 25, and 35 cycles) 
to determine the optimal conditions. The best electro-
chemical performance in terms of capacitance, energy 
density, and power density was observed at 35 cycles 
within the 0.0–1.3 V window. After deposition, the 
steel meshes developed a distinct green PANI coat-
ing, which was rinsed with deionized water to remove 
residual monomers or byproducts and then air-dried 
at room temperature to maintain the coating’s struc-
tural integrity.
Assessing the impact of deposition cycle 
on supercapacitor performance
The performance of a supercapacitor is strongly influ-
enced by the design and characteristics of its electrode 
material, particularly the electropolymerization pro-
cess used to synthesize PANI. CV tests were carried 
out using a three-electrode setup to evaluate the redox 
stability and supercapacitive behavior of electrodepos-
ited PANI. PANI-coated woven steel meshes with 
four different mesh sizes served as the working elec-
trodes in this configuration. These were submerged 
in a 0.1 M  H2SO4 aqueous electrolyte with a stand-
ard Ag/AgCl reference electrode and a platinum wire 
counter electrode. CV measurements were performed 
over OPW of − 0.4 V to 0.8 V, using a fixed scan rate 
of 50 mV/s, as shown in Figure S2. This experimental 
setup was designed to systematically investigate the 
electrochemical performance of the PANI layers, with 
a particular focus on how varying the number of depo-
sition cycles affects the overall efficiency of the super-
capacitor. The choice of OPW and scan rate was critical 
for gaining detailed insights into the electrochemical

ML methodology
Data collection used for the ML model
A data-led method is explored to forecast the effi-
ciency of PANI on woven steel mesh current collectors. 
The forecast-centric analysis is essential because no 
recognized pattern links material attributes to usage 
in understanding the efficacy of supercapacitors for 
novel materials. Experimental measurements were 
conducted on several mesh sizes using a woven steel 
mesh current collector, and later, ML was employed 
to validate the optimal condition of the experimental 
outcome. During data preprocessing, approximately 
4 features listed in the introduction were used to build 
the models, serving as the input parameters. The data-
sets generated during the experimental measurements 
were used for training and testing the models. Specifi-
cally, 70% of the datasets were used for training the 
ML models, while the remaining 30% were used as 
the testing datasets. Overall, around 145 datasets were 
generated during the experimental measurements. 
Codes were written using Python and executed in a 
Google Colab environment.
Feature selection
Accurate feature selection is crucial for predicting 
capacitance and energy density in electrochemical 
energy storage systems, including supercapacitors 
and batteries. The performance of these systems is 
highly sensitive to a range of experimental and mate-
rial synthesis parameters, making it vital to identify 
the most relevant factors. Selecting the appropriate 
features reduces computational complexity, enhances 
model interpretability, and improves prediction accu-
racy. In this context, mesh size, deposition cycle, tested 
voltage, and deposition potential window represent 
key experimental variables that directly influence the

--- PDF页 7 ---

electrochemical behavior and storage capabilities of 
electrode materials. Mesh size plays a crucial role in 
determining the surface morphology and porosity of 
electrode materials, which in turn affects the accessible 
surface area for ion adsorption and charge storage. 
Larger mesh sizes can result in coarser electrode struc-
tures, reducing active surface area, while finer meshes, 
such as 20 mesh, can enhance surface uniformity and 
increase capacitance. Similarly, the deposition cycle 
influences the thickness and structural integrity of the 
electrode coating. Excessive deposition cycles may 
lead to cracking or poor adhesion, thereby impairing 
electrochemical performance, whereas insufficient 
cycles can result in thin coatings with limited active 
material. Electrolyte concentration has a significant 
impact on ionic conductivity and ion transport effi-
ciency. Higher concentrations generally improve ionic 
conductivity, facilitating faster charge–discharge rates 
and higher capacitance. However, excessively high 
concentrations may lead to ion aggregation or reduced 
ion mobility, lowering performance. Tested voltage 
determines the OPW, affecting both capacitance and 
energy density. Higher OPWs often enhance energy 
density but can accelerate degradation and increase 
the risk of electrolyte breakdown. The deposition 
potential window is another influential parameter 
that governs the nucleation and growth of electrode 
materials during electrodeposition. It determines 
the crystalline structure, porosity, and particle size, 
directly impacting capacitance and energy storage 
efficiency. The interplay between these parameters is 
often non-linear and complex. For instance, increas -
ing electrolyte concentration may improve capacitance 
under one deposition potential but reduce it under 
another. Such interactions underscore the need for 
robust feature selection techniques to identify the most 
influential variables. Understanding the individual 
and combined effects of mesh size, deposition cycle, 
electrolyte concentration, tested voltage, and deposi-
tion OPW are essential for optimizing electrochemical 
performance and developing predictive capacitance 
and energy density models.

of regression trees, where each subsequent tree cor-
rects the prediction errors (residuals) of the preceding

ones. Initially, the model is trained on the input data 
to make baseline predictions. The residuals, or errors, 
from this prediction, are then used to train the next 
tree, which focuses on minimizing the remaining 
error. This iterative refinement continues across mul-
tiple trees, resulting in a strong predictive model. The 
GBR framework was applied separately to each output 
variable, leveraging its strength in capturing complex 
non-linear dependencies between material character-
istics and electrochemical performance metrics.
The model is trained sequentially, where each 
regression tree learns to correct the residual errors of 
its predecessor. The first tree is trained on the origi-
nal data (X, y), producing an initial prediction y^1. 
The subsequent trees are trained on the residuals 
(r = y − y^1) to improve model accuracy iteratively. 
This ensemble learning approach enables the model 
to capture complex non-linear relationships between 
the input features and the output performance met-
rics. The final prediction is obtained by aggregating 
the outputs of all individual trees.
Random forest regressor (RFR)
RFR is an ensemble learning algorithm based on deci-
sion trees that has proven particularly effective in 
materials science applications due to its robustness 
and interpretability. RFR constructs multiple deci-
sion trees during training and aggregates their out-
puts to enhance predictive accuracy while reducing 
overfitting. A key advantage of RFR is its ability to 
estimate feature importance scores. The importance of 
each feature is determined by evaluating how much 
it improves the model’s accuracy when splitting the 
data in the decision trees. In electrochemical perfor-
mance prediction, RFR can reveal which experimen-
tal variables, such as the tested voltage or electrolyte 
concentration, consistently contribute to variations in 
capacitance and energy density. Unlike artificial neu-
ral networks (ANNs) and convolutional neural net-
works (CNNs), RFR is less susceptible to overfitting 
with small datasets and is computationally efficient, 
making it an attractive choice for experimentalists 
seeking interpretable models [18]. Figure 2 shows the 
RFR model architecture.
Combining these machine-learning approaches 
can further enhance feature selection. Hybrid mod-
els, where RFR identifies key features that are sub-
sequently fine-tuned using an ANN or a CNN, are 
gaining traction in electrochemical performance 
modelling. For instance, RFR may highlight the 
deposition cycle and electrolyte concentration as 
primary drivers of capacitance, prompting ANN or 
CNN models to focus on their interactions with sec-
ondary parameters, such as mesh size and deposition

promise the performance of the model. Techniques 
like data augmentation, synthetic data generation, 
and transfer learning are being explored to address 
these limitations in electrochemical research [40]. 
Ultimately, leveraging ANN, CNN, and RFR algo-
rithms allows researchers to uncover non-obvious 
relationships among experimental parameters, facili -
tating the development of more accurate and robust 
models for capacitance and energy density predic-
tion [41, 42].
Performance metrics of the ML models
Statistical methods and correlation-based techniques 
are commonly employed as initial steps in identify-
ing the most relevant features for predicting capaci-
tance and energy density in electrochemical systems. 
These approaches help quantify the relationships 
between inputs variables, such as mesh size, depo-
sition cycle, electrolyte concentration, tested volt-
age, deposition potential window, and the target 
performance metrics. Statistical evaluations enhance 
the efficiency and accuracy of predictive models by 
revealing potential dependencies and eliminating 
redundant or weakly correlated features.
Pearson correlation coefficient The Pearson correlation 
coefficient is a widely used technique that meas-
ures the linear relationship between two variables. 
Values range from − 1 to 1, with positive values 
indicating a direct correlation and negative values 
representing an inverse relationship. For instance, 
electrolyte concentration may exhibit a positive cor-
relation with capacitance in specific systems, while 
excessive deposition cycles could display a negative 
correlation due to material degradation. However, 
Pearson correlation is limited to linear dependen-
cies, which may overlook non-linear interactions 
commonly observed in electrochemical processes. 
This necessitates the use of complementary methods 
for a comprehensive evaluation of features.
Mean absolute error The mean absolute error (MAE) is a 
standard metric used in regression analysis to assess the 
accuracy of model predictions. Unlike the residual sum 
of squares (RSS), which squares the errors, MAE calcu-
lates the absolute differences between predicted and 
actual values, offering a precise measure of the aver-
age error size. Unlike RSS or mean squared error, MAE 
does not square the residuals, making it less sensitive to 
outliers. This means that a single significant error won’t 
significantly skew the MAE, thus providing a more reli-
able metric in the presence of outliers. Essentially, MAE 
represents the average magnitude of errors the model 
makes without any modifications that could distort 
their real-world impact.
Visualization tools Visualization tools, such as scatter 
plots, heatmaps, and pairwise correlation matrices, com-
plement statistical evaluation. Scatter plots reveal data 
distributions and potential outliers, while heatmaps 
visually represent the correlation strengths between all 
variables, enabling the rapid identification of highly 
correlated features. During this representation, the pre-
dicted values from the ML models are plotted against 
the actual values from the experimental measurements. 
Pairwise correlation matrices further help detect situ-
ations of collinearity, where two or more predictors 
exhibit strong interdependence, thereby complicating 
the interpretation of their individual effects on perfor-
mance metrics. Addressing collinearity is crucial when 
evaluating the significance of experimental parameters. 
Highly correlated features, such as deposition cycle and 
deposition potential window, may confound predictive 
models by inflating variance and reducing interpretabil-
ity. Statistical and correlation-based evaluations are an 
essential foundation in the feature selection process. By 
quantifying linear, non-linear, and information-based 
relationships, researchers can prioritize the most influ-
ential experimental parameters, such as mesh size, dep-
osition cycle, electrolyte concentration, tested voltage, 
and deposition potential window. These insights inform 
the development of more accurate and interpretable 
prediction models for electrochemical performance.

Feature importance as used in the ML 
prediction
Deposition cycles significantly influence superca-
pacitor performance, with capacitance and energy 
density generally increasing up to an optimal point 
before declining due to reduced ion accessibility or 
material degradation. IR drop typically decreases ini-
tially with improved conductivity but increases with 
excessive cycles due to higher resistance. The key 
is to find the right balance through material selec-
tion, deposition technique, and cycle optimization 
tailored to the specific application (e.g., high-power 
vs. high-energy devices). Recent advancements in 
supercapacitor research emphasize the use of hybrid 
materials and precise control of deposition processes 
(e.g., pulsed electrodeposition or atomic layer depo-
sition) to mitigate these trade-offs. Techniques like 
in-situ monitoring during deposition can help iden-
tify the optimal cycle number for specific materials, 
ensuring high capacitance and energy density with 
minimal IR drop. Additionally, integrating ML or 
modelling to predict deposition outcomes is becom-
ing common, allowing for tailored electrode designs 
that balance these metrics effectively.
Additionally, the potential window and its influ-
ence on capacitance, energy density, and IR drop 
must be considered. The deposition process can also 
affect the stability and electrochemical window of 
the electrode material. For instance, thicker or poorly 
adhered deposits might reduce the voltage window 
due to increased internal resistance or electrolyte 
decomposition, potentially offsetting gains in capaci-
tance and lowering energy density. Furthermore, in 
terms of the scan rate, the capacitance decreases with 
increasing scan rate due to limited ion diffusion and 
slower redox kinetics, particularly for pseudocapaci-
tive materials [43– 46]. Additionally, energy density 
follows a similar trend, declining as capacitance 
decreases, although the voltage window’s stability 
also plays a role. Additionally, IR drop increases 
with higher scan rates due to greater ohmic losses 
resulting from rapid potential changes and lim -
ited ion/electron transport. Optimizing scan rates 
requires balancing metrics based on the application, 
with low scan rates suitable for energy-dense stor-
age and high scan rates for power delivery. Material 
selection, electrode design, and electrolyte proper-
ties mitigate these effects. Table 2 summarizes some 
models that have been reported in the literature.

Limitations of ML models
ML models need large, high-quality datasets to make 
accurate predictions. In supercapacitor research, 
experimental data on specific capacitance, energy den-
sity, and IR drop can be scarce, especially for new mate-
rials or untested conditions. Noisy or inconsistent data 
(e.g., measurement errors, varying experimental proto-
cols, or incomplete metadata) can lead to poor model 
performance or overfitting. Supercapacitor testing 
methods (e.g., CV scan rates, GCD rates) vary widely, 
making it challenging to combine data from different 
studies into a cohesive dataset for ML training. Super-
capacitor performance relies on complex, multiscale 
processes, including ion diffusion, electron transport, 
electrode–electrolyte interactions, and material-spe-
cific redox reactions. ML models, especially black-box 
approaches like deep neural networks, may struggle to 
capture these underlying physical and chemical mech-
anisms without sufficient domain-specific features or 
physics-informed constraints. Training complex ML 
models (e.g., deep learning) requires significant com-
putational power, which may not be available to all 
research groups or industries, especially for iterative 
optimization of supercapacitor designs.
