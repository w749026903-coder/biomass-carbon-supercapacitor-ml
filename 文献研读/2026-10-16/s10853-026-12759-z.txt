Energy materials 
Machine learning‑based energy density prediction 
of bio‑based supercapacitor with Shapley additive 
model interpretation
Damilare Samuel Oyebamiji1 and Davannendran Chandran1,* 
1 Department of Mechanical Engineering, Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS, 32610 Seri Iskandar, Perak, Malaysia
ABSTRACT
This paper aims to develop machine learning (ML) models to predict bio-based 
supercapacitor energy density from electrode physical and elemental composi-
tion, as well as potential window (PW), to accelerate materials discovery and 
reduce the resources and time required for experimental optimization. Extreme 
gradient boosting (XGB), random forest (RF), extremely randomized tree (ERT) 
and categorical boosting (CB) ML algorithms were employed to develop predic-
tive ML models. The training datasets for ML models are extracted from experi-
mental data from existing published articles on bio-based supercapacitors. XGB, 
RF, ERT and RF models’ performance was evaluated with coefficient of deter-
mination (R2), root-mean-square error (RMSE), mean absolute percentage error 
(MAPE) and mean absolute error (MAE). Among the models, XGB had the best 
performance with R2 value of 0.885, followed by CB, RF and ERT at 0.841, 0.781 
and 0.637, respectively, when tested with new experimental data. With testing 
data, XGB recorded the lowest MAE of 4.81 Wh/kg, ERT, CB and RF recorded 
MAE of 9.23 Wh/kg, 6.51 Wh/kg and 8.14 Wh/kg, respectively. Shapley additive 
explanations (SHAP) analysis indicated that PW exhibited the highest impact 
in all models used to predict energy density as compared to other independent 
variables, such as specific surface area (SSA), intensity ratio (IR) and average 
pore size (PS), emphasizing its critical influence in developing predictive models 
and enhancing supercapacitor energy density. The impact of PW in predicting 
energy density of bio-based supercapacitors can be attributed to its quadratic 
relationship with energy density. Consequently, the models accurately pre-
dict bio-based supercapacitor energy density, demonstrating the role of PW in 
designing and optimizing bio-based materials for the development of sustainable 
supercapacitors.

Introduction
Global energy supply remains dependent on fos-
sil fuels, which adversely contribute to greenhouse 
gas emissions (GHG) and accelerate climate change. 
Between 2000 and 2023, global GHG emission was 
reported increased by 32% [1 ]. The rise in global 
average temperature up to 1.5 °C resulting from the 
increase in GHG emissions has increased the occur-
rence of floods, drought and earthquakes, emphasiz-
ing the need for sustainable energy alternatives [2]. In 
response, renewable energy sources, such as sunlight, 
wind and hydroelectric power, have gained attention 
as cleaner and more sustainable pathway towards 
carbon (C) neutralization. However, effective utili-
zation of renewable energy across key sectors, espe-
cially transportation, demands for stable and efficient 
energy storage system. Among existing technologies, 
supercapacitors and lithium-ion batteries are at the 
forefront for storing renewable energy for utiliza -
tion in transportation sector. Supercapacitors’ high-
power density, broad operating temperature range, 
long cycle life (ability of supercapacitor to maintain 
its performance and reliability over extended periods) 
and rapid charge–discharge, make them suitable for 
regenerative braking and energy storage systems of 
hybrid electric vehicles [3, 4].
Supercapacitors are categorized into electric double 
layer capacitor and pseudocapacitors. Electric double 
layer capacitors mainly store electric energy through 
attraction of ions on electrode and electrolyte interface, 
while a pseudocapacitor includes reduction and oxi-
dation reaction [5, 6]. Supercapacitors face challenges 
such as low energy density as compared to batteries, 
as well as the use of toxic materials, which limits its 
development for electric vehicle [7 , 8]. To overcome 
these challenges, existing studies explored alternative 
materials such as metal oxide, composite, biomass 
materials and conductive polymers for the develop-
ment of supercapacitor’s electrode, electrolyte and 
binder. Poudel et al. [9] utilized iron oxide nanorod 
to prepare anode for supercapacitor. The fabricated 
asymmetric supercapacitor exhibited energy den-
sity of 111.72 Wh/kg. Tian et al. [10] used manganese 
oxide/carbon composite cathode to develop asymmet-
ric supercapacitor having energy density of 50.20 Wh/
kg. Zeng et al. [11] utilized kelp-derived biomass as a 
multifunctional material to prepare electrode, electro-
lyte, binder and separator for bio-based supercapaci-
tor. Although the energy density of the supercapacitor 
was 8 Wh/kg, the study highlighted trade-off between 
conventional high-performance materials and more 
environmentally friendly alternatives. To provide bal-
anced assessment of bio-based supercapacitor perfor-
mance, metrics such as biodegradability, or life-cycle 
environmental impact could complement energy den-
sity, enabling direct evaluation of the environmental 
benefits alongside electrochemical performance.
Bio-based supercapacitor’s performance is influ-
enced by the electrode’s active materials’ specific 
surface area (SSA) and average pore size (PS), which 
affects charge storage, as well as conductivity for effi-
cient charge transfer [3 ]. High SSA and well-devel-
oped pore active materials directly enhance superca-
pacitor performance, owing to more available surface 
for ion adsorption. Song et al. [12] prepared activated 
carbon from Eucommia Ulmoides Oliver wood with 
enhanced SSA and PS of 1589.00  m2/g and 2.51 nm, 
respectively. The assembled supercapacitor achieved 
energy density of 95.58  Wh/kg. Electrode material 
with enhanced electrical conductivity is crucial for 
efficient ion transport and reduced internal resistance 
in the supercapacitor. In the study by Tabarov et al. 
[13], the authors reported that incorporating carbon

Optimizing supercapacitor electrode materials’ 
properties is crucial for achieving enhanced perfor-
mance. Tri Yulianti et al. [14] optimized ratio of KOH 
to oil palm empty fruit bunch in which high SSA 
of 763.73  m2/g and pore volume of 0.44  cm3/g was 
achieved with optimum ratio (1:2) compared to 1:3 
(659.29  m2/g and 0.43  cm3/g) and 1:1 (300.44  m2/g and 
0.17  cm3/g). The supercapacitor assembled with the 
optimized carbon achieved enhance energy density 
of 4.45 Wh/kg. Ayinla et al. [15] reported that wood 
pine bark-derived activated carbon synthesized using 
different activating agents  (H3PO4,   K2CO3, and  ZnCl2) 
showed high specific capacitance of 578.5 F/g when 
activated with  H3PO4 compared to  K2CO3 (315.3 F/g) 
and  ZnCl 2 (293 F/g), which resulted in enhanced 
energy density of 14.75 Wh/kg for the assembled 
supercapacitor. These show that optimizing electrode 
active materials results in enhanced electrochemical 
performance of supercapacitor. Traditionally, acti-
vated carbons derived from coal tar, a toxic by-product 
of coal processing, have been widely utilized as active 
materials in supercapacitor electrodes despite their 
environmental and health concerns [16, 17]. With this 
toxicity of coal tar-based activated carbon, biomass-
derived activated carbon has received researchers’ 
attention as alternative supercapacitor material due 
to its abundance, sustainability, non-toxic and high 
performance, more than commercial carbon electrode 
[18].
Porous activated carbon with hierarchical SSA have 
been synthesized from various biomass precursor 
including cottonseed meal [19], cashew nut shell [20], 
camphor leaves [21], rice husk [22], green tea waste 
[23], Job’s tears straw [ 24], pistachio shell [ 25], sun -
flower stalks [26], Eucommia Ulmoides Oliver wood 
[12], purple sweet potato [27] and macadamia nutshell 
[28] for supercapacitor electrode application. Xu et al. 
[21] synthesized porous activated carbon from cam-
phor leaves, achieving SSA and PS of 2401.30  m2/g 
and 2.31 nm, respectively. The assembled supercapaci-
tor exhibited enhanced energy density of 10.83 Wh/
kg. Yao et al. [22] utilized rice husk to produce acti-
vated carbon with SSA of 3272.50  m2/g, resulting in 
supercapacitor energy density of 14.5  Wh/kg. Also, 
Chaiammart et al. [20] KOH-activated cashew nut shell 
yielded activated carbon with SSA of 1698.00  m2/g and 
the energy density of supercapacitor fabricated was 
2.43 Wh/kg. Chen et al. [24], reported synthesis of 
activated carbon from Job’s tears straw exhibited SSA 
of 2149.79  m2/g, which resulted in enhanced superca-
pacitor energy density of 20.3 Wh/kg. These studies 
highlight the potential of biomass-based activated car-
bon as electrode materials to enhance supercapacitor 
energy density for different applications, including 
electric vehicles.
In comparison with conventional activated carbon 
derived from fossil resources, biomass-derived acti -
vated carbon contributes to sustainability of super-
capacitor materials and reduces GHG emissions 
associated with the mining and production of fossil/
coal-based activated carbon. Zeng et  al. [11] com-
pared performance of conventional activated carbon 
with kelp-derived activated carbon as supercapacitor 
electrode active materials. They reported that kelp-
derived activated carbon electrode achieved specific 
capacitance of 277 F/g, which is higher compared to 
195 F/g achieved with conventional activated carbon 
electrode. Biomass-based activated carbon outper-
forms conventional activated carbon as supercapacitor 
electrode material by offering enhanced surface area 
and porosity for ion adsorption. This highlights the 
importance of replacing conventional materials with 
activated carbon derived from biomass sources.
Utilization of biomass-based activated carbon in 
fabrication of supercapacitor electrode offers advan-
tages, such as waste minimization, abundance, 
enhanced performance and cost effectiveness. To 
enhance performance of bio-based supercapacitors, 
optimization and functionalization of different bio-
mass feedstocks are required to improve the material 
properties of biomass-derived activated carbon. This 
optimization and functionalization process consumes 
considerable time and resources, making effective 
determinations of bio-based supercapacitor energy 
density a challenging and slow progress towards 
commercialization. However, integration of compu-
tational methods, such as machine learning (ML) and 
simulation, presents a promising pathway to acceler-
ate materials discovery, design and optimization of 
supercapacitor materials, which enable faster assess-
ment of energy density. Computational approach 
holds potential to advance development of efficient, 
cost-effective and sustainable energy storage systems.

Computational studies have advantages of low 
cost, in-depth understanding of physical and chemi-
cal properties and high accuracy when compared to 
experimental studies. Density function theory as a 
first-principle simulation is one of the computational 
methods used to study electrode properties and their 
interaction with electrolyte in supercapacitor. In the 
study by Yang et al. [29], first-principles density func-
tion theory was employed to investigate the capacitive 
performance of supercapacitor electrode. The study 
revealed that capacitive behaviour of the electrode is 
influenced by electrostatic potential surface and potas-
sium ion adsorption properties. Dong et al. [30] uti-
lized density function theory to calculate potassium 
ion adsorption energy on different oxygen functional 
groups on carbon surface. The calculation shows that 
introduction of oxygen functional group enhances the 
wettability of electrolyte ions to the carbon electrode, 
resulting in higher adsorption energy of − 151.13 kJ/
mol in lactone group sample compared to − 91.02 kJ/
mol for undoped sample. Density functional theory 
calculations can predict materials properties, which 
helps in optimization and functionalizing of electrode 
materials to expedite materials discovery for improv-
ing supercapacitor electrochemical performance, such 
as capacitance, power density and energy density. 
Density function theory calculation helps identify 
materials with enhanced electrochemical performance 
and structural stability to reduce cost and time associ-
ated with experimental optimization of supercapacitor 
materials. However, density function theory applica-
tion in development of bio-based supercapacitor faces 
challenges such as high computational demand, com-
plex system simplification before calculation and high 
computing time and resources, which limit its effec-
tiveness for bio-materials optimization [31].
ML emerged as an alternative computational 
method to overcome limitations of density function 
theory calculations for evaluating performance of 
bio-based supercapacitors. ML is capable of handling 
materials systems with large-scale and multi-compo-
nent systems and can also be combined with differ-
ent computational methods [ 31]. With this, ML can 
be utilized to identify the critical features of electrode 
materials that contribute to discovery of novel and 
sustainable materials for enhancing supercapacitor 
performance, such as energy density, power density 
and capacitance. ML provides data-driven approach 
for developing predictive models for specific capaci-
tance, power density and energy density of bio-based 
supercapacitors by leveraging datasets obtained from 
existing experimental studies [32– 35]. ML provides 
insight into relationship between input and output 
variables without requiring detailed physical expla-
nation of features [36]. Several ML algorithms, such 
as random forest (RF), decision tree, extreme gradient 
boosting (XGB), categorical boosting (CB) and linear 
regression have been employed in existing studies to 
predict supercapacitor energy density, power density 
and specific capacitance [33, 35, 37–40]. In the study 
by Lu et al. [35], light gradient boosting model was 
reported to predict energy density of bio-based super-
capacitors with coefficient of determination (R 2) of 
0.922. Shrivas et al. [33] employed different ML models 
for predicting bio-based supercapacitor specific capac-
itance. They reported that RF regressor (0.845) outper-
form decision tree (0.827), linear regression (0.645) and 
XGB (0.702). Also, CB model was employed by Li et al. 
[34] to predict specific capacitance of bio-based super-
capacitor and R2 value of 0.978 was observed from the 
study. These studies revealed the potential of data-
driven modelling in identifying important materials 
properties and reducing time and resources consumed 
by experimental optimization of bio-based materials 
as electrode for enhancing bio-based supercapacitor 
performance. To accelerate materials discovery for 
supercapacitor application, ML assists with material 
selection, performance prediction and digitalization 
of energy storage systems. However, there are lim -
ited studies on bio-based supercapacitor energy den-
sity predictions at present. Also, no existing study has 
reported ML-based prediction of bio-based superca-
pacitor energy density using the physical properties 
and elemental composition of electrode active materi-
als and potential window (PW). Although it has been 
used to develop model for predicting bio-based super-
capacitor specific capacitance.
Considering the potential of electrode active mate-
rials’ physical properties and elemental composition, 
as well as PW, this study aims to develop predictive 
model for predicting energy density of bio-based 
supercapacitor using physical properties such as SSA 
and PS, chemical properties such as carbon and oxy-
gen percentage, as well as PW. The data used in this 
computational study were extracted from existing 
experimental studies on bio-based supercapacitors. 
XGB, Extremely Randomized Tree (ERT), RF and 
CB algorithms were used for the ML modelling and 
prediction. Additionally, Shapley additive explana-
tions (SHAP) analysis was employed to determine

Data processing methods
For this study, data were extracted from existing 
experimental studies on bio-based supercapacitors 
from Scopus database between 2018 and 2025. The 
source of biomass carbon materials for developing 
bio-based supercapacitor electrode includes but is 
not limited to sugarcane bagasse, pear, bitter melon, 
litchi and soybean straw with specific properties 
and experimental procedures. The dataset’s features 
including physical properties (SSA, PS and intensity 
ratio (IR)), elemental composition (carbon and car-
bon-to-oxygen (C/O)), and PW were chosen to pre-
dict bio-based supercapacitor energy density due 
to their direct and significant impact on bio-based 
supercapacitor performance based on scientific 
knowledge in the area compared to other physical/
electrochemical descriptors such as scan rate [33].
The SSA and PS of biomass-derived carbon were 
determined with Brunauer–Emmett–Teller analyzer, 
which measure adsorption and desorption proper-
ties of electrode active materials. Bio-based material 
with high SSA provides more active surface for dou-
ble-layer capacitance, permitting ion adsorption on 
electrode–electrolyte interface [ 33]. PS influences effi-
cient ion transfer and storage by balancing surface 
area with ion accessibility and diffusion, which are 
crucial for bio-based supercapacitor energy density 
[41]. IR was determined using Raman spectroscopy, 
which measures disorder, defects and degree of gra -
phitization of carbon materials. Existing study has 
highlighted IR as part of the primary factor influenc-
ing performance of bio-based supercapacitors due 
to carbon materials’ defect ability to enhance pseu -
docapacitance, which contributes to enhancing bio-
based supercapacitor energy density [34]. Biomass 
materials’ surface elemental composition in atomic 
percentage (C and O) was determined with X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy. Carbon content is asso-
ciated with the stability and electrical conductivity of 
electrode active materials, while oxygen contributes 
to pseudocapacitance; both are crucial for enhancing 
the energy density of bio-based supercapacitors. Bio-
based electrode PW, measured in volts (V), is crucial 
to energy density prediction, as increase voltage sig-
nificantly contributes to enhancing bio-based super-
capacitor energy density. Carbon pore volume (PV) 
was initially included as descriptor for the modelling 
and prediction of bio-based supercapacitor energy 
density.
For manual extraction of datasets used for model-
ling supercapacitor performance prediction with ML, 
2275 experimental studies was sorted out from Scopus 
database using keywords (“biomass” OR “bio-based” 
OR “organic” OR “plant material”) AND (“activated 
carbon” OR “charcoal” OR “carbonaceous” OR “car-
bon material”) AND (“supercapacitor” OR “ultraca-
pacitor” OR “energy storage” OR “capacitive”) AND 
(“electrode” OR “electrode material” OR “conductive” 
OR “current collector”). 305 datasets were extracted 
for this study after removing datasets with missing 
material properties and energy density values. Miss-
ing PS values were calculated using Wheeling equa-
tion, as shown in Eq. 1 [42]. The manual extraction of 
the 305 datasets from existing published studies was 
carried out by carefully identifying relevant variables 
from text, tables and figures, which were then trans-
ferred into a structured spreadsheet (Microsoft Excel). 
The datasets were extracted from the main article and 
supplementary materials. Quantitative data, such as 
PW, which are presented only in figures, were recov-
ered by using OriginLab. All feature units were har-
monized, and unit conversion was carried out where 
necessary to ensure consistency across the datasets.
Machine learning model and hyperparameter 
optimization
For ML modelling and prediction of bio-based super-
capacitor energy density, dataset was randomly 
divided into 90% for training and 10% for testing. 
This study employed four (4) ML algorithms, which 
include XGB, ERT, RF and CB to create model for pre-
dicting energy density of bio-based supercapacitors. 
XGB creates prediction model via weighted summa-
tion of responses from various decision trees. Each 
decision tree in model created by XGB influences 
the prediction through mapping of input column to 
specific weights assigned to leaf nodes. It iteratively 
builds and optimizes decision tree, leverages gradi-
ent descent and adds regularization to prevent overfit-
ting, resulting in robust and accurate model suitable 
for prediction [43].
RF is a homogeneous supervised ML algorithm that 
combines numerous decision trees to reduce overfit-
ting and improve prediction performance [44]. RF cre-
ates several decision trees using bootstrap aggregating

ERT is an ensemble learning algorithm similar to 
RF, as it is also based on building multiple decision 
trees, reducing overfitting and averaging performance 
of all trees. ERT is known as extension of RF due to 
its ability to recognize relevant features for splitting 
each node. Additionally, ERT employed full datasets 
to train each decision trees unlike RF, which utilizes 
bootstrap [45].
CB, known for speed and accuracy, is a novel gradi-
ent boost decision tree. CB employed ordered boosting 
and permutation-driven techniques to resolve estima-
tion deviation of utilizing similar datasets in boosting 
iteration repeatedly in traditional gradient boosting 
decision tree algorithm [34]. Additionally, CB is effi-
cient for categorical features with small number of 
preprocessing steps, as it does not require converting 
categorical features into numerical values [34].
For generalization, enhancement of ML models 
performance and reducing overfitting, hyperparam -
eters tunning is employed during cross-validation of 
the 90% datasets assigned for training the model to 
prevent model underfitting or overfitting. Grid search 
cross-validation strategy was implemented in obtain-
ing optimal hyperparameters for enhancing the model 
performance due to its integrations, exhaustive search 
and evaluation of all possible combinations of hyper-
parameter to reduce errors. Tenfold cross-validation 
was used in the hyperparameter tuning of each regres-
sion model developed. Table 1 shows the parameters 
range and optimum used for tuning the training data-
sets for all models. Learning curve was used to discuss 
how training and validation errors change with data-
set size to diagnose underfitting or overfitting, while 
bias-variance decomposition quantifies the contribu-
tion of systematic error (bias) and prediction variabil-
ity (variance) to the model’s overall error.

RMSE utilize difference between predicted and 
actual data to measure model performance and 
accuracy. Lower RMSE value indicate model’s high 
prediction accuracy and fitness with the datasets. 
MAPE is another model performance metric that 
measures average magnitude error in prediction sets 
and expressed it as percentage of actual data. MAE 
measure average error between predicted and actual 
values of the model without considering their direc-
tion. Model with low MAE indicates good model accu-
racy, consistency and performance prediction. MAE 
was utilized as loss function for regression model in 
this study, aligning with its scale-invariant properties 
suitable for bio-based supercapacitor datasets. R 2 is a 
common model performance metrics which quantify 
proportion of variance in the output variable that is 
explained by the model [46]. A model with good per-
formance is expected to have R2 value close to 1, which 
indicates that the model can explain nearly all vari-
ation in the output values, signifying a near-perfect 
fit. For model interpretation, SHAP was employed. 
SHAP estimates Shapley values of each feature with 
respect to the corresponding output, thereby quan-
tifying their individual contributions to prediction 
[47, 48]. This approach draws inspiration from game 
theory’s allocation method, ensuring a fair distribution 
of contributions among features. To determine how 
the model learn from the data, ablation testing was 
employed for the best performing model developed 
by modelling with PW and electrode active materials 
properties separately.
Result and discussion
This study utilized datasets containing 6 independ-
ent variables, which include SSA, PS, IR, PW, C, and 
C/O to develop model for predicting energy density 
of bio-based supercapacitor. Table 2 presents statis-
tical analysis of the datasets, including count, mean, 
minimum, maximum, first quartile, second quartile, 
third quartile and standard deviation. Notably, energy 
density range was 0.34–124.00 Wh/kg, which is higher 
compared to the energy density range reported in 
the study by Lu et al. [35], where predictive model 
was developed for bio-based supercapacitor energy 
density. Energy density range highlights the repre-

PW of bio-based supercapacitors strongly depends 
on type of electrolyte used, electrode stability win-
dow, and experimental configuration. In this study, 
the datasets were primarily obtained from symmetric 
two-electrode configurations to ensure consistency in 
experimental configuration. Electrolyte type signifi-
cantly influences the operating PW of the bio-based 
supercapacitors. Aqueous electrolytes such as KOH, 
 N a2SO4, and  H2SO4 typically operate within PW 
range of ~ 0.8–1.8 V. In contrast, organic electrolytes 
such as TEABF4/acetonitrile, operates at a wider PW 
range of ~ 2.0–4.0 V. Ionic liquid electrolytes, such 
as  EMIMBF4, operate within a range of ~ 2.2–3.6 V. 
Solid-state electrolytes show variable PW depending 
on their chemical composition. Additionally, stability 
window of bio-based supercapacitor electrodes varies 
depending on ion-electrode surface interactions. These 
surface reactions influence the achievable device PW.
Conversely, modelling PW with electrode active 
materials properties such as SSA, PS, and IR may lead 
to overweighing PW due to its sensitivity to experi-
mental conditions and electrolyte type, as well as its 
interdependence with electrode properties which 
influences bio-based supercapacitor performance. 
However, PW is highlighted in several existing stud-
ies as a critical factor in determining supercapacitor 
performance, such as energy density [57, 58]. For 
developing the predictive models, PW was treated as 
a critical electrochemical feature due to its direct influ-
ence on energy density, thereby enabling all models 
to capture nonlinear relationship of PW to bio-based 
supercapacitor energy density.

Conclusion
The paper developed a predictive model for bio-based 
supercapacitor energy density to speed up materials 
discovery and reduce resources and time consumption 
associated with experimental optimization. RF, XGB, CB 
and ERT model was developed with PW and bio-based 
electrode physical and chemical properties. 305 datasets 
from existing experimental studies on bio-based super-
capacitors were extracted and split into 90% for training 
and 10% for testing. Metrics employed for the model 
performance analysis were R2, RMSE, MAPE and MAE. 
With R2 value of 0.885, XGB model was deemed the 
best ML model for predicting bio-based supercapacitor 
energy density with PW and electrode properties when 
compared with RF (0.781), CB (0.841) and ERT (0.637). 
ERT exhibited larger MAE of 9.23 Wh/kg, followed by 
RF with 8.14 Wh/kg. With XGB MAE value of 4.81 Wh/
kg, the developed model achieves accurate, precise 
and consistent prediction for bio-based supercapacitor 
energy density. SHAP analysis confirmed that PW is the 
most influential independent variable in model develop-
ment and prediction of bio-based supercapacitor energy 
density. This highlights that the incorporation of PW 
in supercapacitor energy density predictive model is 
essential. This study shows possibility to determine bio-
based supercapacitor energy density using ML model 
to reduce time and cost associated with experimental 
investigation of bio-based materials in development of 
supercapacitors. Also, it can assist to expedite discovery 
of sustainable materials from biomass for development 
of advanced and high-performance electrochemical 
energy storage.
