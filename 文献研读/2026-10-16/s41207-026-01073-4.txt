Abstract
Lithium	titanate	( Li2T iO3,	LT)	has	emerged	as	a	promising	material	owing	to	several	favorable	characteristics,	includ-
ing	 superior	 performance,	 structural	 stability,	affordability,	and	 low	 toxicity.	As	 a	 result,	 it	 is	 an	 excellent	 candidate	 for	
use	 in	 energy	storage	 and	 conversion	 applications.	The	 mechanical	 properties,	 including	elastic	 anisotropy,	shear	 modu-
lus,	 bulk	 modulus,	 and	 Poisson’s	ratio,	 are	 critical	 for	 assessing	 the	 material’s	structural	 integrity	 and	 durability	 under	
operating	 conditions,	 particularly	 in	 demanding	 applications	 such	 as	 lithium-ion	 batteries	 and	 fusion	 reactors.	 Here,	 we	
benchmark	three	supervised	learning	models	(linear	regression,	random	forest,	and	XGBoost)	to	predict	these	properties	
from	 simple	 structural	 descriptors,	 including	 unit-cell	 volume	 and	 the	 number	 of	 sites.	 Despite	 the	 limited	 dataset	 size,	
the	 models	 achieve	 strong	 predictive	 accuracy,	highlighting	 the	 potential	 of	 machine	 learning	 to	 accelerate	 mechanical-
property	screening	and	support	materials	optimization.

The	technology	and	application	of	artificial	intelligence	
have	reached	a	high	level	of	maturity,	with	many	scholars	
introducing	 experimental	 data	 and	 artificial	 intelligence	
modelling	 theory	 into	 rolling	 process	 optimization	 and	
mechanical	property	prediction	(Kibrete	et	al.	2023;	Amal-
lah	et	al.	2020;	Tanasehte	et	al.	2020;	El	Hana	et	al.	2024).	
This	has	led	to	the	emergence	of	new	data-driven	methods	
based	 on	 big	 data,	 such	 as	 machine	 learning	 (ML),	 in	 the	
field	of	materials	science	(Elkhal	et	al.	2021,	2022;	Hader	et	
al.	2020;	Tanasehte	et	al.	2022).	The	application	of	machine	
learning	 reduces	 overall	 computational	 costs	 and	 shortens	
the	development	cycle	(Huang	et	al.	2023),	and	its	use	has	
been	 prevalent	 across	 diverse	 domains	 such	 as	 computer	
vision,	data	analytics,	and	bioinformatics	(Iqbal	and	Kumar	
2023;	 Nafea	 et	 al.	 2024).	 It	 has	 also	 played	 a	 key	 role	 in	
material	 characterization,	 enabling	 the	 discovery	 of	 novel	
material	 structures	 and	 facilitating	 the	 accurate	 prediction	
of	material	and	molecular	properties	(Akinpelu	et	al.	2024;	
Fedik	 et	 al.	 2022).	 Tawfik	et	 al.	 (2020)	 applied	 ML	 algo-
rithms	to	predict	the	thermal	properties	of	crystals,	demon-
strating	the	potential	of	data-driven	approaches	for	property	
estimation	in	complex	materials	(Tawfik	et	al.	2020).	And	
several	studies	have	successfully	applied	ML	to	predict	the	
mechanical	properties	of	metals,	composites,	and	ceramics,	
as	 shown	 by	 Kibrete	 et	 al.	 (2023).	 Similarly,	Gogineni	 et	
al.	(2024)	demonstrated	that	ML-based	models	can	reliably	
correlate	structural	descriptors	with	macroscopic	mechani-
cal	performance	(Manhas	et	al.	2025).
Furthermore,	 the	 integration	 of	 machine	 learning	 has	
enabled	 the	 development	 of	 models	 that	 predict	 both	 the	
functional	 relationship	 describing	 a	 material’s	response	 to	
an	 electric	 field	 and	 its	 electronic	 properties,	 such	 as	 the	
band	 gap	 (Sudha	 et	 al.	 2022).	 Similar	 approaches	 have	
been	 proposed	 as	 an	 alternative	 route	 for	 predicting	 the	
mechanical	 strength	 of	 concrete.	As	 a	 branch	 of	 artificial	
intelligence,	machine	learning	leverages	large	datasets	and	
statistical	learning	algorithms	to	construct	predictive	mod-
els	with	improved	accuracy	and	reduced	computational	cost	
(Ben	Chaabene	et	al.	2020).
Three	 machine	 learning	 models	 were	 employed	 in	 an	
approach	 to	 supervised	 learning	 (Liu	 Duanyang	 2023).	
The	 predictive	 models	 of	 linear	 regression	 (LR),	 random	
forest	(RF),	and	XGBoost	were	employed	to	forecast	spe-
cific	 mechanical	 properties	 of	 Li2T iO3,	 including	 elas-
tic	 anisotropy	 (A),	 the	 shear	 modulus	 (G_VRH),	 the	 bulk	
modulus	(K_VRH)	using	the	VRH	approximation,	and	the	
Poisson’s	ratio	(ν)	(Ji	et	al.	2018).
While	 machine	 learning	 has	 seen	 increasing	 adoption	
in	 materials	 science,	 its	 application	 to	 specific,	 under-
represented	 systems	 such	 as	 lithium	 titanate	 ( Li2T iO3,)	
remains	 limited.	 This	 study	 addresses	 that	 gap	 by	 devel-
oping	 predictive	 models	 for	 the	 mechanical	 properties	 of	
Li2T iO3,	a	perovskite	compound	with	promising	applica-
tions	in	energy	storage	and	nuclear	fusion	(Sengodan	et	al.	
2022;	Stepišnik	et	al.	2021).
To	this	 end,	 we	 construct	 and	 benchmark	 three	 super-
vised	 learning	 models:	 Linear	 Regression	 (LR),	 Random	
Forest	 (RF),	 and	 XGBoost	 using	 physically	 interpretable	
descriptors	 derived	 from	 density	 functional	 theory	 (DFT)	
calculations,	 including	 volume,	 nsites,	 space	 group,	 and	
kpoint_density,	obtained	 via	 the	 Matminer	 framework.	 In	
this	work,	we	combine	DFT	derived	data	with	explicit	fea-
ture	engineering	on	structural	descriptors	to	construct	a	com-
pact,	 physically	 interpretable	 input	 space	 for	 the	 machine	
learning	models.	The	combination	of	domain	relevant	fea-
tures	 and	 systematic	 model	 comparison	 distinguishes	 this	
work	from	broader,	less	material-specific	studies	and	con-
tributes	to	the	growing	body	of	ML-assisted	materials	prop-
erty	prediction.
This	 work	 contributes	 to	 Sustainable	 Development	
Goals	 related	 to	 clean	 energy	 technologies	 and	 industrial	
innovation	 by	 accelerating	 the	 prediction	 and	 optimiza-
tion	of	energy-relevant	ceramic	materials	using	data-driven	
machine	learning	approaches.
Methodology
Dataset
The	 machine	 learning	 models	 were	 trained	 using	 DFT	
derived	 physical	 descriptors	 obtained	 from	 the	 Matminer	
library	(Ward	et	al.	2018),	including	elastic	constants,	struc-
tural	features,	and	compositional	attributes.	These	descrip-
tors	served	as	the	input	variables.	The	outputs	were	the	target	
mechanical	 properties	 of	 Li2T iO3,	 namely	 the	 elastic	
anisotropy	(A),	shear	modulus	(G_VRH),	bulk	modulus	(K_
VRH),	and	Poisson’s	ratio	(ν).	Prior	to	training,	the	dataset	
was	standardized	using	a	Scikit	Learn	scaler,	and	the	mod-
els	were	evaluated	using	a	5-fold	cross-validation	scheme	to	
ensure	robustness	and	reduce	overfitting.	Linear	Regression

Random forest model
The	RF’s	algorithm	builds	upon	the	principles	of	decision	
trees,	yet	introduces	a	fundamental	distinction.	In	contrast	
to	 the	 standard	 decision	 tree	 algorithm,	 which	 employs	 a	
single	 decision	 tree	 to	 derive	 predictions,	 random	 forests	
utilise	 an	 ensemble	 of	 trees	 to	 form	 a	 ‘forest’,	 thereby	
enhancing	 both	 the	 predictive	 accuracy	 and	 robustness	 of	
the	resulting	model.	Random	forest	algorithms	are	classified	
into	two	principal	categories:	regression	and	classification.	
The	 regression	 variant	 produces	 outputs	 in	 numerical	 or	
continuous	formats,	whereas	the	classification	variant	gen-
erates	outputs	in	discrete	or	categorical	forms.	In	the	context	
of	continuous	target	parameters,	the	random	forest	regres-
sor	is	the	appropriate	choice	for	this	particular	application	
(Y ang	et	al.	2023;	Zhang	et	al.	2022)	(Fig.	1).
XGBoost

based	 on	 Boosting	 Tree	 models.	 It	 introduces	 several	
enhancements,	 including	 support	 for	 linear	 classifiers	and	
the	 use	 of	 Taylor	 expansion’s	 second-order	 cost	 function	
(Sinha	et	al.	2025).	The	incorporation	of	the	second	deriva-
tive	facilitates	enhanced	accuracy	of	the	results.

Model evaluation
Model	performance	was	quantified	using	the	coefficient	of	
determination	R²;	the	root	mean	squared	error,	RMSE;	and	
the	mean	absolute	error,	MAE.	R²	gives	the	proportion	of	
variability	of	the	target	mechanical	properties	explained	by	
the	 model.	 RMSE	 is	 a	 measure	 of	 the	 typical	 size	 of	 the	
prediction	error	in	the	same	physical	units	as	the	target,	such	
as	GPa,	with	larger	deviations	being	penalized	more	heav-
ily.	 Complementing	 RMSE,	 MAE	 represents	 the	 average	
absolute	deviation	between	predictions	and	references	and	
is	 less	 sensitive	 to	 outliers.	This	 set	 of	 metrics	 provides	 a	
good	balance	between	goodness	of	fit	and	absolute	predic-
tive	accuracy	(Gogineni	et	al.	2024).

such	 as	 bulk	 modulus,	 shear	 modulus,	 and	Y oung’s	mod-
ulus	 from	 single	 crystal	 elastic	 constants,	 and	 are	 particu-
larly	 valuable	 for	 materials	 science	 applications	 ranging	
from	 geological	 rock	 physics	 models	 to	 design	 for	 highly

Results and discussion
Feature engineering and descriptor analysis
The	 input	 dataset	 consists	 of	 various	 perovskite	 compo-
sitions	 along	 with	 structural	 and	 mechanical	 properties	
derived	 through	 DFT.	Therefore,	 each	 entry	 includes	 the	
number	 of	 atomic	 sites	 (nsites),	 the	 space	 group	 number	
(space_group),	the	cell	volume,	the	kpoint_density,	and	four	
target	quantities:	elastic	anisotropy,	G_VRH,	K_VRH,	and	
Poisson’s	ratio.
Explicit	feature	engineering	for	a	richer	input	space	was	
performed	 with	 the	 help	 of	 Matminer.	First,	 the	 chemical	
formula	was	converted	into	a	Composition	object	through	
the	StrToComposition	featurizer;	then,	the	Magpie	Element-
Property	preset	was	applied,	generating	a	high	dimensional	
set	of	composition-based	descriptors:	averages,	ranges,	and	
moments	 of	 elemental	 properties	 such	 as	 atomic	 number,	
atomic	 weight,	 electronegativity,	valence	 electron	 counts,	
etc.	These	features	at	the	composition	level	were	combined	
with	the	simple	structural	descriptors	(nsites,	space_group,	
volume,	 kpoint_density)	 as	 the	 final	 feature	 matrix	 for	
model	training.
Figure	 2	 summarizes	 the	 linear	 relationships	 between	
structural	 descriptors	 and	 target	 properties.	 As	 one	 might	
expect,	 volume	 and	 nsites	 are	 highly	 correlated	 (r	≈	0.94),	
since	 both	 reflect	 the	 overall	 size	 and	 atomic	 packing	 of	
the	unit	cell.	The	space	group	number	has	only	a	weak	cor-
relation	 with	 the	 mechanical	 properties,	 reflecting	the	 fact	
that	it	is	a	discrete	symmetry	label	rather	than	a	continuous	
structural	parameter.	kpoint_density	shows	weak	linear	cor-
relations	with	most	target	properties,	but	exhibits	a	moder-
ate	positive	correlation	with	Poisson’s	ratio	(r	≈	0.32).	This	
pattern	is	consistent	with	kpoint	sampling	acting	primarily	
as	a	numerical	convergence	control	rather	than	an	intrinsic	
structural	 descriptor.	The	 shear	 and	 bulk	 moduli	 are	 well	
correlated	 with	 each	 other	 (r	≈	0.82),	 while	 elastic	 anisot-
ropy	 is	 related	 to	 the	 other	 variables	 only	 via	 weak	 linear	
trends.
In	the	interest	of	clarity,	Fig.	2	only	includes	these	physi-
cally	 transparent	 structural	 descriptors;	 the	 high	 dimen-
sional	set	of	Magpie	composition	features	is	not	represented	
directly,	but	their	effective	contribution	to	the	predictions	is	
considered	in	section	“SHAP	based	feature	importance	and	
physical	interpretation”	by	means	of	SHAP	values.	In	a	gen-
eral	sense,	the	correlation	map	in	Fig.	2	shows	that	already

weak	or	nonlinear,	which	is	a	motivation	to	use	flexible	ML	
models	and	a	dedicated	interpretability	analysis.

Model training and validation
In	 this	 study,	 three	 supervised	 learning	 algorithms	 were	
selected	 for	 predicting	 the	 mechanical	 properties	 of	
Li2T iO3:	Linear	Regression	(LR),	Random	Forest	(RF),	
and	XGBoost.
LR	was	chosen	as	a	simple	and	fully	interpretable	base-
line,	 providing	 a	 reference	 for	 how	 much	 of	 the	 variance	
in	 the	 target	 properties	 can	 be	 captured	 by	 purely	 linear	
relationships	between	the	DFT	derived	descriptors	and	the	
mechanical	response.	RF	was	included	as	a	non-parametric	
ensemble	 method	 that	 can	 model	 non-linear	 interactions	
between	 physically	 meaningful	 features	 (such	 as	 volume,	
nsites,	 space	 group,	 and	 kpoint_density)	 and	 is	 known	 to	
perform	reliably	on	small-	to	medium-sized	tabular	datasets.	
XGBoost,	a	gradient-boosting	algorithm,	was	selected	due	
to	its	strong	performance	on	structured	data	and	its	ability	to	
capture	complex	feature	interactions	while	remaining	com-
patible	with	SHAP-based	interpretability.
Together,	these	three	models	span	a	progression	from	a	
simple	linear	baseline	to	more	expressive	tree-based	ensem-
bles,	 allowing	 us	 to	 systematically	 assess	 the	 benefits	 of	
increasing	 model	 complexity	 for	 Li2T iO3	 property	 pre-
diction	while	retaining	physically	interpretable	descriptors.

Presentation of model strengths and limitations
The	results	show	that	the	models	can	learn	meaningful	phys-
ical	relationships	between	the	structure,	chemistry,	and	elas-
tic	anisotropy	of	perovskite	materials.	This	is	supported	by	
SHAP-based	 interpretability:	 the	 most	 influential	 features	
such	 as	 symmetry,	volume,	 and	 the	 number	 of	 d-valence	
electrons	are	consistent	with	well-established	principles	in	
solid-state	physics.	As	a	result,	the	predictions	are	not	only	
statistically	robust	but	also	physically	meaningful.
At	the	same	time,	the	study	is	explicit	about	the	limita-
tions,	 in	 line	 with	 transparent	 and	 responsible	 model	 use.	
Although	the	dataset	is	sufficiently	large	for	ensemble	learn-
ing,	it	is	still	small	compared	to	the	full	chemical	space	of	
perovskites.	 Consequently,	the	 models	may	not	generalize	
well	 to	 compositions	 or	 structures	 that	 lie	 far	 outside	 the	
training	 domain.	 The	 work	 also	 notes	 that	 some	 features	
are	derived	from	external	databases	or	from	DFT	calculated	
quantities,	 so	 any	 uncertainties	 or	 biases	 in	 these	 sources	
can	propagate	into	the	model	predictions.
Another	 important	 point	 is	 that	 the	 models	 are	 inher-
ently	statistical:	they	identify	correlations	rather	than	strict	
causal	 mechanisms.	 While	 the	 analysis	 indicates	 that	 the	
models	capture	trends	consistent	with	known	physical	laws,	
directionality.	Lower-symmetry	space	groups	also	push	pre-
