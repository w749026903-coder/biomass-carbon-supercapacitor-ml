[P0001] In the process of global energy transition, the dual-carbon goals have become the core driving force for promoting the restructuring of the electricity market and the development of ecological economy1. With the large-scale grid connection of wind, solar and other renewable energy sources, there arises an urgent need for efficient energy storage technologies. However, the intermittent and fluctuating characteristics of renewable energy2 have led to prominent problems of imbalance between supply and demand in the power system, which seriously restrict its absorption efficiency in the electricity market3. Large-scale wind and solar grid integration causes significant power fluctuations, demanding high-power, long-life energy storage—addressed by supercapacitors. To meet low-carbon, efficient, stable electricity market needs under dual-carbon goals, this study develops high-performance electrode materials and optimized matching schemes.
[P0002] High-performance energy storage devices are therefore urgently required to achieve the temporal and spatial transmission and dynamic balance of electric energy. As a new type of energy storage device that bridges the gap between traditional capacitors and secondary batteries, supercapacitors4, with fast charge-discharge performance, extremely long cycle life and high safety, have become a key supporting technology to solve the problem of grid connection of clean energy5, showing broad application prospects in scenarios such as power system frequency modulation, backup power supplies and distributed energy storage. The continuous expansion of the dual-carbon economy6 market has promoted the iteration of energy storage technologies towards higher energy density, reduced cost and environmental compatibility. The performance optimization of electrode active materials7 and the rational matching design of cathodes and anodes are the core breakthroughs to enhance the energy storage potential of supercapacitors. Nickel molybdate (NiMoO₄), as a typical pseudocapacitive electrode material, has attracted extensive attention on account of its abundant redox active centers8 and elevated theoretical specific capacitance. Nevertheless, single-phase nickel molybdate has defects such as poor electronic conductivity and slow ion transport, which restrict its efficiency in practical application. Rare earth element9,10 doping, as an effective strategy for modifying electrode materials, can boost the electrochemical activity of materials by regulating the crystal structure and optimizing the distribution of electronic states. Existing studies have shown that doping with La and Ce elements can significantly enhance the conductivity and structural stability of metal oxide materials11–13. However, in-depth reports on the combined application of the two in the dual-doping modification of nickel molybdate and the assembly of devices for the clean energy storage needs of the electricity market have not yet been seen.
[P0003] In terms of the selection of negative electrode materials, carbon nanotubes (CNTs) exhibit superior electronic conductivity and good mechanical flexibility, while tin dioxide (SnO₂) has a high theoretical capacity. The CNTs/SnO₂ composite material14 formed by the combination of the two can not only rely on the three-dimensional conductive network of CNTs to accelerate charge transport15, but also ameliorate the cycling durability of the negative pole by buffering the volume expansion of SnO₂ during charge and discharge, making it an ideal choice for matching with pseudocapacitive positive electrodes. Meanwhile, the development philosophy of ecological economic system requires energy storage technologies to take into account environmental compatibility and sustainability while improving performance16. The chemical pollution-free energy storage mechanism of supercapacitors, the low environmental impact characteristics of rare earth doping modification, and the designability of CNTs/SnO₂ composite materials are highly in line with the development demands of the dual-carbon economy.
[P0004] Under dual-carbon goals, clean energy storage for electricity markets must align with ecological economy principles (environmental compatibility and sustainability). This study’s La-Ce-NiMoO₄ cathode and CNTs/SnO₂ anode use low-toxicity, earth-abundant raw materials: Ni, Mo, Sn (common transition metals with low costs), La/Ce (cost-effective, moderately abundant rare earths), and CNTs (maturely prepared carbon-based material). The asymmetric supercapacitor employs 6 mol·L⁻¹ KOH aqueous electrolyte—a green, non-toxic, organic-solvent-free system. This material and electrolyte design avoids pollution from toxic/rare raw materials, matching ecological economy’s environmental compatibility requirement. Based on this, guided by the energy storage demand of the electricity market under the dual-carbon goals, this paper conducts research on the preparation of La-Ce double-doped nickel molybdate positive electrodes and CNTs/SnO₂ negative electrodes, the assembly of asymmetric supercapacitor devices17, and their performance. By systematically optimizing the doping ratio, composite process, and device assembly parameters, the synergistic mechanism between the double-doped cathode and the composite anode is revealed18. We aim to fabricate supercapacitor energy storage devices featuring both high energy density and robust long-cycle stability, provide technical support for the efficient utilization of clean energy in the electricity market, promote the coordinated development of the dual-carbon economy and ecological economy, and facilitate the market-oriented and large-scale energy storage industry applications. Advance commercialization by optimizing mass production for cost reduction, improving − 20 ~ 60℃ performance stability, and conducting prototype testing/pilots. Future work: develop efficient low-cost large-scale prep tech, enhance material extreme-environment adaptability via surface modification, and partner with enterprises for pre-commercial iteration and certification.

[P0008] As illustrated in Fig. 1, the core of preparing undoped NiMoO₄ by the hydrothermal method19 is to utilize a elevated temperature and pressure water environment to promote the controlled crystallization of nickel sources and molybdenum sources. In specific operations, accurately weigh analytically pure soluble nickel salts (such as nickel nitrate hexahydrate) and molybdates (such as ammonium heptamolybdate), dissolve them separately in ultrapure water and stir until completely dissolved. Then, under continuous magnetic stirring, slowly add the molybdenum source solution added dropwise into nickel precursor solution, and stirred continuously for 30 to 60 min to form a uniform green suspension. The pH value of the mixed solution can be adjusted to 6–8 with ammonia water or sodium hydroxide according to the target morphology. Transfer the above precursor solution to a polytetrafluoroethylene liner, control the filling degree to 70%−80%, seal the hydrothermal reaction kettle, place it in a drying oven, and react at 160–200℃ for 12–24 h. After the reaction, naturally cool to room temperature to avoid damage to the kettle body and distortion of product morphology caused by forced cooling. The reaction product is alternately centrifugally subjected to washing with deionized water and anhydrous ethanol for 3–5 successive times until the pH of the supernatant liquid is close to 7 and no impurity ions remain. Then, the washed precipitate was vacuum-dried at 60–80 °C for 12–24 h to yield undoped nickel molybdate (NiMoO₄) precursor powder. If it is necessary to improve the crystallinity of the product, the dried powder can be introduced into a muffle furnace, Was heated to 400–600 °C at a heating rate of 2–5 °C/min, calcined for 2–4 h, and NiMoO₄ is obtained after cooling.

[P0014] In this study, La and Ce co-doping was adopted for NiMoO₄ because the ionic radii of La³⁺ and Ce³⁺ are close to that of Ni²⁺, enabling their easy doping into the lattice without destroying the crystal structure. Moreover, La and Ce offer a superior cost-performance ratio compared with other rare earth elements. Ce exists in a mixed valence state of Ce³⁺/Ce⁴⁺, which can introduce oxygen vacancies and enhance the electrical conductivity of the material, while La serves to stabilize the crystal lattice. The two elements thus exert a remarkable synergistic effect on performance. The optimal total doping content was determined to be 5 at% with a La: Ce molar ratio of 1:1, which was screened out via electrochemical tests and XRD characterization. Insufficient doping content results in an insignificant modification effect, whereas excessive doping tends to form impurity phases and impair the pure-phase structure of the material. La-Ce co-doping introduces lattice distortion and oxygen vacancies, refines grain size, increases specific surface area, and optimizes the electronic structure. Without damaging the main crystal structure of NiMoO₄, it enhances electrical conductivity and structural stability.
[P0015] As illustrated in Fig. 2, in the research system of supercapacitor electrode materials, compared with undoped NiMoO₄, single La-doped NiMoO₄, and single Ce-doped NiMoO₄, La-Ce co-doped NiMoO₄ exhibits significant regulatory effects on crystal structure, electronic conduction characteristics, the number of electrochemical active sites, and electrochemical performance: Undoped NiMoO₄ usually presents a regular crystal morphology (such as rod-like, sheet-like), with tight lattice arrangement and low defect density. When single La³⁺ or Ce³⁺ is doped, due to the difference in ionic radius between the doped ions (La³⁺ ≈ 1.032 Å, Ce³⁺ ≈ 1.01 Å) and Ni²⁺ (0.69 Å), local lattice distortion is caused, resulting in a small number of lattice defects. However, La-Ce co-doping can further increase the degree of lattice distortion through the synergistic effect of the two heterovalent ions, introducing more oxygen vacancies and lattice defects. At the same time, co-doping can regulate the grain size of the material, making it present a finer and more dispersible nanostructure with abundant pore channels. This change can be verified by the broadening and intensity reduction of characteristic diffraction peaks in XRD patterns, as well as the porous morphology observed under SEM/TEM. Undoped NiMoO₄ is a typical semiconductor material with low electron mobility and elevated charge transfer resistance. In the case for single doping, the introduction of heterovalent cations forms impurity energy levels in the forbidden band, reducing the electron transition energy barrier to a certain extent. La-Ce co-doping can construct multiple electron transport channels, and the energy level difference between the two rare earth ions can form a continuous electron transition path, significantly improving the material’s electrical conductivity. In addition, the oxygen vacancies introduced by co-doping can act as “transfer stations” for electron transport, further reducing the charge-transfer resistance. This advantage can be intuitively reflected by the smaller arc radius in the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy. The number of electrochemical active sites directly determines the electrode materials’ energy storage capacity. The active sites of undoped NiMoO₄ are only limited to the Ni²⁺/Ni³⁺ redox pairs on the material surface. Although single doping can increase some active sites through lattice defects, the effect is limited. The synergistic effect of La-Ce co-doping can expand the active sites in two aspects: first, lattice distortion and oxygen vacancies provide more active sites for redox reactions; second, La³⁺ and Ce³⁺ themselves can participate in redox reactions (such as Ce³⁺/Ce⁴⁺), forming multiple redox couples, which markedly enhance the Faraday pseudo capacitance contribution of the sample. At the same time, the porous structure induced by co-doping increases the contact area of the electrode with the electrolyte, exposing additional active sites. In terms of electrochemical performance, compared with undoped and single-doped samples, La-Ce co-doped NiMoO₄ shows a significant synergistic enhancement effect. At the same current density, the specific capacity of the co-doped sample is much higher than that of undoped and single-doped samples, which is due to the dual effects of the increased number of active sites and the improvement of electronic conduction performance. Its rate performance is also optimized, and it can still maintain a high specific capacity retention rate at high current densities. The porous structure and high conductivity effectively alleviate the charge transfer bottleneck under large currents. At the same time, a reasonable co-doping ratio can inhibit grain agglomeration and structural collapse during charge and discharge processes. The existence of oxygen vacancies can also enhance the structural flexibility of the material, making the capacity retention rate after thousands of cycles significantly higher than that of undoped and single-doped samples. In summary, La-Ce co-doped NiMoO₄ breaks through the performance bottlenecks of undoped and single-doped materials through the synergistic regulation of crystal structure, electronic properties, and electroactive sites, and is an effective strategy for enhancing the comprehensive properties of nickel molybdate-based supercapacitor electrode materials.
[P0016] In the assembly of the asymmetric supercapacitor, a 6 mol·L⁻¹ aqueous KOH solution was employed as the electrolyte, a cellulose membrane served as the separator, and nickel foam was adopted as the current collector. The mass loadings of the active materials on the positive and negative electrodes were regulated in accordance with the charge balance principle (Q⁺=Q⁻) to guarantee charge matching between the two electrodes.

[P0020] As illustrated in Fig. 3, First, commercial carbon nanotubes (CNTs)20 are purified to remove impurities and amorphous carbon, and to introduce oxygen-containing functional groups. The specific operations are as follows: the raw carbon nanotubes are dispersed in a mixed solution of concentrated nitric acid solution and concentrated sulfuric acid solution (volume ratio 3:1), and refluxed and stirred at 80 °C for 6 h. The strong acid etching process can not only remove impurities and residual amorphous carbon on the carbon surface nanotubes, but also graft hydrophilic functional groups (e.g., hydroxyl groups) (-OH) on their surface, thereby significantly improving the surface reaction activity and dispersion stability of carbon nanotubes in aqueous solutions. After the etching is completed, the as-prepared sample was thoroughly rinsed with deionized water for several cycles until the filtrate was neutral (pH ≈ 7); the resulting product was then vacuum-dried at 80 °C for 12 h to obtain the purified carbon nanotube powder.
[P0021] Tin tetrachloride pentahydrate (SnCl₄·5 H₂O) was used as the tin source, sodium hydroxide (NaOH) as the precipitating agent, and deionized water as the solvent to prepare the CNTs/SnO₂ composite anode material. A certain amount of purified carbon nanotube powder was weighed and dispersed in deionized water, followed by ultrasonic dispersion at a power of 300 W for 30 min to form a uniform and stable suspension. Then, according to the preset molar ratio of 1:3 between carbon nanotubes and tin tetrachloride pentahydrate, the tin source was added to the suspension, and magnetic stirring was performed for 2 h until the tin source was completely dissolved. During the stirring process, a 0.5 mol/L aqueous NaOH solution was added dropwise at a slow rate to tune the pH of the mixed system to the range of 8–9, promoting the sufficient hydrolysis of Sn⁴⁺ to form a tin hydroxide (Sn(OH)₄) precipitate precursor. This precursor can easily combine with the surface oxygen-containing functional groups of carbon nanotubes through chemical bonding or physical adsorption, laying a foundation for the subsequent in-situ growth of tin dioxide (SnO₂).
[P0022] The obtained suspension was moved to a stainless steel reaction kettle with a polytetrafluoroethylene lining (with a filling degree of approximately 80%), sealed, and then placed in an electric thermostatic blast drying oven for a hydrothermal reaction at 150 °C for 12 h. These hydrothermal parameters are consistent with the typical preparation process of cathode materials, which can ensure the stability and repeatability of the reaction. During the hydrothermal process, the tin hydroxide precursor grows in situ on the surface of carbon nanotubes and gradually crystallizes into uniformly dispersed tin dioxide nanoparticles.
[P0023] After the hydrothermal process, the autoclave was cooled naturally to ambient temperature, and the product was collected via filtration. The solid sample was alternately rinsed with deionized water and anhydrous ethanol for 3–5 times to remove residual impurity ions such as chloride ions (Cl⁻) and sodium ions (Na⁺) in the system. The washed product was dried under vacuum at 60 °C for 10 h to yield the CNTs/SnO₂ composite precursor material. Finally, the composite precursor material was placed into an alumina crucible and calcined under the protection of an argon atmosphere (flow rate 50 mL/min). The heating rate was maintained at controlled at 5 °C/min, and the temperature was maintained at raised to 450 °C and then kept for 2 h. This calcination step can not only promote the further crystallization of tin dioxide nanoparticles, enhance the interfacial bonding force between tin dioxide and carbon nanotubes, but also remove the crystal water and residual organic impurities in the precursor. Finally, a stable CNTs/SnO₂ composite anode material with uniformly dispersed tin dioxide nanoparticles was prepared.
[P0024] The electrochemical performance of the prepared electrode materials and assembled La-Ce-NiMoO₄//CNTs/SnO₂ asymmetric supercapacitors was systematically characterized by a series of electrochemical tests, including cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and long-term cycling stability tests. All tests were carried out in a three-electrode system (for single electrode) and two-electrode system (for asymmetric device) with a standard electrochemical workstation, using 6 mol·L⁻¹ KOH aqueous solution as the electrolyte. CV tests were performed to analyze the redox reaction characteristics and electrochemical reversibility of the materials under different voltage windows and scan rates; GCD tests were conducted to calculate the specific capacitance and energy density of the electrodes and devices under various current densities; EIS tests were carried out in the frequency range of 0.01 Hz to 100 kHz to evaluate the charge transfer resistance and ion diffusion resistance of the electrochemical system; long-term cycling stability tests were performed at a fixed current density to verify the structural stability and performance retention capability of the materials and devices after repeated charge-discharge cycles.

The structure and element characteristics play a crucial role in supporting the electrochemical performance: the flower-like elevated specific surface area provides sufficient redox active centers, the pores shorten the pathway for ion diffusion, and combined with the buffering effect of uniform element distribution on volume changes, it can improve the specific capacitance, rate performance, and the cycle stability of the electrochemical system; simultaneously, the uniform active sites, efficient mass transfer pores, and single-phase composition can also enhance the efficiency and stability of the catalytic process. Among them, the formation of the flower-like structure stems from the self-assembly growth mechanism of NiMoO₄ under specific synthesis conditions. The thickness of the flake-like units is usually between tens of nanometers and hundreds of nanometers. This hierarchical structure is not only conducive to exposing more surface active sites but also can alleviate the structural damage caused by the material’s volume expansion and contraction during charge and discharge cycles. The uniform distribution of elements is further confirmed by X-ray energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis. The atomic ratio of Ni, Mo, and O is close to the stoichiometric ratio between Ni: Mo: O = 1:1:4, indicating that the sample has good chemical composition uniformity and avoids problems such as local charge transfer obstruction or reduced catalytic activity caused by element segregation. The crystal structure of single-phase NiMoO₄ is usually a tetragonal system with a space group of I4₁/amd. The layered structure enables the rapid intercalation and deintercalation of ions, and the abundant pore structure facilitates electrolyte penetration21, thereby accelerating the charge transfer kinetics. These microscopic structural characteristics and chemical composition properties work together, enabling undoped NiMoO₄ to exhibit excellent comprehensive performance in energy storage and catalytic applications.

[P0036] Figure 7(f) displays the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of four materials: NiMoO₄, Ce-NiMoO₄, La-NiMoO₄, and La-Ce-NiMoO₄. The abscissa is the 2θ angle (10°–70°), and the ordinate is the diffraction intensity (a.u.). The characteristic diffraction peaks of NiMoO₄ appear in all patterns, corresponding to the (111), (311), (400), (511), (440), and (531) crystal planes, respectively, indicating that all four materials retain the basic crystal structure of NiMoO₄. After single doping with Ce or La (Ce-NiMoO₄, La-NiMoO₄), the positions of the characteristic diffraction peaks do not shift significantly, but the intensity increases slightly. However, for the La-Ce co-doped La-Ce-NiMoO₄ sample, the intensity corresponding to the characteristic diffraction peaks is further boosted, and the corresponding peak shape is sharper, which indicates that co-doping exerts a more significant effect on enhancing the crystallinity of the NiMoO₄ crystal28. Appropriate La-Ce co-doping promotes grain refinement and improves crystallinity, leading to enhanced diffraction peak intensity and a sharper peak shape. Excessive La-Ce doping would induce lattice distortion due to the ion radius mismatch between La³⁺/Ce³⁺ and Ni²⁺, resulting in peak broadening and intensity reduction. The optimized doping ratio in this work effectively enhances the crystallinity without obvious lattice distortion, which reasonably explains the observed XRD results. At the same time, no new impurity peaks are introduced, which proves that La and Ce elements enter the NiMoO₄ lattice in the form of doping without forming independent oxide phases, and the material has high phase purity.

[P0049] To clarify the charge storage mechanism of La-Ce-NiMoO₄ cathode material, detailed electrochemical kinetics analysis was carried out, including the calculation of b-value from the relationship between peak current and scan rate, and the quantitative analysis of capacitive contribution rate. The b-value is an important index to judge the electrode reaction kinetics, which is calculated by the power law relationship between the redox peak current (Iₚ) and the scan rate (v): Iₚ = avᵇ, where a and b are constants. When b = 0.5, the reaction is dominated by diffusion control; when b = 1.0, the reaction is dominated by surface control. As shown in Fig. 11a, the b-values of the cathode and anode peaks of La-Ce-NiMoO₄ are 0.62 and 0.68, respectively, indicating that the charge storage process of the material is co-dominated by surface control (pseudocapacitance) and diffusion control (battery-type behavior), and the surface control contribution is more significant. This result is attributed to the abundant oxygen vacancies and active sites introduced by La-Ce co-doping, which accelerate the surface redox reaction of the material. The capacitive contribution rate was further calculated to quantify the proportion of surface control and diffusion control in the total capacitance. As shown in Fig. 11b, with the increase of scan rate from 5 mV·s⁻¹ to 100 mV·s⁻¹, the capacitive contribution rate of surface control decreases from 82% to 61%, while the diffusion control contribution increases from 18% to 39%. At low scan rates, ions have sufficient time to diffuse into the bulk of the electrode material, so the surface control and diffusion control both play an important role; at high scan rates, the ion diffusion time is insufficient, and the charge storage reaction is mainly limited to the electrode surface, so the diffusion control contribution increases. The capacitive contribution spectrum at 30 mV·s⁻¹ (Fig. 11c) intuitively shows the current response of the two processes at different potentials, where the surface-controlled pseudocapacitance provides the main capacitance contribution. The above electrochemical kinetics analysis clearly reveals the charge storage mechanism of La-Ce-NiMoO₄: the material mainly relies on the surface redox reaction (pseudocapacitance) to store charge, and the diffusion of ions in the bulk phase provides an auxiliary capacitance contribution. La-Ce co-doping optimizes the electrochemical kinetics of the material by increasing the surface active sites and reducing the ion diffusion resistance, which is the fundamental reason for the excellent electrochemical performance of the co-doped material.

[P0080] Guided by the increasing demand for high-performance efficiency and stability of clean energy storage apparatuses in the electricity market under the dual-carbon goals, this study designed and fabricated an asymmetric supercapacitor with La-Ce co-doped NiMoO₄ as the cathode and CNTs/SnO₂ composite as the anode. Through systematic structural characterization and electrochemical performance testing, the material modification mechanism and device energy storage characteristics were clarified: The hydrothermal method successfully achieved uniform co-doping of La and Ce elements in the NiMoO₄ lattice. XRD and XPS characterizations confirmed that the co-doping did not destroy the basic crystal structure of NiMoO₄, but significantly improved the material’s crystallinity, introduced abundant oxygen defects, and regulated the distribution of metal valence states. SEM, TEM, and nitrogen adsorption-desorption tests showed that La-Ce-NiMoO₄ exhibited a flower-like three-dimensional hierarchical porous structure with a specific surface area increased to 157.1 m²/g, providing ample active sites and efficient mass transfer pathways for electrochemical reactions. Electrochemical capability tests revealed that La-Ce co-doping synergistically optimized the electrochemical kinetic properties of NiMoO₄. The specific capacitance reached 2063 F/g at a specific current density of 1 A/g, which was significantly higher than that of the pure phase and single-doped samples. Moreover, it still maintained a capacity retention rate of 55.6% at a high current density of 10 A g⁻¹, as well as a 93% capacitance retention rate following 5000 cycles. Its excellent performance stems from the improved electron conduction efficiency, reduced ion diffusion resistance, and enhanced structural stability brought by co-doping. The CNTs/SnO₂ composite prepared with purified CNTs as the substrate, Through the close combination of SnO₂ nanoparticles and CNTs, the advantages of high capacity of SnO₂ are exerted, and the volume expansion problem of SnO₂ is alleviated by the three-dimensional conductive network of CNTs. The specific capacitive value reaches 312 F/g at 1 A/g, showing favorable rate performance and structural stability, rendering it an ideal anode material matching the La-Ce-NiMoO₄ cathode. The assembled La-Ce-NiMoO₄//SnO₂/CNTs asymmetric supercapacitor achieves efficient complementarity of the electrochemical performance of the positive and negative electrode materials. A gravimetric specific capacitance of 195 F g⁻¹ is attained at a current density of 1 A g⁻¹ for this material system, a high gravimetric energy density of 71.2 Wh·kg⁻¹, 94% capacitance retention after 10,000 repeated cycles, and reliable electrochemical operation within an extended voltage window of 1.6 V, combining high energy density, high-rate power density and outstanding cycling stability over long-term charge-discharge processes.
