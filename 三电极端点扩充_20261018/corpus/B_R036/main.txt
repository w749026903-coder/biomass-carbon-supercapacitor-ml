Potassium gluconate‑assisted synthesis of biomass derived porous 
carbon for high‑performance supercapacitor
Yu Feng1 · Ge Li1 · Xiaoliang Wu1 
Published online: 20 June 2023 
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2023
Abstract
Porous carbon has attracted enormous attentions as electrode materials for supercapacitor due to its sufficient porosity and 
large specific surface area. In this paper, we reported a convenient approach for the preparation of activated porous carbon 
(APC) by one-step activation method using bacterial cellulose as carbon source and potassium gluconate as activator. The 
obtained porous carbon possesses a unique porous framework with suitable specific surface area and rich oxygen content. 
Benefiting from the unique structure, the obtained electrode shows a high specific capacitance of 437 F  g−1  at 0.5 A  g−1  
and excellent electrochemical stability. More interestingly, the constructed APC-700//APC-700 symmetrical supercapacitor 
exhibits a high energy density of 23.5 Wh  kg−1  and good cycling stability in 1 M  Na2SO4 aqueous electrolyte. Therefore, the 
work provides a new and facile method to prepare porous carbon-based electrode materials from biomass for supercapacitor.
Keywords Bacteria cellulose · Potassium gluconate · Hierarchical porous carbon · Supercapacitor
1 Introduction
Along with the fast increasing of portable electronic devices 
and new energy vehicles, high-efficiency energy storage 
devices have become a promising choice. Among current 
energy storage equipment, supercapacitors have gained 
extensive research as a result of their high power density, 
fast charging and discharging rate and good electrochemical 
stability [1 –5]. Porous carbon with excellent conductivity 
and high specific surface area are often the first choice for 
commercialized electrode materials [6 –9]. But traditional 
porous carbon materials are often derived from non-renew-
able fossil energy. In recent years, for the sustainable devel-
opment, biomass porous carbon materials, which have the 
advantages of wide source, green and low cost as a suitable 
alternative material are widely used in electrode materials, 
such as eggshell [10], plant straw [11], poplar catkins [12] 
and various pollens [13].
Bacterial celluloses (BCs), a porous network nanoscale 
biopolymer synthesized by microbial fermentation, can be 
efficiently produced by metabolism of acetic acid bacteria 
[14]. In addition, bacterial cellulose has a natural porous net-
work nanostructure. This network porous structure ensures 
high specific surface area and low electron transport resist-
ance of carbon electrode materials [15–18]. Recently, bacte-
rial cellulose-derived electrode materials have been studied 
by researchers. Such as, Wang et al. prepared a new type of 
flexible electrode by using bacterial cellulose as a biologi-
cal template with KOH as activator. The prepared electrode 
exhibits a specific capacitance of 241.8 F  g−1  [19]. Pei et al. 
synthesized nanobelt-carbonized bacterial cellulose nano-
composite by activating bacterial cellulose with  V2O5. The 
prepared electrodes display a specific capacitance of 281 
F  g−1  at 0.25 A  g−1  [20]. However, the traditional way to 
activate bacterial cellulose is mainly through alkaline acti-
vators. This method is mainly activated by external etching 
under high temperature conditions. Therefore, the obtained 
carbon materials often have uneven pore distribution in the 
microstructure and poor connectivity between pores, further 
restricting the electrochemical property of the materials. The 
obtained electrode has a specific capacitance usually low 
300 F  g−1 , which is far from meeting the needs of practi-
cal applications. Accordingly, it is worth looking forward 
to exploring an efficient, convenient and economical way 
to prepare high performance bacterial cellulose electrode 
materials.

Herein, we  developed a convenient method for the 
preparation of porous carbon by one-step activation method 
using bacterial cellulose as carbon source and potassium 
gluconate as activator. The optimized electrode possesses 
a distinct hierarchical porous structure with suitable spe -
cific surface area and rich oxygen content. Benefiting from 
the unique structure, the obtained electrode exhibits high 
specific capacitance and excellent electrochemical stabil-
ity. More interestingly, the constructed APC-700//APC-700 
symmetrical supercapacitor exhibits high energy density and 
good cycling stability.
2  Experimental sections
2.1  Synthesis of the porous carbon
The bacterial cellulose was added into distilled water and 
stirred for 24 h, followed by freezing and evacuating in the 
vacuum freeze dryer. After drying, 1 g bacterial cellulose 
were added into 0.5 mol  L−1  potassium gluconate solution 
and stirred magnetically for 12 h. The obtained product was 
filtered and freeze-dried. The dried product was pyrolyzed 
at 600 °C, 700 °C and 800 °C for 2 h with a heating rate 
of 5 °C   min−1  in  N2 atmosphere, respectively. Then, the 
obtained product was dried after washed with 2 M HCl for 
12 h and deionized water. Then, the samples were named 
as APC-x (which x represents the activation temperature). 
For comparison, the bacterial cellulose was directly carbon-
ized at 700 °C in  N2 for 2 h, and the obtained sample was 
recorded as PC.
Characterization ways and electrochemical characteriza-
tion were recorded in supporting information.
3  Results and discussions
Scheme  1 illustrates the synthetic route of APC-x. The dried 
BC was soaked in 0.5 mol  L−1  potassium gluconate solu-
tion, and activated at high temperature after freeze drying. 
After removing impurities with dilute HCl, the hierarchical 
porous framework was formed. The microstructures of the 
obtained materials were explored by scanning/transmission 
electron microscopy (SEM/TEM). As seen in Fig. S1a, the 
materials from the direct carbonization of bacterial cel -
lulose (PC-700) shows a big block-like structure without 
obvious pore structure. After introducing potassium gluco-
nate as activator, the SEM images of the APC-700 materi-
als (Fig.  1a, b) reveal a three-dimensional interconnected 
hierarchical porous structure compose of rich micrometer 
sized macropores between carbon skeleton, which can as 
ion-buffering regions to shorten the diffusion distance of 
ions. As reported in the literature [21], potassium gluconate 
is used as an activator, when the temperature is below 500, 
it is mainly the decomposition of organic matter. when the 
temperature is above 600, the generated potassium carbon-
ate will enhance its activation ability with the increase of 
temperature, further generating potassium oxide and carbon 
dioxide, further enlarging the pore structure. In addition, as 
shown in Fig.  1c, d, TEM further confirms that the APC-
700 samples possess the three-dimensional interconnected 
porous structure with massive macropores. The APC-600 
(Fig. S1b) and APC-800 samples (Fig. S1c) possess the 
similar three-dimensional interconnected porous structure 
with APC-700. Furthermore, as shown in Fig. S2b, c, the 
corresponding elements mapping images regarding SEM 
show that the samples are composed of C and O elements.
The crystal structure of the obtained carbon materials 
was explored through X-ray diffraction (XRD). As seen 
in Fig.  2a, all materials have a weak peak at 43.5° and a 
broad peak at 23.6°, representing the 002 and 101 crystal 
planes of carbon, respectively [22, 23]. As the temperature 
increases, the (002) crystal plane reflection intensity of the 
samples gradually decreases, indicating that the degree of 
graphitization reduces gradually and the increase of tem-
perature causes more amorphous and disordered structures 
in the carbon samples [ 24]. The element compositions of 
the obtained samples were tested by X-ray photoelectron 
spectroscopy (XPS). As shown in Fig.  2b, it is obviously 
evident that all the APC samples are composed of C and O 
elements. As the temperature increases, the oxygen content 
decreases from 13.84 at% (APC-600) to 11.24 at% (APC-
700) and finally to 8.86% (APC-800). The C 1 s spectra of 
APC-700 (Fig.  2c) consists of four different peaks centering

In order to evaluate the supercapacitive performance of 
the obtained porous carbon, cyclic voltammetry (CV) and 
galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) was tested by a three-
electrode system in 6 M KOH aqueous electrolyte. Figure 4a 
presents the cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves of all samples 
displays quasi-rectangular shape at 50 mV   s−1 , demonstrat-
ing typical double layer capacitance characteristics. Moreo-
ver, the quasi-rectangular profile of the wide hump is repre-
sented by the CV curves of APC-600 and APC-700 samples, 
which indicates that massive oxygen-containing functional 
groups can provide extraordinary pseudocapacitance. It is 
obviously found that the APC-700 electrode has the largest 
CV curve integral area, suggesting the highest specific 
capacitance among all electrodes. As shown in Fig.  4b, the 
GCD curve of APC-700 owns the longest charge–discharge 
time, further demonstrating the largest capacitance for APC-
700 with the same consequence of CV curve. Meanwhile, 
the GCD profiles of all electrodes exhibits nearly isosceles 
triangular shape, which confirm excellent electrochemical 
performance and desirable rate characteristic. In addition, 
as shown in Fig.  4c, the charge–discharge curve of APC-
700 also presents a symmetrical triangular shape at vari-
ous current densities. As presented in Fig.  4d, according 
to the GCD curves, the specific capacitance of APC-700 
is 437.3 F  g−1  at 0.5 A  g−1 , which is much higher than PC

the electrode materials exhibit outstanding rate character -
istics. In addition, with the increase of current density, the

important index for showing the charge storage kinetics. 
when b value approaches 0.5, the reaction is controlled by a 
slow kinetic process as a result of pseudocapacitance, while 
the b value of 1.0 represents that the reaction is controlled 
by EDLC-controlled fast reaction kinetics. For APC-700, 
the obtained b-value is 0.95, indicating that this reaction is 
mainly controlled by EDLC (Fig.  5a). Moreover, the contri-
bution of EDLC and pseudocapacitance in the capacitance 
can be analyzed according to the equation: i(v) = k1v + k2v1/2, 
where k1v represents the contribution of EDLC-controlled 
fast kinetics and k2v1/2 corresponds to the slow kinetic pro-
cess dominated by pseudocapacitance. Figure  5b and Fig. 
S4 exhibit the kinetic process of EDLC-controlled behavior 
(green region) and pseudocapacitance-controlled behavior 
(blue region) at different scanning rates. As the scanning rate 
increases, the pseudocapacitance-controlled kinetic process 
gradually decreases and this variation is usually attributed 
to the restriction of ion diffusion. In addition, the large dis-
tribution of green regions further confirms the good dou-
ble layer properties of the carbon-based material and the 
still remained blue regions confirms the pseudocapacitance 
coming from the rapid redox reaction of oxygen. Accord-
ing to the Fig.  5c, APC-700 electrode shows an EDLC con-
trolled contribution rate of 74.01% at a low scanning rate of 
2 mV   s−1  and increased to 94.91% at 100 mV   s−1 , demon-
strating the outstanding ion storage capacity of hierarchical 
porous carbon at high scanning rate.
To further test the practicability and operation stabil -
ity of APC-700 electrode materials, a symmetric superca-
pacitor was constructed by using two APC-700 electrodes 
with equal mass as positive and negative electrodes in 1 M 
 Na2SO4 electrolyte due to  Na2SO4 electrolyte can provide 
wider decomposition voltage region (1.6 V) than KOH 
electrolyte. Figure  6a clearly shows that the voltage win-
dow of the assembled symmetric supercapacitor could be 
enormously extended to 1.8 V and anodic current without 
increase evidently. As shown in Fig.  6b, when the scanning 
speed reaches 200 mV   s−1  with the increase of voltage scan-
ning speed, the CV curve still maintains a rectangular with-
out appearing obvious distortion, meaning outstanding rate 
performance. The GCD cruves (Fig.  6c) of the APC-700//
APC-700 symmetric supercapacitor present a nearly sym-
metric triangular shape at different current densities, indi-
cating superior electrochemical invertibility. Moreover, the 
reason for the large deviations in linearity of GCD curves at 
0.25 A  g−1  is due to the presence of the pseudo-capacitance 
attributed to the quick redox reactions at the electrode–elec-
trolyte interface with the rich oxygen content [43]. Accord-
ing to the Fig.  6d, the specific capacitance of the APC-700//
APC-700 symmetric supercapacitor is 52.2 F  g−1  at 0.25 A 
 g−1 . Duing to the wide potential region and high specific 
capacitance, the energy density of the APC-700//APC-700 
symmetric supercapacitors (Fig.  6e) can reach 23.5 Wh 
 kg−1 , which is higher than previously reported carbon-based 
symmetric supercapacitors [44–50]. The Nyquist plot of the 
symmetric supercapacitor (Fig. S5) displays a low equivalent 
series resistance of 0.87 Ω in the highest frequency and a 
nearly vertical line in the low frequency, revealing excel-
lent electric conductivity and rapid electron/ions migration. 
Finally, the electrochemical stability of the APC-700//ACP-
700 symmetric supercapacitor was also measured by CV. 
The results exhibit that the capacitance retention rate of the 
10,000th cycle is 99.7% of the initial cycle. And the above 
test results further confirm that APC-700 electrode possesses 
good cycle durability and practicability.

as activator. The obtained porous carbon possesses a unique
