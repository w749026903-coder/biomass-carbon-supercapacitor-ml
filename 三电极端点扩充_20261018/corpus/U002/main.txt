Abstract: Porous carbon materials as one of the most promising electrodes for energy storage and 
conversion devices have attracted many attentions because of their large specific surface area, 
well-developed pore structure, excellent electrical conductivity and low cost. However, the 
traditional production of porous carbon is often accompanied by complex synthesis conditions, 
environmental pollution and large energy consumption. Herein, a new kind of B/N/P co-doped 
porous carbon (OPBNP) were prepared by simple hydrothermal doping method combined with a 
low-temperature carbonization-activation process using orange peel (OP) as the precursor, boric 
acid and diammonium phosphate as co-dopant. The careful regulated element doping and low 
carbonization temperature (600 oC) enable the prepared OPBNP material to have a high specific 
surface area (1774.8 m 2 g -1), microporous-dominated hierarchical structure, larger graphite 
interlayer spacing (0.392 nm), and more defects. The combination of structurally strategic design 
and multi-atom doping promotes fast charging and long-lasting stability. As electrodes in 
supercapacitors (SCs), the OPBNP delivers superior specific capacitance of 289 F g -1 at 5 A g-1 with 
93.6% capacitance retention after 10000 cycles. Besides, the assembled OPBNP//OPBNP 
symmetric supercapacitor could reach a high energy density of 8.9 Wh kg -1 at a power density of 
499.7 W kg -1. Furthermore, as anodes for sodium ion batteries (SIBs), the OPBNP also exhibits a 
high reversible capacity of 292.3 mAh g -1 after 100 cycles at 0.1 A g -1, and excellent cycling 
performance of 206.6 mAh g -1 after 1000 cycles at 1 A g -1. The B/N/P co-doped porous carbon 
behaves the characteristics of low cost, simple preparation and excellent properties, making them 
great potential electrodes for advanced supercapacitors and sodium ion batteries.
Keywords: Porous carbon; Orange peel; B/N/P co-doped; Anode; Supercapacitors; Sodium-ion 
batteries

1. Introduction
Supercapacitors (SCs) and sodium-ion batteries (SIBs) have become the most promising 
energy storage conversion devices today. They have the potentials to significantly alleviate the 
lithium resource scarcity inherent in traditional lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) and demonstrate 
promising market prospects. 1-3 Developing electrode materials with low cost and high performance 
is the crux to improve the performance of energy storage and conversion devices (SCs and SIBs). 4-6 
Porous carbon materials have been extensively studied due to their large specific surface area 
(SSA), enriched pore structure, excellent electrical conductivity and low cost. 7-10 However, the 
traditional production of porous carbon is often accompanied by complex synthesis conditions, 
serious environmental pollution and large energy consumption. As a renewable green raw material, 
biomass is considered to be a very promising precursor for the manufacture of functional carbon 
because of its rich resources, helping to sustainable energy utilization and environmental protection, 
low cost, etc. In particular, biomass-based carbon, such as rice husk, 11 wheat-straw, 12 bagasse, 13 
pomelo peels, 14 waste palm, 15 etc. have unique structural properties as bioresidual materials. 
Besides, their inherent hierarchical porous structure can provide not only a large accessible surface 
area but also smooth transfer paths for electrolytes ions. Furthermore, most of the biomass contain 
the elements of N, S, and P, and therefore, heteroatom self-doping can be realized in the synthesis 
of the materials to form additional active sites in the biomass-based carbon materials. Therefore, the 
development of high-performance porous carbon-based materials with controlled pore structure and 
composition using biomass as a precursor can not only save costs and alleviate environmental 
pollution, but also achieve the high-value utilization of biomass.
Activation conditions and heteroatom doping also show significant effects on the pore 
structure and surface chemistry of porous carbon. 16, 17 Traditional porous carbon material is usually 
obtained by one-step or two-step high temperature pyrolysis (usually above 700 °C) combined with 
activation, resluting in a high energy consumption. 9, 18 In recent years, hydrothermal carbonization 
technology (HCT) has been used for waste biomass treatment due to its low energy consumption 
and high efficiency. 19-22 At relatively mild temperatures (180-250 °C), waste biomass in the water 
environment tends to be rich in oxygen-containing functional groups, which has considerable 
activation potential and can produce porous structures with high porosity. At the same time, 
hydrothermal method can effectively stabilize heteroatom doping and improve graphitization,

provide more electrochemical active sites to enhance the capacity of the materials, thus effectively

performance for both SIBs and potassium ion batteries (PIBs), which can be attributed to the fact 
that N, P elements can effectively increase the interlayer spacing of the hard carbon and provided 
more active sites. 26 Muruganantham et al. also confirmed that the reversible capacity of N/S 
co-doped carbon derived from mango peel is significantly higher than that of the un-doped carbon 
material.27 Orange juice is one of the most widely consumed beverages today. Its large-scale 
production meets people's needs, but it also produces a large amount of waste orange peel (OP). 28,29 
OP is mainly composed of cellulose, hemicellulose, pectin and lignin, which are known as good 
carbon precursors. It is reported that OP-derived carbon materials have been employed in 
catalysis,30 gas sensors, 31 Pb-C mixed SCs, 32 LIBs/SIBs, 33 PIBs, 34 etc. However, most of OP are 
discarded and burned as waste due to the lack of effective and inexpensive synthesis methods, 
resulting in the environmental pollution and resource waste. Therefore, it is necessary to explore 
how to prepare a high-performance biomass carbon material from OP through hydrothermal 
heteroatom doping and low-temperature activation technology. To our knowledge, there is no report 
on OP-derived B/N/P co-doped porous carbons as electrodes for SCs and SIBs. 
Herein, we report a new B/N/P co-doped porous carbon (OPBNP) employing OP as carbon 
sources, boric acid and (NH 4)2HPO4 as dopant, via hydrothermal heteroatom doping and 
low-temperature KOH activation method. The OPBNP behaves a high SSA of 774.8 m 2 g-1, larger 
interlayer spacing of 0.392 nm, and rich interpenetrate network porous structure, which could 
facilitate the transport of ions and electrons in the electrodes. At the same time, the synergistic

2. Experimental Section 
2.1 Materials
Orange peels (OP) were collected from Zhenjiang fruit market. The orange peels were first 
rinsed in deionized water to remove impurities such as fouling and then vacuum dried at 80 oC 
overnight. After that, the clean OPs were crushed into fine powder by a pulverizer and stored in a 
clean flask under dry condition for further use. All chemicals in our research were used directly as 
received without further treatment. Distilled water was used throughout the whole experiment.
2.2 Synthesis of B/N/P co-doped porous carbon
Firstly, 2 g of OP powder, 1 g of (NH 4)2HPO4, and 1 g of boric acid were added into 60 ml of 
deionized water to stir for 4 h to receive a suspension. Then, the suspension was transferred into a 
100 mL stainless steel sealed high-pressure reaction kettle and kept at 180 °C for 12 h. After natural 
cooling to room temperature and vacuum filtration, the as-obtained sample were collected by 
washing with deionized water and dried overnight at 80 °C to obtain hydrothermally doping 
products. Next, the products and KOH were dissolved in 50 ml of deionized water at a mass ratio of 
1:3, after be stirring at room temperature for 10 h then dried at 100 °C for later use. The obtained 
products were then tiled in a nickel porcelain boat and placed in a tube furnace to calcination 
activation 3 h at 600 °C with the heating rate of 3 °C min -1 under nitrogen atmosphere obtain. The 
obtained black samples were washed with 2 M HCl to remove the salt residue then washed with 
deionized water to neutral, dried overnight at 80 °C to obtain the resultant B/N/P co-doped porous 
carbon (OPBNP). The detailed synthesis process of OPBNP in this study is shown in Scheme 1.
For comparison, the sample prepared via the similar method without the addition of 
(NH4)2HPO4 and boric acid was denoted as OPC. While the sample synthesized via the similar 
method with only (NH 4)2HPO4 was denoted as OPNP, and the sample with only boric acid was

2.3 Characterizations
The phase structure and chemical composition of the porous carbon materials were 
characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Shimadzu XRD-6000) with Cu Ka radiation (λ=0.15406 
nm). The morphology was investigated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Quanta FEG 
250) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM, Tecnai G2 F20). The specific 
surface areas, pore volume and pore diameter of carbon materials were analyzed by nitrogen 
adsorption-desorption measurements (Micrometric, JW-BK200B), and calculated by 
Brunauer-Emmett-Teller theory and non-local density functional theory methods, respectively. The 
chemical states of elements were further investigated by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, 
Thermo Fisher Nexisa). Raman spectra of the as-synthesized sample were carried out on a 
Renishaw Raman spectrometer.  
2.4 Electrochemical measurements for supercapacitors
The electrochemical properties of the samples for supercapacitors were evaluated both in 
three-electrode half-cells and two-electrode systems within 6 M KOH electrolyte. Working 
electrode were prepared by mixing the active carbonaceous material (80%), the conductive agent 
acetylene black (10%), and the binder polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) (10%) in 
N-methyl-pyrrolidone (NMP) to produce a uniform thick slurry, then the mixed slurry was coated 
on nickel foams (1×2 cm2) which were pretreated with acetone and hydrochloric acid. The electrode 
materials were then placed in a vacuum drying oven, dried for 12 h at 80 oC, followed by pressing 
into a film at a pressure of 10 MPa. The loading mass of active material was 1-2 mg cm -2. The Pt 
foil and Hg/HgO electrodes were used as the counter and reference electrodes, respectively. 
Galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD) and cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were conducted 
in the voltage range of -1–0 V (vs. Hg/HgO), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) with 
the frequency range of 10 5–10-2 Hz and amplitude of 5 mV were conducted on the electrochemical 
workstation (CHI660E, Shanghai, China). Cycling stability was tested using a battery test 
instrument (CT2001A, Wuhan Blue Electronics Co., LTD.). The symmetrical supercapacitors 
devices were assembled by using two working electrodes with the same mass of active substances 
(loading weight of about 2 mg cm -2) in the CR2032 button cell, Whatman glass fiber (GF/D) as 
separator, and 6 M KOH as electrolyte solution. The measurements of full cell were performed over

2.5. Electrochemical measurements for SIBs 
For SIBs tests, the electrode materials were assembled into a button half-cell of CR2032 for 
measuring the electrochemical performance. The preparation of working electrodes was the same as 
that of supercapacitors, the difference was that the slurry is uniformly smeared on the Cu foil 
current collector. After being dried at 70 °C for 12 h in a vacuum drying oven, and the electrode 
pieces were cut into circular plates with a diameter of 12 mm. The cells were assembled with 
metallic Na for both reference and counter electrodes in a glovebox in Argon with the moisture and 
oxygen content all under 0.01 ppm. Glass microfiber filter (Whatman GF/D) was used as the 
separator, sodium perchlorate (NaClO 4, 1M NaClO 4 in EC: PC=1:1 Vol % with 50 % FEC) was 
used as the electrolyte. The assembled cells were evaluated by galvanostatically discharge-charge 
on the LAND battery test system. CV measurements were completed on an electrochemical 
workstation (CHI 760E, Chenhua Instruments Co. Ltd., Shanghai) in the voltage range of 0.01-3.0 
V and a scan rate of 0.1-1 mV s –1. The EIS spectra were measured on an Autolab-PGSTAT 302N 
electrochemical workstation with frequency ranging from 100 kHz to 10 mHz and an amplitude of 
5 mV.

also favors the full contact of the electrode active substance and the electrolyte, improving the

pore volume of OPBNP is 0.81 cm3 g-1, and the average pore size is 1.82 nm, indicating that its pore

its specific surface area, pore volume and pore size distribution. It is well known that micropores 
can accommodate charges in the electrical double layer and provide more electrochemical reaction 
active sites for ion storage, while mesopores can provide fast ion diffusion paths, further boosting 
the rate performance of the electrodes. 12,21 Therefore, these hierarchical porous carbon materials 
doped with heteroatoms have a great potential as electrode materials in SCs and SIBs.

energies of 284.75, 286.05, 287.61 and 288.91 eV, corresponding to C-C/sp 2C, C-B/C-P/sp 2C-N, 
C=O and C-O functional groups, respectively (Fig. S3). 12,42 The N 1s spectrum contains three peaks 
with binding energies of 398.49,400.32 and 401.9 eV, respectively (Fig. 3f). The corresponding 
valence structures include pyridine N (N-6)/N-B, pyrrole N (N-5) and graphite N/N=P. 41,43 N-6 and 
N-5 mainly act as the electron donor and generate defects to increase the active sites as well as 
provide pseudocapacitance contribution, 44 while graphitic N can increase the conductivity of the 
material.44 For the O 1s spectrum (Fig. 2g), the characteristic peaks at 532.57, 533.87 and 535.2 eV 
can be attributed to C-O-C/C-O-H, O-B and -COOH functional groups, respectively. 41 The three 
characteristic peaks of B 1s are shown in Fig. 2h, and the corresponding functional groups are B-C 
(189.84 eV), B-N (190.97 eV) and B-O (191.82 eV); the P 2p spectrum has three characteristic 
peaks (Fig. 2i) with binding energies of 131.79 eV P-O group, 133.43 eV C-PO 3/C2-PO2 group and 
134.66 eV P-C group, respectively. 42,46 The doping of heteroatoms can significantly improve the 
properties of porous carbon materials, in terms of increasing conductivity, modulating disorder and 
defects of materials, thus promoting the formation of surface pseudocapacitance, which will bring 
benefits to the application of samples in the field of electrochemistry.5,47,48

4. Conclusion 
In summary, new B/N/P co-doped porous carbon (OPBNP) were successfully prepared by 
hydrothermal doping combined with KOH activation using low-cost orange peel as raw material for 
SCs and SIBs energy storage applications. Structural characterization and chemical composition 
analysis showed that OPBNP material had the high SSA, large interlayer spacing, rich 
micro/mesoporous structure, and enhanced disorder, which were favorable for the improvement of 
electrochemical performance. Benefiting from these structure features, when employed as 
supercapacitor electrodes, the OPBNP delivered a high specific capacity of 289 F g -1 at 5 A g -1, 
with only 6.4% of capacitance loss after 10000 cycles, and could still maintain 248.0 F g -1 at 20 A 
g-1. Moreover, the assembled OPBNP//OPBNP symmetric supercapacitor exhibited an energy 
density of 8.9 Wh kg -1 at a power density of 499.7 W kg -1. As anodes for SIBs, the OPBNP 
electrode also showed outstanding rate capability and excellent long cycling life of 63.79% capacity 
retention after 1000 cycles. The method adopted in this paper not only realizes the scientific 
"transformation" of agricultural waste, but also promotes the development of energy storage 
devices, solves the problems caused by the irrational use of agricultural waste, and provides a new 
way for the balance of the ecological environment.
