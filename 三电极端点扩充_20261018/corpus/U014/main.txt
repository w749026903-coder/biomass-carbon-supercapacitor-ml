Peanut oil cake biomass is a major by-product of oil industry and can be a suitable precursor for the production of biomass-based
porous carbon. Herein, dual-stage activation, physical activation at 400 °C, and chemical activation using KOH between 600 and
900°C are adopted to prepare hierarchical porous carbons (HPCs) from peanut oil cake biomass for use as electrode material in
electrochemical supercapacitors. The textural and structural properties of the HPC were evaluated by N
2-adsorption/desorption,
X-ray diffraction, Raman, FT-IR spectroscopy, and scanning electron microscopy. The prepared HPC inherited suf ﬁcient micro-
pores and mesopores distribution with a maximum BET surface area of 640 m 2/g. The electrochemical properties of the HPC
electrodes were evaluated by cyclic voltammetry, galvanostatic charge –discharge, and electrochemical impedance spectroscopic
analysis. Among different HPC electrodes prepared, the carbon prepared at 800 °C (HPC-P8) electrode material exhibited a high
speciﬁc capacitance of 187 F/g at 0.5 A/g in 1M Na2SO4 electrolyte in three-electrode systems. Symmetric supercapacitors were also
fabricated using HPC-P8//HPC-P8 as positive and negative electrodes using 1M Na 2SO4 electrolyte, in a two-electrode cell
conﬁguration via split cell method. Maximum speci ﬁc capacitance of 30 F/g was achieved with superior cycle stability of 93.8 %
capacitance retention over 5,000 cycles for the symmetric device. The device operated at a wide potential window (0–2.0 V) resulted
in appreciable speci ﬁc energy and power density of 15.89 Wh/kg and 493 W/kg, respectively. HPC-P8 had an ion diffusion
coefﬁcient of 7.79 × 10−8 cm2/s which is higher than the other prepared carbon samples. The symmetric device showed relatively
high charge storage capacity and cyclic stability without any material degradation. The fabricated prototype portable super-
capacitor module was tested for red LED glow with maximum intensity brightness up to 4 min. Thus, the present study reveals
that HPC derived from peanut oil cake biomass can behave as a promising electrode material for supercapacitor applications.
1. Introduction
In the present days, supercapacitors are gaining importance due
to the need for the advancement of energy storage devices for
societal applications. Supercapacitors constitute an effective
energy storage behavior compared to the traditional capacitors
and batteries. Supercapacitors, also known as ultracapacitors, are
energy-storage devices that are used as stand-alone power source
due to technological advancements and their needs. Supercapa-
citors have advantages over traditional condensers and batteries,
for higher energy and power densities, faster charge and dis-
charge rates, longer cycle life, and safer operation [1]. Because
of these special qualities, supercapacitors are increasingly used in
consumer electronics, industrial power management, and heavy
electric vehicles. These features enable supercapacitors to be
used as the primary or secondary power sources in various
devices such as heavy machines and versatile gadgets [2].
Supercapacitors are divided into two types such as electric
double layer capacitors (EDLCs) and pseudo capacitors based
on the charge storage mechanism. In EDLCs, energy is stored by
the electrostatic charge accumulating at the electrode–electrolyte
interface [3, 4]. EDLC electrodes are generally made of porous
carbon (PC) based materials. In pseudocapacitors, the capaci-
tance arises from reversible redoxprocesses at the electroactive

the need of environment concerns, recently, researchers are
aligned toward the use of sustainable biomass waste precursors
to prepare the PC materials for supercapacitor electrodes. Plenty

value can be used as low-priced source of carbon for the high-

biowaste into carbonaceous materials has gained attention as an
alternative pathway to lessen the issues related to waste disposals.
Thus, biowastes are now gaining importance as they are easily
converted to energy storage materials for energy storage technol-
ogies. This strategy, in turn, eases the energy crisis problems and
at the same time reduces the pollution-related problems. In this
connection, several carbon materials are produced from biomass
waste precursors like cow dung [9], peanut shell [10, 11], soy-
bean cake [2, 12], sunﬂower seed oil cake [13, 14], soybean root
[15], etc., because of their natural abundance and low cost.
Among the PCs category, hierarchical porous carbons (HPCs)
having multimodal pore size distributions via micro–meso- and
macro-pores have so much consideration in supercapacitor
applications as a result of their high surface area, good adsorp-
tion capability, and well-developed interconnected pore net-
works. Because of industria lization and advancement in
technologies, effective energy use and storage is yet another
major challenge in the present days. Therefore, it is necessary
to develop an eco-friendly, environmental sustainable, and low-
cost energy storage materials using an easily available precursors
or raw material by environmentally benign process for an effec-
tive energy storage applications.
Peanuts, also called as groundnuts, are traditionally an
important grain legume crop cultivated globally for oil pro-
duction by both small- and large-scale farmers as they have
signiﬁcant commercial value. Among many oil-seeded crops,
peanut is one of the leading oil seeds used for the production
of cooking oil in many other countries till today. India is one
of the second largest producers of peanuts with annual
production of 101 lakh tonnes [16]. Though peanut is directly
edible and is an important ingredient in bakeries, confection-
ery industries, soaps making, yet, they are preferred mostly for
oil production as it generates considerable revenue. Therefore,
after extraction of oil from peanuts by cold press technique,
the cake remains as a major by-product and hence arises the
problem of useful utilization of this waste cake. These peanut
oil cakes are known to possess a good source of protein and
are used as one of the ingredients in animal feed.
Therefore, the present work is focussed on to prepareprepar-
ing HPC electrode material from peanut oil cake biomass by
dual-stage activation method, and subsequently exploring the
same for electrochemical performance by cyclic voltammetry,
galvanostatic charge–discharge and electrochemical impedance
spectroscopic analysis. The electrodes are also characterized to
study their textural and structural properties by N
2-adsorption/
desorption, X-ray diffraction,Raman, FT-IR spectroscopy, and
scanning electron microscopy techniques. The electrochemical
performances of PC electrode materials were analyzed in 1M
Na
2SO4 aqueous electrolyte, as neutral electrolytes are ecolog-
ically well disposed in nature, eco-friendly, safe handling, low
cost, and have good ionic conductivity. The electrochemical
studies were performed by three-electrode technique as well as
two-electrode technique for symmetric cell devices.

Figure 1 depicts the prototype supercapacitor module,

The prototype mini module cell was built by sandwiching
two active material electrodes facing each other with a sepa-

Teﬂon plates were attached to the adjacent sides of the elec-
trodes, and the entire cell was wrapped in Te ﬂon tape, and
completely closed with a small opening at the top to ﬁll the
electrolyte.
2.5. Electrochemical Studies. The electrochemical studies of the
HPC electrodes and symmetric device were performed by using
ZIVE-SP2 electrochemical workstation. Active material-coated
nickel foam electrode was used as the working electrode, while
an Ag/AgCl and platinum wire were used as the reference and
counter electrodes, respectively. The electrochemical studies
(CV, GCD, and EIS) of synthesized HPC carbon samples were
evaluated using three-electrode system and two-electrode system
for symmetric device using 1M Na
2SO4 electrolyte at room
temperature.

3.2. Surface Area and Pore Volume. The HPC-PC sample
showed lower surface area of 0.54 m 2/g. During precarboni-
zation process, the temperature employed (400° C) was only
able to convert the peanut oil cake into carbon char leading
to the formation of a mere rudimentary carbon structure by a
release of loosely bound volatiles and hence possessed low
surface area. For the chemically (KOH) activated PC samples
(HPC-P6, HPC-P7, HPC-P8), the total surface area increases
with increasing activation temperature from 600 °C to 800 °C
and thereafter decreased for HPC-P9 sample (900 °C). The
surface area values for HPC-P6, HPC-P7, HPC-P8, and
HPC-P9 carbon samples are 313, 380, 640 and 430 m
2/g,
respectively. The increase in the surface area may be attrib-
uted to the release of certain volatile and organic components
from the carbon matrix, which leads to pores development
caused by the KOH activation and impregnation. The
decrease in surface area at 900 °C (HPC-P9) might be attrib-
uted to different processes such as contraction, agglomera-
tion, and coalitions of pores as a result of the higher
temperature (900°C).
The micropore surface area ( S
mic) was calculated by sub-
tracting the mesopore surface area (Smeso) from the total BET
surface area. The micropore surface area increased from 269
m
2/g (HPC-P6) to 530 m 2/g (HPC-P8) as activation temper-
ature increased from 600 to 800 °C, respectively, as shown in
Table 1. This moderate rise in micropore surface area ( Smic)
can be attributed to the substantial liberation of bound vola-
tiles within the temperature range (600–800°C). This explains
the pore drilling effect taking place between 600 and 800 °C
due to the ejection of tightly bound volatile compounds.
Thereafter, micropore surface area decreased to 263 m
2/g
(HPC-P9) at 900 °C. The decrease in micropore surface area
at 900 °C is due to substantial thermal activation, where
micropore drilling stops and widening into mesopores and
macropores occurs. The mesopore surface area ( Smeso) also
moderately increased from 44 m 2/g (HPC-P6) to 110 m 2/g
(HPC-P8) for prepared PC at 600 and 800 °C and abruptly
increased to 167 m2/g at 900°C activation. Total pore volumes
were calculated using nitrogen adsorption curves at 0.99 rela-
tive pressures. The t-plot method was used to determine the
micropore volumes. By subtracting the micropore volume
from the total pore volume, the mesopore volume was deter-
mined. It is observed that the pore volume slightly increased
from 0.1618 (600 °C) to 0.2785 cm
3/g (800°C) and thereafter
decreased to 0.1959 cm3/g.

3.5. Morphology Analysis. Surface morphology studies of
HPC-PC samples are shown in Figures 5(a), 5(b), 5(c),
5(d), and 5(e). The HPC-PC showed only a plain surface
and there are no visible pores on the surface of sample,
indicating insufﬁcient energy for pore creation in the sample
at 400 °C. However, after KOH activation between 600 and
900°C (HPC-P6 to HPC-P9), samples showed quite well-
deﬁned pore structure with different pore sizes on the surface
of the samples, as evidenced in the SEM images. The pore
development might be due to the expulsion of noncarbonac-
eous materials and removal of other minerals that were
bound inside the precursor matrix. Deep burrow-like pores
were observed as a result of pore drilling and pore widening
effect. Looking at the SEM images of hierarchical porous
samples, KOH activation causes a strong chemical etching
on the carbon surface creating well-developed pore structure
at each applied temperature.

recorded in the range of 4,000 –500 cm
−1 in order to investi-
gate the functional groups available on the surface of the PC.
It clearly shows that all spectra are almost similar. A broad

appears at 3,427 cm
−1. The bands around 2,865 and 2,925
cm−1 are attributed to C ─H stretching vibrations [20]. Band

and esters, respectively [22].

other PC samples showed two major peaks observed at 1,317
and 1,578 cm
−1, corresponding to the D and G bands, respec-
tively [23]. The D peak (disordered peak) is attributed to the
defects and disorder in the carbon structure arising due to the
vibrations of sp
3-bonded carbon atoms. The G peak is related to
the graphitic order arising due to sp2 hybridization in the carbon
material. Another prominent 2D band appearing in the spectral
region between 2,780 and 3,024 cm
−1 is related to the stacking
order of the graphene layers [19].
In this study, it is noticed that the 2D band position for
HPC-P6, HPC-P7, HPC-P8, and HPC-P9 varies by increas-
ing the activation temperature and appears at 2,780, 3,024,
2,897, and 2,853 cm
−1, respectively This variation in band
position might be attributed to the interaction between the
constructed graphene layers, making it possible to distin-
guish between graphene and graphite, based on the number
of layers [24]. The intensity of the G band is lower than the
intensity of the D band in all PC materials [25], indicating a
certain level of disorderness.
The degree of disorders in the carbon materials is evalu-
ated from the intensity ratio of the two bands ( I
D/IG). Higher
the ID/IG values, larger is the degree of defects in the carbon
materials. The calculated ID/IG values for HPC-P6, HPC-P7,
HPC-P8, and HPC-P9 are 1.02, 1.21, 1.42, and 1.47, respec-
tively. Conversely to the XRD results, the increasing trend in
defect concentration or disorder with increasing carboniza-
tion temperature as reﬂected from the Raman analysis (ID/IG)
shall be due to the pore widening effect dominating the pore
drilling effect leading to the formation and increase in meso-
pore surface area (Table 1) in the carbon structure as a result
of KOH activation as the temperature is increased. Hence, the
synergistic effect between graphitization degree and pore size
distribution could contribute to electrical conductivity as well
as enhanced charge storage behavior.

5. Conclusion
This work proposed a concept of “Waste to Energy” applica-
ble in the waste management. Using an effective and conve-
nient method, hierarchical HPC from waste peanut oil cake
has been produced and it exhibited an extraordinary electro-
chemical property with well-developed textural and struc-
tural characteristics. In comparison to all the prepared
HPC samples, HPC-P8 exhibited excellent electrochemical
performance in three-electrode system. The fabricated sym-
metric supercapacitor cell using HPC-P8//HPC-P8 electrode
material showed 93.8% electrochemical stability over 5,000
charge/discharge cycles. The fabricated device operated in a
wide potential window of 2 V in 1M Na
2SO4 showed high
speciﬁc energy density of 15.89 Wh/kg at a speci ﬁc power
density of 493 W/kg. The glow of red LED from a prototype
device proves that HPCs prepared from peanut oil cake will
be a promising biomass material for high-performance elec-
trode material in supercapacitor applications.
