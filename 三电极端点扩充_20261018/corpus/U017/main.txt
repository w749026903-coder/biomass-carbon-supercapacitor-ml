Preparation of microwave-assisted expired
honey-derived electrodes with excellent
performance for supercapacitors
Xinting Yu, †a Yuxin Zhu, †b Yunqiang Zhang* b and Mei Li* b
Microwave and hydrothermal methods were employed to prepare expired honey-based biochar,
respectively, followed by KOH activation, carbonization and Co 2+ catalysis. The eﬀect of microwave
heating and the mechanism of Co 2+ catalysis were investigated using XRD, Raman, XPS and BET results.
The biochar heated using microwave technology has interconnected pores, a higher specific surface
area (1404.6 m 2 g/C0 1), a greater macropore volume (1.081 cm 3 g/C0 1), oxygen-rich atoms (9.71 at%), and
highly graphitized structural characteristics, compared with that obtained using a hydrothermal method
which has a lower specific surface area (451.2 m 2 g/C0 1) and a lower oxygen atom content (8.71 at%).
Due to its dominant structural characteristics, supercapacitors (SCs) based on as-prepared expired
honey biochar (CMC) exhibit superior capacitive performance, including ultra-high capacitance (up to
780.3 F g
/C0 1 at a current density of 0.2 A g /C0 1) and good cycling stability (over 96.7% capacitance
retention at 10.0 A g /C0 1 after 10 000 cycles of constant current charge and discharge) in a three-
electrode system containing 2.0 M H 2SO4 electrolyte solution. The excellent electrochemical properties
and eﬃcient synthesis of biomass-derived carbon materials provide an economical and feasible strategy
for large-scale production of high-performance electrodes.
1. Introduction
Researchers are looking for eﬃcient and low-cost alternative
clean energy sources, including lithium ion batteries, lithium
sulfur batteries, sodium ion batteries, zinc ion batteries, fuel
cells, and supercapacitors (SCs).
1–5 Supercapacitors are widely
studied for their high capacity and ability to meet the growing
energy demand due to their stable cycle performance, high
energy conversion efficiency and distinctive power density.
6
However, their low energy density significantly limits their
further application as the main energy source, and the specific
capacitance (C
sp) and operating voltage ( DV) of the electrode are
the key factors affecting the energy density. 7,8 Since carbon
materials efficiently store and release electrical energy through
electrostatic interactions between electrolyte ions and the elec-
trode surface, their C
sp can be improved by increasing the
effective specific surface area (SSA). 9,10 The fractional micro–
meso–macroporous structure is more advantageous, which can
effectively improve the ion accessible specific surface area of
materials and the kinetics of electrochemical reactions.
11 Meso-
pores and macropores can be used for the storage of electrolyte
ions to shorten the ion transport path, so that electrolyte ions
can be transferred from mesopores and macropores to micro-
pores, which greatly increases the ion diffusion rate and
effectively improves the capacitance and rate performance of
materials.
12,13 Furthermore, increasing attention has been paid
to enhancing the wettability and pseudocapacitance of carbon
materials by doping with various heteroatoms (such as N, O, S,
B, and P) to enhance the electrochemical performance.
14,15
In practical applications, biochar materials are promising
electrodes due to the low-cost recycling of biomass waste and
eﬃcient energy storage, and great research eﬀorts have been
focused on tailoring advanced porous biochar electrodes for
achieving a tunable pore structure, higher available SSA and
higher conductivity.
16–18 Heat treatment of biomass materials
with KOH activation at high temperature is one of the most
important ways to increase their surface area and adjust
their pore structure, but most biochar-based SCs show poor
capacitance (usually below 300 F g
/C0 1).19–21 Long-time high-
temperature treatment destroys electrochemically-active func-
tionalities.
22 Besides, conventional high-temperature heating
methods are relatively limited in producing high-quality mate-
rials, due to obvious thermal gradients, slow reaction kinetics

and inhomogeneous heating. 23 Thus, developing short-time or
low-temperature carbonization processes is crucial for obtaining
functional carbon with rich functionalities. Recently, microwave
heating techniques have become effective methods to prepare
micro- and nanomaterials. Microwave irradiation induces dipole
rotation and ionic conduction at the molecular level, leading to
homogeneous and quick thermal reactions with low thermal
gradients.
24 Compared with conventional heating techniques,
microwave heating techniques provide advantages such as higher
heating/reaction rates, more homogeneous thermal transmission
and easier formation of porous structures.
25,26
In this work, a three-dimensional (3D) interconnected porous
carbon (IPC) with a high O and N atom content was fabricated
through a developed microwave-assisted activation method from
biomass waste. Expired honey, as waste biomass, can enhance
resource utilization through reuse while its high glucose and
fructose content provides abundant oxygen-containing functional
groups, conferring an additional pseudocapacitive eﬀect.
27,28
The as-prepared three-dimensional interconnected porous
carbon from expired honey exhibits a large accessible SSA
(1404.6 m
2 g/C0 1), an ordered hierarchical porous structure,
and a high level of heteroatomic doping. Benefiting from the
synergistic eﬀects of the above merits, the as-made carbon
materials exhibit a large gravimetric specific capacitance ( C
sp)
of 508.2 F g /C0 1 at a current density of 1.0 A g /C0 1 in 2.0 M H 2SO4,
high rate capability (78.2% capacitance retention at 10.0 A g /C0 1),
and excellent cycling stability (96.7% capacitance retention
after 15 000 cycles). In addition, a high energy density of
24.7 W h kg
/C0 1 at a power density of 102.9 W kg /C0 1 was obtained
in a 2.0 M H 2SO4 electrolyte, which shows its potential as a
promising electrode for biochar-based SCs in practical applica-
tions. The present work presents a simple and economic way to
obtain biochar-based electrodes for high-performance SCs and
advances our understanding of eﬀective strategies to engineer
the microstructure of carbonaceous materials.
2. Experimental section
2.1. Material synthesis
Expired honey was placed in a microwave reactor for heating to
prepare high-quality biochar. Specifically, the honey was six
months past its labeled expiration date and exhibited natural
crystallization and a higher solid content (determined with an
Abbe refractometer) compared to fresh honey. The micro-
wave reactor (model XH-300UL) was operated at a frequency
of 2450 MHz in continuous mode. The temperature was moni-
tored using an infrared sensor, and the sample was heated at
180 1C (with a fluctuation of /C6 5 1C) under a constant power of
200 W for 4 hours. After heating, the sample was washed with
deionized water and dried. An appropriate amount of the dried
sample was then immersed in a solution containing KOH and a
certain concentration of Co(NO
3)2, stirred for 6 hours, and
subsequently dried. The obtained product was placed in a tube
furnace and sintered under an argon atmosphere, with the
temperature ramped from room temperature to 700 1C at a rate
of 5 1C min
/C0 1. After cooling to room temperature, the sample
was immersed in 0.1 M HCl solution and washed with deio-
nized water until neutral, and the final product was denoted as
CMC. For comparison, the sample prepared using the same
microwave heating method without Co(NO
3)2 immersion was
labeled as MC. The sample prepared by heating in a conven-
tional drying oven with Co(NO
3)2 immersion was designated as
CHC, while the counterpart without Co(NO 3)2 immersion was
labeled as HC.
2.2. Material characterization
A scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi S4800) equipped
with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS, Oxford) and
a transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM-2100) were
employed to investigate the microstructure and morphology
of the samples. The Fourier-transform infrared spectra (FTIR)
of the samples were recorded with a TENSOR 27 FTIR spectro-
meter using KBr pellets. The X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) patterns of
the samples were recorded using a D8-ADVANCE (Bruker AXS,
Germany) X-ray diﬀractometer (Cu K a radiation, l = 1.5406 Å)
with a scan rate of 10 1C min
/C0 1. The Raman spectra were
recorded on a laser confocal micro-Raman spectrometer (Lab
RAM HR800) with an excitation wavelength of 532 nm, and the
argon ion laser source ranged from 400 to 2600 cm
/C0 1. The X-ray
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were per-
formed with a multifunctional imaging electron spectrometer
(Thermo ESCALAB 250XI) with a monochromatic source of
Al-Ka (1253.6 eV photons). The S
BET and pore size were deter-
mined by the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) and Barrett–
Joyner–Halenda (BJH) methods, respectively.
2.3. Electrochemical measurements
The two and three electrode cell systems were designed to eval-
uate the electrochemical performance of the prepared electrode
materials in 2.0 M H
2SO4 aqueous electrolyte. For electrode
preparation, 80.0 wt% active material, 10.0 wt% acetylene black
and 10.0 wt% polyvinylidene fluoride binder were mixed,
with N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone being used as the solvent to form
a well-dispersed slurry. Besides, the slurry is evenly coated
on a stainless steel net collector and then dried in an oven at
60 1C for 4 h.
A platinum sheet and a standard calomel electrode were
used as the counter electrode and reference electrode, respectively.
Furthermore, cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge–
discharge (GCD) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy
(EIS) measurements were performed using a CHI660E electro-
chemical workstation.
In the three-electrode cell, the active material was used as
the working electrode, a standard calomel electrode (SCE) was
used as the reference electrode, a platinum sheet was used as
the counter electrode, and 2.0 M H
2SO4 acted as the electrolyte,
respectively. The CV and GCD curves were collected in the
potential range of /C0 0.3 to 0.9 V in 2.0 M H 2SO4 electrolyte
with a sweep rate of 10–100 mV s /C0 1 and a current density of
0.2–50.0 A g /C0 1. EIS was carried out in the frequency range from

3. Results and discussion
This work proposes a novel and feasible heating strategy to
obtain a three-dimensional (3D) interconnected porous carbon
(IPC) electrode for high-performance SCs, and Scheme 1 pre-
sents a schematic diagram of the preparation of expired honey-
based biochar with a high oxygen atom content. Instead of
traditional heating techniques, expired honey was heated in a
microwave reactor at 180 1C for 4 h to gain high quality biochar,
followed by activated carbonization at 700 1C after impregna-
tion in a KOH and Co(NO
3)2 solution. Molten KOH and Co 2+
catalytic graphitization treatment were employed under high-
temperature-activated carbonization, enabling the successful
in situ fabrication of O-doped interconnected hierarchical por-
ous carbon from honey. A large number of pores were gener-
ated by KOH activation, inevitably resulting in the reduction of
graphitization, while the addition of Co
2+ improved the graphi-
tization degree by converting it to cobalt oxide and metallic
cobalt during carbonization to transform the amorphous
carbon into graphitized carbon.
29,30 After the removal of the
residues of KOH, Co and its compounds with hydrochloric acid,
a carbon material characterized by rich porosity, a large surface
area, and abundant oxygen-rich functional groups was
obtained and named CMC. In order to highlight the eﬀects of
the heating method and Co
2+ treatment, expired honey was
subjected to hydrothermal heating and microwave heating,
respectively, and the diﬀerence with Co
2+ or not was compared.
Samples were prepared by microwave heating without cobalt
nitrate addition (MC), by hydrothermal heating with cobalt
nitrate addition (CHC) and by hydrothermal heating without
cobalt nitrate addition (HC). Expired honey biochar samples
prepared only through hydrothermal and microwave treatment
are called BH and BM, respectively.

the micropores, mesopores and macropores after microwave

analysis reveals a hierarchical porous structure within CMC,
which endows the material with rapid reaction kinetics and
superior capacitive performance. Notably, the significantly
increased number of mesopores not only offers more accom-
modation sites for electrolyte ions but also shortens their
diffusion paths to the micropores.
The phase compositions of HC, CHC, MC, CMC were
characterized by XRD analysis (Fig. 2c). Two primary peaks at
21.41and 44.41 are observed which correspond to the (002) and
(101) planes of graphitic carbon.
33 The ratio of (002) to (101)
peak intensities ( I(002)/I(101)) was calculated for each sample to
assess changes in crystallinity. Compared to CMC, CHC exhib-
ited a smaller I(002)/I(101) ratio, indicating decreased crystallinity
after microwave treatment due to increased porosity. Conver-
sely, CMC underwent graphitization catalyzed by Co ions at
elevated temperatures,
34 resulting in an enhanced I(002)/I(101)

1600 cm

the D-band, which corresponds to disordered sp 2-hybridized
carbon atoms, and the G-band, which relates to the graphitic
mode of carbon materials.
35,36 The samples of CMC showed a

CHC (ID/IG = 1.053) and MC ( ID/IG = 1.191), indicating that Co 2+
can balance the graphitization degree reduced by KOH activa-
tion. KOH activation and Co 2+ catalytic graphitization promote
the formation of the honey-based biochar with an appropriate
pore structure and graphitization degree, consistent with XRD
results.

4. Conclusions
In this work, expired honey was heated at the molecular level by
microwave heating to obtain high-quality precursor materials,
and honey-based derived carbon (CMC) was further prepared
by KOH-activated carbonization and Co
2+ catalytic graphitiza-
tion. CMC demonstrated a three-dimensional interconnected
porous structure with a large SSA (1404.6 m
2 g/C0 1) and large pore
volume (1.081 cm 3 g/C0 1), which can increase the eﬀective con-
tact area between the material and the electrolyte ions and
provide enough active sites for the rapid redox reaction;
Oxygen-rich atoms (9.71 at%) can improve the wettability of
the electrode material and provide substantial pseudocapaci-
tance, and the improvement in the graphitization degree pro-
motes electron conduction, which is assisted by Co
2+. Due to
the above dominant structural characteristics, CMC shows
superior capacitive performance (up to 780.3 F g
/C0 1 at 0.2 A g /C0 1)
and good cycle stability (over 96% capacitance retention
after 15 000 constant-current charge and discharge cycles at
10.0 A g
/C0 1). The excellent electrochemical properties and rapid
and eﬃcient synthesis of expired honey-based carbon materials
provide an economical and feasible strategy for large-scale
production of high-performance porous carbon.
