ABSTRACT
The development of sustainable and effective electrode materials is essential to 
the advancement of high-performance supercapacitors. In this study, we report a 
low-cost, eco-friendly way of producing samarium-doped activated carbon from 
mosambi peel (sweet lime) waste using the KOH activation process for utilization 
in supercapacitors. The addition of samarium to the biomass carbon framework 
increased the number of active sites and enhanced the material’s characteristics. 
Electrochemical analysis demonstrates that the samarium-doped mosambi peel-
activated carbon (Sm-doped MS-AC) electrode achieves a remarkable specific 
capacitance  (Cs) of 560 F/g at 1 A/g current density. The material demonstrates 
a high energy and power densities of 68.80 Wh/kg  (Ed) and 470 W/kg  (Pd). Fur-
thermore, the electroactive material revealed excellent kinetic features with a low 
solution resistance  (Rs) of 0.6 Ω and retained exceptional cycling stability over 
6500th cycles. The practical capabilities of the material were evaluated through 
two-electrode symmetric studies. These analyses yielded  Cs of 182 F/g,  Ed of 
20.54 Wh/kg, and  Pd of 1800 W/kg. These results demonstrate the crucial role 
that heteroatom engineering plays in improving the electrochemical behaviour 
of carbons derived from biomass, offering an adaptable approach for sustainable 
energy storage devices.

1 Introduction
The global utilisation of conventional fuel brought 
about by the industrial revolution caused a significant 
negative influence on environment [1 ]. Fossil fuels 
are running out, there is an enhanced requirement to 
develop sustainable sources. However, external factors 
must be constantly focussed on the impact of renew-
able energy sources like water, wind, and solar [2 ]. 
These resources also have a number of serious draw-
backs, such as their complete reliance on nature for 
example, the need for wind to produce electricity or 
inability to utilise solar cells at night [3 , 4]. In recent 
decades, researchers have emphasized their efforts 
creating energy conserving and storage applications. 
Batteries, capacitors, and supercapacitors  (SCs) are 
example of energy storing electronic devices that are 
essential for overcoming these obstacles. Nevertheless, 
fuel cells and batteries are much more costly, have less 
ion mobility and limited durability than  SCs [5, 6].
As a result,  SCs contributed to closing the gap 
between minor energy density  (Ed) of capacitors and 
lower power density  (Pd) of batteries.  SCs are viewed 
as one of the leading prospects for energy storage 
innovations due to its quick charging, long lifespan, 
high  Pd and environmental safety [ 7, 8]. As a result, 
 S Cs are a superior choice for electronic devices that 
demand a constant supply of energy, together with 
portable electrical appliances and other electric vehi-
cles such as hybrid electric cars [9 , 10]. These  SCs are 
categorised as electric double-layer capacitor (EDLC), 
pseudo capacitor (PC), or hybrid supercapacitors rely-
ing on charge storage approach [11]. In order to store 
charges, the EDLC gathers double-layer charges at 
electrode/electrolyte interface [12]. In EDLC, carbo-
naceous substances containing carbon, graphene and 
graphite are typically used electroactive materials [13, 
14]. However, PC enable charge storage through redox 
reactions that include electron transport between elec-
trode and electrolyte [ 15]. Furthermore, because of 
their enormous  Ed and ability attained by integrating 
complementary qualities of PC and EDLC with high 
voltage hybrid supercapacitor (HSC) believed to be 
efficient of preserving energy [16].
Moreover, carbon materials having special qualities, 
such as their affordability, chemical inactivity, high 
surface area and modifiable pore size provide attrac-
tive options for  SCs. Activated carbon (AC) is most 
effective adsorbent in world and surface accumula-
tion is one of the most used techniques for recovering

The objectives of this study, to explore effect of Sm

material for advanced SC.
2  Experimentalanalyses
2.1  Reagentsutilised
Chemicals utilised for fabrication of Sm-doped 
MS-AC composite were samarium nitrate (III) hexa-
hydrate (Sm(NO 3)3.6H2O, 98%), potassium hydrox-
ide (KOH, 99.9%), citric acid  (C6H8O7, 99%) ethanol 
 ( C2H5OH, 99.4%), hydrochloric acid (HCl, 37%), poly 
(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, 99.9%) all are acquired 
from Sigma–Aldrich, while mosambi peel gathered 
from local market.
2.2  Fabricationofmosambipeel(sweetlime)
activatedcarbon(MS‑AC)
Waste mousambi peel biomass was converted into 
porous hard carbon using chemical and physical acti-
vation approaches. Peels from biomass, cleaned using 
ethanol and distilled water eliminate of contaminants 
and dehydrated for 12 h at 100 °C. 10 g of already-pre-
pared mosambi peel powder were mixed with KOH 
in ratio of 1:3 and calcined for 4 h at 500 °C in order 
to produce AC. Derived material first purified using 
distilled  H2O. After being drying at 80 °C to create AC 
and stored for further characterization [31].
2.3  SynthesisofSm‑dopedMS‑AC
nanocomposite
1 g of mosambi peel (sweet lime) activated carbon (MS-
AC) was dissolved in 50 mL of distilled  H2O in order 
to create Sm-doped MS-AC using a hydrothermal 
method. For half an hour, this dispersion was agitated. 
0.3 g Citric acid used to make the carbon more porous 
with a hierarchical structure [32] followed by 0.03 g of 
samarium nitrate hexahydrate [Sm(NO 3)3.6H2O] cor-
responding to 3 wt.% weight of activated carbon. This 
combination shifted to autoclave and heated for 4 h at 
180 °C. Once precipitates collected, they repeatedly 
purified with distilled  H2O. After that, precipitates 
dried for 24 h at 80 °C, display in scheme 1 [33].
2.4  Fabricationoftheelectrode
Nickel-foam (Ni-foam) was cut into (1 × 1  cm2) small 
pieces and cleaned via sonication with HCl, ethanol 
and then distilled water. The nanocomposite (0.015 g), 
carbon black (0.0025 g) used to increase the conduc-
tion, PVDF (0.0025 g) act as a binder used to bind the 
material on Ni-foam, and binder solution n-methyl-
epyroledone (75 μL) were mixed for 2 h in an HPLC 
tube to create the required slurry. The resulting slurry 
was then applied to Ni- foam utilising the drop-cast-
ing approach with help of a micropipette. Finally, the 
deposited nickel foam was then dried at 90 °C.
2.5  Characterizationtechnique
The XRD (X-ray Diffraction) technique was utilised 
to observe crystalline attributes of generated sub-
stance activated carbon (MS-AC) and Sm-doped 
MS-AC. This was determined using the D8 Advance 
Bruker X-ray diffractometer with Cu-Kα source 
(40 kV, 100 mA, λ = 1.5418 A°) with 2θ in 10° to 70°. 
Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET), a surface area ana-
lyser technique, is used to look at a certain surface 
area utilising Nova-2200 Quantachrome instrument. 
A Zeiss Sigma-500VP scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM) equipment utilised to take SEM images, while 
FT-IR confirmed the position of functional groups uti-
lising FT-IR spectrometer (Thermo Scientific Systems 
Nicolet-6700).
2.6  Electrochemicalcharacterizationtechnique
Cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge–dis-
charge (GCD), electrochemical surface area (ECSA), 
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), and 
chronoamperometry (CA) executed on three-electrode 
setup in 3 M KOH using lab’s CS Corrtest 350 M equip-
ment. Ag/AgCl were employed as reference electrodes, 
Pt wires as auxiliary electrodes, and Sm-doped MS-AC

4  Conclusion
In the present research, biomass-derived activated 
carbon (AC) from mosambi (sweet lime) peels was 
hydrothermally combined with samarium salt to 
create Sm-doped MS-AC, which resulted in high SC 
electrode productivity. Based on GCD tests, electro -
chemical characterisation revealed that the Sm-doped 
MS-AC composite electrode had a higher  Cs of 560 
F/g at 1 A/g, with  Ed of 68.80 Wh/kg and  Pd of 470 W/
kg. The Nyquist plot illustrates doped electrodes with 
 Rs values of 0.60 Ω, while an excellent cyclic stability 
over 6500th cycles. For the creation of MS-AC nano-
structures, the usage of samarium offers a number of 
benefits. Increased faradic sites on the MS-AC matrix 
within the nanostructures, reduced  Rs, and improved 
conduction are all benefits of Sm doping. As a result, 
the addition of Sm stabilize the MS-AC nanostruc-
ture which is confirmed by its consistent performance 
across several charge–discharge cycles. While sym -
metric study demonstrates  Cs,   Ed and  Pd of Sm-doped 
MS-AC was 182 F/g, 20.54 Wh/kg and 1800 W/kg, simi-
larly. These results indicate that our created Sm-doped 
MS-AC nanocomposite material can also be employed 
in other energy storage applications like batteries.
