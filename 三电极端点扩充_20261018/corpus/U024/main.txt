ABSTRACT: The production of value-added materials from waste
resources is a sustainable approach for solid waste management. Facile
yet economic conversion of carbon-neutral biowastes into a metal-free
porous carbon material with a high oxygen reduction reaction (ORR)
and capacitive energy storage efficiency is critical for renewable energy
conversion and storage technologies. Herein, a microporous carbon
with a self-doped N heteroatom (ALPC) was successfully prepared
from waste agarwood leaves through a chemical activation process.
The synthesized ALPC-800 material containing N (11.23 atom %)
and O (8.54 atom %) with a high surface area as an electrocatalyst for
ORR shows an onset potential (E
onset
) of 0.98 V and excellent limiting
current density (J
L
) of 6.09 mA cm
−2
, which are comparable to 10 wt % Pt/C. ALPC-800 as an electrocatalyst shows a four-electron
transfer-mediated ORR process which is supported by both experimental and theoretical findings. Also, ALPC-800 in a three-
electrode configuration for supercapacitors presented a high specific capacitance of 421 F g
−1
at a current density of 1 A g
−1
in an
acidic electrolyte. More prominently, the assembled symmetrical supercapacitor device based on ALPC-800 demonstrated a high
capacitance retention of 91% after 6000 cycles at 1 A g
−1
with a remarkable energy density of 21.15 Wh kg
−1
and a power density of
4.93 kW kg
−1
. Thus, this work provides an environmentally benign, simple, efficient, and economical method for energy storage and
conversion applications. Being a metal-free catalyst, heteroatom-doped ALPC-800 carbon material synthesized from biowaste has the
potential to replace the commercially available high-cost Pt/C catalyst for fuel cell application.
KEYWORDS: waste leaves, N-doped graphitic carbon, metal-free ORR, supercapacitor, clean energy conversion

INTRODUCTION
In the past few decades, with the ever-increasing depletion of
fossil fuel reserves and rising environmental pollution,
extensive research efforts have been dedicated for the
technological advancement of sustainable energy storage and
conversion systems.
1−3
Within the context of fossil-free and
efficient energy applications, the oxygen reduction reaction
(ORR) and supercapacitors have been enormously inves-
tigated, and yet, the fundamental challenge remains in the
design and development of highly efficient, low-cost electro-
active materials.
4
In fuel cells, ORR is an important
electrochemical reaction which takes place at the cathodic
site.
5,6
Pt-based electrocatalysts are generally recognized to
deliver excellent ORR activities due to their high current
density and remarkable electrochemical kinetic process.
7
However, high cost, scarcity, substandard durability, and
susceptibility to methanol tolerance are the major inherent
hurdles for bulk-scale commercial application.
8,9
Though
transition metal-based electrocatalysts have become alterna-
tives to Pt/C for boosting the performance of the ORR
process,
10,11
critical issues still remain in terms of their poor
chemical stability, irregular distribution of active sites, high
price, and eco-unfriendliness. In this regard, carbon-based
nanomaterials like graphene and carbon nanotubes have been
widely explored as electrode materials for ORR on account of
their robust stability, high electrical conductivity, and large
surface area.
12−14
Despite their excellent performances, wide-
reaching utilizations for practical application are strictly
controlled by the high cost, low yield, and complex synthetic
conditions.
15
Nowadays, supercapacitors are widely used in

16
Supercapacitors have several advantages over
traditional storage devices such as high power density, rapid
charging, long life cycle, and wide operating temperatures.
17
But, traditional supercapacitors mostly suffer from low energy
density, which suppresses their far-reaching applications with

18
Bio and plastic waste management are a major challenge for
modern civilization. Nowadays, scientists are very much
engaged in environmental remediation through conversion of
waste materials (such as leaves, crops, waste municipal
materials, etc.) to value-added products and its application in
harvesting, conversion, and storage of clean energy.
19,20
In
recent years, transforming waste biomass resources into
multifunctional porous carbon materials has gathered immense
universal acceptance for energy security due to their distinct
advantages in terms of low processing cost, high availability,
abundant reservation, recyclability, and great potential for
commercial production.
21−24
Moreover, managing and recy-
cling biomass waste into value-added carbon could also solve
the emerging pollution concerns surrounding disposal
problems and improve the decarbonized economic efficiency.
Biomass and its corresponding natural waste products enriched
internally with heteroatoms (N, O and others) are expected to
produce N- and O-rich functionalities in the resultant carbon
matrix without introducing any external doping agents.
25
The
modification of the carbon material by heteroatom doping not
only introduces more defects and additional pseudocapacitance
from surface faradaic reactions, but also increase the exposed
active sites for O
2
surface adsorption in ORR by modulating
the electronic structure.
26−28
Additionally, porous nanostruc-
tures with tailored pore size distribution could accelerate the
transport of ions as well as provide accessible active sites, both
of which are critical for accomplishing high-performing ORR
and supercapacitor activities.
29,30
To date, much biomass and biowaste as carbon precursors
coupled with appropriate synthesis processes (e.g., pyrolysis,
template method, chemical vapor deposition, etc.) have been
employed for the fabrication of advanced heteroatom-doped
nanostructured carbon materials.
31−33
However, most of these
reported techniques involve relatively intricate templates,
tedious processes, and hazardous organic precursors, which
may not necessarily meet the principles of green chemistry.
34
Among the many techniques available, chemical activation
using KOH is the most preferred approach to efficiently
control the composition or structure of a biomass carbon with
desirable pores.
35,36
Besides KOH, activating agents such as
ZnCl
2
, H
3
PO
4
, etc. are also widely employed. However, of the
many reagents available, KOH activation takes precedence in
terms of delivering higher specific surface area (SSA) and well-
developed microporosity in the resultant porous carbon, apart
from minimizing tar formation and improving the activation
reaction efficiency.
37,38
For example, KOH-activated spruce
bark-derived porous carbon presented the highest SSA of 2209
m
2
g
−1
, followed by ZnCl
2
(1019 m
2
g
−1
) and MgCl
2
(98 m
2
g
−1
).
39
In another instance, rice-husk-derived graphene-KOH
showed better physicochemical properties with regard to pore
volume and sp
2
carbon content than the carbon material
prepared by H
3
PO
4
activation. This observation suggests that
KOH as an activating agent is more effective than H
3
PO
4
.
40
Moreover, KOH is considered to be less toxic to the
environment in comparison to ZnCl
2
or H
3
PO
4
, making it a
reliable choice.
41
Lin et al. observed that soybean-derived
nitrogen-doped porous carbon as an ORR electrocatalyst
demonstrated a current density of 5.09 mA cm
−2
.
42
It also
exhibits a high specific capacitance of 243.2 F g
−1
at a current
density of 0.5 A g
−1
.
42
Harraz and co-workers reported that
nitrogen-doped porous carbon materials from B. vulgaris leaves
attributed a current density of 5.74 mA cm
−2
and 0.8 V (vs.
RHE) onset potential for the ORR electrocatalyst in an
alkaline medium.
43
Taro stem-derived N-doped porous carbon
exhibited excellent stability of 96.5% after 20,000 s for ORR
and exceptional power density (5 kW kg
−1
at 5.56 Wh kg
−1
).
44
Biomass-derived O/N co-doped porous carbon material
synthesized by Liu et al. shows a specific capacitance of 251
F g
−1
at a current density of 1 A g
−1
as well as electrocatalytic
activity toward ORR.
45
Perez-Salcedo and co-workers reported
that KOH-activated biocarbon showed good current density of
5.2 mA cm
−2
in an alkaline medium for ORR application along
with a high capacitance of 207.3 F g
−1
at 0.5 A g
−1
.
46
Likewise,
mulberry leave-derived N, S-porous carbon presented impres-
sive specific capacitance of 214.5 F g
−1
at 0.5 A g
−1
, onset
potential (E
onset
) of 0.86 V, and excellent catalytic selectivity.
47
The focus of this work is to synthesize a value-added
product from a biowaste precursor for catalytic conversion of
clean energy through ORR and capacitive energy storage
applications. Agarwood leaves (Aquilaria malaccensis) are
mostly a kind of common biowaste in India and are rich in
phenolic acids, carbohydrates, and alkanes as well as nitrogen-
rich alkaloids, flavonoids, etc.,
48
which make them a valuable
and reliable feedstock to prepare heteroatom-doped activated
carbon. In this paper, waste agarwood leave-derived N-doped
porous carbon (ALPC) was prepared via a template-free
chemical activation process using KOH as the activating
reagent. As heteroatoms are already there in the chemical
composition of agarwood leaves, nitrogen is inherently doped
in the synthesized catalyst. In-depth characterization studies
revealed that the as-obtained ALPC materials possessed a
nanoflake morphology, a favorable degree of graphitization,
microporosity, large SSA, and an optimal amount of inherent
heteroatoms. Based on the exclusive structural and composi-
tional properties, the porous carbons delivered interesting
ORR and supercapacitor characteristics. The synthesized
ALPC-800 as an electrocatalyst exhibited excellent ORR
efficiency having a current density (6.09 mA cm
−2
) and
positive onset potential (0.98 V vs. RHE) which is almost
comparable to Pt/C. ALPC-800 also demonstrated maximum
efficiency through a four-electron pathway for electrocatalytic
ORR activity. Moreover, ALPC-800 showed better efficiency
in terms of a stability test (70% retention) and methanol
tolerance test compared to a commercially available 10 wt %
Pt/C catalyst in a O
2
-saturated 0.1 M KOH media. In terms of
the supercapacitor application, the ALPC sample obtained at
800 °C (ALPC-800) in a three-electrode configuration
presented a high specific capacitance of 421 F g
−1
at a current
density of 1 A g
−1
using a 0.5 M H
2
SO
4
aq. electrolyte.
Moreover, as a fabricated symmetrical supercapacitor (SSC)
device, a high energy density of 21.15 Wh kg
−1
and a high
power density of 4.93 kW kg
−1
along with excellent durability
(91% capacitance retention after 6000 cycles at 10 A g
−1
) was
achieved. Thus, this work follows an affordable, eco-friendly,
and facile approach for the conversion of waste material to the
value-added product for the conversion of clean energy and
capacitive energy storage applications.

EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Preparation of Agarwood Leave-Derived N-Doped Porous
Carbon (ALPC). As presented in Figure 1a, the ALPC materials were
prepared via a potentially scalable chemical activation process using
KOH as an activator. At first, the agarwood leaves were washed
thoroughly with double-distilled water and dried at 80 °C for 12 h
before being crushed into a fine powder with a high-speed grinder
(Philips Hl1631). Secondly, the powdered leaves were mixed with
NH
4
Cl in a mass ratio of 1:1 for 10 mins and kept in a vacuum oven
at 80 °C for 24 h. A similar procedure was also followed with KOH.
Subsequently, the preactivated reaction mixture was carbonized in a
tube furnace at 700 °C (ALPC-700), 800 °C (ALPC-800), and 900
°C (ALPC-900) under Ar atmosphere for 2 h with the heating rate of
5 °C min
−1
, releasing no toxic emissions. The carbonized samples
were washed several times with 3 M HCl and double-distilled water
along with ethanol and finally dried at 90 °C overnight.
Electrocatalytic Measurements for ORR. The typical three-
electrode system consisted of a 3 mm polished glassy carbon (GC)
disk electrode as the working electrode, a Pt wire as the counter
electrode, and Hg/HgO as the reference electrode. The slurry for the
working electrode was prepared by mixing 5 mg of the ALPC
materials in ethanol and isopropanol (790 μL:200 μL) solution and
Nafion solution (10 μL) under ultrasonication for 1 h. A total of 5 μL
of the as-prepared ink was drop cast on the GC disk electrode and
dried at room temperature. The catalyst ink for the commercial Pt/C
(10 wt %) was prepared by the same procedure. The voltammograms

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Physical Characterizations. The crystalline nature of the
as-obtained ALPC-800 was characterized by a powder X-ray
diffraction (PXRD) technique. In Figure 1b, the PXRD pattern
exhibits two broad low-intensity peaks centered around 24.9°
and 42.4°, which are indexed to the (002) and (100)
diffraction planes, respectively, for a typical graphitic carbon.
9
In order to further elaborate the structural features of the
synthesized carbonaceous materials, the comparative Raman
spectra of the ALPC samples carbonized at different
temperatures are presented in Figure 1c. Two distinct bands
are located at ∼1357 and ∼1591 cm
−1
, corresponding to the D
band (defect or disordered carbon) and G band (ideal
graphitic order). Generally, the value of the relative intensity
ratio of the D band and G band (I
D
/I
G
) gives the
graphitization degree of a carbonaceous material. The
appearance of a 2D band (overtone of D band) at ∼2855
cm
−1
indicates formation of graphite-like domains, which can
provide the ALPCs with superior electrical conductivity.
63
It is
found that the I
D
/I
G
values of ALPC-900, ALPC-800, and
ALPC-700 are 0.97, 0.97, and 0.96, respectively, indicating that
the defects of sp
2
carbons increase with higher carbonization
temperature. The presence of structural defects in the carbon
framework, which arise either from the heteroatom functional
groups or micro/mesoporous formation during the chemical
activation, facilitates high electrochemical performances by
enhancing the electronic conductivity and number of active
sites of the materials.
27
The evolutions of the surface morphology of the raw
biomass during the carbonization process at different high
temperatures and the corresponding derived carbons were
systematically studied by field emission scanning electron
microscopy (FESEM) analysis. The representative images of
the as-obtained ALPC materials (Figure 2a−c) show
substantial changes in the morphology of the raw agarwood
leaves after the chemical treatment with KOH, resulting in the
transformation of the bulk structures of the nontreated
precursors (Figure S1) into porous structures. KOH as a
chemical activation reagent is known to increase the surface
area of a sample as well as introduce tunable porosity via the
etching process due to its high solubility and chemical
reactivity. During the process of carbonization, different
carbonates are formed from the reaction involving KOH and
the carbon atoms of the biowaste, while the decomposed
metallic K vapor is embedded into the carbon layers, which in
turn results in the expansion of the carbon lattice.
64
Moreover,
as displayed in Figure 2b, ALPC-800 showed interconnected
3D cross-linked nanoflake morphology in comparison to
ALPC-900 and ALPC-700, inferring here that an optimal
temperature of 800 °C in combination with KOH is most
favorable for the formation of a highly porous nanostructure.
The subsequent EDX mapping of ALPC-800 confirmed the
uniform distribution of C, N, and O elements throughout the
carbon framework and is shown in Figure 2d−g and Table S1.
The existence of the heteroatoms is due to the natural
availability of such elements present in the biomass source, as
illustrated in Figure S2.
A transmission electron microscopic (TEM) study was
performed to further comprehend the nanostructural proper-
ties of the as-obtained ALPC-800 material. Ultrathin nano-
flakes with abundant micropores were observed from the TEM
analysis under different magnifications (Figure 2h and i),
which support the FESEM results. The micropores are known
to provide adsorption sites for ions, which is favorable for the
enhancement of the electrochemical performance. Figure 2j
shows the diffused ring-like selected area electron diffraction
(SAED) pattern of ALPC-800, which is indicative of an
amorphous carbon.

CONCLUSION
In summary, bifunctional N-doped microporous carbon
materials have been successfully prepared from low-cost
waste agarwood leaves by a simple and safe two-step chemical
activation method. The structural characterization studies
reveal that this series of resultant porous carbon samples
possesses highly developed nanoflake morphology with large
SSA (718 m
2
g
−1
), balanced content of self-doped N (11.23
atom %), O (8.54 atom %)-rich functional groups, and high
degree of graphitization. Given their unique physiochemical
properties, the as-obtained porous carbon materials are shown
to manifest good metal-free ORR and supercapacitor perform-
ance. The ALPC-800 electrocatalyst exhibits outstanding ORR
activity under basic conditions with high E
onset
(0.98 V vs.
RHE) and small E
1/2
(0.66 V vs. RHE) together with good
stability (70%) and methanol tolerance, which are comparable
to the Pt/C electrocatalyst. Theoretical calculation shows that
O
2
adsorption on N-doped graphene is exothermic in nature
which resulted in the high ORR activity of the synthesized
ALPC-800 electrocatalyst. In terms of supercapacitor applica-
tion, the ALPC-800 electroactive material presents the highest
specific capacitance of 421 F g
−1
at 1 A g
−1
and an excellent
rate capability in 0.5 M H
2
SO
4
. Meanwhile, the fabricated SSC
device based on the ALPC-800 sample possesses energy
density and power density as high as 21.15 Wh kg
−1
and 4.93
kW kg
−1
, respectively, and superior cyclic stability (91%
retention at 10 A g
−1
after 6000 cycles) in 0.5 M Na
2
SO
4
. This
work contributes to the all-inclusive exploration of utilizing
renewable and sustainable carbon-based biowastes rich in
inherent N and O heteroatoms to fabricate microporous
carbon architectures as efficient candidates for energy-related
applications. Thus, the waste agarwood-derived microporous
carbon material synthesis strategy has potential not only in
solid waste management but also in cost-effective energy
storage and fuel cell device applications through clean energy
conversion for sustainable development.
