Abstract
This research investigates the use of machine learning (ML) to improve the performance of conducting polymer-based 
electrodes in supercapacitors, which leverage both electric double-layer capacitance (EDLC) and pseudocapacitive charac-
teristics. Six ML models—Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Decision 
Tree (DT), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and Artificial Neural Network (ANN)—are evaluated for their ability 
to predict the specific capacitance of electrodes using an experimental dataset comprising Polyaniline (PANI), Polypyrrole 
(Ppy), and Polythiophene (PTh). Performance metrics included Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), 
and R-squared  (R2). Among the models, MLP demonstrates superior predictive accuracy, achieving the lowest MAE of 0.1452 
and MSE of 0.0373, along with the highest  R2 of 0.9622. In contrast, Decision Tree and SVM exhibited higher error values, 
with MAEs of 0.2107 and 0.2267 and  R2 values around 0.885. Although Random Forest and XGBoost achieved competitive 
 R2 values of 0.9399 and 0.9354, their errors are comparatively higher than MLP. These results highlight the effectiveness of 
advanced ML techniques in enhancing supercapacitor technology and indicate the potential of these models to predict and 
optimize conducting polymer-based electrode materials for improved performance.

1 Introduction
Supercapacitors, often referred to as electrochemical capaci-
tors or ultracapacitors, are devices used for storing energy. 
They can store a large amount of power, have a long lifes -
pan, and can charge and discharge quickly. These charac-
teristics render them appropriate for a wide range of uses 
such as portable electronic devices, electric cars, and renew-
able energy systems. The performance of supercapacitors 
is largely determined by the properties of the electrode 
materials used. Conducting polymers and their composites 
have emerged as promising electrode materials due to their 
high electrical conductivity, pseudocapacitive behavior, and 
ease of synthesis [1 ]. Pseudocapacitors, particularly those 
utilizing conducting polymers, have garnered significant 
attention recently because of their remarkable capacity 
for storing energy with extended periods of maintenance-
free durability, high cycle efficiency, and substantial power 
density. Pseudocapacitors are poised to narrow the current 
divide in energy storage between traditional capacitors and 
electrochemical batteries, offering a balance of energy and 
power density that is advantageous for various applications. 
Conducting polymer-based pseudocapacitors are promising 
due to their high specific capacitance, flexible structure, and 
high-speed charging and discharging capabilities (Fig.  1). 
These attributes make them suitable for use in industrial 
sectors and consumer electronics, including electric vehi-
cles, portable electronics, and backup power supplies [2 ].
However, a critical challenge remains in enhancing their 
energy density which continues to be a significant limiting 
factor preventing more widespread adoption. Addressing this 
issue is crucial for the advancement and integration of pseu-
docapacitors in broader energy storage applications. Pseu-
docapacitors utilize rapid and reversible redox processes

contribute to lower capacitance due to the inaccessibility

that there exists a network of interconnecting mesopores and 
macropores, combined with an optimized polymer structure, 
can lead to both satisfactory capacitance and power density 
by balancing ion transport and electroactive surface area. To 
tailor conducting polymers for enhanced pseudocapacitance 
performance, it is essential to establish quantitative corre-
lations between their structural features and the resulting 
power and energy densities under operational conditions. 
Such correlations will aid in the design and synthesis of 
next generation conducting polymer materials for high-per-
formance pseudocapacitors [3 ]. Specific capacitance is an 
essential criterion for evaluating performance of supercapac-
itor electrode materials. It quantifies the quantity of electric 
charge accumulated per unit mass or volume of the material.
Accurate prediction of specific capacitance can significantly 
assist in the design and optimization of high-performance 
supercapacitors [4]. In recent years, machine learning (ML) 
techniques have become increasingly popular in materials sci-

performance [5–8]. ML methods can describe complex physi-

ods have emerged as an alternative that is robust to traditional

2  Methods and models
2.1  Methods
2.1.1  Data collection and preprocessing
The dataset utilized for this investigation comprises exper -
imental data on the specific capacitance of various con-
ducting polymers and their composites. Features include 
electrode materials type, method of synthesis, electrolytes 
used, specific energy and specific capacitance. The data is 
preprocessed to handle missing values, standardize fea-
ture scales, and divided into training (80%) and testing 
sets (20%). Both the Decision Tree and Random Forest 
models are utilized on a dataset that included a range of 
features pertaining to supercapacitor materials and condi-
tions. These features covered various material properties, 
such as the type of electrode material (like Polythiophene, 
Polyaniline, Polypyrrole etc.). They also included elec-
trolyte properties like type and concentration, as well as 
process or method of synthesis such as in-situ polymeriza-
tion. Using the Decision Tree model, a clear visualization 
of feature importance was obtained. This allowed for the 
identification of crucial determinants that exert a substan-
tial impact on specific capacitance. The interpretability 
of the data was crucial in gaining a deeper understanding 
of the complex connections between various features and 
how they influenced performance.
On the other hand, the Random Forest model, which 
uses a collection of decision trees, showed better accuracy 
and the ability to generalize. Through meticulous manage -
ment of intricate feature interactions, it offered a strong 
evaluation of feature significance. By analyzing the aggre-
gated feature importance scores from the Random Forest 
model, valuable insights into the key variables with the 
most significant influence on specific capacitance can be 
gained. This helps us to develop a thorough comprehen-
sion of the fundamental processes.
Overall, the combination of Decision Tree and Random 
Forest models allowed for precise prediction of specific

For this study, target encoding is employed to effectively 
capture the correlation between the categorical features and 
the specific capacitance. This is particularly suitable as the 
specific capacitance is influenced by various factors includ-
ing the type of electrode material, the fabrication method, 
specific energy, and the electrolyte used. Target encoding 
is a sophisticated preprocessing technique that transforms 
categorical variables into numerical values by substitut-
ing each category with the mean of the target variable for 
that specific category. In this study, the target variable is 
'Specific capacitance (F/g)', while the categorical features 
include 'Electrode materials', 'Method', and 'Electrolyte'. The 
target encoding process involves calculating the mean spe-
cific capacitance for each category within these features and 
then replacing the categorical values with these mean values. 
The use of traditional encoding methods, such as one-hot 
encoding, often leads to the creation of a feature space with

2.2  Models
2.2.1  Artificial neural network (ANN) ML model
ANNs are computational models inspired by the human 
brain, capable of capturing complex patterns in data. The 
model utilized in this study is composed of an input layer, 
several hidden layers that employ nonlinear activation func-
tions, and an output layer. The training of the model is per -
formed using the back propagation algorithm to minimize 
the mean squared error between the predicted and actual 
specific capacitance values.
2.2.1.1 Predictive model for  specific capacitance 
through TensorFlow ML model To create a reliable predic-
tive model for specific capacitance, TensorFlow ML model, 
which is an open-source machine learning framework 
(Fig.  5), in conjunction with the Keras API is employed. 
First, the dataset is divided into training and testing sets 
using the train_test_split function from the sklearn.model_
selection module. This ensures that the model is evaluated 
on data that it has not been trained on. Next, a Sequential 
model is initialized, representing a linear stack of neural net-
work layers. The model architecture consisted of an input 
layer followed by two dense hidden layers, each utilizes 
rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation functions to intro-
duce non-linearity and improve the capacity of the model to 
capture intricate patterns in the data. The output layer com-
prises of a single neuron to predict the specific capacitance 
value. The model is compiled using a popular optimizer 
(Adam) and a commonly used loss function mean squared 
error (MSE), which are commonly used for regression tasks. 
Subsequently, the model underwent 100 epochs of training 
using a batch size of 32. Throughout the process, its per -
formance is closely monitored on a validation subset that 
accounted for 20% of the training data. By understanding 
the fundamental patterns in the data, the model can fine-
tune its parameters and minimize the loss function. Follow-
ing training, the performance of the model is evaluated on 
the test set yielding MSE as a metric to assess its predic-
tive accuracy. After training, the model is used in making 
predictions on the test data. This gave us valuable insights 
into the ability of the model to make accurate predictions on 
unseen samples and effectively forecast specific capacitance 
values.
2.2.2  Decision Tree (DT) ML model
Decision Trees (Fig.  6) are incredibly effective models that 
can split data based on feature values to make accurate pre-
dictions. They are a straightforward yet highly impactful 
tool. Every node in the tree represents a feature, and each 
branch represents a decision rule that leads to leaf nodes 
that make predictions about specific capacitance. The tree 
is pruned to avoid overfitting.
2.2.3  Random Forest (RF) ML model
Random Forests are ensemble models that combine multiple 
Decision Trees to enhance prediction accuracy and robust-
ness. Every tree in the forest is trained using a random subset 
of the data, and the ultimate prediction is determined by 
averaging the predictions of all the trees.

3  Improving the predictive performance 
of ML models through hyperparameter 
tuning
The process of hyperparameter tuning emphasizes the 
significance of optimizing machine learning models to 
improve their predictive performance. The SVM regres-
sor is configured with a range of hyperparameters, includ-
ing the kernel type (linear, rbf, and poly), regularization 
parameter C (0.1, 1, 10), kernel coefficient gamma ( scale 
and auto), and the epsilon parameter (epsilon) for the 
epsilon-tube within which no penalty is associated in the 
training loss function (0.1, 0.2, 0.5). The kernel type is 
responsible for selecting the appropriate function to map 
the input data into the feature space. Linear kernels are 
used to represent linear relationships, while RBF (radial 
basis function) and polynomial kernels can capture more 
complex and non-linear interactions. The regularization 
parameter C is responsible for balancing the objective of 
minimizing the error on the training data with the goal 
of reducing model complexity to avoid overfitting. The 
gamma parameter determines the extent to which a single 
training sample affects the model, with low values indi-
cating a wider influence and high values indicating a nar -
rower influence. The epsilon parameter defines the thresh-
old within which forecasted values are deemed acceptable 
without incurring any penalty. The scoring function is 
defined using MSE. Lower MSE value of 0.050 indicates 
a better model performance. To optimize model perfor -
mance, Grid Search Cross-Validation (GridSearchCV) is 
applied to the Random Forest and Decision Tree Regres-
sors. For the Random Forest Regressor, the hyperparam-
eter grid examined configurations including 100, 200, and 
300 trees (`n_estimators`), three options for maximum 
features (`max_features`): 'auto', 'sqrt', and 'log2', as well 
as varying the maximum depth (`max_depth`) with val-
ues of 10, 20, 30, and None. Additionally, the grid tested 
`min_samples_split` values of 2, 5, and 10, and `min_sam-
ples_leaf` values of 1, 2, and 4, along with bootstrap sam -
pling options of True and False. For the Decision Tree 
Regressor , the tuning focused on `max_depth` values of 
3, 5, 10, and None, alongside `min_samples_split` and 
`min_samples_leaf` with values of 2, 5, and 10, and 1, 
2, and 4, respectively. Both models underwent a system-
atic evaluation using fivefold cross-validation (`cv = 5`), 
targeting negative mean squared error (`scoring = 'neg_
mean_squared_error'`) as the performance metric. This 
comprehensive grid search approach enabled the identifi-
cation of optimal hyperparameter configurations, thereby 
improving the accuracy and reliability of the models. The 
process of hyperparameter tuning for the Multi-layer Per-
ceptron Regressor (MLPRegressor) is conducted using 
GridSearch cross validation (CV), a more robust method 
for enhancing model performance. The MLP Regressor is 
configured to allow a maximum of 1000 iterations with 
a comprehensive parameter grid encompassing various 
hyperparameters. These include different configurations 
for hidden layer sizes, activation functions such as ReLU, 
tanh, and logistic, optimization algorithms (Adam and 
SGD), regularization strength (alpha), and learning rate 
options. The tuning process employed fivefold cross-vali -
dation across 720 different combinations of these parame-
ters. A warning surfaced indicating that the target variable 
y is input as a 2D column vector rather than a 1D array 
suggesting that reshaping the target variable could resolve 
this issue. The grid search reveales optimal hyperparam-
eters notably a configuration with two hidden layers of 100 
neurons each, the ReLU activation function, and the Adam 
solver. This results in MSE of approximately 0.1507, an 
 R2 score of around 0.962, and a MAE of roughly 0.1452. 
The tuned XGBoost regression model achieves a signifi-
cant performance enhancement through hyperparameter 
optimization. The grid search reveals optimal parameters: 
learning rate = 0.1, max depth = 3, n_estimators = 200, and 
subsample = 0.8. These settings results in an  R2 score of 
approximately 0.910, indicating that the model explains 
over 91% of the variance in the target variable. The hyper -
parameter tuning for the neural network model is con-
ducted using Keras Tuner's RandomSearch method, with 
the search focused on optimizing MAE on the validation 
set. The model architecture included two hidden lay -
ers, with the first having between 32 and 128 units and 
the second between 16 and 64 units. The tuning process 
involved a maximum of 5 trials and 2 executions per trial 
over 50 epochs. The best hyperparameters identified were: 
units_1 = 128, units_2 = 64, and optimizer = 'adam'. 
The model achieved a training MAE that decreased from 
0.7474  to 0.1750 and a validation MAE that improved 
from 0.7077 to 0.1930 over 48 epochs.

In this study, a rigorous approach is adopted to validate 
the Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Support Vector Machine 
(SVM), XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), and Decision Tree 
(DT) models, ensuring that the results are both reliable 
and generalizable (Fig.  9). 9 K-fold cross-validation is 
employed, dividing the dataset of 62 samples into 5 subsets. 
Each model was trained on 80% of the data (approximately 
50 samples) while the remaining 20% (about 12 samples) 
served as the test set during each iteration. This method 
allows each fold to function as the test set once providing 
a robust estimate of model performance and reducing the 
variance associated with a single train-test split.
5  Conclusion
This study showcases the efficacy of different machine 
learning models in predicting the exact capacitance of con-
ducting polymers and their composites as electrode mate -
rials for supercapacitors. Data from various conducting 
polymer-based supercapacitors is gathered and utilized as 
input for different machine learning models. Although the 
random forest model and XG Boost show higher  R2 and 
lower RMSE values, their inaccuracies make the MLP the 
best model for predicting the specific capacitance of the 
electrodes. The performance of the Models is assessed uti-
lizing measures such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean 
Squared Error (MSE), and R-squared  (R2). The ranking of 
the models is as follows: MLP > RF > XGB > DT > SVM > 
ANN. Out of all the models, the MLP model stands out 
with its exceptional performance, accurately pinpointing 
the contribution of each variable to the specific capacitance. 
The electrode material has a major impact on the specific 
capacitance of conducting polymer-based supercapacitors. 
MLPs demonstrate a strong ability to capture the non-linear 
relationships in complex datasets, achieving a 95% accu -
racy on a dataset of 62 entries from research papers. This 
outperformed SVM and XGBoost, which achieved 85% and 
89% accuracy, respectively. The features—Electrode Mate-
rials, Method, Electrolyte, Specific Energy, and Specific 
Capacitance—show complex interdependencies, making 
MLPs especially effective as they learn feature interactions 
automatically without manual engineering. Unlike SVM, 
which requires specific kernels and can be sensitive to noise, 
or XGBoost, which needs careful tuning, MLPs produce 
smooth, continuous outputs ideal for predicting continuous 
values like specific energy and capacitance. Their layered 
structure also makes MLPs robust to noise, contributing to 
more generalizable pattern learning. It is anticipated that this 
approach will serve as a catalyst for additional endeavors 
in utilizing machine learning in the fields of electrochem-
istry and chemical engineering, specifically in the realm of 
enhancing supercapacitor electrode materials. These find-
ings indicate that utilizing advanced ML techniques can 
greatly assist in the research and improvement of materi-
als for energy storage purposes. Future research should
