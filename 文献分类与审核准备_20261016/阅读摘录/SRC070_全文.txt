ABSTRACT 
At present, the global energy supply is generally sufficient but the structure is tight. Supercapacitors have the 
characteristics of high power density, extremely fast charging and discharging, long cycle life, and wide operating 
temperature range. This article reviews t he research progress of the application of biomass-based materials in supercapa 
citor electrode materials, and elabo rately discusses the types of bio mass materials, their p reparation methods, th e 
structure and working principle of supercapacito rs, as well as th e performance of biomass-based materials as electrode 
materials. Biological-based materials can significantly enhance the performance of supercapacitors through various 
preparation methods. In  particu lar, biomass carb on materials, due to their high specific surface area and excellent 
conductivity, have become a research hotsp ot for electrode materials in supercapacitors.  This article provides a 
theoretical basis for future research to further optimize the preparation process of biomass-based materials and 
enhance their electrochemical performance, thereby promoting their development in practical applications. 
Keywords: Biomass; Electrode Material; Supercapacitor; Capacitive Performance; Research Progress. 
1. INTRODUCTION
With the steady rise in global energy consumption and the escalating severity of environmental challenges, advancing 
high-performance and sustainable energy storage technologies has emerged as a central focus of contemporary scientific 
research. [1] As an emerging energy storage technology, supercapacitors have attracted considerable interest owing to their 
outstanding power density, fast charge–discharge performance, and excellent cycling stability. They have promising 
applications in electric vehicles, portable electronic devices, and renewable energy systems. However, the energy density 
of supercapacitors is relatively low, which limits their application in certain scenarios with high energy demands. Therefore, 
improving the energy density and overall performance of supercapacitors through material selection and structural design 
has become the current research focus. 
In recent  years, biomass-based  electrode materials have gradually become a new research  direction in the  field of 
supercapacitor electrode materials due to  their wide sources, s trong renewability, environmental friendliness, as well as 
their unique structural and functional characteristics. Biological materials mainly come from animals and plants, 
microorganisms and their derivatives, as well as biological waste. Their main components are organic polymer substances 
composed of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. The biomass materials processed and prepared through physical, chemical or 
biological methods can not only effectively utilize renewable resources, but also significantly enhance the performance of 
supercapacitors through  various carbonization methods and composite material preparation methods. Sun et al. [2] 
synthesized a heteroatom-doped carbon material (CHPC‑X) with a three‑dimensionally interconnected network by 
employing biomass‑derived precursors such as chitosan and sodium lignosulfonate, where boric acid served as both a 
structural template and an auxiliary agent, and KOH was used as  the activating agent. Among them, the best-performing 
part has a high specific surface area of 2700.65 m2 g-1. In a three-electrode system using 6 M KOH as the electrolyte, the 
specific capacitance of CHPC-X at 1 A g-1 is 332 F g-1, and the energy density of the CHPC-X//CHPC-X symmetric 
electrode can reach 17.7 W h kg-1 (166.4 W k g-1). In addition, Rui Ding[3] reported a method for preparing three-
dimensional network porous carbon materials by using intermittent activation and freeze-drying pretreatment. Due to its 
high porosity, after 100 cycles at a charging and discharging c urrent density of 0.2 A g⁻¹, the specific capacity exceeded 
900 mA h g⁻¹, and after 500 cycles, the specific capacity exceeded 500 mA h g⁻¹, demonstrating excellent electrochemical 
performance.

materials. Firstly, it introduces the basic concepts, types and characteristics of biomass-based materials; Secondly, it

2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
2.1 Biomass based materials 
Biomass materials refer to materials that are processed from renewable organic substances through physical, chemical or 
biological methods and have specific properties and functions. Biomass materials are mainly obtained from plants, animals, 
microorganisms and their derivatives, as well as biological was te. They are a type of organic polymer composed mainly 
of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. The most significant feature is  that they are based on biomass resources, emphasizing 
the regenerability and wide sources of the resources. 
Based on the composition of their materials, they can be classified into natural polymer materials, such as cellulose, lignin, 
starch, chitin/chitosan, alginate, proteins, etc. According to their sources, there are plant-based biomass materials (wood, 
non-wood, extractives),  animal-based biomass materials (animal fur, etc.), and microbial-based biomass materials 
(bacterial cellulose, etc.). Among the non-wood part, it also i ncludes common materials like fruit shells and plant stalks. 
After obtaining the biomass materials, they can be combined thr ough physical blending, chemical grafting or nano-
composite methods to prepare biomass composite materials or be processed into biomass charcoal materials through 
carbonization.

2.3 Introduction to Supercapacitors  
Supercapacitors represent an energy storage technology that bridges the gap between traditional dielectric capacitors and 
rechargeable batteries. Unlike batteries, which rely predominan tly on bulk redox reactions and ion diffusion within 
electrode materials, supercapacitors store energy through elect rostatic charge separation at the electrode–electrolyte 
interface and, in some cases, through rapid and reversible surf ace or near-surface Faradaic reactions. This unique energy 
storage mechanism endows supercapacitors with several outstandi ng advantages, including exceptionally high power 
density and remarkable long-term cycling stability with minimal  capacity degradation over tens or even hundreds of 
thousands of cycles. Despite these merits, the amount of energy  that can be stored per unit mass or volume in 
supercapacitors is still generally lower than that of batteries , which limits their application in scenarios requiring high 
energy density.

From a structural perspective, supercapacitors possess a relati vely straightforward and well-defined configuration. A 
typical device consists of two electrodes with opposite polarit y, commonly referred to as the positive and negative 
electrodes, which are responsible for charge storage and release. These electrodes are immersed in an electrolyte that serves 
as the ionic conduction medium, enabling ion transport during t he charge–discharge process. To prevent electrical short 
circuits while allowing free ion migration, a porous separator is placed between the two electrodes. In addition, current 
collectors are employed to facilitate efficient electron transp ort between the external cir cuit and the active electrode 
materials, thereby minimizing internal resistance and improving overall device performance. The fundamental architecture

2.4 The principle of supercapacitor energy storage 
The energy storage mechanism of supercapacitors mainly relies on the formation of an electric double layer (EDL) at the 
electrode–electrolyte interface , as illustrated schematically i n Figure 2. When an external potential is applied to an 
electrode immersed in an electrolyte, excess charge builds up on its surface, generating a strong  interfacial electric field. 
In response, counter-ions of opposite charge in the electrolyte  a r e  e l e c t r o s t a t i c a l l y  d r a w n  to the electrode–electrolyte 
interface and arrange themselves into a highly ordered layer adjacent to the surface.This redistribution of ions leads to the 
formation of two closely spaced layers of opposite charges, commonly referred to as the electric double layer. 
During this process, ions migrate from the bulk electrolyte tow ard the electrode surface driv en by electrostatic forces, 
while electrons are transported through the external circuit to maintain charge neutrality. The separation of charges across 
an extremely short distance enables efficient energy storage, analogous to the behavior described by the classical Helmholtz 
model, in which the electrode and the adjacent ionic layer function similarly to the two plates of a conventional capacitor. 
Importantly, the formation and dissolution of the electric doub le layer involve only physical processes such as ion 
adsorption and desorption, without any accompanying Faradaic ch arge transfer or chemical redox reactions. As a result, 
this mechanism contributes to the rapid charge–discharge capability and excellent cycling stability characteristic of electric 
double-layer capacitors.

By comparing the key indicators of different biomass-based electrode materials as shown in Table 2 in a horizontal manner, 
it can be observed that there are significant differences in specific capacitance, energy density, power density, and cycling 
performance among the materials - for instance, the energy density of Canadian marigold (high-temperature carbonization 
+ KOH activation) reaches 40 Wh/kg (the current highest value),  while that of bamboo (K₂FeO₄ activation) is only 6.68 
Wh/kg; the power density of sorghum straw (microwave deposition  + Ni(OH)₂) is 588.1 W/kg, while that of marigold is 
only 9 W/kg (10 A/g); sorghum straw maintains 95.9% stability after 30,000 cycles, while citrus peel only drops to 87.58% 
after 3,000 cycles. The core factors influencing performance ca n be summarized as follows: the carbonization method 
(such as high-temperature or microwave) determines the pore str ucture and conductivity; the type of activator (KOH for 
strong alkaline etching of micropores, ZnCl₂ for mild pore formation) regulates the specific surface area and ion transport 
efficiency; the composite strategy (such as the introduction of  pseudocapacitance by MnO₂ or Ni(OH)₂) compensates for 
the insufficiency of double-layer capacitance to enhance energy  density; the intrinsic struct ure of the raw material (such 
as the natural porosity of marigold) affects the initial number  of active sites; and the preparation process (such as 
pretreatment and doping amount) determines the structural cycling stability. These factors collectively govern the material's 
performance, and in the future, it is necessary to optimize the  variables to balance energy, power, and lifespan, thereby 
promoting practical applications. 
In terms of cycling stability, Qiang Yang[9] reported that the active doped porous biomass carbon electrode material (YB-
K5-N) prepared from discarded pomelo peels, as shown in the figure 4, maintained a specific capacitance retention rate of 
approximately 87.58% after 3,000 cycles, demonstrating relative ly good cycling stability. Moreover, the shapes of the 
charge-discharge curves, the positions of the voltage plateaus, and the overall trends in the first 10 cycles and the last 10 
cycles were very similar. The correspondence between the charge-discharge times and voltage plateaus in the first and last 
cycles was basically consistent, indicating that the reversibil ity of the electrode reaction was well maintained throughout 
the long-term cycling process, without significant polarization  intensification or irreversible phase transformation due to 
an increase in the number of cycles.

Mengying Chen[15] reported that agarose and gelatin, two common biomass substanc es, were used as carbon sources and 
were synthesized using simple methods to prepare porous carbon-based materials. Electrochemical performance tests were 
conducted in a wide temperature range of -80 to 100 ℃. Through the tests, it was found that the porous carbon GHPC-KO, 
which has a high specific surface area, high conductivity, and multi-scale pore structures of large pores, mesopores, and 
micropores, exhibited relatively excellent capacitance performance in the wide temperature range. The specific capacitance 
of GHPC-KO at -70 ℃ can reach 123.3 F g-1 (0.2 A g-1), and afte r 10,000 cycles of constant current charging and 
discharging at 100 ℃, it still retains 82.88% of its capacitance. 
The core of optimizing the pretreatment technology for biomass materials lies in regulating their composition, 
microstructure and surface chemistry through physical, chemical, biological or their combined methods, in order to achieve 
better pore structure, conductivity and ionic/electronic transm ission channels, thereby enhancing the electrochemical 
performance.[16]  
Specifically, physical methods (such as ball milling, ultrasoni c treatment, and steam explosion) can be used to reduce 
particle size, disrupt the dense structure, and increase porosity and thermal decomposition uniformity; chemical methods 
(such as acid, alkali, and oxidation treatments) can be employe d to remove ash, hemicellulose, and lignin, and introduce 
oxygen-containing/nitrogen-containing functional groups to wide n the carbon layer spacing a nd regulate the pore size 
distribution; biological methods (such as enzymatic hydrolysis and microbial fermentation) can selectively decompose 
specific components under mild conditions, improving the pore s tructure and surface chemistry. Further, by adopting 
synergistic pretreatment strategies such as "acid washing + hydrothermal treatment + activation" or "ball milling coupled 
with microwave", the synergistic enhancement of component separ ation, framework reconstruction, and pore channel 
optimization can be achieved. This enables the pre-treatment stage to "pre-set" an ideal microstructure for the carbonization 
and activation processes, ultimately maximizing the specific capacitance, rate performance, and cycle stability of biomass 
carbon.

3. SUMMARY AND PROSPECT 
This article reviews the research progress of the application o f biomass-based materials in supercapacitor electrode 
materials. By summarizing the research progress of recent liter ature, it focuses on analyzing the types of biomass-based 
materials, their preparation methods, the structure and working principle of supercapacitors, as well as the performance of 
biomass-based materials as elect rode materials. Although biomass-based materials have demonstrated many advantages 
as electrode materials for super capacitors, there are still som e challenges. Future research should further optimize the 
preparation process of biomass-based materials, explore new car bonization methods and preparation methods for 
composite materials, in order to enhance their electrochemical performance. At the same time, it is also possible to further 
improve their specific capacita nce and energy density through m ethods such as doping and composites. In addition, 
promoting its development in practical applications is also an effective means for its further advancement.
