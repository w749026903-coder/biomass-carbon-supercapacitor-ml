ABSTRACT
Deep eutectic solvents (DESs) represent a new generation of environmentally friendly solvents that show great 
promise for sustainable lignocellulosic biomass separation. The article presents an original assessment of DES 
formulations through advanced computational modeling and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, which 
reveal essential mechanistic details that previous reviews have not fully explored. This work reviews MD sim -
ulations, DFT, and ML methods to study detailed hydrogen-bonding systems and proton-catalytic and ionic in -
teractions between DESs and biomass components, which leads to their high selectivity for lignin and 
hemicellulose extraction. The review establishes a fundamental understanding of DESs by studying their 
chemical makeup and physical characteristics, and experimental results when processing different biomass 
materials. The review combines molecular-level findings with process optimization methods, economic assess -
ments, and sustainability impact evaluations. The review establishes a complete understanding by connecting 
atomic-scale interactions to industrial-scale applications through computational modeling, which enables the 
creation of optimized DES-based biorefinery systems.
1. Introduction
Growing global demand for energy and materials, coupled with 
climate concerns and fossil fuel depletion, drives the search for renew -
able alternatives. Lignocellulosic biomass and microalgae offer prom -
ising, sustainable raw materials used in producing energy sources, 
chemical products, and industrial compounds, lessening the usage of oil, 
along with limiting environmental outcomes.
The environmental viability and process effectiveness of the different 
methods of breaking down biomass face multiple essential barriers in 
their conventional approaches. The NaOH/urea pretreatment from 
agricultural residues of rice straw enhances plant-based polysaccharides 
like cellulose and hemicellulose, achieving high yields but producing 
excessive salts, which need costly removal procedures ( Dong et al., 
2018 ). Application of organic liquids for hemicellulose and lignin 
removal from poplar through organic acid/solvent blends proves effec -
tive yet remains impractical because it requires substantial solvent 
volumes and produces harmful compounds ( Yang et al., 2020 ). The 
conventional methods experience three major drawbacks, which include 
equipment corrosion, high operational costs, and complex chemical 
recovery procedures, thus limiting their suitability for large-scale 
sustainable biomass processing ( Rastogi and Shrivastava, 2017 ). 
Organic solvents used in conventional processes face multiple draw -
backs, including their volatile nature and corrosive effects on equipment 
and toxic properties, which prevent their use in biomass processing 
( Hong et al., 2020 ). Specific advantages associated with ionic liquids 
(ILs) make them attractive for lignocellulosic biorefinery applications 
because they are non-flammable, have low vapor pressure, and lack 
volatility ( Wang et al., 2017 ). The extensive deployment of ILs is limited 
by their expensive production costs and challenging preparation 
methods.
Deep eutectic solvents (DES) present an environmentally friendly 
solution to traditional biomass pretreatment procedures through their 
ability to overcome major challenges, including high energy re -
quirements and corrosive or expensive chemical usage. The pretreat -
ment process with DES produces better glucose yields and maintains 
stable cellulase enzymes while decreasing both energy requirements and 
environmental effects ( Liu et al., 2019; Tan et al., 2018; Sai and Lee, 
2019 ). The DES method operates without requiring severe processing 
conditions or expensive solvents that traditional methods need. The 
fractionation of complex biomass microalgae through biomass process -
ing using DES composed of lactic acid (LA) and choline chloride (ChCl)

DESs have the highest potential among other solvents to be consid -

tics. The solvents possess several key characteristics, which include 
structural integrity, high dissolution efficiency, excellent biological 
tolerance, affordable manufacturing expenses, and reduced evaporative

distinctive features, which include low toxicity and tunability, thus 
showing promise for pollution prevention and sustainability initiatives 
( Ee et al., 2023 ).
While the potential of DESs is widely acknowledged, this review aims 
to provide an original assessment by leveraging advanced computa -
tional modeling and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to reveal 
essential mechanistic details that previous reviews have not fully 
explored. Our objective is to establish a fundamental, molecular-level 
understanding of DES-biomass interactions and connect these atomic- 
scale phenomena to industrial-scale applications and economic viability.
The review follows this structure to achieve its purpose. It starts by 
explaining how lignocellulosic biomass exists and why it needs efficient 
fractionation methods. Thereafter, it explains what DESs consist of and 
their industrial value and chemical properties. Our main contribution 
appears in the following sections, which explain biomass fractionation 
through DESs and provide detailed molecular dynamics and thermo -
dynamic studies of DES systems. The study also examines simulation and 
modeling approaches, including machine learning and kinetic models, 
which serve as essential tools for DES performance prediction and 
optimization beyond experimental data. Furthermore, it moves into 
practical aspects, which include industrial process optimization, DES- 
based process sustainability evaluation, economic feasibility assess -
ment, and scalability issues. Finally, there is a summary of acquired 
knowledge and proposed research paths for the field. This review unites 
fundamental molecular studies with process optimization and economic 
evaluations to create a complete system for building efficient, environ -
mentally friendly, and cost-effective DES-based biorefineries.
2. Lignocellulosic biomass: challenges and fractionation 
requirements
Biorefineries use lignocellulosic biomass (LCB) as their primary 
feedstock because this renewable resource exists abundantly in quanti -
ties reaching 200 gigatons per year. The development of sustainable bio- 
based industries depends heavily on lignocellulosic biomass which 
comes from agricultural and forest residues and energy crops including 
both herbal and woody types and specialty crops ( Kumar et al., 2020; 
Rai et al., 2022 ). The production of LCB through halophytes which are 
salt-resistant plants growing on salt-damaged lands provides a 
cost-effective sustainable solution that frees up arable land and fresh -
water resources ( Fugol et al., 2023; Jankowski et al., 2020; Li et al., 
2023a; Rajput and Sheikh, 2019 ).
Lignocellulosic biomass (LCB) contains three primary biopolymers 
which include cellulose as a linear crystalline glucose polymer and 
hemicellulose as a branched amorphous heteropolysaccharide and 
lignin as a complex aromatic polymer that together form more than 70% 
of the biomass. Recent studies have identified proteins along with in -
organics and resins and other compounds as minor constituents in 
addition to the main three biopolymers ( Hallac and Ragauskas, 2011; 
French, 2017 ). The chemical diversity of these components is demon -
strated through their molecular structures which are shown in Fig. 2 .
Cellulose contains a highly structured and resistant framework that 
consists primarily of glucose units that bond through glycosidic linkages. 
The biomass section contains high crystallinity because of its intra- and 
intermolecular hydrogen bond connections. Different biomasses show 
varying levels of polymerization. The degree of polymerization in agri -
cultural residues reaches 900 units, but woody biomass contains be -
tween 4000 and 5500 units ( Hallac and Ragauskas, 2011 ).
Lignin, the second most abundant natural polymer, is a hydrophilic 
phenolic substance present in the plant cell wall that provides greater 
resistance to microbial attacks ( Sakakibara, 1980 ). Chemically, it is a 
highly complex polymer with plant-specific compositions and linkage 
motifs, lacking a defined primary structure; instead, it consists of 
random phenylpropanoid (C9) polyphenols primarily linked by aryl -
glycerol ether bonds between the phenolic units of para-coumaryl 
alcohol (H-type), coniferyl alcohol (G-type), and sinapyl alcohol 
(S-type). The majority of a plant's lignin (75 – 85%) is located within the 
considerably larger volume of the secondary cell wall ( Holtman et al., 
2003 ).
LCB maintains its structural integrity because of the complex 
bonding between its components, which creates a major processing 
obstacle. The biopolymers maintain strong covalent bonds between 
lignin-carbohydrate complexes (LCCs) and hydrogen bonds that create a 
three-dimensional cross-linked matrix structure ( Sun et al., 2023 ). Fig. 3
shows detailed lignin linkage types which demonstrate the complex 
nature of the structure. The strong bonding network protects poly -
saccharides from enzymatic and microbial breakdown while making it 
difficult to separate individual components, and affects both biomass 
physical characteristics and processing complexity. The strategic

The European Union has established lignocellulosic biomass as a 
strategic primary source, which includes (1) forest biomass, (2) agri -

The chemical composition of LCB shows significant variations based 
on source material and depends on wood species, plant varieties, and 
residue types. The lignin content in hardwoods, including Poplar, 
Eucalyptus, and Beechwood, ranges between 18% and 25% but soft -
woods such as Pine and Spruce, along with cork, contain higher lignin 
levels (27 – 33%) with distinct cellulose crystallinity and cell wall density 
that impact fractionation processes ( Soares et al., 2021; Suota et al., 
2021 ). Leftover biomass from crops, including corn stalks, wheat straw, 
rice straw, and Bagasse, contains lower lignin amounts (12 – 24%) along 
with less rigid structures that simplify the separation process ( Arora 
et al., 2020 ). Large amounts of industrial wastes and energy crops like 
Spent Coffee Grounds, Oil Palm Empty Fruit Bunches (OPEFB), Mis -
canthus, and Switchgrass are available yet contain high ash levels and 
particular lignin types which need specific attention ( Jablonsky et al., 
2019; Rahighi et al., 2021; Yen et al., 2022 ), thus demonstrating the 
significance of compositional differences for process optimization.
The complex network of LCB creates significant resistance to con -
verting its valuable products directly. The process of fractionation 
followed by pretreatment represents a vital step, which consumes 
approximately 40% of the total process expense ( Haldar et al., 2018 ). 
The pretreatment method destroys the lignin barrier and breaks LCCs 
and reduces cellulose crystallinity, which results in increased biomass 
porosity and accessibility for subsequent processing ( Sun et al., 2023; 
Tuck et al., 2012 ). The structural modification required for efficient 
biomass utilization becomes clear through this concept.
The biorefinery utilizes fractionation to separate cellulose, hemi -
cellulose, and lignin components from LCB, which then become valuable 
products. Cellulose can be converted into glucose for fermentation, as 
well as nanocellulose, regenerated fibers, and materials with improved 
downstream yields because of its increased crystallinity ( Lim et al., 
2021; Smirnov et al., 2020; Mnasri et al., 2022 ). Separation of hemi -
cellulose from biomass yields oligosaccharides together with xylose and 
furfural (2-F) and functional chemicals ( Roy et al., 2023; Gundupalli 
et al., 2023 ) while the non-carbohydrate fraction of plant biomass can be 
converted into aromatics, polymers, carbon materials, and binders 
( Varilla-Mazaba et al., 2022 ). The sustainable biorefinery depends on 
fractionation because this method creates various valuable streams 
while extracting the full potential from each biomass component.

Therefore, the combination of multiple HBAs and HBDs in DESs 
significantly lowers the melting point, allowing them to remain liquid 
under ambient conditions ( Ee et al., 2023 ).
DESs are considered promising substitutes for traditional organic 
solvents, primarily because they can be prepared directly from naturally 
abundant and readily available compounds. Their physicochemical 
properties can be tailored by adjusting the type of individual constitu -
ents, their molar ratios, operating temperature, and the presence of 
water ( Liu et al., 2018 ).
As shown in Table 1 , DESs are divided into five types according to the 
nature of their complexing agents. Type I involves quaternary ammo -
nium salts (e.g., ChCl) and metal halides like AlCl ₃ , but their use in 
lignocellulosic biomass is restricted due to the high melting points of 
non-hydrated metal halides. Type II comprises quaternary ammonium 
salts paired with hydrated metal halides, such as AlCl ₃ ⋅ 6H ₂ O ( Bajpai, 
2021 ).
Due to their resistance to air and moisture, these chemicals are well- 
suited for industrial use. Type III DESs, made from quaternary ammo -
nium salts and hydrogen bond donors like carboxylic acids or amides, 
are widely applied in biomass pretreatment thanks to their affordability 
and ease of preparation ( He et al., 2023 ).
Type IV DESs are formed from transition metal salts and HBDs like 
urea, although ionization of the metal component is generally poor in 
nonaqueous systems ( Liang et al., 2023; Kalyniukova et al., 2021; Yu 
et al., 2019 ).
A newer category, Type V DESs, consists exclusively of non-ionic 
molecules and are usually hydrophobic under normal conditions. Iden -
tifying distinct HBAs and HBDs in these mixtures is challenging. The 
formation of these solvents is driven by the strong interaction due to the 
acidity contrast between phenolic and aliphatic hydroxyl groups. 
Typically, Type V solvents display hydrophobicity and relatively 
increased volatility ( Abranches et al., 2019 ).
Unlike the other four types, this class allows for the eutectic solvent 
to be regenerated and recovered via evaporation, making it a promising 
candidate for developing efficient recycling technologies ( Li et al., 
2023b ).
In DESs made from carbohydrates, sugars such as fructose, glucose, 
and xylose act as hydrogen bond donors. A ChCl-fructose mixture can 
dissolve about 0.065 g of rice straw biomass per gram, with fructose- 
based solvents being more effective than those made from xylose, 
mannose, glucose, or ribose ( Florindo et al., 2017 ). Carbohydrate-based 
DESs can dissolve biomass, but they are less effective than other types of 
DESs ( Pan et al., 2017 ) therefore, they have been mostly used in food 
and pharmaceutical processing instead of lignocellulosic biomass 
treatment ( Vannuchi et al., 2022; Mukhopadhyay et al., 2016; Marchel 
et al., 2020 ).
The formation of DESs occurs through the precise mixing of natural 
compounds, which creates a strong hydrogen-bonding network. The 
interactions between DESs allow them to break down plant fiber struc -
tures effectively while releasing valuable lignocellulose components 
( Rokade and Bhate, 2015 ). The use of DESs allows the sustainable 
extraction of pure high-quality chemicals from biomass through envi -
ronmentally friendly methods ( Francisco et al., 2012 ). The preparation 
of DESs demands minimal energy input and uses accessible chemical 
materials, which makes it ideal for sustainable large-scale plant fiber 
pretreatment. The approach enables environmentally friendly biomass 
processing through reduced energy requirements and simplified 
manufacturing steps. DESs provide unique benefits for lignocellulose 
separation through their ability to extract lignin nanoparticles and 
improve cellulose glycosylation efficiency while preserving cellulose 
crystallinity ( Liu et al., 2023; Ivanovi ´c et al., 2020 ). DESs can be used as 
a versatile tool because they enable improved biomass-derived product 
yields and quality while supporting sustainable processing techniques. 
DES functions as an environmentally friendly pretreatment system for 
plant fibers because it provides low toxicity and high efficiency while 
producing minimal pollution ( Francisco et al., 2012 ). The sustainability 
of DESs' biomass processing depends on the fact that DESs can be 
recovered and purified for reuse while maintaining their functional 
properties Table 2 .
The DES system, composed of ChCl, ethylene glycol (EG), and glyc -
erol, enhances the efficiency of lignin and cellulose extraction from 
biomass. The combined components facilitated the fractionation pro -
cess, which results in increased yields and superior quality of extracted 
components ( Samarov et al., 2022 ). The DES system, which contains 
amino acids, glucose, and other natural components, provides special -
ized extraction and separation capabilities for biomass materials. The 
natural composition of this system allows for precise separation of 
valuable components from biomass materials ( Chatterjee et al., 2020 ). 
The term Natural DESs (NADES) describes DES systems that derive their 
components from natural products. The integration of DES systems in 
real-world applications, according to expert analyses and summaries, 
shows that DESs consist of three types, which are acidic, neutral, and 
basic, based on the HBD properties used in their formulation ( Mohd 
Fuad and Mohd Nadzir, 2022 ). The DES system's hydrogen-bonding 
interactions make biomass components more accessible, which en -
ables their dissolution and separation process. The functionality and 
performance of the DES system can be substantially enhanced for spe -
cific biomass processing through the identification and utilization of 
unique HBD characteristics ( Guo et al., 2022a ).

4. Mechanisms of biomass fractionation using deep eutectic 
solvents
The increasing adoption of DESs stems from their ability to selec -
tively dissolve biomass while maintaining environmentally friendly 
characteristics. The DES system exhibits greater solubility toward lignin 
and hemicellulose instead of cellulose, thus enabling the selective 
extraction of these components. The process uses hydrolysis to break 
down hemicellulose and lignin extraction through LCC bond disruption, 
along with the cleavage of lignin-hemicellulose ester and ether bonds. 
The DESs use their selective action to extract valuable biomass com -
pounds while maintaining the structural integrity of cellulose ( Yu et al., 
2019; Bao et al., 2025 ).
DESs demonstrate their effectiveness in biomass processing because 
they possess the ability to cut specific chemical bonds. The compounds 
focus their action on breaking ether and ester bonds linking lignin to 
hemicellulose, as well as disrupting the hydrogen bonding between 
lignin and cellulose. The DESs break down essential intra-lignin bonds, 
which include aryl ether linkages (β-O-4, α -O-4, 4-O-5) and carbon- 
carbon bonds (β-5, β-β, 5–5), which enable lignin depolymerization 
and extraction ( Kumar et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2023; Scelsi et al., 2021 ). 
The DESs achieve higher separation efficiency because they precisely 
break complex biomass bonds.
The potentially "green" solvent characteristics of DESs make them 
valuable beyond their functional performance since they support sus -
tainable processing goals. DESs show excellent performance in addition 
to meeting key sustainability criteria, which include renewability and 
low toxicity, together with biodegradability and recyclability, and 
thermal stability. DESs presents a promising replacement for traditional 
solvents because they maintain efficient biomass fractionation while 
providing environmentally friendly properties ( Das et al., 2018 ).
The three-dimensional aromatic polymer lignin constitutes a com -
plex structure that acts as a resistant network in plant cell walls through 
its main components of guaiacyl (G), syringyl (S), and p-hydroxyphenyl 
(H) monolignol subunits. The majority of lignin links arise from β-O-4 
aryl ether bonds and strong carbon-carbon (C-C) bonds, which make up 
50–70% of lignin's total linkages. The complex arrangement within 
lignin usually creates difficulties during biomass processing; however, 
DESs represent essential solvents to overcome these difficulties associ -
ated with lignin dissolution and fractionation ( Scelsi et al., 2021 ).
The dissolution of lignin by DESs occurs through strong hydrogen 
bonding forces. The hydrogen bond interactions between DES compo -
nents such as HBDs, HBAs, and chloride anions (Cl⁻), and lignin hydroxyl 
and phenolic groups determine the effectiveness of lignin dissolution. 
Lignin internal hydrogen bonds break when DESs dissolve lignin frag -
ments. The effectiveness of DESs dissolution power can be predicted 
based on their depends on their Kamlet-Taft parameters. Since their 
dissolution abilities are based on their hydrogen bond donation ( α ) and 
acceptance (β) abilities to break lignin's structural framework ( Zhang 
et al., 2023a; Chen et al., 2023a; Ji et al., 2021 ).
In acidic DESs, the proton is the catalyst that drives the key mech -
anism for lignin breakdown. As the H⁺ ions dissociate from acidic HBDs 
such as carboxylic acids, they act as catalysts to aid the breakage of both 
the ether and ester bonds through multiple chemical reactions. The first 
step involves protonating the benzylic carbon (C α ), which creates a 
benzylic carbocation through dehydration. The intermediate state al -
lows β-O-4 bond cleavage through two pathways, which include the loss 
of formaldehyde when Cβ is protonated or the formation of an enol ether 
that hydrolyzes into a ketone and phenol after losing a CH
₂
OH group 
from Cγ. These mechanisms improve the process of lignin depolymer -
ization while also enabling its solubilization ( Schutyser et al., 2018; Bai 
et al., 2022 ).
Lignin dissolution benefits substantially from electrostatic and ionic 
interactions, together with hydrogen bond interactions. The electro -
negative anion Cl⁻ from ChCl interacts with lignin to break chemical 
bonds while increasing its solubility. The electropositive nature of DESs 
helps maintain stability of charged intermediates during bond destruc -
tion processes, which enhances lignin solubilization. DESs demonstrate

The efficacy of biomass fractionation through DESs strongly depends 
on their composition because the selection of HBD affects their behavior 
as solvents. Carboxylic acid-based HBDs, including LA, oxalic acid, and 
acetic acid, show excellent proton-catalytic properties, which enhance 
their efficiency. Low pKa values in these solvents create ideal conditions 
for extensive β -O-4 bond cleavage, which leads to better delignification. 
The application of acidic DESs leads to lignin condensation and acyla -
tion reactions, which affect the extracted lignin quality according to ( Liu 
et al., 2020; Provost et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2023a ). Polyol-based HBDs 
such as glycerol and EG perform lignin dissolution through hydrogen 
bonding, which protects most β -O-4 bonds from cleavage. The efficiency 
of these systems faces limitations from extended carbon chains and 
increased viscosity, which necessitate elevated temperatures and longer 
reaction times for effective fractionation according to ( Hou et al., 2018; 
Zhao et al., 2023 ).
Amine or amide-based HBDs such as urea and triethanolamine 
(TEOA) produce average results during delignification processes. The 
extensive hydrogen bonding in urea-based DESs results from their strong 
HBA capability (high β Kamlet-Taft parameter), but they deliver less 
delignification performance than acidic HBDs. Ethanolamine-based 
DESs demonstrate superior performance to both urea and acetamide- 
based systems because of their enhanced solvation properties ( Zhu 
et al., 2023a; Zhao et al., 2018a ). The selection of HBA is critical for DES 
applications because ChCl represents the most effective HBA. The 
effectiveness of DESs decreases with increasing length of alkyl chains in 
HBA cations or anions, while amino acid-based HBAs show promise for 
developing more environmentally friendly DES systems ( Jablonsky 
et al., 2019; Nawaz et al., 2022 ).
The molar ratio of the HBA to HBD has an influence on the perfor -
mance of DES as it controls the solution's viscosity and acidity/basicity 
levels and overall fractionation efficiency. Lower viscosity ratios work 
best for DES systems, while the optimal ratio selection depends on the 
specific DES composition. The performance of DESs decreases when 
using excess HBD because it increases viscosity and modifies the solvent 
properties, which decreases their ability to dissolve lignin and hemi -
cellulose effectively ( Zhou et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023c; Deng et al., 
2023 ). The optimization of the HBA: HBD ratio serves to achieve equi -
librium between solvent properties and fractionation results, which 
depend on the specific biomass type and target products. Table 4 dem -
onstrates how these properties ultimately influence how well a DES can 
soak into biomass, dissolve lignin, or make the material more digestible 
for enzymes. It may sound a little abstract, but comparing values such as 
viscosity, surface tension, and the α , β , and π * parameters can reveal 
clear trends. In a sense, that comparison offers a practical way to see 
how tweaking the HBA: HBD ratio can shift pretreatment performance.
The ratio of HBA to HBD in the DES also has a pivotal function in 
regulating the physico-chemical properties, which determine the frac -
tionation efficiency of the biomass. Generally, when the HBA/HBD ratio 
is low, the strength of hydrogen bonding is increased, effectively dis -
rupting the interconnected structures of lignin-cellulose. Enhanced 
delignification of the lignocellulosic materials can thus be achieved. For 
example, when the ratio of BTEAC:LA was maintained at 1:4, the best 
interactions were established towards lignin structures, showing high 
lignin removal efficiency as well as high glucose yield due to the high 
hydrogen bonding strength of the system as well as the molecular dy -
namics ( Ji et al., 2025 ). These results also show the potential of opti -
mizing the ratio of the hydrophilic to hydrophobic compounds in 
achieving high lignin extraction while maintaining the sustainability of 
the ChCl:Malonic acid-based solvents ( Long et al., 2025 ).
Viscosity and surface tension determine in more detail the efficiency 
of mass transfer and DES penetration in biomass structures. Lower vis -
cosity will facilitate diffusion, improving interactions between solvent 
and substrate. Reduced surface tension will promote better wettability 
and infiltration in the lignocellulosic matrix. Systems exhibiting low 
surface tension and proper fluidity, the cetyltrimethylammonium bro -
mide/LA/AlCl ₃ ternary DES has been reported to show a surface tension 
of 29.3 mN/m-have been recently reported to act as effective catalysts 
for complex biomass transformations at high conversion levels, such as a 
71.6% furfural yield ( Fan et al., 2025 ). A correlation analysis showed 
that DESs with lower surface tension and higher polarity parameters, α 
and π *, present higher lignin removal and glucose recovery, confirming 
again that interfacial properties critically mediate the accessibility of 
solvents and fractionation efficiency ( Ji et al., 2025; Khanbabaeimoghri 
et al., 2025 ).
The Kamlet-Taft solvatochromic parameters( α , β , and π *) provide a 
molecular-scale understanding of DES reactivity. A high α value en -
hances DES – lignin hydrogen bonding, while an elevated π * supports 
charge delocalization and disrupts aromatic stacking in lignin ( Ji et al., 
2025 ). These parameters collectively contribute to selective dissolution 
of lignin over cellulose, ensuring high-purity fractionation as seen in 
ChCl:5-sulfosalicylic acid: γ -valerolactone systems yielding 98.77% pure 
lignin ( Yao et al., 2024 ). Balancing these physicochemical features 
(optimal HBA:HBD ratio, moderate viscosity, low surface tension, and 
strong α and π *) defines the high efficiency and scalability of DESs in 
sustainable biomass valorization.
Biomass processing with DES depends heavily on reaction conditions 
such as temperature and time duration, and water content levels. High 
temperatures enhance delignification because they decrease DES vis -
cosity which facilitates better mass transfer and accelerates bond 
cleavage kinetics especially for β -O-4 bonds. Operating at temperatures 
above 140

with unwanted lignin condensation and repolymerization, which 
negatively affects lignin quality ( Provost et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2023; 
Wang et al., 2024a ). The length of the reaction period enables better 
delignification, but extended processing time following depolymeriza -
tion can cause lignin repolymerization, thus requiring proper duration 
optimization ( Wang et al., 2024a ).

and structural complexity of the biomass. Furthermore, the difference in 
lignin type also differs from biomass species, for instance, between

The β -O-4 bond serves as a fundamental structural component in 
lignin, which determines its potential for valuable use during DES 
biomass fractionation processes. Certain conditions and DES composi -
tions that use polyol-based DESs at lower reaction temperatures and 
shorter processing times, with higher water content and diols for sta -
bilizing reaction intermediates, help preserve this bond. The conditions 
protect β -O-4 bonds from breaking, which helps maintain lignin struc -
ture for applications requiring these linkages to remain intact. β -O-4 
bond cleavage occurs predominantly in acidic DESs at elevated tem -
peratures, which causes extensive depolymerization but reduces lignin 
applicability for particular high-value uses ( Liu et al., 2021; Lou et al., 
2019; Nie et al., 2023 ).
During DES processing, lignin undergoes fundamental structural 
changes that result in altered properties and multiple end-use possibil -
ities. The initial depolymerization process of lignin leads to lower mo -
lecular weight (Mw) values and greater polydispersity ( Đ ) because of the 
breakdown of its complex network. The process of prolonged treatment 
and harsh processing conditions results in repolymerization, which 
generates new structures that are undesirable ( Wang et al., 2024a ). 
During β -O-4 cleavage and demethoxylation reactions, lignin experi -
ences an increase in phenolic hydroxyl (OH) content, which improves its 
reactivity but creates distinct chemical changes ( Lyu et al., 2019 ). These 
modifications enable scientists to customize lignin for industrial appli -
cations such as biofuels and adhesives.
The process of lignin modification through DESs leads to condensa -
tion reactions that create carbon-carbon bonds ( β - β , β -5, 5 – 5), which 
decreases the material's solubility and its ability to be converted into 
value-added products. The formation of undesirable side reactions oc -
curs frequently when acidic DESs are used in combination with high 
temperatures and prolonged reaction times ( Hong et al., 2020; Wang 
et al., 2024a ). Acylation represents another structural transformation, 
which adds carboxylic acids (such as LA) to lignin primarily at the C γ 
position, which modifies its functional properties ( Zhu et al., 2023a ). 
Demethoxylation processes that eliminate methoxy (-OCH ₃ ) groups 
produce additional modifications to lignin's chemical structure that may 
affect its solubility characteristics and reactivity profile.
The DES processing leads to variations in the syringyl/guaiacyl (S/G) 
ratio because the S/G ratio increases when guaiacyl (G) units become 
preferentially removed or modified. Lignin becomes more or less suit -
able for particular applications based on the S/G ratio because this ratio 
determines its physical and chemical characteristics ( Yan et al., 2023 ). 
Selecting appropriate DES components together with optimized reaction 
times and temperatures enables researchers to manage β -O-4 bond 
preservation and structural transformations and S/G ratio alterations for 
better lignin application in industry.
Biomass fractionation through TDESs becomes more effective than 
binary DESs when a third component like Lewis acids (FeCl ₃ , AlCl ₃ ), 
diols (EG), or metal salts (K ₂ CO ₃ ) is added to the mixture. The acidity of 
Lewis acids in solvents enables better bond cleavage and produces lignin 
with both purity enhancement and lower molecular weight (Mw) ( Chen 
et al., 2023b; Wang et al., 2020 ). Diols serve as essential components 
that protect reactive carbocations while stopping unwanted lignin 
condensation reactions, which helps maintain important β -O-4 bonds 
useful for premium lignin applications ( Liu et al., 2021; Chen et al., 
2023b ). The addition of K ₂ CO ₃ enables the solvent to undergo 
alkaline-catalyzed depolymerization reactions that target phenolic 
units, thus expanding its reactive properties ( Chen et al., 2023b ).
The addition of a third component in TDESs enables users to control 
reaction mechanisms with better acidity regulation and hydrogen 
bonding properties, and reaction pathway management than binary 
systems. The flexible nature of these processes allows for customized 
fractionation methods to maximize lignin extraction and structural 
characteristics needed for particular applications, including biofuels and 
biopolymers ( Ee et al., 2023; Yan et al., 2023 ). The third component in 
TDESs enables users to reach equilibrium between delignification effi -
ciency and protection of essential lignin structures, which leads to 
improved extracted product yields and quality while sustaining DES 
sustainability advantages.
Acidic DES containing carboxylic acid as HBDs demonstrate excep -
tional biomass fractionation capabilities because their proton-catalyzed 
action breaks ether and ester bonds as well as glycosidic bonds and LCC 
bonds. The HBD's low pKa value, combined with its high H ⁺ concen -
tration, enables the DESs to deliver high delignification outcomes but 
results in extensive β -O-4 bond cleavage, which promotes lignin 
condensation. Polyol-based DES performs moderately in delignification 
of biomass yet maintains better β -O-4 bond integrity, although their 
increased viscosity may decrease operational performance. The 
delignification ability of amine or amide-based DESs varies based on 
their strong hydrogen bond acceptance (high β ), while ethanolamines 
show better performance than urea and create less bond breakage than 
acidic DESs.
The effectiveness of DES types increases through the addition of Cl ⁻ 
as a halogen anion, which improves bond cleavage and hydrogen 
bonding for efficient separation. The addition of universal enhancers, 
including temperature control and optimal water content, enhances 
performance by reducing viscosity, accelerating reaction kinetics, con -
trolling acidity, and improving mass transfer to achieve specific lignin 
extraction outcomes for yield-structure balance.
5. Molecular dynamics and thermodynamics insights into DES 
systems
DESs form on the basis of the eutectic process, which leads to a 
considerable lowering of the melting point compared to the components 
taken individually. This is due to the creation of a strong network of 
hydrogen bonds between HBAs and HBDs. Although the traditional DESs 
have low melting points, the data relating to PEGylated DESs indicate 
the short-term thermal stability, which is the initial point of degrada -
tion, varies between 136.2
◦

◦
C ( Zhang et al., 2012; Ma et al., 
2024 ). The exact adjustment of these components together with their 
ratios enables specific solvent characteristics that enhance their effi -
ciency in lignin dissolution applications.
The expansion of eutectic systems happens through traditional 
hydrogen-bonding interactions, while new DES designs introduce novel 
systems. The eutectic system of "Inverted" DESs operates through cation- 
driven interactions, while halogen-bonded DESs made from quaternary 
ammonium salts and dihalogens create different intermolecular forces. 
The addition of ions, molecules, or metals to low-melting mixtures en -
ables the development of diverse DES formulations that enhance flexi -
bility for particular applications ( Yu et al., 2019; Shi et al., 2022; Chen 
and Yu, 2024 ). Modern DES design methods extend their functional 
capabilities for sustainable biomass processing by utilizing multiple 
thermodynamic interactions.
The solvent characteristics of DESs can be effectively described 
through the Kamlet-Taft (K-T) parameters, which consist of α (hydrogen 
bond donation ability), β (hydrogen bond acceptance ability), and π * 
(dipolarity/polarizability). The effectiveness of DESs in dissolving 
lignocellulosic biomass depends heavily on these metrics because they 
determine how DES interacts with lignin and cellulose, and hemicellu -
lose. The disruption of hydrogen bonds in biomass components becomes

fundamental for DES optimization in fractionation tasks ( Mishra et al.,

The K-T parameter changes indicate how DES composition affects 
their solvent characteristics. Metal halide-based DESs show higher π * 
values than ammonium salt-based systems, which results in better lignin 
solvation because of their enhanced polarizability ( Zhang et al., 2012 ). 
DES components with longer alkyl chains cause π * to decrease because 
molecular interactions become weaker, yet β values increase as 
cation-anion interactions become weaker, which enhances hydrogen 
bond acceptance. The dissolution efficiency of DESs becomes reduced 
when carboxyl groups exceed their optimal amounts in HBDs, as 
observed in CTAB:LA: Fe³ ⁺ /Sn ⁴⁺ /Al³ ⁺ systems. These design trends help 
create DESs that have optimal solvent properties for effective and se -
lective biomass fractionation.
The thermal stability of DESs stands as an essential factor for biomass 
pretreatment because it affects both solvent effectiveness and recycla -
bility at temperatures near 100
◦
C. DESs break apart into their HBA and 
HBD components at high temperatures because thermal energy weakens 
hydrogen bonds, which results in decomposition and reduced efficacy. 
The solvent's eutectic properties become compromised when thermal 
energy causes dissociation because this process negatively affects lignin 
and hemicellulose dissolution during biomass fractionation ( Chen et al., 
2022 ).
The stability of DESs depends mainly on the characteristics of the 
HBD component. DESs that use low-boiling-point HBDs such as ChCl 
with malonic acid or EG tend to decompose at high temperatures 
because they have lower thermal stability ( Marchel et al., 2022; 
Delgado-Mellado et al., 2018 ). DESs that use high-boiling-point or 
thermally stable HBDs such as glycerol or glucose demonstrate better 
resistance to thermal breakdown because they preserve their structural 
integrity during processing. The length of alkyl chains in HBDs de -
termines thermal stability because longer chains create stronger inter -
molecular bonds, which makes DESs suitable for high-temperature 
biomass pretreatment applications ( Ghaedi et al., 2017 ).
DES transport properties, including viscosity and conductivity, play 
essential roles in biomass fractionation since they determine both mass 
transfer rates and process performance. DESs maintain elevated vis -
cosity at room temperature because their extensive hydrogen bonds and 
large ions restrict molecular movement while making it difficult for 
biomass to dissolve. DES viscosity lowers substantially when tempera -
ture increases, thus enabling improved mass transfer alongside better 
penetration of the solvent into biomass structures ( Chen et al., 2019 ). 
The temperature rise increases conductivity because ions move better, 
but large components like PEG: TBAC restrict this improvement 
compared to PEG: ChCl, thereby reducing ionic transport ability.
DES ionic properties and aggregation patterns derive from their 
chemical composition. The length of alkyl chains in DES components 
weakens ionic properties because they create barriers that slow down 
ion disassociation and movement ( Wang et al., 2020 ). The presence of 
highly mobile metal ions in metal-based DESs made with FeCl ₃ ⋅ 6H ₂ O 
creates high conductivity that makes them suitable for applications 
needing efficient charge transfer. DES properties affect biomass pre -
treatment outcomes because they determine how well the solvent in -
teracts with lignocellulosic components.
Computational modeling and molecular dynamics simulations pro -
vide critical atomic-scale insights into the design and function of DES for 
biomass processing. As demonstrated in one study, ReaxFF molecular 
dynamics simulations were used to investigate the pretreatment of a 
lignin-carbohydrate complex, revealing that key bonds were cleaved at 
an optimal temperature of 373 K in a ChCl/LA acid solvent. The simu -
lations identified free protons as crucial for delignification and quanti -
fied how solvent composition dictates efficiency, showing that a ChCl/ 
glycerol DES achieved superior interaction energies with carbohydrate 
and phenolic products ( Xu et al., 2021 ). This approach effectively maps 
the hydrogen-bonding networks and energetic landscapes that govern 
solvation and degradation mechanisms ( Jablonsky et al., 2019 ).
The integration of multi-scale computational methods is equally 
powerful for understanding polysaccharide dissolution. Another inves -
tigation combined classical molecular dynamics with quantum me -
chanical calculations to study cellulose dissolution in a 
benzyltriethylammonium/urea DES. The molecular dynamics analysis 
tracked the disruption of cellulose crystallinity through hydrogen-bond 
density and radial distribution functions, while density functional the -
ory (DFT) provided a deeper quantum topological analysis of the 
interaction sites. By applying the Quantum Theory of Atoms in Mole -
cules (AIM) and non-covalent interaction (NCI) analysis, the research 
attributed the successful dissolution primarily to strong physical 
hydrogen-bond interactions, offering a comprehensive atomic-level 
rationale for the solvent's performance as a green medium ( Azougagh 
et al., 2023 ).
The atomistic mechanisms through which DES enhance biomass 
processing are effectively elucidated using molecular dynamics simu -
lations and complementary computational techniques. In one applica -
tion focused on enzyme stabilization, simulations of β -glucosidase 
revealed that DES components form specific interactions, primarily 
through their hydrogen bond donor ends, with the enzyme structure 
( Wang et al., 2024b ). These interactions induce a more compact 
conformation by altering the secondary structure, increasing α -helix and 
reducing β -sheet content, and crucially restricting amino acid movement 
to stabilize the active site, explaining the experimental observation of 
enhanced activity and prolonged half-life ( Wang et al., 2024b; Yan et al., 
2023 ). In another study probing pretreatment mechanisms, ab initio and 
classical molecular dynamics simulations tracked the dissociation of 
lignin-carbohydrate complexes in a ChCl/oxalic acid DES ( Yu et al., 
2024b ). These simulations identified the precise cleavage of the 
guaiacyl-xylose ether bond by oxalic acid, a process not observed in 
aqueous solution and subsequently modeled the resulting molecular 
expansion of lignin ( Yu et al., 2024b ). This computational discovery of 
lignin's structural rearrangement provides the fundamental explanation 
for improved inter-particle binding and densification. Together, these 
cases demonstrate how simulation moves beyond correlation to deliver 
causal, mechanistic understanding of solvent-biomass interactions, 
directly guiding the rational design of more effective systems.
The interfacial characteristics of DESs are determined by their sur -
face tension alongside their hygroscopic properties. The strength of 
hydrogen bonds between HBD and HBA determines surface tension, 
which results in lower values for DESs containing weak hydrogen 
bonding, like ZnCl ₂ :LA, to improve their wetting properties during 
biomass penetration ( Chen et al., 2019 ). The high hygroscopic nature of 
ChCl: glucose DES arises from its hydrophilic groups, which lead to 
increased water absorption that modifies solvent properties and impacts 
fractionation efficiency. DES formulations can be optimized for efficient 
and sustainable biomass processing through a combination of interface 
characteristics and customized transport properties.
Through Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, researchers gain a 
thorough comprehension of DESs' chemical interactions with biomass 
constituents when fractionating them. To model the molecular behavior 
accurately, researchers implement MD simulations by using the

with energy minimization and then move to NVT and NPT ensemble 
equilibration ( Chen et al., 2025 ). The simulations demonstrate how 
bromide ions (Br ⁻ ) in CTAB:LA: Fe³ ⁺ DESs break hydrogen bonds, which 
improves the separation of lignin and hemicellulose from biomass ( Tang 
et al., 2024 ). The aromatic structure of lignin enables its higher nonpolar 
dissolution compared to cellulose through van der Waals forces, so DESs 
can be designed for specific fractionation applications Table 5 .

Recent case studies show DESs display unique interaction patterns 
when they interact with galactomannan polysaccharides. Type II DESs, 
consisting of quaternary ammonium salts and metal hydrates, dissolve 
galactomannan through anionic disruption of its hydrogen-bond net -
works. Type III DESs composed of quaternary ammonium salts with 
HBDs target glycosidic bonds to achieve controlled molecular weight 
recovery of galactomannan through selective cleavage ( Guo et al., 
2025 ). The customized DES interactions demonstrate their capability 
to process polysaccharides through controlled dissolution and structural 
modifications, which depend on the solvent composition.
Advanced computational methods, including Electrostatic Potential 
(ESP) and Independent Gradient Model (IGM) analysis, show that DESs 
interact with lignin units through non-covalent forces, which affect G, S, 
and H units ( Chen et al., 2025 ). The research demonstrates DES-lignin 
binding mechanisms through hydrogen bonding and van der Waals 
forces, which lead to lignin unit solvation. The analysis of non-covalent 
interactions through these case studies helps researchers develop 
improved DES formulations for better lignin extraction and fraction -

Among the most widely applied approaches, MD simulations have 
demonstrated particular utility in elucidating the dissolution mecha -
nism of lignocellulosic components. In a study, it was explored that the 
molecular mechanism of cellulose solubility in oxaline versus reline, 
revealing that oxalic acid more effectively weakens the interaction be -

cellulose dissolution, while urea cannot fulfill the same role in reline

showing that the number and type of oxygen atoms in the HBD deter -

these capabilities, classical MD is constrained by the accuracy of its 
underlying force fields and cannot capture polarization or 
charge-transfer effects inherent in DES hydrogen-bonding networks, 
limiting quantitative reliability without careful parameterization.
DFT offers a complementary quantum mechanical perspective

a combined study of DFT and MD, the lactic acid-based DES with

ML has emerged as a transformative approach that increasingly

6. Simulation and modelling techniques in biomass treatment 
with DES
The main goal of modeling a DES-based biomass fractionation pro -
cess is to understand lignin and hemicellulose dissolution processes as 
well as to predict the outcomes and optimize operational parameters to 
achieve better efficiency and yield. These models help researchers to 
understand how DES compositions and treatment conditions affect 
fractionation by simulating hydrogen bonding along with proton catal -
ysis and bond cleavage specifically targeting β -O-4 linkages. These tools 
allow for exact adjustments of temperature, time, and moisture content 
to achieve maximum delignification while maintaining valuable lignin 
structures that can be used in high-value applications. Furthermore, 
scale up the DES-based operations by predicting industrial performance 
at larger production levels to establish economic viability and ensure 
sustainable operation can be achieved.
Biomass fractionation mechanisms using DES as solvent have been 
studied using molecular-level simulations, including Ab Initio Molecular 
Dynamics (AIMD) and Molecular Dynamics (MD). According to Yu et al. 
(2024) , AIMD and MD simulations showed that the ChCl: OA DES system 
breaks down LCCs through the cleavage of guaiacyl: xylose bonds, which 
operates differently than water-based systems. The breaking process 
generates lignin expansion, which researchers confirmed through 
Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and fluorescence microscopy 
alongside Fourier-Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) measure -
ments. The simulations link molecular-level lignin expansion to biofuel 
production improvements through densification at the macroscopic 
level, according to ( Yu et al., 2024b ).
These simulations are supported by experimental analysis, which 
validates the predicted changes in biomass structure. The research by 
Zhao et al. (2023) resulted in an increased cellulose degree of crystal -
linity of their XRD analysis and FTIR results showing lignin and 
hemicellulose peak at 1737, 1602, 1510, and 1245 cm ⁻ ¹ disappearance 
and SEM results showing increased surface porosity, which confirms 
dissolution, in line with model prediction regarding amorphous 
component removal and ester bond cleavage. The integration between 
theoretical simulations and experimental methods provides a bridge 
between theoretical findings and real-world outcomes, which allows for 
exact optimization of DES-based fractionation operations for better ef -
ficiency and scalability ( Zhao et al., 2023 ).
The development of ML as a prediction tool enables a better under -
standing of fractionation efficiency while providing insights into DES 
biomass processing mechanisms. The ML models developed by Xu et al. 
(2023b) identified temperature as the primary influential factor for DES 
fractionation efficiency, while biomass hemicellulose content and DES 
hydrophilicity also impact lignin removal efficiency. The research 
demonstrated that DES acidity, along with polarity, serves as an essen -
tial determinant that affects the process efficiency ( Xu et al., 2023b ). Ge 
et al. (2023a) employed ML predictions to determine both delignifica -
tion efficiency and lignin structural parameters, including bond types, 
molecular weight (Mw), syringyl/guaiacyl (S/G) ratio, as well as the 
hydroxyl group content. The research model demonstrated that effective 
lignin depolymerization requires optimal temperatures and HBD po -
larity and Lewis acidity in TDESs through their analysis of HBD inter -
molecular bonds and physico-chemical properties, which drive 
fractionation results ( Ge et al., 2023a ).
Advanced ML techniques, together with multivariate analysis 
methods, improve the understanding of intricate DES systems. Ge et al. 
(2023b) applied PCA and PLS regression and Spearman correlation 
analysis together with advanced ML algorithms, including RF, XGBoost, 
and DNN, to study lignin depolymerization and separation mechanisms 
in Lewis acid-containing TDESs. The XGBoost model achieved the 
highest performance through its feature importance metrics (F Score and 
SHAP), which showed that intermolecular forces, along with HBD dipole 
moment, surface tension, and Log P, acted as primary drivers of success. 
PLS analysis demonstrated strong explanatory power (R²X = 0.959, 
R²Y = 0.894, Q² = 0.743), which made it possible to accurately predict 
specific bond cleavage, like β– O – 4 and lignin structural transformations. 
The developed approaches offer a strong methodology to improve DES 
formulations and reaction conditions for creating efficient biomass 
fractionation systems at a large scale ( Ge et al., 2023b ).

7. Process optimization for industrial-scale biomass 
fractionation using DES
The main goals of DES biomass fractionation optimization focus on 
maximizing both lignin and hemicellulose extraction and glucan pres -
ervation and product quality while reducing energy requirements and 
expenses. The achievement of these objectives demands the right 
adjustment of fundamental trade-offs that include high temperature for 
better delignification yet potential biomass degradation, and high vis -
cosity that hinders mass transfer against lower viscosity for better sol -
vent penetration and solvent loading that enhances efficiency but 
creates economic and sustainability challenges.
DES biomass fractionation depends heavily on temperature as a 
critical factor because optimal ranges usually exist between 80 and 
200
◦
C based on both biomass type and solvent selection. Lignin removal 
becomes more efficient at higher temperatures because elevated tem -
peratures both break chemical bonds and decrease solvent viscosity, 
which leads to better mass transfer. The optimization of temperature 
must be done precisely to achieve a proper balance between efficient 
delignification and maintenance of valuable biomass components.
The final outcomes of DES fractionation depend on both the time 
needed for processing and the ratio of biomass to solvent. Poplar 
treatment for 2 h resulted in 53.2% glucan retention with 73.2% lignin 
removal, but extended processing time to 6 h led to 90.2% lignin 
removal at the expense of glucan retention, which dropped to 42.8%. 
The optimal ratio of wheat straw to solvent in biomass fractionation was 
determined by Jan ˇcíkov ´a et al. (2023) ( Jan ˇcíkov ´a et al., 2023 ) as 1:27 
(w/w), which produced 16.44% residual lignin. The fractionation effi -
ciency improves with increased solvent load, but this results in higher 
costs, so researchers must find the best ratio to maximize both perfor -
mance and economic benefits.
The molar ratio between HBA and HBD in DES determines the 
maximum possible efficiency of biomass fractionation processes. The 
best ratio for wheat stalk treatment with cetyltrimethylammonium 
chloride (CTAC) and LA was determined to be 1:4, which resulted in 
88.3% delignification and 89.0% xylan removal. The combination of 1:2 
ChCl to LA proves effective for multiple biomass materials, including 
Atriplex, which achieves 69.3% delignification. The specified ratios 
create optimal solvent acidity and viscosity and solvation capacity to 
break lignin-carbohydrate complexes effectively without compromising 
process efficiency.
DES performance receives additional enhancement through the 
addition of water and salts as additives. The addition of 10 – 15% water to 
ChCl: LA makes its viscosity lower, which promotes better mass transfer 
and leads to an 84.6% delignification rate when using 10% water con -
tent. The addition of water beyond 15% percentage causes DES dilution, 
which reduces its ability to solvate and decreases the process efficiency. 
The addition of 5 – 10 wt% CaCl ₂ ⋅ 6H ₂ O salt to ChCl:LA (1:2) produces 
significant bamboo delignification up to 84.7% because ionic in -
teractions between the components enhance bond cleavage. The addi -
tives enable researchers to adjust DES properties which helps achieve 
better fractionation results for various biomass samples.
The development of surfactant-based and carboxylic acid-based DES 
systems creates advanced methods for fractionation applications. The 
delignification process reaches higher efficiency levels when using 
CTAB/CTAC: LA as a surfactant-based DES because these compounds 
successfully break C-O and C-C bonds in lignin ( Chen et al., 2025 ). The 
carboxylic acid-based DES system ChCl: benzoic acid shows high spec -
ificity by removing 67.8% of lignin from poplar at 140
◦
C for 3 h and 
maintaining 95.8% of cellulose ( Li et al., 2024 ). The development of new 
DES formulations increases their adaptability, which enables users to 
design customized procedures that simultaneously remove lignin and 
protect cellulose during sustainable biomass treatment processes.
The optimal DES fractionation of wheat straw and canola requires 
specific solvent blends and operating parameters that manage both 
lignin extraction and glucan conservation. DES solutions made from 
CTAB/LA and metal salts SnCl ₄ or CoCl ₂ achieve greater than 88% 
glucan preservation, but AlCl ₃ diminishes glucan retention to 45.4% due 
to its destructive effect on cellulose while providing 87.5% delignifica -
tion. The solid-to-liquid ratio plays a vital role because a 1:10 ratio 
produces the best results for canola fractionation by enabling effective 
mass transfer and achieving high yields with reduced solvent 
consumption.
The combination of TDES solutions with appropriate additives de -
livers superior results in fractionation processes for both poplar and pine

◦

maximize lignin extraction and protect valuable carbohydrates in 
different biomass samples.
DES biomass fractionation requires proper viscosity management

decrease that transforms CTAC: LA from 1223.1 cP at 1:2 ratio to 
212.0 cP at 1:6 ratio, thus improving both solvent penetration and mass

◦

The integrated process design of DES biomass fractionation depends 
on mechanism-driven optimization to achieve better efficiency and 
product quality. DESs made from ChCl and benzoic acid (BA) success -
fully break β -O-4 linkages, which produce lignin with 53.01% β -O-4 
content that has high value for subsequent uses. The surfactant-based

DES interactions with biomass enables the selection of optimal DES 
compositions that optimize the fractionation process for large-scale 
operations and sustainable practices.
The design of integrated DES processes requires a careful approach to 
managing multiple conflicting objectives. The removal of lignin at 
90.2% from poplar requires sacrificing glucan retention which decreases 
to 42.8% thus requiring optimization to protect valuable carbohydrates. 
The DES treatment process boosts biomass crystallinity to 68.6% from 
55.6% in poplar cellulose isolation structures (CIS), which improves 
nanocellulose production for premium applications. The process re -
quires adjusting parameters, including temperature and time, and DES 
composition to achieve desired results while keeping costs affordable 
and maximizing the useful output from fractionated biomass 
components.

amounts of energy. Water dilution serves as a practical method because 
it enables phase separation and DES recovery, which improves sus -
tainability and decreases operational expenses Table 8 .

a fundamental requirement. The combination of surfactants like cetyl -
trimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) and cetyltrimethylammonium 
chloride (CTAC) in DESs demonstrates potential because they effectively

Beyond solvent selection and extraction efficiency, scale-dependent 
transport and engineering constraints emerge as major challenges 
when transitioning DES-based processes from laboratory to industrial 
scale. Laboratory optimization is typically performed in small glass re -
actors, whereas industrial implementation relies on large stainless-steel 
equipment with fundamentally different heat transfer coefficients, 
heating rates, and hydrodynamic behavior ( Rente et al., 2022 ). These 
differences necessitate re-optimization of critical operating parameters 
such as temperature, residence time, agitation intensity, and solid -
– liquid ratios during scale-up. Such limitations are particularly pro -
nounced in biomass fractionation processes, where feedstock 
heterogeneity, variable particle size, and complex solid matrices further 
exacerbate mass transfer resistance and diffusion limitations. In addi -
tion, equipment compatibility becomes a non-negligible concern at 
larger scales ( Rente et al., 2022 ). Certain DES formulations, especially 
acid-based systems, have been reported to exhibit corrosive behavior 
toward common construction materials such as carbon steel, potentially 
requiring specialized alloys or protective coatings and thereby 
increasing capital investment ( Amin et al., 2007 ). These issues are 
especially critical for continuous or large-batch biomass processing fa -
cilities, where long-term material stability and operational reliability are 
essential.
Downstream processing technology and techno-economic perfor -
mance are also equally important and are largely determining factors for 
the industrial practicability of DES-based biomass fractionation. As the 
vapor pressure of DES is close to zero, any effective solvent recovery 
technique such as evaporation cannot be conducted; hence, the exclu -
sive focus on solvent recycle, product purification, and DES-based 
extract usage can be point blanket considerations. The fact that the 
bypass of the entire product purification process and the preparation of 
pure DES extracts can significantly lower the downstream processing 
complexities and costs, whereas the entire product purification process 
will significantly enhance the sustainability and processing costs. At the 
same time, it may be noted that the consideration of cost of the solvent, 
energy for the transport and processing of viscous fluids, and the need 
for pilot-plant validation are critical factors that generally receive little 
emphatic attention during laboratory-scale investigations ( Rente et al., 
2022 ).
The future development of DES biomass fractionation requires all 
laboratory, pilot, and large scale optimization for particular biomass 
types and the combination of DES with enzymatic or mechanical pro -
cesses to enhance efficiency and the implementation of circular econ -
omy principles through lignin chemical production for maximum 
resource utilization and sustainability.
8. Sustainability and environmental impact of DES-based 
fractionation
Biomass fractionation using DES provides substantial sustainability 
benefits compared to conventional approaches because these solvents 
are non-toxic and biodegradable and sourced from renewable materials 
Fig. 5 . The DES-based processes eliminate the need for dangerous 
chemicals while extracting lignin, cellulose, and hemicellulose effi -
ciently through environmentally friendly operations ( Ruengsrichaiya 
et al., 2024 ).
The main sustainability benefit of DESs as potentially green solvents 
stems from their environmentally friendly chemical makeup. The pro -
duction of DESs from bio-derived ChCl and LA compounds eliminates 
petroleum-based reagent use, which decreases their environmental 
impact ( Shen et al., 2019 ). The low volatility properties of DESs reduce 
air pollution risks better than conventional volatile organic solvents do, 
which results in safer workplaces and decreased atmospheric emissions 
( van Osch et al., 2017 ). DESs that derive from lignin phenolics represent 
fully renewable substances, which support circular economy principles 
by using biomass-derived components to create their formulation, thus 
completing the resource cycle ( Kim et al., 2018 ).
The energy and resource efficiency of DES-based processes further 
underscores their sustainability. The glucose yield from enzymatic hy -
drolysis reaches 94.3% after DES pretreatment, but remains at 9.6% for 
untreated biomass, thus enabling the highest possible conversion of 
biomass into valuable products ( Shen et al., 2019 ). The integrated DES 
processes simplify downstream operations by cutting the number of 
bleaching stages in pulp production from 4 to 5 to one stage, which 
decreases both chemical usage and energy needs. DESs show superior 
recyclability because they recover more than 90% of their material in 
consecutive cycles, which reduces waste output and decreases the 
requirement for fresh solvent additions, thus enhancing process resource 
efficiency ( Ruengsrichaiya et al., 2024 ).

The environmental advantages of DES processes become quantifiable 
through life cycle assessments (LCAs), which show a 35 – 50% reduction 
in greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions compared to IL-based methods. The 
substantial reduction stems from DESs requiring less energy while using 
fewer chemicals and being more easily recycled. The implementation of 
DES processes helps achieve global sustainability targets by lowering 
biomass fractionation's carbon emissions while protecting the environ -

The combination of advantages makes DESs a revolutionary tech -
nology for sustainable biomass processing. DESs demonstrate strong 
potential as sustainable solvent alternatives because they possess envi -
ronmentally friendly characteristics and operate with high energy effi -
ciency, minimal environmental impact, and positive LCA results. Future 
research on DES formulation optimization and green technology inte -
gration will enhance their sustainability advantages, which will drive 
industrial biorefinery adoption.
The implementation of DESs in biomass fractionation encounters 
major obstacles, especially with solvent recycling, since this process 
remains essential for maintaining sustainable and affordable operations. 
The repeated use of recycled DES leads to hemicellulose degradation 
product accumulation, which negatively affects delignification effi -
ciency throughout multiple cycles. The combination of hydrothermal 
pretreatment with DES application proves successful in maintaining 
delignification performance at high levels throughout at least five cycles 
because it reduces the negative effects of degradation byproducts. The 
preprocessing technique preserves solvent solvation properties, which 
results in steady fractionation outcomes ( Zhang et al., 2025b ).
The purification of recycled DES is essential for enhancing its reus -
ability and maintaining consistent performance in biomass processing. 
Activated carbon treatment has been shown to effectively remove im -
purities and degradation products from recycled DES, restoring its 
original efficiency. This purification approach not only ensures the 
consistent quality of lignin and cellulose across processing cycles but 
also contributes to the operational reliability of DES in industrial ap -
plications. In addition to activated carbon, ultrafiltration has emerged as 
a promising method for DES purification. In one study, ultrafiltration 
using regenerated cellulose membranes (RC70PP and Ultracel 5 kDa) 
was employed to purify spent DES, a mixture of ChCl and LA (1:10), after 
lignin extraction. The incorporation of ethanol as a cosolvent reduced 
DES viscosity, facilitating membrane filtration. A two-pass ultrafiltra -
tion process with 10 kDa and 5 kDa membranes removed approximately 
95% of the dissolved polymeric compounds, including lignin and 
hemicelluloses. Moreover, the membranes enabled the fractionation of 
these polymers into high and low-molecular-weight fractions. Notably, 
the 10 kDa cellulose-based membrane demonstrated strong chemical 
stability over three weeks of continuous exposure to the DES-ethanol 
solution, with only a 3% decrease in water permeability ( Ippolitov 
et al., 2022 ). The development of such efficient and sustainable recy -
cling methods is a critical step toward minimizing waste generation and 
reducing operational costs, thereby reinforcing the suitability of 
DES-based fractionation systems for large-scale biorefinery applications 
( Zhang et al., 2025b ).
The recovery of solvents faces substantial energy-related challenges 
in DES processes. The common DES recycling method of distillation 
requires 73% of process heat demand in certain systems, which 
demonstrates the need for more efficient energy solutions. Scientists are 
currently developing low-energy separation methods, including mem -
brane filtration and phase separation with water dilution to decrease 
thermal energy consumption. The implemented methods work to 
decrease the total energy consumption of DES fractionation while sup -
porting sustainability goals and enhancing economic viability ( Bartling 
et al., 2021 ).
The development of TDES systems, which combine ChCl with oxalic 
acid and EG, presents a promising approach to decreasing the energy 
requirements of depolymerization steps. These systems protect reactive 
intermediates during lignin breakdown, which reduces the energy 
needed to break bonds and process the material. TDESs achieve better 
process efficiency through optimized reaction pathways, which also 
protect valuable lignin structures, including β -O-4 linkages for high- 
value applications, thus improving both energy efficiency and product 
quality ( Liu et al., 2021 ).
Industrial adoption of DES fractionation faces major challenges 
because of its scalability and cost requirements. The production ex -
penses of DESs amount to 30 – 50% less than ILs because they require 
basic synthesis techniques and employ common bio-derived materials, 
including ChCl and LA. DESs present an economically favorable solution 
for extensive industrial operations because of their lower production 
expenses. Techno-economic assessments show that optimized reactor 
pressure and solvent loading can decrease capital expenses by 20 – 25% 
thus improving the financial feasibility of DES-based processes 
( Ruengsrichaiya et al., 2024; Bartling et al., 2021 ).
The path to overcoming scalability challenges requires ongoing 
innovation in reactor design and process integration methods. Better 
mass transfer and heat distribution in advanced reactor designs enable 
more efficient fractionation operations at increased production scales. 
The combination of DES pretreatment with enzymatic hydrolysis or 
lignin valorization downstream processes enables operational stream -
lining, which reduces costs. The combination of solvent recycling with 
energy optimization and cost reduction strategies makes DESs a trans -
formative technology for sustainable biomass fractionation, which has 
the potential to transform biorefinery operations.
Research priorities include developing low-vapor-pressure DES to 
simplify recycling ( Bartling et al., 2021 ), integrating AI for solvent 
formulation optimization ( Ruengsrichaiya et al., 2024 ), and leveraging 
lignin-derived DES to close the biomass valorization loop ( Magalh ˜aes 
et al., 2022 ). These advances could position DES as a cornerstone of 
sustainable biorefineries, aligning with global decarbonization goals 
( Ruengsrichaiya et al., 2024 ).

9. Economic viability and scale-up challenges of biomass 
fractionation using DES
The market has adopted DES as a new green biomass fractionation 
approach that combines unique benefits from conventional pretreat -
ment systems but faces major obstacles for industrial deployment 
( Cheng et al., 2022 ). The economic feasibility of biomass processing 
with DES depends heavily on three essential parameters, which include 
solvent recovery performance, energy usage, and production scalability 
( Bartling et al., 2021 ). The techno-economic evaluation shows that DES 
pretreatment exhibits technical effectiveness through superior deligni -
fication and improved enzymatic digestion, yet commercial adoption 
requires significant improvements to process design and cost optimiza -
tion ( Preethi and R Banu, 2023 ).
Multiple economic factors impact the market competitiveness of 
DES-based biomass fractionation systems when compared to traditional 
pretreatment systems. Pretreatment processes consume more than 40% 
of biomass processing expenses, so cost reduction remains vital for 
establishing commercial operations. The techno-economic assessments 
demonstrate that DES pretreatment costs between $28 and $33 per ton 
of biomass, but the expenses differ based on processing intensity and 
biomass feedstock properties ( Preethi and R Banu, 2023 ).
The main elements that determine the cost profile of DES-based 
systems include raw material procurement costs for solvent prepara -
tion, together with heating requirements, separation needs, and solvent 
recovery expenses ( Li et al., 2023b ). The economic challenges of 
implementing DESs at scale exist despite their favorable raw material 
costs because they are nature-derived materials. The renewable nature 
of various DES components provides cost benefits, especially when they 
originate from biomass-derived compounds in closed-loop biorefinery 
systems ( Rachiero et al., 2022 ).
Multiple economic assessments exist regarding DES pretreatment 
compared to standard methods of pretreatment. DES pretreatment sys -
tems require higher costs during processing than both dilute acid pre -
treatment and ammonia fiber explosion (AFEX) pretreatment systems, 
according to research ( Singh et al., 2015 ). DES systems generate lower 
environmental effects than dichloromethane and ethyl acetate, but 
create greater environmental impact than methanol and ethanol, ac -
cording to LCA ( Zaib et al., 2022 ).
The economic value of DES pretreatment increases because it gen -
erates high-quality lignin co-products that increase total biorefinery 
profits. DES-fractionated lignin contains valuable β-O-4 structures that 
preserve between 31.82% and 59.06% of their structure, which makes 
them suitable for high-value applications ( Cheng et al., 2022 ). The 
economic viability of DES-based biorefineries strongly depends on suc -
cessful co-product valorization strategies.
DES-based biomass fractionation capital expenditure (CAPEX) costs 
depend heavily on the process design, along with the facility size. A 
recent assessment shows DES fractionation facilities operating at 
220,000 tons per year require € 105–145 million in capital expenditures, 
while kraft facilities need € 111 million. The main reason for high capital 
expenditures lies in reactor systems, which need a special design to 
manage DES viscosity and corrosion properties according to ( Bartling 
et al., 2021 ).
DES system performance depends largely on two key factors, which 
are the efficiency of sugar yield production and the enhancement of 
enzymatic hydrolysis. The enzymatic hydrolysis process following DES 
pretreatment produces glucose yields between 90% and 99% while 
untreated biomass yields remain significantly lower. Better sugar re -
covery efficiency leads to improved economic outcomes because it en -
ables higher production levels of bioethanol and biochemicals ( Kim 
et al., 2020 ).
The economic feasibility of DES fractionation depends heavily on the 
successful implementation of continuous processing systems. Contin -
uous operation of DES fractionation has been successfully demonstrated 
in pilot-scale settings, although researchers continue to work on 
enhancing liquid-to-solid ratios and residence times. The LigniSep pro -
cess, along with other innovative separation technologies, demonstrates 
potential for better economic performance through continuous lignin 
precipitation ( Smit et al., 2022 ).
Several basic technical hurdles must be overcome for the industrial 
development of DES-based biomass fractionation systems. The viscosity 
increase of DES during biomass dissolution poses the greatest technical 
challenge since it negatively affects mass transfer rates and mixing ef -
ficiency. Industrial-scale reactors need to handle rheological changes to

Large-scale engineering operations for solvent recovery, along with

The industrial adoption of DES presents substantial opportunities for 
sustainable processing because they offer environmentally friendly so -
lutions. The transition process encounters various obstacles during its 
implementation. The unique characteristics of DESs require industrial 
equipment designers to create specific management systems for their 
high viscosity and potential corrosive nature. The degradation of ChCl- 
based DESs at elevated temperatures produces trimethylamine as a 
harmful byproduct, which demonstrates the requirement for properly 
designed safe systems. DESs gain popularity because they are inexpen -
sive and non-toxic, and biodegradable, which makes them suitable for 
green technology development despite existing safety concerns. The 
technology demonstrates excellent capabilities for processing plant 
biomass and for minimizing sulfur-based pollutants in fuel products. 
Laboratory and pilot-scale research advancements indicate DESs will 
become essential components for developing sustainable industrial 
systems, which benefit human populations and environmental health 
through continued safety and sustainability research.
A sophisticated control system is necessary for implementing DES- 
biomass interactions at the industrial level because of their complex 
nature. The optimization of temperature, pressure, and residence time 
becomes more difficult during larger-scale operations because of heat 
transfer constraints and reaction kinetic differences. The diverse 
composition of lignocellulosic biomass makes process control more 
difficult because of its heterogeneous characteristics ( Logaru ˇsi ´c et al., 
2024; Antunes et al., 2019 ).
The deployment of DES technology into current biorefinery opera -
tions requires handling both positive and negative aspects that arise 
from integration efforts. The addition of DES pretreatment systems to 
existing pulp mills becomes less capital-intensive but requires thorough 
evaluation of process compatibility and material handling systems. The 
high absorptivity of biomass materials generates difficulties in transport 
because it leads to pipe erosion, as well as clogging and mechanical wear 
( Antunes et al., 2019 ).
The environmental trade-offs of DES-based biomass processing 
become complicated when analyzed through LCA methods. The envi -
ronmental sustainability of DES depends on its specific chemical 
composition, together with the design of the processing system. The 
environmental effects of DES systems based on ChCl and citric acid 
become more significant because of elevated water usage and 
fermentation-generated CO ₂ emissions ( Song et al., 2023; Zaib et al., 
2022; Nejrotti et al., 2022; del Mar Contreras-G ´amez et al., 2023 ).
The environmental benefits of DES pretreatment consist of minimal 
vapor pressure, which reduces atmospheric contamination risks as well 
as potential biodegradability. DES systems demonstrate environmental 
risks because they generate LC ₅₀ (lethal concentration, causes 50% 
mortality) values between 10²-10 ³ mg/L when exposed to aquatic or -
ganisms. The evaluation of environmental impact must encompass both 
the DES lifecycle stages, which include production and disposal 
( Rachiero et al., 2022; Nejrotti et al., 2022; del Mar Contreras-G ´amez 
et al., 2023 ).
The integration of DES technology within sustainable biorefinery 
frameworks needs to perform a complete sustainability assessment of 
economic, environmental, and social elements. DES-based biorefineries 
demonstrate improved efficiency and lower energy usage when 
compared to traditional approaches based on techno-economic and LCA 
studies ( del Mar Contreras-G ´amez et al., 2023 ).
Many DES components derive from renewable sources, which follow 
circular economy principles when biomass-derived compounds are used 
for preparing DES for biomass processing. The closed-loop system en -
ables improved economic and environmental results because it mini -
mizes both external chemical inputs and waste generation. The overall 
sustainability benefits rely on achieving successful scale-up along with 
an optimized recovery process ( Rachiero et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2020; 
del Mar Contreras-G ´amez et al., 2023 ).
The development of efficient solvent recovery systems represents an 
essential research priority for DES-based biomass fractionation, together 
with the optimization of process integration methods and commercial- 
scale facility economic modeling. Research on new DES formulations 
with better thermal properties and decreased viscosity addresses major 
scale-up obstacles, according to ( Li et al., 2023b; Bartling et al., 2021; 
Logaru ˇsi ´c et al., 2024; del Mar Contreras-G ´amez et al., 2023 ).
The validation of commercial viability and industrial partnership 
attraction needs funding for pilot-scale demonstration facilities. The 
400-liter reactor systems operated by Fraunhofer CBP serve as valuable 
demonstration tools for scale-up, yet require further development for 
commercial application, according to ( Smit et al., 2022 ).
DES commercialization depends on step-by-step development by 
joining existing biorefineries instead of building completely new facil -
ities. The use of DES pretreatment for retrofitting current pulp mills and 
bioethanol facilities presents affordable entry points for operational 
experience development ( Antunes et al., 2019 ).
The development of DES technology needs strategic collaborations 
between research teams and experienced biomass processors to resolve 
scaling issues and achieve industrial market readiness. Standardization 
of process designs and equipment specifications helps speed up indus -
trial adoption by reducing capital costs through scaled production.
DESs show potential as an advanced biomass fractionation method 
that promises better environmental results while improving the eco -
nomic efficiency of biorefineries. Laboratory and pilot-scale demon -
stration of DES technology does not guarantee commercial success 
because substantial improvements are needed in process optimization, 
equipment design, and economic performance ( Cheng et al., 2022; 
Bartling et al., 2021; del Mar Contreras-G ´amez et al., 2023 ).
DES-based biomass fractionation becomes economically viable when 
the process optimizes solvent recovery and minimizes energy usage 
while establishing profitable co-product markets. Techno-economic 
studies demonstrate that DES systems have commercial potential 
against traditional pretreatment methods only through extensive pro -
cess improvements and scale optimization ( Li et al., 2023b; Bartling 
et al., 2021; Preethi and R Banu, 2023; Zhang et al., 2022 ).
The extensive scale-up challenges for DES technology demand 
concentrated research with strategic industry partnerships and staged 
commercial deployment strategies to overcome these barriers. DES- 
based biomass fractionation can become a key technology for sustain -
able biorefinery development through the successful resolution of these 
challenges, according to ( del Mar Contreras-G ´amez et al., 2023 ).

determinants of economic uncertainty. The influence of the cost, con -
sumption rate, and recycle ratios of the components of DES and auxiliary 
solvents (acetone and LA) is found to have a substantial influence on the 
minimum selling prices, often dominating the influence of the cost of the 
feedstocks. This has been the case for bio-based fuel as well as bio-based 
chemical conversion processes and hence reveals the importance of 
solvent utilization as a major bottleneck in the commercialization route. 
Additionally, the efficiency of enzymatic hydrolysis has been found to 
have a substantial influence on the yields as well as economics, thereby 
indicating the importance of the correlated influences of upstream 
treatment efficacies and downstream conversion costs.
The relevance of solvent recovery can also be validated by the cel -
lulose nanocrystal production case study, in which the recovery of DES 
had a significant impact on economic parameters. Although the TEA 
calculation in this research, along with those done in the biorefinery 
case studies, explicitly show the effectiveness of TEA in evaluating the 
importance of economic viability in the context of DES-based applica -
tions, the TEA calculations in this case only show the relevance of sol -
vent recovery in economic viability ( Putranto et al., 2025 ). It thus 
explicitly reveals the importance of solvent recovery not only in the 
context of environmental sustainability but also in the context of eco -
nomic viability in the context of DES-based applications. In combina -
tion, the TEA calculations show the economic viability of DES-based 
biomass conversion processes, in which high solid content processes and 
the importance of solvent recovery would play important roles in 
commercializing the technology.
10. Conclusion
The review evaluates DESs for biomass conversion through advanced 
computational methods, which reveal their fundamental operational 
mechanisms. Our research combines molecular dynamics simulations 
with DFT calculations and machine learning models to study DES in -
teractions through hydrogen bonding and van der Waals forces and 
proton transfer mechanisms. The molecular-level analysis serves as the 
essential framework to understand experimental results and forecast 
solvent behavior while creating new DES formulations for specific 
applications.
The simulation results about bond cleavage kinetics, lignin-unit in -
teractions, and solvent transport properties help solve the main chal -
lenges of viscosity and mass transfer and selective fractionation at 
industrial scales. The existing challenges in industrial deployment, 
including solvent recycling and energy-intensive recovery and equip -
ment design, can be solved through computational modeling tools. The 
discovery of low-viscosity high-performance DESs depends on MD and 
ML models to optimize process parameters virtually before experimental 
testing.
The complete realization of DES potential depends on implementing 
multiscale modeling, which connects quantum mechanics to process 
simulation. The review establishes a computational framework that both 
summarizes current DES research and outlines essential steps for 
developing sustainable biorefineries that will use DESs as transformative 
agents.
