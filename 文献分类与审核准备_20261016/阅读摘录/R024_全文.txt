Abstract
Modern technology research is more focused on renewable, sustainable, and affordable clean energy (SDG 7). Biomass, is
one of the promising renewable and sustainable sources of energy, owing to its abundant availability and process simplicity.
In this work, carbonaceous material processed from waste biomass was used as an electrode material in supercapacitors for
energy storage. Porous-activated carbon was prepared from wasteArtocarpus hirsutus seeds through chemical activation with
phosphoric acid as an activating agent. The chemically activated carbon was further doped with a combination of heteroatoms
N, Si, and Ti through a solvothermal process to enhance the electrical conductivity of the material. The doped electrode
material showed outstanding electrochemical performance with a speciﬁc capacitance of 974.6 F g
− 1 at a current density of
1Ag − 1 in a three-electrode system. The asymmetric supercapacitor device exhibited an energy density of 27 Wh kg− 1,ap o w e r
density of 2700 W kg − 1, and excellent cycling stability of 94.5% initial capacitance retention after 5000 charge–discharge
cycles at a current density of 1 A g − 1. Thus, the biomass-derived carbonaceous material is a capable energy storage material
for supercapacitor applications.

1 Introduction
Energy is an essential resource globally. During the past
few decades, there is an ever rise in global energy demand
with a growing population and technological development
[1]. Due to the alarming scarcity of fossil fuel, there is
an urgent need to focus on natural energy resources, such
as wind, solar, tidal, hydro, and other such sources that
are indeed eco-friendly and clean [ 2]. However, the all-
time availability of energy produced by the above renewable
sources is unfeasible [ 3]. Therefore, moving toward efﬁ-
cient energy storage devices has become predominantly
important. Biomass materials that are considered waste
can be effectively used to synthesize carbonaceous mate-
rial for storing energy in batteries, supercapacitors, solar
cells, and fuel cells [ 4]. Supercapacitors (SCs) are efﬁ-
cient energy storage devices due to their long cycle life,
high power density, fast charge–discharge, and a good
range of operating conditions [ 5]. Based on the storage
mechanism, SCs are classiﬁed into electrical double-layer
capacitors (EDLCs), pseudocapacitors, hybrid supercapaci-
tors, and battery-type capacitors [6]. EDLCs that work on the
double-layer capacitance mechanism store electrical energy
at the electrode–electrolyte interface with applied voltage,
and have high power density, long cycle life, and high
cycle efﬁciency [ 7]. They are commercially used in mem-
ory backup devices, hybrid vehicles, and aerospace ﬁelds
[8].
Activated carbon (AC), carbon nanotubes, graphene, car-
bon aerogels and carbon xerogels, carbon ﬁbers, carbon
nanoﬁbers, carbon black, and mesoporous carbon are various
carbonaceous materials that can be used in SCs applica-
tions due to their high surface area, electrical conductivity,
and chemical stability. However, AC is favorable compara-
tively due to its cost-effectiveness, high surface area, tunable
pore structure, scalability, commercial availability, versatil-
ity in precursor selection, chemical stability, and ease of
processing [ 9]. Biomass-derived AC is one of the pioneer
materials that has been used as an electrode to facilitate
energy storage in EDLCs [ 10]. AC is employed as an elec-
trode material in SCs on account of its large surface area,
high porosity, chemical inertness, and better thermal sta-
bility [ 11]. It is synthesized either by physical or chemical
activation method, but the chemical activation method pro-
duces a higher speciﬁc surface area carbonaceous material
comparatively. In physical activation, the biomass is initially
carbonized at low carbonization temperature followed by
elevated temperature conditions in an inert gas atmosphere;
whereas chemical activation requires a chemical activating
agent and process conditions without oxygen content [ 12].
Pelin et al. prepared AC from hazelnut shell waste by single-
step phosphoric acid activation. This resulted in AC with a
speciﬁc surface area of 1363 m
2 g− 1 and a speciﬁc capac-
itance of 247.8 F g − 1 at 0.75 A g − 1 [13]. However, the
major drawback of using porous AC as electrode is their
low electrical conductivity with high resistance, less active
sites, reduced energy density, self-discharge, limited operat-
ing voltage, and rate capability. To overcome these issues,
the AC is doped, codoped, and combined to form com-
posites which will improve the overall electrochemical and
physicochemical properties [ 14]. From the literature, it is
evident that doping the AC with nitrogen (N) will provide
additional charge carriers that improve the electrical con-
ductivity of the AC facilitating faster charge–discharge rates
in SCs. This also introduces redox-active sites that help in
enhancing the capacitance and improving the overall perfor-
mance. Titania (Ti) doping contributes to the electrochemical
stability of the SC reducing the degradation of the elec-
trode material over repeated charge–discharge cycles. Silica
(Si) doping prevents the agglomeration of carbon particles,
optimizes the pore structure, and enhances the thermal sta-
bility of the AC. Doping with Ti or Si extends the operating
voltage window of the SCs. Table 1 shows the enhanced
electrochemical performance of AC when doped or codoped
with heteroatoms, such as N, S, O, Ti, Si, etc., to increase
the charge-storage capacity by forming surface functional
groups, inducing defects, and optimizing the carbon struc-
ture [15, 16].
Moreover, dual or multiple heteroatom doping is much
more efﬁcient than single doping due to the synergy effect
of the combined heteroatoms. Thus, doping of heteroatom
enhances the capacitive energy storage by increasing the
electroactive sites, improving electrical conductivity, tuning
pore size distribution, enhancing electrochemical stability,
tailoring redox properties, and extending the voltage win-
dow and pseudocapacitance [ 17]. Junkai et al. prepared N,
P codoped porous carbon with TiO
2 hybrid nanoparticle
through a sol–gel method that exhibited enhanced electro-
chemical performance of 687.3 mAh g − 1 at 0.1 A g− 1 [18].
Luyu Ji et al. synthesized porous carbon from chitosan using

Despite extensive studies on biomass-derived AC for
SCs, carbon materials derived from Artocarpus hirsutus
(AH) seeds have not yet been explored for electrochemical
energy storage applications. In this work, biomass-derived
porous activated carbon (PAC)-based electrode materials
are synthesized from waste AH seeds, through a chemi-
cal activation method, with H
3PO4 as a chemical activating
agent with carbonization at a relatively moderate temper-
ature (650 °C) is adopted, enabling the formation of a
hierarchically porous carbon structure while reducing energy
consumption compared to conventional high-temperature
KOH activation routes. Then, combined heteroatom dop-
ing was performed with N, Si, and Ti on chemically
activated carbon using a single-step solvothermal method.
Simultaneous doping of heteroatoms on the AC matrix
results in synergistic effects that enhance the overall electro-
chemical performance of the SC by introducing additional
electrochemically active sites and improving structural sta-
bility. The characterizations for physical, chemical, and
electrochemical properties were carried out to analyze the
performance of the prepared carbonaceous material on
supercapacitive energy storage. The overall electrochem-
ical performance of the biomass-derived doped carbona-
ceous electrode material exhibited excellent energy storage
capacity, thus contributing a promising energy storage mate-
rial.
2 Experimental Section
2.1 Materials
The Artocarpus hirsutus (AH) seeds were collected from
the Southern part of Tamil Nadu, India. Phosphoric
acid extrapure (H
3PO4, ≥ 99.9%), melamine ≥ 99.9%,
tetraethoxysilane 99.9%, titanium dioxide 99.9%, and
ethanol 99.9% Lab Reagent (LR). All the chemicals used
in the experiment are of Analytical Grade and were pur-
chased from Sigma-Aldrich. Double-distilled water that is
free of dissolved salts was used throughout the experi-
ment.
2.2 Preparation of Carbonaceous Electrode Material
The waste AH seeds were collected from the agricultural
ﬁeld and washed thoroughly with double-distilled water to
remove the dirt and sand particles. It was then sun-dried for
a week, followed by oven drying at 150 °C for complete
removal of moisture content. The seeds were subjected to
chemical activation followed by a doping process.
For chemical activation, the dirt and moisture-free size-
reduced seeds were mixed with H
3PO4 at an impregnation
ratio of 1:1 and sealed completely; it is then kept undis-
turbed overnight. As shown in Fig. 1, the soaked content
was transferred to an air-tight container and carbonized at a
temperature of 650 °C for 1 h at inert condition in a hori-
zontal tubular furnace [45] (Heating rate: 5 °C min− 1). After
cooling down to room temperature, the material was washed
thoroughly with distilled water to attain a neutral pH of 7. The
obtained phosphoric acid-activated porous activated carbon
was named HPAC.
To carry out doping and composite formation, HPAC was
mixed with titanium dioxide and melamine of equal weight
ratio. Then, tetraethoxysilane was added to the mixture, and
ethanol was added as the solvent. The entire content was
transferred into an autoclave and maintained at a temperature
of 140 °C for 10 h. Thus, doped phosphoric acid-activated
porous activated carbon was obtained and named DHPAC.

2.4 Electrochemical Measurements
The electrochemical measurements were carried out in
Origaﬂex-OGF 500, a three-electrode system with 6 M KOH
as electrolyte. The electrode material to be analyzed was pre-
pared by mixing the active material (HPAC and DHPAC) with
carbon black and polyvinylidene ﬂuoride at a ratio of 8:1:1
and it was made into a slurry using the solvent N-methyl-2-
pyrrolidone. The as-prepared mixed slurry was coated on
the pretreated Ni foam (1 cm × 1c m )a n dd r i e di na n

electrode along with Ag/AgCl as reference electrode and Pt
wire as counter electrode for the three-electrode system. The
electrochemical performance of the as-prepared porous acti-
vated carbon material was analyzed to determine the cyclic
voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge/discharge (GCD),
and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) at room
temperature.

3.2.4 N
2 Adsorption–Desorption Isotherm
The BET method evaluates the speciﬁc surface area of porous
and ﬁnely divided materials through well-deﬁned adsorp-
tion–desorption isotherm [ 64]. The hysteresis loop in Fig. 8
of HPAC and DHPAC conﬁrms the presence of micro-
mesoporous carbons which looks similar to the Type IV
isotherm curve with the H3 type of hysteresis loop [ 65]. The
physisorption ﬁlling of the micropore occurs at a low relative
pressure (P/P
o ≈ 0.01–0.15 at N 2 adsorption atmosphere at
77 K). From the BET isotherms, it is evident that the HPAC
samples have high micropore ﬁlling comparatively. The plot-
ted N
2 adsorption curves begin at relatively higher pressures
(> 0.05) due to a combination of restricted pore accessibility
and kinetic constraints due to narrow micropores, which are
common for biomass-derived ACs. The initial uptake region
appears at slightly different relative pressures for HPAC and
DHPAC, which arises from doping-induced modiﬁcations in
the pore structure. The surface functional groups and het-
eroatom dopants in DHPAC modify the surface polarity and
pore entrance environment. These dopants partially block or
narrow micropore entrances and increase adsorption energy
heterogeneity, thereby delaying the uptake of N
2 molecules
to enter the pores for DHPAC, resulting in an apparent shift

4 Conclusions
In this paper, waste AH seeds biomass-derived porous acti-
vated carbon electrode material was synthesized through
a chemical activation method. Then, the heteroatom dop-
ing of chemically activated carbon HPAC by a single-step
solvothermal process with N, Si, and Ti to produce DHPAC
was carried out. Physical, chemical, and electrochemical
characterizations were performed to evaluate the electro-
chemical performance of the as-synthesized carbonaceous
electrode material, and the following conclusions were
arrived.
1. TGA analysis reveals that the synthesized material has
excellent thermal stability with a carbonization temper-
ature of 620 °C. FTIR depicts the change in surface
functional groups due to temperature and chemical
agents. XPS analysis reveals the presence of C 1 s,O
1s,S i2 p,N1 s, and Ti 2 p with 60.2%, 23.5%, 11.9%,
3.3%, and 1.1%, respectively.
2. SEM analysis shows the honeycomb-type pores with
interconnected pore clusters for HPAC electrode mate-
rials. FESEM depicts the even dispersion of N, Ti, and
Si elements on DHPAC that enhances the pseudocapaci-
tance. EDAX analysis discloses the percentage of N, Si,
and Ti dopants with 4.17%, 14.3%, and 3.3%, respec-
tively, in the DHPAC electrode material.
3. XRD patterns prove the amorphous nature of the syn-
thesized carbon. Raman analysis shows a low degree
of graphitization, proving minimal defects in carbon,
and which improves on doping. BET analysis shows
that HPAC and DHPAC exhibit speciﬁc surface areas of
587 m
2 g− 1 and 373 m 2 g− 1, respectively.
4. The speciﬁc capacitance of the DHPAC and HPAC
measured in the three-electrode system is 974.6 F g − 1
and 105 F g − 1, respectively. The Ohmic resistance and
charge-transfer resistance are comparatively low and
absent in DHPAC.

5. The DHPAC//HPAC asymmetric supercapacitor device
exhibited an energy density of 27 Wh kg − 1, power den-

94.5% up to 5000 cycles. The combination of long charge
and quick discharge is suitable in high-power electronics
and energy recovery systems where the energy release is
critical.
Hence, it is evident that decorating heteroatoms on the
surface of the electrode material enhances the overall elec-
trochemical performance, thus contributing to a promising
energy storage material that is renewable, sustainable, and
reliable.
