ABSTRACT: Activated carbon (AC) materials were developed via a single-step method from three different biowaste sources and tailored 
for application in carbon dioxide (CO
2
) capture and electrochemical energy storage, combining the concept of biowaste valorisation with 
sustainable environmental and energy applications. This work investigated the use of three underexplored agro-food waste streams as 
precursors, namely flour mill waste (BW1), old corn waste (BW2), and feed waste (BW3), and performed a systematic comparison of their 
conversion under the same activation conditions, as well as characteristics and performance. The developed AC materials demonstrated 
properties such as high graphitization, favorable pore-structure configuration and surface area, and favorable surface characteristics. CO
2 
adsorption experiments at 298 K showed a capacity of 2.55 mmol/g at 1 bar with stability over multiple cycles for AC2, as well as high CO
2
/ 
N
2 
selectivity, reaching 48 at 1 bar (ideal adsorbed solution theory (IAST)) for a 15:85 v/v CO
2
/N
2 
mixture for AC1, suggesting a high 
selective affinity for CO
2 
capture at ambient conditions. Furthermore, electrochemical experiments revealed stable performance as electric 
double-layer capacitors as a result of low solution and charge-transfer resistance, demonstrating capacitive performance in the order of 250 F/g 
at a scan rate of 5 mV/s for AC3, which was tested at different increasing scan rates and determined to retain a capacitance value higher than 
90 F/g at a high scan rate of 100 mV/s. Galvanostatic charge−discharge (GCD) investigations revealed virtually symmetric charge−discharge 
curves with an acceptable rate capability for a symmetric AC3 full cell, retaining ∼70% of initial capacitance despite a 4-fold increase in current 
density, and preserving ∼78% of initial capacitance after 1000 cycles. These results reveal the potential of agro-industrial waste streams- 
derived AC materials for selective carbon capture and electrochemical energy storage, enabling them a simple, potentially scalable, and 
sustainable material platform for such applications. The key contribution is the processing of three previously underutilized agro-food 
industrial biomass wastes into functional AC materials for energy storage and CO
2 
capture via a single-step KOH activation approach, 
allowing for direct comparison and correlation of precursor chemistry. Furthermore, this provides a solid basis for future precursor-specific 
tuning of KOH loading, activation temperature and time, pore structure, and surface functioning to optimize properties and performance. 
KEYWORDS: biomass waste and upcycling, activated carbon and functional materials, CO
2 
capture and adsorption, adsorbents, super-
capacitor and energy storage

electrochemical supercapacitor systems, because of the avail-
ability of various source materials and the highly favorable 
physicochemical properties of the resulting products.
2,3

ally, agricultural waste generation exceeds 1 billion tonnes

Recent reviews have highlighted that biomass- and waste-

25

On the other hand, supercapacitors, also known as electro-
chemical capacitors, are energy storage devices with high power 
density, cyclic performance, and fast charge−discharge rates, 
making them suitable options for applications requiring rapid 
energy delivery, such as electric vehicles, portable electronics, 
and renewable energy systems.
29,30 
Yet, developing cost- 
effective, high-performance supercapacitors that can meet ris-
ing energy demands remains a challenge. AC is one of the most 
popular materials for supercapacitor electrodes due to its large 
surface area, electrical conductivity, and favorable electro-
chemical characteristics.
31 
Based on this, using biomass as a 
precursor, efforts have been made to generate AC exhibiting 
improved supercapacitor performance while reducing the en-

32,33 
For ex-

34 
Titania-supported AC produced from Oleander

35 
A nitrogen self-doped AC was synthesized

37 
Nitrogen-doped carbon derived from tea seed shells via

functional groups.

Pollen typhae derived porous carbon

Despite extensive research into 
biomass-derived AC materials for CO
2 
capture and electro-
chemical energy storage applications, the majority of previous 
reports focus on a single biomass precursor or application, and 
the synthesis and evaluation conditions frequently differ sig-
nificantly between studies. This makes it difficult to distinguish 
between the effects of precursor chemistry and activation pa-
rameters on porosity, surface functionality, and performance. 
Thus, this work was designed to develop a multifunctional 
material from three different underutilized industrial agro-food 
waste through a simple, scalable, and single-step synthesis under 
identical conditions, while simultaneously achieving effective 
CO
2 
adsorption and electrochemical energy storage. Further-
more, there is still a lack of understanding regarding the direct 
comparison and correlation of precursor chemistry, as well as 
the connections between synthesis conditions, pore 
architecture, surface chemistry, CO
2 
affinity, and charge-storage 
behavior in such dual-function systems. Addressing these re-
search gaps is critical for the development of sustainable ma-
terials that integrate carbon capture and energy storage into an 
integrated platform. 
Along these lines, agro-food industrial biowaste-based AC 
materials with dual functionality to act as a CO
2 
capturing 
adsorbent and electrochemical supercapacitor electrode were 
developed in this work, enabling an economical and sustainable 
material platform for such applications. AC was produced by 
upcycling three different agro-food industrial biowaste sources, 
namely flour mill waste, old corn waste, and feed waste, under 
the same synthesis conditions. The physicochemical, morpho-
logical, and textural characteristics of the produced AC materials 
were thoroughly assessed. Then, CO
2 
capture performance was 
tested in ambient conditions, and selectivity against N
2 
was 
evaluated experimentally as well as via ideal adsorbed solution 
theory (IAST) predictions. Subsequently, their electrochemical 
performance was investigated utilizing half-cell and symmetric 
full-cell experiments in 2 M KOH under different conditions, 
focusing on specific capacitance, electrical resistance, and cyclic 
performance evaluation. 
2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION 
2.1. Materials 
Agthia group PJSC (Abu Dhabi) provided three types of biowaste 
materials, namely flour mill waste (BW1), old corn waste (BW2), and 
feed waste (BW3). Before KOH impregnation, the precursors were 
dried in an oven at 100 °C for 24 h, pulverized with a powder grinder, 
and sieved to obtain particles <250 μm. Potassium hydroxide (KOH, 
99.99%) and N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, 99.5%) were obtained 
from Sigma-Aldrich. Air Products (UAE) supplied N
2 
and CO
2 
gases 
with purity of 99.999%. 
2.2. Synthesis of Activated Carbon 
The three biowaste samples, namely, BW1, BW2, and BW3, were used 
as starting materials to synthesize three AC materials named AC1, AC2, 
and AC3, respectively, using a single-step approach. First, a 1:3 weight 
ratio solution of biomass and KOH was prepared using 75 mL of DI 
water. To achieve homogeneous impregnation, the solution was stirred 
at room temperature for 24 h, followed by 12 h drying at 110 °C. After 
that, the samples were activated at 700 °C for 1 h with N
2 
flow. The 
samples were then thoroughly washed with DI water until the su-
pernatant achieved a neutral pH (∼7), ensuring the removal of any 
residual KOH. Finally, they were dried at 110 °C for 12 h under vacuum 
to obtain the activated carbon products. Figure 1 shows an outline of 
the synthesis process. In this investigation, all three underutilized agro-

conducting domains, favorable pore connectivity with major 
microporosity and balanced mesoporosity, and improved 
electrode−electrolyte interface. On the other hand, restricted 
ion routes and higher internal resistance may be the cause of the 
poorer electrochemical performance of AC1.
