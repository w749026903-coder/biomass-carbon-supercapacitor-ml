ABSTRACT: Currently, the primary bottlenecks that hinder the
widespread application of supercapacitors are low energy density
and narrow potential windows. Herein, the hybrid supercapacitor
with high energy density and wide potential window is constructed
via an in situ self-assembly method employing RGO-induced
flower-like MOF(Ni). Benefiting from the synergistic effect
between RGO and MOF(Ni), the interfacial interactions are
effectively improved, and the contact area with the electrolyte is
enhanced, which increases the ion transfer kinetics and overall
electrochemical performance. The MOF(Ni)@RGO electrode
exhibits a specific capacitance of 1267.73 F g
−1
at a current
density of 1 A g
−1
. Crucially, the assembled MOF(Ni)@RGO//
BC with a broad potential window and good stability employing a
MOF(Ni)@RGO anode and biomass carbon cathode, combined with a 2 M PVA-KOH gel-electrolyte, achieves a maximum energy
density of 70.16 Wh kg
−1
at a power density of 2200.09 W kg
−1
, outperforming most reported supercapacitors. This hybrid
supercapacitor exhibits excellent stability and high energy density, providing a novel strategy for further large-scale applications.
KEYWORDS: supercapacitors, induced, self-assembly, metal−organic framework, RGO
1. INTRODUCTION
Metal−organic frameworks (MOFs) represent a burgeoning
category of materials distinguished by metal clusters intricately
connected to organic ligands through coordinated bonds.
Remarkably, the meticulous choice of metal and organic
ligands during synthesis engenders the substantial latitude in
tailoring MOFs with diverse morphologies, sizes, and pore
architectures.
1−3
Consequently, MOFs hold significant poten-
tial for applications across various fields, including adsorption,
4
detection,
5
catalysis,
6−8
separation,
9,10
and energy storage.
11−13
The MOFs with high specific surface area, controllable
structure, and inherent pseudocapacitive sites make them
exceptionally promising contenders for deployment as
electrode materials in supercapacitors. Currently, two primary
strategies are employed for MOFs in supercapacitor electrodes:
(1) direct utilization of MOFs to store electrical energy via the
electrochemical double-layer capacitor mechanism, leveraging
their inner surfaces;
14,15
(2) MOFs employed as sacrificial
templates or precursors, facilitating the transformation into
final materials (e.g., metal-based hydroxides, oxide, sulfides,
and porous carbon materials) by a straightforward annealing
method.
16−19
Despite notable advancements in energy storage,
the challenges of insufficient conductivity, electrochemical
instability, and limited capacitance in MOFs persist as current
obstacles demanding resolution.
To address the challenges of MOFs, the research focus has
shifted to composites with carbon-based materials possessing
superior electrical conductivity and structural stability (such as
carbon nanotubes, graphene oxide (GO), reduced graphene
oxide (RGO), and metal carbides).
20−24
Therefore, the GO
has attracted great interest in supercapacitor composites. Xiao
et al.
25
fabricated a GM-LEG@Ni-MOF hybrid electrode by
solvothermal methods, which exhibited a capacitance of 987.6
F g
−1
at a current density of 0.5 A g
−1
and a capacitance
retention of 85.6% after 3000 cycles. Additionally, an
asymmetric supercapacitor (GM-LEG@Ni-MOF//AC) was
assembled with GM-LEG@Ni-MOF and activated carbon
(AC) as the positive and negative electrodes, respectively. The
GM-LEG@Ni-MOF//AC energy density was 32.7 Wh kg
−1
at
a power density of 750 W kg
−1
. Zhang et al.
26
prepared NiO/
C/rGO by pyrolysis of the Ni-MOF precursor on GO. The
capacitance of the NiO/C/rGO hybrid electrode was 496 C
g
−1
at a current density of 1 A g
−1
. Furthermore, the hybrid
supercapacitor composed of NiO/C/rGO and sodium citrate-

Despite RGO with a distinctive thin-layer structure, remark-
able electrical conductivity, robust mechanical properties, and
substantial specific surface area that can accelerate the
electron/ion transport and diffusion of the composite material,
the supercapacitors assembled with the prepared electrode
materials still suffer from narrow voltage window, suboptimal
cycling performance, and relatively low energy/power density.
Based on the existing issues of MOFs/GO composites and
drawing inspiration from cauliflower plants, which employ
their leaves not only for nutrient transport but also to protect
themselves from external damage. Herein, we design RGO-
induced flower-like MOF(Ni) in situ self-assembled compo-
sites (MOF(Ni)@RGO) with high electrical conductivity and
excellent electrochemical performance. This remarkable out-
come can be primarily ascribed to the synergistic effect of
RGO with MOF(Ni), effectively enhancing the interfacial
interaction and overall electrochemical performance. Further-
more, the substantial specific surface area and abundant pore
structure of MOF(Ni)@RGO contribute to an increased
contact area with the electrolyte, ensuring efficient charge
transport and storage and improving ion transport kinetics.
Notably, a hybrid supercapacitor is assembled with MOF(Ni)
@RGO and biomass carbon as the anode and cathode,
respectively. The supercapacitors have high energy/power
densities and good cycling stability under a wide potential
window.

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Synthesis and Characterization of MOF@RGO
Composites. As depicted in Figure 1a, the MOF(Ni)@RGO
composite can be conveniently prepared by a solvothermal
method. The morphology of MOF(Ni) is a spherical flower
cluster structure assembled by a flake (Figure 1b). Figure S1a,b
shows the SEM images of the MOF@RGO composites at
different preparation temperatures. The observation reveals
that the thickness of RGO-induced MOF(Ni) nanosheets
exhibits an initial increase followed by a subsequent decrease
with rising temperature, indicating that the nanosheet
thickness can be regulated by adjusting the temperature. The
electrochemical test results also further validate that the
experimental conditions of 180 °C are more advantageous for
both the reduction of GO and the formation of porous thin
nanosheets of MOF(Ni). To further refine the experimental
conditions, the solvothermal time is investigated. The SEM
image (Figure S1c,d) reveals that when the solvothermal time
is 12 h, RGO almost completely covers the MOF, hindering
the unfolding of the flower-like MOF(Ni). With an increase in
time to 36 h, the thickness of MOF(Ni) nanosheets notably
increases due to the prolonged duration, which is not
conducive to electron transport. The electrochemical perform-
ance test reveals that the solvothermal time of 24 h is the most
suitable, which shows that the self-assembly induction of
flower-like MOF nanosheets via RGO is affected by the time of
MOF nucleation and growth.

poor stability at high temperatures. Herein, the prepared

3.2. Electrochemical Characterization. In Figure 4a and
Figure S7a, the CV curves of the prepared samples exhibit a

electrode materials possess battery-type characteristics. As
displayed in Figures S7a and S8a, CV curves and GCD plots of
MOF(Ni) at different temperatures and reaction times find
that the sample at 180 °C and 24 h has the largest integral area,
indicating that the MOF(Ni) prepared under this condition
has the best performance. Further, for a compound with RGO
(Figure 4a), it is found that the CV curve integral area of the
sample with 5% RGO is the largest, indicating that it has the
optimal capacitance performance. As illustrated in Figure 4b,
the plateaus that appear in the GCD plots further confirm that
the prepared electrodes have battery characteristics. Further-

4. CONCLUSIONS
In summary, an RGO-induced flower-like MOF(Ni) in situ
self-assembled electrode (MOF(Ni)@RGO) is designed
successfully via a simple, efficient, and low-cost one-step
solvothermal method. The semienclosed structure of RGO on
MOF(Ni), the RGO-induced MOF(Ni) ultrathin porous
nanosheet structure, the large specific surface area, and the
abundant mesoporous structure of the composite material
provide more charge transfer channels for the Faradaic redox
reaction and effectively reduce the resistance of the
composites. The MOF(Ni)@RGO-5% electrode can provide
a high specific capacitance of 1267.73 F g
−1
at a current density
of 1 A g
−1
, with good rate performance and cycling stability.
Surprisingly, further using MOF(Ni)@RGO-5% and biomass
carbon as the anode and cathode, we have developed an all-
solid-state hybrid supercapacitor (MOF(Ni)@RGO//BC)
with a high working voltage range of 0−2.2 V in a 2 M
PVA-KOH gel-electrolyte. The energy density of MOF(Ni)@
RGO//BC was 70.16 Wh kg
−1
at a power density of 2200.09
W kg
−1
. Additionally, even after 4000 cycles at a current
density of 10 A g
−1
, it exhibits high capacitance retention rate,
affirming its robust cycle life. They will open up the possibility
of numerous applications of MOF-based composites in hybrid
supercapacitors. Meanwhile, these findings also provide an
effective strategy for developing novel electrode materials that
meet the requirements of high operating voltage, high energy
density, and power density for energy storage devices.
