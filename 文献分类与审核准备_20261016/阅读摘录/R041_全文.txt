In the 21st century, the depletion of non-renewable energy
sources has driven the pursuit of sustainable, efficient, and
environmentally friendly energy conversion and storage devices
to meet the growing global demands. Electrochemical super-
capacitors have emerged as the leading choice owing to their
high energy density, long durability, and eco-friendly nature.
This review highlights the potential of nanomaterial-decorated
biomass-derived carbon (BDC) as an advanced electrode mate-
rial in high-performance supercapacitors (SCs). BDC is gener-
ally recognized as a good SC material. When nanomaterials,
including heteroatoms, metal compounds, and conducting poly-
mers, are introduced into BDC, they enhance their properties,
making it a compelling choice due to its renewable nature,
abundant availability, remarkable surface area, and excellent
electrochemical performance. Based on these advantages, this
study explores various synthesis methods and strategies to
optimize the specific power, durability, and electrochemical
efficiency of nanomaterial-decorated BDC, as BDC is a cost-
effective precursor. These attributes make BDC a promising
candidate for sustainable energy storage. Additionally, this
review addresses current challenges and proposes innovative
approachestoovercomethem,offeringnew directionsforfuture
research and industrial development. This work underscores the
role of nanomaterial-decorated BDC in advancing eco-friendly,
high-performance energy storage technologies for a sustainable
future.
1.Introduction
Researchers are attempting to develop novel materials in
terms of structures and formulations to achieve highly efficient
responses in energy storage devices.[1] Carbon is a promising
electrode material for supercapacitors and is commonly used
because of its intermittent electricity production. It is a sustain-
able, low-cost, high-response, and ecosystem-friendly material
device that can store energy. The primary carbon sources are
coal, plants, animal waste, and microorganisms. Owing to the
widespread availability, cost-effectiveness, and sustainability of
biomass in the environment, carbon is mainly obtained from
biomass. Tons of biomass waste are obtained from cultivation,
the food industry, and living stuff, from which carbon is pro-
duced in environmentally friendly ways to contribute to green
energy technologies.
Carbon materials exhibit high capacitance, making them
superior electrode materials for electrochemical supercapacitors.
However, they cannot be directly used in practical applications
because of their low energy densities.[1,2] This is why other atoms
and compounds are doped on the carbon surface to increase
the energy density while the power density remains unchanged
for the electrode material. Nanomaterials are suitable electrode
materials used to decorate carbon because they enhance the
faradic capacity or pseudocapacitance that boosts the overall

2.TypesofSupercapacitorsandTheirWorking
Principle
In electrochemical energy storage devices, both ions and elec-
trons participate in the galvanostatic charge-discharge process.
The energy stored in these systems follows two mechanisms.
The first mechanism is electrostatic energy storage, which
occurs when a dielectric medium separates two electrodes. The
second mechanism involves storing electrical energy derived
from chemical energy through a Faradaic process. Therefore,
based on their charge storage mechanisms, supercapacitors are
broadly divided into electric double-layer capacitors (EDLCs) and
pseudocapacitors (PCs), as shown in Figure1.[5] They also exhibit
a different kind of charge storage mechanism that combines
EDLC and pseudocapacitance called hybrid supercapacitors. Of
the three types of supercapacitors, the EDLC is the most
investigated and well-developed; hence, this type is the most
commercially used in practical applications.[22] Besides them, the
supercapacitors can also be sub-classified into two categories:
symmetric supercapacitors and asymmetric supercapacitors,
which are constructed on the cathode and anode.[2] If the same
cathode and anode materials are used, it is called a symmetric
supercapacitor. However, when utilizing different cathode and
anode materials, the resulting device is referred to as an
asymmetric supercapacitor.[6,23,24] The symmetric or asymmetric
supercapacitor may be prepared by following any of the above
mechanisms to accomplish superior specific capacitance and
high energy density devices.[8,25,26]
2.1. EDLCs
The primary mechanism of EDLCs is based on electrostatic forces
generated at the interfaces of electrical double layers. Therefore,
this process does not involve Faradaic (redox) reactions and is
called a capacitive or non-Faradaic process. In EDLCs, energy
storage occurs through surface-level ion adsorption from the
electrolyte, forming a bilayer of charges at the electrode-
electrolyte interface. Electrons and ions move in opposite direc-
tions during the charging and discharging processes. Electrons
are transferred from the cathode to the anode through an
external circuit during charging. Simultaneously, cations in the
electrolyte move toward the cathode (negative electrode), and
anions move toward the anode (positive electrode) to maintain
charge balance.[27] During discharging, the process reverses:
electrons flow from the anode to the cathode, and the ions
in the electrolyte move accordingly. Therefore, in EDLC-based
supercapacitors, the overall charge-discharge mechanism oper-
ates through these movements of electrons and ions without
involving chemical reactions.

2.3. Hybrid Supercapacitor
This type of supercapacitor is the amalgamation of mutually
double-layer-based supercapacitor and pseudocapacitor, which
encompasses two types of storage mechanisms, both faradic
and non-faradic mechanisms, to develop a high-energy-storage
supercapacitor with high specific power and stability is attained
for a large number of cycles. It includes two types of electrodes:
one is polarizable, and the other is a redox type or non-
polarizable electrode. They can be sub-classified as asymmetric
supercapacitors, symmetric supercapacitors, and composite-
type and battery-type supercapacitors. Carbon-based material
conductivity is significantly large and mostly delivers long-
term cyclic stability, with an efficiently high specific surface
area.[33,34] When carbon is decorated through a heteroatom, a
metal oxide, or an electronically conducting polymer that acts
as a redox-active material, it delivers a considerable quantity
of electrons, increasing the supercapacitor’s capacitance.[9,35,36]
Hybrid supercapacitors enhance the capacitance retention of the
supercapacitor with increasing cyclic stability.[23,37,38]

3.4. Cycle Life
The overall performance of a supercapacitor also depends on
the stability of the device, which is a crucial parameter. This
stability can be estimated by evaluating the life cycle using
CV and GCD responses. Carbon materials are among the most
capable electrode constituents for supercapacitors due to their
excellent cycling stability; this is because their charge storage
mechanism is highly reversible and involves physical adsorption
and desorption processes. In contrast, pseudocapacitive elec-
trode materials exhibit poor cyclic stability as chemical processes
decrease the material’s reversibility. The cyclic performance
of a supercapacitor is influenced by the choice of electrode
materials and electrolytes, the operating voltage, the working
temperature, and the frequency of charging and discharging
cycles.
3.5. Equivalent Series Resistance
Equivalent series resistance (ESR) is vital in optimizing a superca-
pacitor’s power output. ESR represents the sum of the intrinsic
resistances of the electrode materials and the electrolyte. Dif-

influencing the ESR and, consequently, the power performance.
Ideally, an electrolyte should possess both high ionic conduc-
tivity and a wide OPW. However, aqueous electrolytes offer
high ionic conductivity but a limited OPW. In contrast, organic
electrolytes and ionic liquids provide a wider OPW but have
lower ionic conductivity. This lower ionic conductivity leads

3.6. Self-Discharge Rate
Self-discharge can be defined as the decrease in a superca-

voltage because the charged electrodes lose electricity through
the outflow of current. When the self-discharge rate is very
high, the usage of these devices may be limited due to the
specific energy retention required. However, self-discharging
occurs mainly by these three mechanisms: 1) leakage of current,
2) the Faradic process, and 3) charge redistribution.[40]

6.2. Heteroatom Nanomaterials Decorated BDC for
Supercapacitor Application
In recent decades, the consumption of fossil fuels has increased
more rapidly, and as a result, the pollution level of the envi-
ronment has also increased. So, it is high time that the known
renewable energy storage devices were enhanced by improving
their energy and power density. Amongst diverse renewable
energy storage devices, supercapacitors provoked boundless
attention for storing energy due to several advantages of using
supercapacitors, such as better rechargeability than batteries
and other conventional capacitors. They also show a faster
charge transfer process. Generally, supercapacitor electrode
materials are mostly carbon-based because they are found easily
in the environment, the environment has a large stock of carbon,
their cost is very low, they are very friendly for the environment,
and they show significant electrical conductivity and a large
charging–discharging time from others. There are heteroatoms,
i.e., nitrogen, oxygen, sulfur, boron, nitrogen, oxygen, iron,
and nickel, used as dopants in BDC that increase supercapac-
itor power density, energy density, and capacitance retention.
Doping of heteroatoms mainly enhances the active surface
area, increases the conductivity, and enhances capacitance by
the contribution of pseudocapacitance. Heteroatom causes the
defect on the carbon surface, which causes an additional
reactive site. Heteroatom doping can change the hybridization
of carbon atoms, improve the wettability, and facilitate the redox

8.RecentChallengesandFuturePerspective
In the past few decades, as energy demand has increased
and consumption of non-renewable energy sources like fos-
sil fuels has rapidly grown, environmental pollution has also
escalated. Therefore, developing new, globally favorable, and
high-performing renewable energy storage systems is crucial.
Electrochemical supercapacitors are modern, newly developed,
sustainable energy conversion and storage devices with numer-
ous applications. High power density, long capacitance retention
(cyclic stability), low cost, and environmental friendliness make
them robust alternatives to batteries, fuel cells, and other
conventional devices. However, supercapacitors have extremely
lower specific energy compared to batteries, even though their
power density is high. Another disadvantage of these devices
is their working potential and cost, which can sometimes be
higher than batteries. If these problems, particularly the low
energy density, are addressed, supercapacitors could entirely
replace batteries. BDC materials have excellent supercapacitive
responses due to their sustainable nature, extensive availability,
affordability, and low-cost energy storage. In this review, it is
noted that incorporating nitrogen, sulfur, or other heteroatoms,
as well as metal oxides, hydroxides, carbides, or conducting
polymers, into the carbon structure improves conductivity and
electrochemical performance. Decorating the carbon structure
with multiple heteroatoms generates a synergistic effect and
enhances the overall supercapacitor performance. Biomass-
derived nitrogen-doped porous carbons can also be used in

For a better understanding of carbon obtained from waste
products or biomass, one must understand the relationship
between the composition of the composite and the charac-
teristic features of carbon atoms. To comprehend this rela-
tionship, researchers may focus on interlayer modulation and
self-doping, which are precise synthesis techniques for bet-
ter outcomes in preparing tailor-made electrode materials.
The capacitance of a supercapacitor mainly depends on the
prepared material’s surface area, composition, and porosity.
When the surface area of the prepared material is higher, its
specific capacitance value also increases. Many related studies
show that prepared materials containing porous carbon with
a larger surface area exhibit better capacitive properties in
the presence of the same electrolyte. Carbon with higher
surface area and porosity can be obtained from plants or plant
waste and is broadly used in fields such as drug delivery,
solar cells, and catalysis due to the requirement of high-
capacitance electrode materials in these areas. Commonly,
heteroatom doping enhances the material’s surface area, as
shown in this review. Innovative synthesis approaches, advanced
design strategies, and a clear understanding of the correla-
tion between the structures and properties of the prepared
materials are crucial steps for the further development of
supercapacitors.
Despite various advancements, there have been some major
challenges with nanomaterial-decorated BDC, which are summa-
rized below.
• The first challenge is to select a proper precursor. Precur-
sors can be from plant sources, animal sources, or from
microorganisms. To know the proper structures and chemical
characteristics of the precursors, a high-quality carbon can be
obtained.
• The production method of carbon must be cost-effective.
So, the preparation method selection is also an important
challenge to obtain cost-effective production of carbon.
• The carbonization procedure and activation process are also
major challenges for researchers to obtain a high-yield carbon
from biomass.
• The composite electrode selection for electrochemical super-
capacitors is another challenge, because the SCs energy
density depends on the electrode material most prominently.
• Another challenge is keeping the large surface area of BDC
constant while maintaining various pore size distributions.
• Since heteroatom doping is very advantageous for BDC for
enhancing the performance as a whole of supercapacitors, but
doping with S and O2-containing groups in large amounts can
demerit the performance, so it must be considered during
doping.
Since biomass carbon is obtained from natural sources
such as plants, algae, animals, or biowaste from water
treatment plants, leather waste, and other industrial waste,
the electrode materials derived from them are cost-effective,
which is significant for industrial-scale applications. Advances
in nanomaterial-decorated BDC materials for high-performance
SCs include large energy densities, mechanical stability, and
high cyclic stability, which are key factors for the mass
production of SCs in commercial-scale applications. The
development of nanomaterial-decorated BDC has enabled
advancements in energy storage devices, both renewable
and non-renewable, such as the combination of batteries and
supercapacitors, which can enhance the overall energy storage
capacity, enable rapid charging and discharging, and provide
excellent capacitance retention for prolonged cyclic stability.
To keep focusing on those challenges, future researchers
must focus on significant factors that could pave the way
for providing various electrode materials for supercapacitor
applications.
• Researchers need to concentrate on increasing the prepared
material’s overall surface area to overcome these devices’
primary challenge, increasing the specific energy value while
keeping the specific power constant high.
• To enhance the electrochemical performance, multiple pore
sizes, mesopores, macropores, and micropores should coexist.
However, excessive macroporosity can decrease electrolyte
diffusion and transfer efficiency, consequently decreasing
energy density, which is detrimental in practical supercapac-
itor applications.
• As discussed above, heteroatom-decorated carbon materials
can enhance the electrochemical response of a supercapacitor,
but functional groups containing sulfur and oxygen can
also decrease the material’s conductivity. Therefore, future
researchers must carefully select the type, quantity, and
method of doping heteroatoms into BDC materials.
• The component of the electrode material must have a signifi-
canttheoretical capacity thatimproves theperformanceof the
supercapacitor as well as the high structural stability of the
electrode materials.
• The researcher must focus on the environmental effect as well.
An effective clean energy supercapacitor will be obtained if it
can be combined with the disposal of waste from various SC
industries can promote an effective and feasible solution of
waste management, reduce CO2 emissions, and decrease the
energy crisis all over the world as well as can contribute to
global carbon management plans.
• Finally, future research must focus on the practical appli-
cation of nanomaterial-decorated BDC in supercapacitors.
Nanomaterials have nanostructures, such as tubes, rods, or
flower-like shapes, that enhance supercapacitor performance
by providing short diffusion paths for ions and electrons. This
indicates rapid charge transport and provides more active
sites for charge storage, mainly through Faradaic processes.
Therefore, the future synthesis of various nanostructured
electrode materials by varying their morphology and porosity
is required for constructing supercapacitors. Addressing these
challenges will allow nanomaterial-decorated BDC materials to
contribute significantly to advancing and preparing.
By overcoming these challenges, nanomaterial-decorated
BDC materials have a promising future in supercapacitor
applications, offering a sustainable and efficient alternative
energy storage device. For these reasons, nanomaterial-
decorated BDC is well-suited for practical applications in energy
storage.

9.Conclusions
With the impending depletion of non-renewable energy sources,
researchers are striving to develop more efficient energy conver-
sion and storage devices like supercapacitors, which offer large
capacitance retention over numerous cycles, high power density,
and superior durability. These promising properties make super-
capacitors more applicable than conventional capacitors, fuel
cells, and lithium-ion batteries. This review demonstrates that
nanomaterial-decorated BDC can be favorable electrode mate-
rials for supercapacitors. It also identifies the challenges associ-
ated with these materials and proposes strategies to overcome
them. The nanomaterials discussed include heteroatoms (such as
sulfur, nitrogen, and boron), metal oxides, carbides, nitrides, sul-
fides,and conductingpolymers.DopingBDCstructureswithhet-
eroatoms enhances the overall electrochemical performance of
supercapacitors by increasing electrical conductivity and pseu-
docapacitance. We have discussed various preparation methods
for these electrode materials, such as hydrothermal processes,
carbonization, and pyrolysis, highlighting the diverse sources of
BDC from plants, animals, and microorganisms and their suitable
applications in supercapacitors. Due to their widespread avail-
ability, renewable nature, extraordinary surface area, porosity,
and electrochemical properties, nanomaterial-decorated BDCs
emerge as the most promising materials for supercapacitors.
This review also analyzes the electrochemical performance of
nanomaterial-decorated BDCs, demonstrating their superiority
as electrode materials. They exhibit high capacitance and
extended energy density while maintaining high power density
and excellent durability. Furthermore, the suitability and benefits
of these materials are emphasized, considering present limita-
tions, renewable biomass sources for carbon preparation, cost-
effectiveness, and high capacitance consistency. Moreover, the
review highlights future perspectives and challenges, including
advanced preparation methods, innovative design strategies,
and enhanced structural properties of BDC. It underscores the
significant effects of nanomaterials on BDC for supercapacitor
performance, paving the way for advanced and cost-effective
energy storage devices with environmentally friendly character-
istics. Comparative analyses reveal that heteroatom-decorated
carbons exhibit more efficient responses than metal compounds
or polymer-decorated BDCs. This opens a new area of research
for supercapacitors, focusing on nanomaterial-decorated carbon
derivatives from different biomass sources. The energy density
can be increased by enhancing the surface area of the prepared
electrodes and developing various carbon nanostructures. This
leads to faster kinetics and a larger number of electroactive
sites for Faradaic charge storage, thereby expanding sustainable
energy storage and improving the electrochemical performance
of supercapacitors. The review also provides insights into
biomass carbon sources from the environment and indicates
the importance of enhanced synthesis technologies. The valu-
able information presented will assist future researchers in
generating new ideas for green energy storage devices with
environmental sustainability. The paper comprehensively covers
nanomaterial-decorated BDCs for supercapacitors, highlighting
their tremendous electrochemical responses. Therefore, this
review emphasizes that by addressing existing problems, the
discussed electrode materials can be developed into high-
performance supercapacitor electrodes. It provides insights into
future innovations in designing supercapacitor electrode materi-
als, paving the way for sustainable and environmentally friendly
energy conversion and storage technologies.
