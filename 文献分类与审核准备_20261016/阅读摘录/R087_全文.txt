Abstract
This study investigates the influence of oxygen functional groups in biomass-based activated carbon (AC) as an efficient, 
high-performance supercapacitor electrode. To create activated carbons with variable oxygen concentration, the surface of 
produced AC was altered using oxygen as an oxidizing agent at various temperatures. Nitrogen adsorption–desorption iso-
therms, scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy dispersive X-ray analyzer (EDX) analysis, thermogravimetric analysis 
(TGA), Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) spectroscopy, Boehm titration and X-ray Photoelectron Spectrometer 
(XPS), are used to characterize the physico-chemical characteristics of the obtained carbons. Afterward, cyclic voltammetry, 
constant current charge–discharge and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were used to evaluate the electro-
chemical properties and behavior of ACs based electrodes. Indeed, the oxidative treatment increases the oxygen functional 
content while reducing the specific surface area. According to the results of the characterization, the electrode test reveals 
that the modified materials improve the electrochemical properties and enhance the material’s double-layer capacitance. The 
AC treated with oxygen at 623 K displayed outstanding capacitance of 550 F  g−1  at specific current of 0.1 A  g−1  in NaOH 
(2 M) electrolyte and exhibits an excellent stability after 1000 cycles with a capacitance retention of 85.5%. In addition to 
being inexpensive, the prepared materials have excellent electrochemical performance and good stability, which make them 
appropriate for supercapacitor applications.

1 Introduction
The world is facing an energy crisis due to the scarcity of 
primary resources. It is therefore, important to develop clean 
and renewable energy sources by improving high-perfor -
mance energy storage technology [1 , 2]. Electrochemical 
capacitors (ECs), also called supercapacitors, have garnered 
a great deal of interest among many technologies for energy 
storage, due to their high power density, rapid energy stor -
age rate, and long life cycle [3 , 4]. Consequently, electro-
chemical capacitors are gradually finding their way into 
applications such as electric vehicles, portable electronics, 
heavy-duty machinery, storing the energy produced by solar 
cells and other fields [ 5–8]. The supercapacitor’s ultimate 
performance is significantly influenced by the electrode 
material and electrolyte [9, 10]. The development of highly 
efficient electrode materials with a high energy density is 
one important challenge [9 , 11]. Supercapacitors can be 
divided according to their charge storage mechanism into 
three types; (i) Double-layer capacitors, which are associated 
with electrostatic charge accumulation. They are character -
ized by rectangular CV curves and triangular GCD curves; 
(ii) Pseudo-capacitors, where charge storage comes from 
surface faradic reactions and the intercalation mechanism. 
They behave like EDLC materials, with almost rectangular 
CV curves and quasi-triangular GCD curves; Finally, (iii) 
Battery-type SCs, where reactions are controlled by ionic 
diffusion from the electrolyte, with a phase transformation 
reflected in the CV curves by obvious redox peaks, leading 
to the appearance of the potential plateau in the GCD curves 
[12, 13].
Carbon electrode materials such as graphene, carbon 
nanofibers, carbon nanotubes, and activated carbon have 
generally demonstrated promise as active materials for 
supercapacitors [11, 14]. Within these materials, activated 
carbon is most frequently utilized as an electrode due to its 
high surface area, chemical stability, environmental friend-
liness, relatively low cost and well-established scalability 
[15, 16].
One of the major challenges for high performance elec-
trochemical capacitors is the wettability of activated carbon. 
Coating the surface of activated carbon with oxygen func-
tional groups (OFGs) improves surface area utilization and 
capacitance at high current densities [15]. For instance, the 
effect of oxygen functional groups such as carboxyl, phe-
nol, carbonyl, and quinone on electrochemical behavior of 
incompletely reduced graphene oxides (RGOs) was studied 
by Young Joon Oh et al. [17], revealing a specific capaci-
tance of 220 F  g−1  at the scan of 5 mV   s−1 . Xi-ran Li and his 
team modify activated carbon by  (NH4)2S2O8 oxidation [18],

It has been established that oxygen-containing func-
tional groups (OCFG) scan produce pseudo-capacitance 
by a reversible redox reaction, increasing the total capaci-
tance [19, 20]. Therefore, there is still a need for advanced 
research to optimize AC with high surface area and excel-
lent wettability [21]. Additionally, the oxygen treatment 
encouraged the creation of the oxygen-containing functional 
groups (OCFGs) on the surface of AC, allowing it to be 
more wettable.
Utilizing biomass for the production of activated carbon 
presents an eco-friendly and renewable approach to energy 
storage technology. With proper process optimization and 
consideration of material properties, biomass-derived AC 
has the potential to contribute to sustainable energy storage 
solutions. The surface of activated carbon prepared from 
biomass was modified in this study using oxygen as an oxi-
dizing agent at various temperatures, in the aim of produc-
ing activated carbons with different oxygen contents and to 
apply these materials as electrodes for the supercapacitors. 
The relationship between the number of oxygen-functional 
groups and specific capacitance was investigated. The results 
obtained demonstrate the improved capacitive performance 
of oxygen-functionalized activated carbons, confirming the 
important effect of oxygen functional groups in enhancing 
the properties of activated carbons. As a result, oxygen-
functional biomass activated carbons have great potential 
as electrode materials for supercapacitors [22].
2  Experimental
2.1  Chemicals and apparatus
All chemical substances employed in this work were of ana-
lytical grade, they were therefore, used as they are, with-
out further purification. From Sigma Aldrish-USA:  H3PO4 
(85%), HCl (38%), NaOH (> 99%), NaCl (> 99%,). Ultrapure 
distilled water was used in all the experiments.
2.2  Preparation and characterization 
of functionalized activated carbon
The activated carbon was prepared via chemical activation 
using apricot stones as a precursor, the latter was obtained 
from the N’Gaous region in the wilayah of Batna, in east-
ern southern Algeria. All fabrication steps were conducted 
by Z. Kecira and co-workers and are described in detail 
elsewhere [23]. Briefly, the biomass was impregnated 
with phosphoric acid (1 g  H3PO4/1 g biomass) [23]. The 
impregnated sample was then carbonized at 673 K under 
 N2 flow. The resulting material was cleaned using distilled 
water until a steady pH was achieved. The resulting acti-
vated carbon was named AC.
Functionalization of the prepared activated carbon was 
carried out to introduce oxygen functional groups by oxygen 
treatment (OCFGs), which was capable of increasing the 
electrochemical performance of AC-based supercapacitor. 
The prepared AC was placed into the horizontal furnace and 
heated at 10 °C per minute, in  N2 flow (20 L per hour), from 
ambient temperature to the required oxidation temperature. 
The nitrogen stream was then replaced with the oxidative gas 
 (O2) (flow = 20 L per hour) and the treatment was prolonged 
at different temperatures in the range of 473, 523, 573 and 
623 K for 4 h. Following that, the system was cooled to room 
temperature under  N2 flow (20 L/h). The oxidized ACs were 
then labeled as OAC473, OAC523, OAC573 and OAC623, 
respectively.
N2 adsorption adsorption–desorption isotherms were con-
ducted using a micromeritics ASAP 2010 device at 77 K to 
identify the textural characteristics of each ACs-based elec-
trode [23]. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) pictures 
(FEI Quanta 250) equipped with EDX-ray Tube were used to 
study the morphology and presence of main elements in the 
produced carbons (EDX). The FTIR spectra were captured 
with a Pekin-Elmer Spectrum. According to Boehm method 
[23–26], 0.05 M NaOH aqueous solution was used to titrate 
the total acidic oxygen surface groups. Thermogravimetric 
analysis (TGA) was used to determine the samples’ ther -
mal stability using a handmade device. 200 mg of the mate-
rial were heated at a rate of 10 °C   min−1  under nitrogen 
flow from 25 to 1000 °C. XPS analysis were conducted by 
ESCALAB Xi + from Thermo Scientific highlighting a mon-
ochromatic Al K Alpha X-ray source (1486.68 eV).
2.3  Electrodes preparation and electrochemical 
measurements
The experiments were carried out in a closed vessel with a 
three-electrode setup composed of a platinum plate as the 
auxiliary electrode, and a saturated calomel electrode (SCE) 
as the reference [27, 28]. The working electrode is a pre-
pared oxygen-functionalized AC (OFAC). The quantity of 
OFAC was adjusted and fixed with 24.8 ± 3 mg, inserted in 
a Teflon tip in contact with a graphite pencil that serves as 
an electronic conductor (Fig.  1).
The Cyclic voltammetry (CV), the Galvanic cycle test 
(GC) and the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) 
analyses are performed using an electrochemical interface 
(Solartron, Si: 1287-1260) controlled by the CorrView 
software and impedance/Gain- phase analyzer (SI 1260) 
controlled by the Z-View software which was also used to 
analyze and fit the impedance data.

3  Results and discussion
3.1  Surface properties characterization
To assess the surface morphology of produced carbons, 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was performed, and 
the resulting micrographs are shown in Fig. 2. The produced 
activated carbon's external surface seems visually to have 
a variety of cavities of various sizes and shapes dispersed 
throughout the surface (see Fig.  3). Its texture is a result of 
the depolymerization of the carbonization process and the 
subsequent release of volatile organic compounds [29].
Table  1 displays the outcomes of EDX microanalysis of 
the prepared activated carbon surface. As can be seen, the 
sample exhibited similar elementary composition character-
istic carbonaceous materials.

Figure  4 displays the results of the thermogravimetric 
analysis of prepared activated carbon. TGA plot reveal a 
first mass loss above 150 °C, attributable to the removal of 
water physisorbed in the micropores and mesopores. The 
dissolution of the oxygenated surface groups can be used 
to explain the second minor mass loss that was seen at the 
plateau between 150 and 450 °C.
To qualitatively examine the impact of oxidation on sur-
face chemical functionalities, Fourier transform infrared 
spectroscopy (FTIR) was used [30, 31]. Figure  5 displays 
the spectra that were obtained for the unmodified AC 
and different modified samples. The samples’ identical 
FTIR spectra indicate that the alteration technique did 
not affect the chemical groups’ species but rather their 
intensities. All spectra show a broad absorption band 
at 3600–3050   cm−1 , with a high near 3400   cm−1  due to 
O–H stretching of hydroxyl groups, carboxyl groups, 
and adsorbed water [ 1, 29]. The bands near 2925 and 
2858   cm−1  are caused by aliphatic C-H stretching vibra-
tions. Stretching vibrations -C=C- in aromatic rings affect 
the bands at 1600   cm−1  and 1550   cm−1 , which are ampli-
fied by the presence of phenolic groups [15]. Stretch-
ing vibrations of -CH 3 between 1400 and 1300   cm−1  are 
associated with methyl structures. The bands identified in 
the 1200–950  cm −1  range correspond to C-O single bond 
stretching modes [32]. It is noteworthy that the spectra 
of OAC623 show sharper peaks than those of the other 
materials, which could be attributable to the presence of 
acid groups.

oxidation, which forms new oxygenated functional groups 
as a result of oxidation treatment, significantly increase the 
acid groups on carbon surfaces (Fig.  7). Additionally, it was 
observed that the more surface acid groups there were on the 
AC, the higher the thermal oxidation temperature required 
to treat it. These findings align with the outcomes obtained 
from XPS analysis and are consistent with earlier research 
[38].

3.2  Electrochemical behavior investigation
To evaluate the electrochemical performance of the prepared 
AC and OACs based electrodes, the measurement [15] were 
conducted at a scan rate of 10 mV   s−1  in the potential range 
of 0.0–1.0 V. CV was used to investigate the surface redox 
reaction on OACs-based electrodes in NaOH (2 M) electro-
lyte [32]. Figure  9a shows the CV curves seen on the ACs 
electrodes that are common of electrochemical double layer 
capacitor. The largest rectangular curve was observed on 
the OAC623 electrode, revealing the electrochemical double 
layer area brought about by the high oxygen content.

exhibit a similar allure, with two semi-circles at high and 
low frequency range, each semi-circle represent a resistance/
capacitance phase. The first intersection of the curve with 
the x-axis represent the solution resistance (Rs), which has 
a similar value for all materials due to the use of the same 
electrolyte for all experiments. The semicircle portion at 
high and low frequency range represent the charge-transfer 
resistance (Rct) and CPE is the constant phase element [46, 
47], it is to be noted that OAC623 has the greater CPE values 
compared with the other materials, indicating an enhanced 
capacitance, which is preferable in energy storage materi-
als. In Table  5, the fitting values for the equivalent circuit 
components of OAC473, OAC523, OAC573 and OAC623 
are listed. The curves have the same shapes, it means that 
the interface keeps the same interfacial behavior [48], while 
the electrokinetic parameters are varied. The centers of the 
two semicircles of the Nyquist diagram are located below 
the abscissa, indicating that charge transfer occurs in two 
steps; the first semicircle (at high frequency) is attributed 
to load transfer resistance, while the second semicircle is 
contributed by grain boundaries. The equivalent circuit dia-
gram has identical components (the constant phase element, 
the charge transfer resistance, and the ohmic resistance) [9]. 
It can be noticed that the polarization resistance increases 
along with the increase in oxygen level [49].
Moreover, Fig.  12 illustrates the stability of the OAC623 
electrode after testing for 1000 charge–discharge cycles at a 
current density of 0.1 A.g−1 . The electrode material exhibits 
exceptional stability, with a decrease in specific capacitance 
of only 14.5%, meaning that it retains 85.5% of its original 
capacitance, demonstrating that the elaborated electrode 
material exhibits high cycling stability under the experi -
mental conditions.
