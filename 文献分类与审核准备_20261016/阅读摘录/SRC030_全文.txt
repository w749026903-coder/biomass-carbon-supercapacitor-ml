Integrated Experimental and Machine Learning for Optimization and Performance 
Prediction of Cobalt-Based Metal-Organic Framework-Coated Nickel Foam Electrodes 
for Symmetric Supercapacitors
Radhakrishnan K1, Rohit Kumar Shaw1, D V Siva Krishna Rao K1, Aditya Kumar1,*
Department of Energy and Environment, National Institute of Technology, Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu, India
*Corresponding Author: adityakumar@nitt.edu
Abstract:
Key challenges in designing high-performance metal–organic framework (MOF)-based 
supercapacitor electrodes include optimizing precursor concentration and properly predicting 
electrochemical performance. In this article, we provide an integrated experimental and 
machine learning (ML) framework to optimize Co-MOF-coated nickel foam electrodes 
manufactured via a simple and scalable dip-coating process. Concentration-dependent 
structural and morphological evolution was validated by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM), with the 3 mmol Co precursor electrode (NFC3) showing the most 
uniform coating and superior electrochemical performance. The improved NFC3 electrode was 
built into an NFC3//NFC3 symmetric supercapacitor with a specific capacitance of 19.97 F 
g−1 at 1 A g−1 and 72% capacitance retention after 1000 charge–discharge cycles. 
Electrochemical studies, such as cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge–discharge 
(GCD), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), Ragone analysis, b-value and Dunn's 
analysis, demonstrated that the charge storage process was mostly surface-controlled 
capacitive. Five ML regression models were trained using experimentally generated CV data 
to predict the electrochemical current response. Among these, the Random Forest model had 
the highest prediction accuracy (R² = 0.999825, RMSE = 1.28 × 10⁻⁴ A, MAE = 3.10 × 10⁻⁵ 
A). The proposed experimental-ML framework provides an effective technique for optimizing 
MOF-based electrodes and accelerates the data-driven creation of high-performance symmetric 
supercapacitors.
Keywords: Co-MOF; Nickel foam electrode; Symmetric Supercapacitor; Machine Learning; 
Random Forest Regression; Electrochemical Energy Storage; Current Response Prediction.

1. INTRODUCTION:
The rapid expansion of renewable energy systems, electric vehicles (EVs), portable devices 
and smart power grids has created an urgent demand for energy storage technologies with high 
power density, fast charge–discharge rate and long operating lifetime [1–3]. Renewable energy 
sources such as solar, wind and hydropower are sustainable alternatives to fossil fuels. 
However, the intermittent nature of these sources needs the use of efficient energy storage 
systems to ensure continuous power delivery and increase grid stability [4–7]. Supercapacitors 
are remarkable for their high-power density, quick charge/discharge ability, excellent cycling 
stability, and long service life, and several electrical energy storage technologies have garnered 
considerable attention [8–10]. These advantages make them particularly desirable for 
applications where the power delivery is instantaneous such as electric vehicles, portable 
gadgets, wearable devices and hybrid energy storage systems [2,11,12]. However, further 
research of improved electrode materials and optimized device architectures are required to 
increase the energy and power performance without affecting the long-term cycle stability to 
sustain efficient electrochemical performance at high current densities  [13–15].
Supercapacitors are often categorized as electric double-layer capacitors (EDLCs), 
pseudocapacitors and hybrid supercapacitors according to charge-storage mechanism [16–20]. 
EDLCs store charge via a non-faradaic process of ion adsorption and possess great power 
density and cycling stability, but have a restricted energy density. Pseudocapacitors, in contrast, 
use rapid and reversible faradaic reactions to obtain larger capacitance, whereas hybrid 
supercapacitors combine capacitive and battery-type electrodes to balance energy and power 
densities [18–20].  However, nonlinear electrochemical behaviour arising from the intricate 
interaction between electrode structure, electrolyte and operating circumstances makes 
performance optimization difficult [9,21]. Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have attracted 
considerable attention as supercapacitor electrode materials because of their high surface area, 
tuneable pore structures, and abundant electroactive sites, which facilitate rapid ion transport

and enhanced charge storage [22–24]. However, their intrinsically low electrical conductivity 
limits direct application, necessitating conductive supports or composite architecture [21,24]. 
Nickel foam serves as an effective three-dimensional current collector by providing excellent 
electrical conductivity, efficient electrolyte accessibility, and improved active material

ML algorithms have surfaced as a formidable instrument for forecasting electrochemical 
performance, identify optimal operating conditions, and reduce trial-and-error experiments 
[8,26,27]. Among various ML approaches, several regression algorithms have been 
successfully applied to predict supercapacitor performance, including LR, DT, RF, GBR, and 
KNN [14,28–30]. These models can predict electrochemical current response and specific 
capacitance using experimental parameters such as scan rate, potential, discharge time, 
equivalent series resistance (ESR), and IR drop [9,31]. For instance, GBR and RF have 
demonstrated high predictive accuracy for specific capacitance by learning from experimental 
CV and GCD data [14,30]. Machine learning models enable the identification of complex 
nonlinear relationships between electrochemical parameters and charge-storage behaviour, 
resulting in supporting rapid performance prediction and electrode optimization [32–34]. 
However, many existing models remain limited by small datasets, overfitting, poor 
interpretability, and insufficient validation using practical symmetric supercapacitor devices 
[8,10,16,31,34].Despite significant progress in machine learning (ML)-driven supercapacitor 
research, several critical gaps remain unaddressed. First, only a limited number of studies have 
combined cobalt based metal-organic framework (MOF) coated nickel foam electrodes with 
machine-learning-based prediction [24,30]. The systematic investigation of how precursor 
concentration influences electrochemical performance in MOF-coated electrodes using data-
driven approaches remains absent from the literature [4,30]. Recently, efforts have sought to 
integrate electrochemical kinetics analysis with ML predictions and validate the models using 
practical symmetric device performance [30,34,35]. Scan-rate-resolved electrochemical 
datasets (2-200 mV/s) remain limited, restricting the development of robust machine-learning 
models for capturing nonlinear relationships between scan rate, charge-storage kinetics, and 
electrochemical performance [1,16]. Furthermore, most studies focus on capacitance prediction 
using three-electrode systems, with limited integration of comprehensive electrochemical 
kinetics (CV, GCD, EIS, b-value, and Dunn’s analysis) and validation using practical 
symmetric devices [2,15,33,35]. To address these gaps, this study develops an integrated 
experimental-machine learning framework for concentration-optimization of Co-MOF-coated 
nickel foam electrodes.
A complete experimental and machine learning framework was created to study the effect of 
cobalt precursor concentration on structural, electrochemical and predictive performance of 
Co-MOF coated nickel foam electrodes for supercapacitor applications. In an effort to optimize 
the electrode architecture, a simple and scalable dip-coating process was first employed to 
create Co-MOF electrodes with three different cobalt precursor concentrations (1–3 mmol) in 
a systematic concentration-dependent investigation. Finally, the optimized electrode was 
fabricated into a realistic symmetric two-electrode supercapacitor to test the real-device

2.2 Electrode Fabrication
2.2.1 NF Pretreatment
Nickel foam (NF) strips with measurements of 2 cm x 1 cm and an exposed electrochemically 
active area of 1 x 1 cm2 were used as the substrates for electrode construction. Before coating, 
the NF (nickel foam) strips were subjected to ultrasonic cleaning in 1 M HCl for 15 mis to 
eliminate native oxide files and surface impurities. After cleaning, the nickel substrates were 
subsequently cleaned thoroughly using deionized water followed by ethanol to eliminate 
residual acid and contaminants. After washing, substrates were allowed dry naturally under 
ambient conditions prior to Co-MOF layer. The cleaned nickel foam substrates served as the 
substrates of subsequent Co-MOF deposition and electrochemical evaluation. Finally, the 
measured masses of the bare nickel foam electrodes were 0.0355 g, 0.03621 g, 0.03565 g for 
NFC1, NFC2, and NFC3, respectively. 
2.2.2 MOF Preparation
Co-MOF coatings were deposited directly onto the pre-treated nickel foam substrates to 
improve their electrochemical performance. Cobalt nitrate hexahydrate (Co (NO 3)2.6H2O) 
served as the metal source, whereas 2-methylimidazole was employed as the organic ligand. 
Methanol was selected as the solvent because it provides uniform precursor dissolution and 
promotes controlled MOF crystal growth. Three MOF-coated electrodes (NFC1, NFC2, and 
NFC3) were prepared with cobalt nitrate hexahydrate concentrations of 1, 2, and 3 mmol for 
each and with the same metal-to-linker molar ratio of 1:8 with 2-methylimidazole. For each 
sample, 50 mL of methanol with the respective amount of cobalt nitrate hexahydrate was used 
to dissolve. Subsequently, 2-methylimidazole was added in a fixed molar ratio of 1:8 (metal-
to-linker) with respect to the cobalt precursor concentration. The ensuring precursor solution 
was magnetically stirred it up to 5 minutes to obtain a standardized reaction mixture before 
electrode deposition.
Prior to immersion, the pre-treated nickel foam strips were ultrasonically cleaned using 
deionized water and ethanol to eradicate any outstanding surface contaminants. Furthermore, 
the strips were dipped in the arranged precursor solution and preserved at room temperature 
(25OC) for 10 h, allowing to Co-MOF layer to nucleate and grow directly on the NF surface. 
Following deposition process, the coated electrode was carefully removed and dried at 30 OC 
for 3 hours, followed by ambient drying for 10-12 h to confirm complete solvent evaporation 
and maintenance of the deposited coating. No hydrothermal treatment was used during the 
synthesis. The adopted dip-coating method provides a simple, cost-effective, and scalable route 
for fabricating MOF-coated NF electrodes.

2.3 Structural and Morphological Characterization
The structural and surface characteristics of the fabricated Co-MOF-coated nickel foam 
electrodes (NFC1, NFC2, and NFC3) were systematically examined using X-ray diffraction 
(XRD) and Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) [22,24]. Phase identification and crystal 
structure analysis were performed using an X-ray diffractometer (Rigaku Ultima IV), equipped 
with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5406 Å) [36]. The diffraction patterns were compared with 
standard reference data to evaluate the crystallinity and phase composition of the synthesized 
coatings [23,36–38]. Surface morphology, coating uniformity, and microstructural features of 
fabricated electrodes were studied by ZEISS EVO 18 - SEM. The SEM observations were 
employed to assess the influence of precursor concentration on Co-MOF nucleation, crystal 
growth, and surface coverage of the nickel foam substrate [22,38]. The structural information 
obtained from XRD was correlated with the morphological features observed by SEM to create 
the connection among material characteristics and electrochemical performance.  
Characterization combined approach enabled a full estimation of the outcome of precursor 
concentration on the development of Co-MOF-coated electrodes for SC applications.
2.4 Electrochemical Measurements
The electrochemical performance of the fabricated Co-MOF-coated nickel foam electrodes 
(NFC1, NFC2, and NFC3) was investigated using a conventional three-electrode cell 
connected to an Admiral Instruments Squidstat Solo electrochemical workstation. The Co-
MOF-coated NF was used as the working electrode in a conventional three-electrode 
configuration, with a platinum wire acting as the counter electrode and Ag/AgCl as the 
reference electrode. Electrochemical measurements were carried out in 1 M aqueous KOH at 
room temperature [36].

measurements. OCP was initially recorded to establish the equilibrium potential and confirm 
system stability before electrochemical testing [6]. LSV was conducted to determine the 
electrochemical stability window and identify the safe operating potential by minimizing 
parasitic reactions such as water splitting [6]. CV measurements were performed at scan rates

2.5 Charge Storage Kinetics
To study the charge retention behaviour with the electrode arrangements of NFC3//NFC3 
symmetric device supercapacitor, CV data collected at difference scan rates were analyzed 
using the b-value and Dunn’s methods. These analyses distinguish the contributions of (kinetic 
analysis) surface and diffusion-controlled charge-storage methods [18,25]. The charge-storage 
mechanism was analysed employing the power-law correlation 𝑖 = 𝑎𝑣𝑏 was used, where 𝑖 is 
the current response, 𝑣 is the scan rate, and 𝑏 is derived from the gradient of log (𝑖) in relation 
to log ( 𝑣). A b-value of 0.5 indicates diffusion-dominated (battery-type) behaviour, whereas 
b=1.0 corresponds to an ideal capacitive process [18,39]. The capacitive and diffusion-
dominated offers were quantified with Dunn’s relationship 𝑖(𝑉) =  𝑘1𝑣 +  𝑘2𝑣1/2 which can be 
rearranged, 𝑖(𝑉) 𝑣1/2=  𝑘1𝑣1/2+ 𝑘2.  The coefficients 𝑘1 and 𝑘2 denote the contributions of 
capacitive and diffusion-dominated currents, respectively. The relative contribution of each 
mechanism was evaluated at difference scan rates [14,39].
2.6 Data-Driven Prediction Framework
ML algorithms were established to predict the current response of the NFC3//NFC3 symmetric 
supercapacitor using experimentally generated from cyclic voltammetry (CV) data [1,15,16]. 
The dataset consisted of experimental CV data with scan rates of 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 200 mV/s, 
where potential together with scanning speed were input features, and current was output 
[6,29]. The dataset was pre-processes using moving-average smoothing, normalization (zero 
mean and unit variance), and an 80/20 division of training and testing data [29]. Five regression 
algorithms - LR (baseline), DT, RF (200 estimators, depth = 10), GBR (500 estimators, learning 
rate = 0.05), and KNN (K = 5). The hyperparameters were then tested using grid search cross-
validation [14,15]. Model performance was evaluated with R 2, MAE, and RMSE are the

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1 X-Ray Diffraction Analysis
Figure 2(E) presents the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of bare nickel foam (NF) and the 
Co-MOF-coated nickel foam electrodes (NFC1, NFC2, NFC3). All samples exhibit three 
dominant diffraction peaks located at 44.54-44.86 O, 51.9O, and 76.42 O, corresponding to the 
FCC Ni crystal planes indexed as (111), (200), and (220) confirming the crystalline nature of 
the NF substrate [22]. After coating the MOF layer, the characteristic diffraction peaks of 
nickel clearly visible, demonstrating that Co-MOF deposition preserved the substrate FCC 
crystal structure of the nickel foam substrate [23]. Furthermore, the Ni (111) peak shifts from 
44.54O for NF to 44.70 O, 44.86 O, and 44.58 O for NFC1, NFC2, and NFC3, respectively, 
indicating the interaction of the MOF layer and the nickel substrate at the surface [22]. The 
structural parameters calculated from the XRD data are summarized in Table 1. No additional 
diffraction peaks corresponding to crystalline impurity phases were observed. The FWHM of 
the Ni (111) reflection decreased with increasing precursor concentration, and the crystallite 
size (Scherrer equation) increased from 33.35 nm (NF) to 34.00 nm (NFC1), 37.60 nm (NFC2), 
and 40.18nm (NFC3). Microstrain decreased from 3.92x10 -3 (NF) to 2.12x10 -3 (NFC2), and 
the dislocation density decreased from 8.99x10 -3 nm -2 (NF) to 6.20x10 -4 nm -2 (NFC3) with 
smaller defects. The crystallite size and dislocation density of NFC3 were the largest and 
smallest, respectively, and it showed better structure with no defects, while the FCC nickel 
structure remained preserved [23]. The increase in crystallite size and the reduction in lattice 
defects is expected to improve electron conduction routes and structural stability, leading to 
contributing to the improved electrochemical behaviour of NFC3 observed in subsequent CV 
and GCD measurements [38]. The findings agree with the observation that higher precursor 
concentration increased crystallinity without affecting the FCC nickel structure, further 
indicating that the Co-MOF deposition on the NFC3 electrode was successful and that the 
current collector remains intact. The improved crystallinity and reduced lattice defects 
observed in NFC3 suggest enhanced electrochemical activity, which was further investigated 
by SEM analysis of the surface morphology.

3.4 Linear Sweep Voltammetry Analysis
Figure 3 (B) presents the linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) of the NFC1, NFC2, and NFC3 
electrodes. At lower applied potentials, all electrodes exhibited negligible current responses, 
indicating minimal electrochemical activity within this potential region. As the applied 
potential increased, the anodic current increased progressively owing to Faradaic oxidation 
processes associated with the electroactive cobalt species in the Co-MOF coating [23]. 
Increasing the precursor concentration systematically enhanced the electrochemical activity, 
with the optimization study identifying NFC3 as the optimum electrode [34]. Weak anodic and 
cathodic features observed between 0.10 and 0.35 V are assigned to the reversible surface 
faradaic redox reactions of active cobalt centres within the deposited MOF structure [41]. A 
pronounced rise in anodic current beyond higher applied potentials marks the onset of the 
oxygen evolution (OER), ultimately defining the upper electrochemical stability limit of the 
electrode/electrolyte system [42]. Among the electrodes investigated, NFC3 exhibited the most 
significant anodic current response, which can be attributed to its uniform Co-MOF coating, 
greater surface coverage, combined with its interconnected porous morphology revealed in the 
SEM microstructural analysis [23]. Collectively, the LSV results demonstrate that all 
electrodes remained electrochemically stable within the investigated potential range, with 
NFC3 exhibiting the highest electrochemical activity [23,34,43]. The higher electrochemical 
stability found for NFC3 also supports its choice for the assembling the symmetric 
supercapacitor device. The stable electrochemical potential window identified by LSV was 
subsequently used for CV to conduct a more in-depth examination of the charge-storage 
mechanism of the constructed electrodes.

3.9 Cycling Stability
The cycling durability of the NFC3//NFC3 assembled symmetric device was assessed over 
1000 galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD) cycles using an applied current density of 1 A/g 
(Figure 6B). Initially, measured capacitance improved from 10.54 F/g (1 Cycle) reaching 12.00 
F/g (100 Cycles), which is mainly linked with the progressive electrochemical activation at the 
electrode/electrolyte interface together with enhanced ion penetration of electrolyte ions into 
the interconnected porous Co-MOF framework during initial cycling phase [18]. After the 
initial activation stage, the measured capacitance progressively decreased to 11.38 F/g (200 
Cycles), 10.50 F/g (500 Cycles), 9.25 F/g (750 Cycles), and 7.48 F/g (1000 Cycles), 
corresponding to approximately 72% capacitance retention. The gradual capacitance decay is 
attributed to structural rearrangement of the Co-MOF coating, repeated ion 
insertion/extraction, together with gradual reduction in electrochemically accessible active 
sites during prolonged cycling [18]. Meanwhile, the coulombic efficiency remained between 
98% and 100% during the cycling experiment, representing exceptionally reversible charge 
retention characteristics while minimizing parasitic side reactions [43]. The high coulombic 
efficiency obtained on the cycling test indicates that capacitance loss is mainly due to 
permanent structural changes of the collector rather than irreversible parasitic electrochemical 
reactions. The nearly constant coulombic efficiency demonstrates that the charge-discharge 
process remained highly efficient despite the gradual decrease in specific capacitance. Overall, 
the NFC3//NFC3 symmetric supercapacitor exhibited good cycling stability, which agrees well 
with the CV, GCD and EIS results [43]. Following stability evaluation, impedance analysis 
was performed to investigate the internal resistance and charge-transfer behaviour of the 
assembled device.

3.12 Machine Learning Model Performance
A data-driven ML framework was established to estimate the electrochemical current response 
for the NFC3//NFC3 symmetric supercapacitor from CV data [13,40]. The dataset comprised 
experimentally measured currents at scan rates of 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 200 mV/s, with working 
electrode potential and scan rate as inputs and measured current as the prediction target. Five 
regression algorithms, LR, DT, RF, GBR, and KNN were evaluated (Figure 9A-E) respectively 
of device level electrode [38].
Among all evaluated models, Random Forest algorithm provided the best prediction accuracy 
(R2 = 0.999825, RMSE = 1.28 x 10 -4 A, and MAE = 3.10 x 10 -5 A), demonstrating excellent 
agreement between predicted and actual current responses. KNN is also good (R2 = 0.999673), 
followed by GBR (R 2 = 0.994990), and DT (R 2 = 0.987298). Linear Regression is the worst 
(R2 = 0.774371) due to the very nonlinear electrochemical response of the MOF-coated 
electrode [38].

The improved prediction accuracy of the Random Forest model is mainly associated with its

4. CONCLUSION
Co-MOF-coated nickel foam electrodes with precursor concentrations of 1, 2, and 3 mmol were 
successfully synthesized and comprehensively evaluated to examine how precursor 
concentration influences the structural characteristics and electrochemical behaviour. 
Structural characterization confirmed the successful construction of the Co-MOF coating. The 
proposed concentration-optimization strategy identified the 3 mmol precursor concentration 
(NFC3) as the optimum condition, resulting in enhanced charge-storage behaviour and 
electrochemical stability.
The optimized NFC3 electrode was subsequently employed to assemble an NFC3//NFC3 
symmetric device of supercapacitor. The assembled symmetric device delivered a maximum 
capacitance reaching 19.97 F/g measured when 1 A/g together with maintained approximately 
72% of the early capacitance following 1000 charge-discharge cycles, demonstrating good 
stable cycling performance. Charge-storage kinetic analysis using b-value evaluation and 
Dunn’s analysis found that charge-storage mechanism was primarily dominated by surface-
capacitive processes with a smaller diffusion-controlled contribution. Electrochemical 
impedance spectroscopy further confirmed the favourable charge-transfer properties and 
surface-capacitive processes accompanied by a minor diffusion-controlled contribution.
Machine learning regression models were developed using experimentally generated cyclic 
voltammetry data for predicting the electrochemical current response for the symmetric 
supercapacitor. Among the investigated algorithms, the Random Forest algorithm provided the 
best predictive performance (R 2 = 0.999825, RMSE = 1.28 x 10 -4 A, and MAE = 3.10 x              
10-5 A), and while feature-importance analysis identified the working electrode potential as the 
dominant variable governing the current response. The results demonstrate that experimentally 
derived electrochemical parameters can be accurately predicted using machine-learning 
techniques. The close agreement between structural characterization, electrochemical analysis 
and machine-learning prediction illustrates that the proposed integrated experimental-machine 
learning framework provides a reliable approach for the rapid optimization and design of 
advanced MOF-based supercapacitor electrodes. The proposed experimental-machine learning 
framework provides a transferable methodology that can be extended to other MOF-based and 
transition-metal-based supercapacitor electrode systems.
Overall, this study establishes an integrated experimental-machine learning framework that 
combines material optimization, electrochemical characterization, kinetic analysis, symmetric-
device validation, and data-driven prediction. The proposed framework provides a transferable 
methodology for accelerating the evaluation, optimization, and future development of MOF-

5. FUTURE WORK
Future research should incorporate explainable AI (XAI) to improve the clarity of ML 
algorithms and identify the electrochemical variables governing device performance. 
Integration of real-time prediction models with electrochemical testing systems could enable 
rapid performance assessment during experimentation. Active-learning strategies may further 
minimize experimental effort through the selection of the most informative experiments for 
model refinement. In addition, expanding and more diverse electrochemical datasets covering 
different electrode material, electrolytes, and operating conditions will improve model 
generalization and support the development of robust predictive tools for next-generation 
supercapacitor systems.
