ABSTRACT
The demand for carbon-based materials with superior electrochemical properties 
continues to grow, particularly for scalable supercapacitor applications. In this 
study, we present a hybrid synthesis route for graphene electrodes that combines 
chemical vapor deposition (CVD) and microwave-assisted reduction, guided by 
machine learning (ML) optimization. A predictive neural network trained on over 
100 synthesis experiments enabled precise tuning of key parameters, resulting in 
high conductivity (~ 9.6 × 105 S  m−1). Raman analysis across ≥ 5 spots per sample 
showed hybrid graphene at ID/IG = 0.29 [mean ± SD to be reported], while the 
CVD control exhibited ID/IG ≈ 0.10 [mean ± SD]. These properties underpinned 
the electrodes’ high specific capacitance (up to 500 F  g−1), energy density (120 Wh 
 k g−1), and 95% retention over 10,000 cycles. The synthesized graphene was further 
hybridized with  MnO2,  RuO2, and conductive polymers (polyaniline, polypyr-
role), leading to enhanced specific capacitance, energy density, and power den-
sity (70 kW  kg−1), while maintaining long-term stability. Structural, thermal, and 
electrochemical evaluations confirmed the durability and high performance of 
the optimized electrodes. This work demonstrates a scalable and cost-effective 
graphene synthesis strategy for advanced energy storage, enabled by machine 
learning. The integration of data-driven optimization with roll-to-roll-compat-
ible processing provides a promising pathway toward industrial deployment 
of graphene-based supercapacitors in transportation, grid systems, and flexible 
electronics.

Introduction
The rapid evolution of energy storage technologies 
has intensified the demand for high-performance 
electrode materials that offer excellent electrical con-
ductivity, mechanical robustness, and scalable produc-
tion potential [1–3]. Among these materials, graphene 
has emerged as a highly promising candidate due to 
its exceptional intrinsic properties, including high 
specific surface area, superior electrical conductivity 
(~  106 S/m), mechanical flexibility, and chemical stabil-
ity [4–6]. These characteristics position graphene as an 
ideal building block for next-generation supercapaci-
tors, which require rapid charge–discharge capabili-
ties and long operational lifetimes [5 –7].
Despite its outstanding theoretical potential, 
the widespread industrial application of graphene 
remains hindered by significant challenges in synthe-
sis [8–10]. Conventional techniques such as chemical 
vapor deposition (CVD) offer monolayer graphene 
of high purity and crystallinity but suffer from high 
production costs, limited throughput, and scalabil -
ity issues [11– 13]. Alternatively, chemical exfoliation 
approaches offer greater yield and lower cost but 
introduce structural defects that compromise the 
mechanical and electrochemical integrity of the mate-
rial [14– 16]. Emerging microwave-assisted reduc-
tion techniques have demonstrated improved defect

In this study, we introduce a machine learning-

that integrates CVD and microwave-assisted reduc-

to optimize key process parameters for maximizing

treatments and cost-effective precursors. The inte-
gration of ML optimization, scalable processing, and 
polymer–graphene hybridization offers a holistic 
pathway toward industrially viable energy storage 
systems. The findings of this work aim to contribute 
to the broader effort of developing advanced polymer-
based nanocomposites that combine high performance 
with manufacturing feasibility.
Experimental methods
Graphene synthesis
Graphene was synthesized using four distinct 
approaches for comparative evaluation: chemical 
vapor deposition (CVD), chemical exfoliation, micro-
wave-assisted reduction, and a hybrid method com-
bining CVD and microwave processing. The hybrid 
approach was developed as the primary synthesis 
route for optimizing both quality and scalability.
For CVD synthesis, high-purity copper foil (99.99%) 
served as the substrate. Methane  (CH4) and hydrogen 
 ( H2) were introduced at flow rates of 10 and 20 sccm, 
respectively, within a quartz tube furnace maintained 
at 1000 °C under an argon atmosphere. Growth dura-
tion was 30 min, followed by rapid cooling under 
argon to preserve the structural integrity of the gra -
phene film.
In the chemical exfoliation method, graphene 
oxide (GO) was synthesized via a modified Hummers 
process, employing graphite powder, concentrated 
sulfuric acid  (H2SO4), and potassium permanganate 
 (KMnO4). The resulting GO was reduced using hydra-
zine hydrate or eco-friendly agents such as ascorbic 
acid and tannic acid. After neutralization, the product 
was vacuum-dried to obtain reduced graphene oxide 
(rGO).
Microwave-assisted synthesis involved dispers-
ing GO (1 mg/mL) in deionized water and subjecting 
the solution to microwave irradiation at 800 W for 60 
s. This one-step process enabled simultaneous exfo-
liation and reduction, producing few-layer graphene 
with relatively low defect concentration.
The hybrid method consisted of an initial CVD 
growth step followed by microwave-assisted reduc-
tion. This approach was designed to combine the high-
quality monolayer structure of CVD-grown graphene 
with the enhanced exfoliation and defect passivation 
afforded by microwave processing.
Machine learning optimization
To optimize the graphene synthesis parameters, a 
supervised feed-forward neural network (FNN) was 
implemented using a dataset derived from 112 experi-
mental runs. Each data point included input features 
such as growth temperature,  CH4/H2 gas flow rates, 
microwave power (W), reaction time (min), and reduc-
tion conditions. The target outputs were electrical 
conductivity (S/m), ID/IG Raman ratio, and graphene 
yield (g/h).
The neural network architecture consisted of an 
input layer with five nodes, followed by three hid-
den layers with 64, 32, and 16 neurons, respectively. 
Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) was used as the acti -
vation function for the hidden layers, while a linear 
activation was applied in the output layer. The model 
was trained using the Adam optimizer with an initial 
learning rate of 0.001. The loss function was the mean 
squared error (MSE), minimized over 500 epochs with 
early stopping to prevent overfitting.
The dataset was randomly divided into training 
(80%) and test (20%) subsets. A fivefold cross-valida-
tion strategy was employed to evaluate the model’s 
generalizability. Learning curves were plotted to 
assess convergence and bias–variance trade-offs, indi-
cating that the training and validation errors stabilized 
with increasing data, suggesting sufficient sample size.
The final model achieved a coefficient of determi -
nation (R2) of 0.94, a root-mean-square error (RMSE) 
of 3.7%, and a mean absolute error (MAE) of 2.5%.

Hybrid electrode fabrication
To prepare hybrid electrodes, the synthesized gra-
phene was blended with either metal oxides  (MnO2, 
 RuO2) or conductive polymers (PANI, PPy). The 
composite slurry consisted of 80 wt% active material, 
10 wt% conductive carbon black, and 10 wt% poly-
vinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder, all dispersed in 
N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP). This slurry was uni-
formly coated onto stainless steel or nickel foam sub-
strates using the doctor blade technique. Following 
coating, the electrodes were dried under vacuum at 
80 °C, pressed at 10 MPa for densification, and then 
cut into 1 cm2 disks for electrochemical evaluation.
Electrochemical testing
Electrochemical performance was assessed using 
both two- and three-electrode configurations. The 
graphene-based composite served as the working 
electrode, with a platinum wire as the counter elec-
trode and Ag/AgCl as the reference electrode in three-
electrode setups. For symmetric two-electrode testing, 
identical composite electrodes were used.
Electrolytes included 1 M  H2SO4 and 1 M  Na2SO4 
(aqueous), 1 M tetraethylammonium tetrafluorobo-
rate  (TEABF4) in acetonitrile (organic), and 1-ethyl-
3-methylimidazolium tetrafluoroborate (EMIM-BF 4) 
as an ionic liquid. The testing protocols comprised 
cyclic voltammetry (CV) in the 5–100 mV/s range, gal-
vanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) at current densi-
ties from 1 to 10 A/g, and electrochemical impedance 
spectroscopy (EIS) covering frequencies from 10⁻ 2 to 
105 Hz.
Materials characterization
Morphological analysis was performed using scan-
ning electron microscopy (SEM; Hitachi SU5000, 10 
kV). Structural defects and layer characteristics were 
assessed by Raman spectroscopy (Renishaw inVia, 532 
nm excitation). Thermal stability was evaluated via 
thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) under a nitrogen 
atmosphere up to 800 °C. Additional structural and 
chemical analyses were conducted using X-ray diffrac-
tion (XRD) and Fourier-transform infrared spectros-
copy (FTIR), as appropriate.
Raman processing
Raw spectra (Renishaw inVia, 532 nm, ~ 1 mW) were 
baseline-corrected (second-order polynomial), and 
cosmic rays removed by median filtering with man -
ual verification. Substrate background was subtracted 
using a spectrum from a bare region. D and G peaks 
were fitted with Voigt profiles (D: 1200–1450  cm−1; 
G: 1500–1650  cm−1) under nonnegative constraints. 
The ID/IG ratio was computed from the integrated 
areas (AD/AG). For each sample (CVD, microwave, 
hybrid), ≥ 5 spatially distinct spots were analyzed; 
results are reported as mean ± SD and visualized as a 
box-and-whisker plot (see Fig. 11).
Techno‑economic analysis
A detailed techno-economic model was developed to 
assess the production cost of graphene via the pro-
posed hybrid CVD–microwave synthesis method. The 
model incorporates inputs such as raw material costs, 
energy consumption, labor, equipment depreciation, 
and consumables, based on laboratory-scale operation 
scaled to pilot production.
The baseline cost per gram of graphene produced 
using the hybrid method was estimated at $50–75/g, 
compared to $100–150/g for pure CVD and $10–20/g 
for chemical exfoliation. Table 1 outlines the cost com-
ponents contributing to the total production cost.

Results
Structural characterization
The morphology of graphene synthesized via four dis-
tinct routes—chemical vapor deposition (CVD), chem-
ical exfoliation, microwave-assisted reduction, and the 
hybrid CVD–microwave method—was investigated 
using scanning electron microscopy (SEM). CVD-
derived graphene formed continuous and smooth 
monolayer films with negligible folding or aggrega-
tion, confirming the generation of high-purity, well-
ordered structures. By contrast, chemically exfoliated 
graphene displayed fragmented, wrinkled sheets with 
irregular morphologies, a direct outcome of oxidative 
and reductive steps inherent to the modified Hum-
mers method. Microwave-assisted reduction produced 
larger and flatter graphene domains with fewer wrin-
kles, indicating more efficient exfoliation and preser-
vation of basal plane integrity. Remarkably, the hybrid 
CVD–microwave route yielded uniform few-layer gra-
phene sheets, combining the structural uniformity of 
CVD films with the exfoliation efficiency of microwave 
treatment, while minimizing agglomeration.
Raman spectroscopy supported these observations 
by quantifying defect densities. CVD graphene exhib-
ited a low ID/IG ratio (~ 0.1), indicative of minimal 
structural disorder and superior graphitic quality. 
Microwave-assisted graphene displayed a moderate 
ratio (~ 0.3), whereas chemically exfoliated graphene 
reached the highest (~ 0.8), reflecting significant lattice 
disruption. Importantly, the hybrid graphene retained 
the low ID/IG ratio characteristic of CVD, confirming 
that microwave post-treatment enhanced exfoliation 
without introducing new defects.
To clarify structure–property correlations, density 
functional theory (DFT) simulations showed that low 
defect densities facilitate higher charge-carrier mobil-
ity and bandgap uniformity. Complementary finite 
element modeling (FEM) of graphene–polymer com-
posites indicated a ~ 15% reduction in localized stress 
concentrations for the hybrid samples compared to 
pristine CVD graphene, suggesting stronger interfa-
cial bonding and improved mechanical compliance.
A comparative summary of the four synthesis 
routes is provided in Table 2. CVD achieved the high-
est surface area (~ 2630 m2/g) and electrical conductiv-
ity (~  106 S/m), though limited by high cost ($100–150/
g) and poor scalability. Chemical exfoliation offered 
high throughput and low cost ($10–20/g) but pro-
duced graphene of inferior quality and conductivity 
(~ 104 S/m). Microwave-assisted reduction provided 
an intermediate solution, delivering 1200–1500 m2/g 
surface area and ~ 105 S/m conductivity, with moder-
ate scalability and reduced environmental impact. The 
hybrid CVD–microwave method emerged as the most 
balanced strategy, combining high conductivity (~  106 
S/m), enhanced scalability, and significantly reduced 
cost ($50–75/g), positioning it as a strong candidate for 
industrial adoption.
In summary, CVD produces the highest-quality 
graphene with minimal defects and monolayer struc-
tures suitable for advanced energy storage devices but 
remains constrained by scalability and cost. Chemi-
cal exfoliation ensures scalability but sacrifices con-
ductivity, necessitating further functionalization or 
post-treatment. Microwave-assisted reduction offers a 
compromise, yielding thermally stable graphene with 
moderate conductivity. The hybrid CVD–microwave 
approach stands out as a promising route, integrating 
structural integrity, high conductivity, and practical 
scalability for industrial-scale applications.

Electrochemical performance
Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements, conducted at 
scan rates ranging from 5 to 100 mV/s, demonstrated 
that hybrid graphene electrodes exhibited nearly rec-
tangular CV curves, characteristic of ideal electric 
double-layer capacitor (EDLC) behavior. These elec-
trodes showed the highest current response across all 
scan rates, indicating efficient ion diffusion and rapid 
charge–discharge kinetics. The CVD-based electrodes 
also performed well, while the microwave and chemi-
cally exfoliated electrodes showed broader, distorted 
curves at higher scan rates, reflecting increased inter-
nal resistance and slower ion transport.
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) 
was used to evaluate charge-transfer resistance. 
Nyquist plots revealed that CVD electrodes exhibited 
the smallest semicircle in the high-frequency region, 
corresponding to the lowest charge-transfer resist-
ance. The hybrid electrodes closely matched this 
performance, demonstrating efficient electron trans -
port pathways. In contrast, the microwave-assisted 
and exfoliated samples displayed larger semicircles, 
indicative of higher interfacial resistance.
Galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) tests were 
performed at current densities of 1, 5, and 10 A/g. 
The GCD profiles of hybrid electrodes were linear 
and symmetric, indicative of high coulombic effi-
ciency and excellent electrochemical reversibility. 
Specific capacitance values were highest for the 
hybrid electrodes, reaching up to 500 F/g. In com-
parison, microwave-assisted graphene achieved 200 
F/g, CVD-derived electrodes reached 250 F/g, and 
chemically exfoliated samples delivered 150 F/g. 
These results underscore the synergistic benefits 
of combining CVD quality with microwave-driven 
exfoliation.
Cycling stability over 10,000 charge–discharge 
cycles further highlighted the superior durability 
of the hybrid and CVD electrodes, both of which 
retained over 95% of their initial capacitance. Micro-
wave-assisted electrodes retained approximately 
90%, while chemically exfoliated samples declined 
to 80%, confirming the role of structural defects in 
long-term performance degradation.

Mechanical and electrochemical synergy
The integration of graphene with conductive polymers 
in the hybrid electrodes provided a unique combina-
tion of electrochemical efficiency and mechanical 
resilience. The polymer matrix improved flexibility, 
while the defect-minimized graphene enabled stable 
electrical conductivity under repeated cycling and 
mechanical stress. The reduced interfacial resistance 
and enhanced adhesion observed in hybrid samples 
likely resulted from improved bonding between the 
polymer matrix and graphene sheets. These attributes 
are essential for developing flexible supercapacitors 
and wearable energy storage devices [13].
Scalability and yield analysis
The hybrid synthesis method also demonstrated prac-
tical advantages in production scalability. The CVD 
process alone yielded only 0.1–0.5 g/h of graphene, 
while chemical exfoliation and microwave reduction 
achieved yields of 5–10 g/h and 2–5 g/h, respectively. 
The hybrid method produced graphene at a rate of 
2–3 g/h but at a significantly lower cost—estimated to 
be 40% less than standalone CVD—due to reduced 
reaction time and enhanced process efficiency. These 
findings suggest that the hybrid method is not only 
effective in producing high-quality graphene but also 
economically feasible for industrial-scale applications.

Discussion
Structure–property relationships
The comparative analysis of different synthesis routes 
revealed a clear correlation between structural quality 
and electrochemical performance. Graphene synthe-
sized via the hybrid CVD–microwave method pre-
served the high structural integrity associated with 
CVD, as evidenced by a low ID/IG ratio (~ 0.1) and 
continuous sheet morphology, while benefiting from 
improved exfoliation and yield due to microwave-
assisted post-treatment. This unique combination 
minimized defect concentration and promoted uni-
form charge transport pathways, critical for high-per-
formance supercapacitor electrodes.
SEM and Raman characterization demonstrated 
that hybrid samples retained the long-range order of 
CVD graphene while gaining flexibility and increased 
accessible surface area. These features directly trans-
lated to enhanced conductivity (~  106 S/m), high spe-
cific capacitance, and low charge-transfer resistance. 
DFT simulations confirmed that lower defect densi-
ties support delocalized electronic structures, while 
FEM modeling indicated reduced stress accumulation 
in polymer–graphene composites, supporting their 
potential for flexible and mechanically robust energy 
storage devices [16].
Electrochemical enhancement 
through hybridization
The integration of graphene with conductive poly-
mers (polyaniline and polypyrrole) and metal oxides 
 (MnO2,  RuO2) resulted in electrodes exhibiting both 
electric double-layer capacitance and pseudocapaci-
tive behavior. The combination of these mechanisms 
enabled specific capacitance values up to 500 F/g and 
an energy density of 120 Wh/kg, with power densities 
exceeding 70 kW/kg. These values not only surpass the 
performance of conventional carbon-based superca-
pacitors but also rival those of expensive  RuO2-based 
systems.
The hybrid architecture allowed efficient charge 
separation and ion diffusion, as reflected by the rec-
tangular CV profiles and minimal IR drop observed 
in GCD curves. Furthermore, the use of ionic liq-
uid electrolytes contributed to the stability of the 
electrode–electrolyte interface, enhancing cyclic

Machine learning as a tool for synthesis 
optimization
One of the most significant innovations in this study 
was the incorporation of machine learning (ML) for 
predictive synthesis optimization. The neural network 
model, trained on a comprehensive dataset of over 
100 experimental runs, enabled accurate prediction 
of optimal processing conditions for maximizing con-
ductivity, minimizing defects, and increasing yield. 
The model achieved excellent accuracy (R2 = 0.94) 
and translated successfully into experimental valida-
tion with less than 3% deviation in key performance 
metrics.
The ability to predict synthesis outcomes before 
experimental execution significantly reduces the 
need for trial-and-error and enhances reproducibil-
ity. Moreover, this approach paves the way for the 
development of closed-loop synthesis platforms, 
where in situ diagnostics such as Raman or thermal 
feedback could be integrated into the ML optimiza-
tion framework to further automate and refine mate-
rial production.

costs. By integrating microwave-assisted post-treat-
ment, the hybrid approach retains the structural 
advantages of CVD while significantly improving 
production efficiency and economic feasibility.
Empirical data indicate that the hybrid process 
achieves graphene yields of approximately 2–3 g 
per hour—substantially higher than standalone

Comparison to state of the art
Relative to existing studies, this work provides several 
unique contributions. First, it is among the few reports 
to successfully implement ML for predictive synthe-
sis of graphene with subsequent experimental valida-
tion. Second, the hybrid synthesis approach balances 
material quality, yield, and cost—an often-overlooked 
trio in graphene production studies. Finally, the cou-
pling of graphene with polymers and oxides in a scal-
able, ML-guided framework positions this work as a 
blueprint for future development of multifunctional, 
high-performance, and commercially viable electrode 
materials.
Unlike many previous efforts focused on either 
conductivity or capacitance, this study systematically 
addressed structure–function relationships, scalability, 
and techno-economic metrics. These holistic insights 
make the proposed approach not just scientifically 
sound, but also practically translatable.
Limitations and future work
Despite its strengths, the current synthesis approach 
presents some limitations. Uniformity in microwave 
treatment remains a scale-up challenge due to local-
ized heating effects, which may lead to non-uniform 
reduction in large-area films. Furthermore, the current 
study utilized ex situ characterization to validate ML 
predictions; incorporating real-time feedback mecha-
nisms would enhance adaptive process control and 
offer dynamic learning capabilities.
Future research should explore the integration of 
in situ monitoring tools such as Raman spectroscopy 
or thermal imaging into the ML framework to enable 
real-time process corrections. Moreover, expanding 
the scope of optimization to include environmental 
impact and life-cycle assessments will provide a more 
comprehensive view of sustainability. Lastly, develop-
ing flexible supercapacitor prototypes based on these 
graphene–polymer hybrid electrodes will be an impor-
tant step toward practical implementation in wearable 
and conformable electronics [21].

Conclusion and future directions
This study demonstrates a machine learning-guided 
hybrid synthesis framework for producing high-per-
formance graphene-based electrodes optimized for 
supercapacitor applications. By integrating chemi-
cal vapor deposition (CVD) with microwave-assisted 
reduction, we successfully addressed the persistent 
trade-off between graphene quality and production 
scalability. Raman analysis confirmed a low defect 
density for CVD controls (ID/IG = 0.10 [± SD]), while 
the hybrid approach achieved ID/IG = 0.29 [± SD] 
with high electrical conductivity (~ 9.6 × 105 S  m−1), 
maintaining structural integrity and thermal sta-
bility. The machine learning model, trained on an 
extensive experimental dataset, reliably predicted 
synthesis conditions, thereby enhancing reproduc-
ibility and yield.
The hybrid electrodes, further engineered with 
metal oxides  (MnO2,  RuO2) and conductive polymers 
(polyaniline, polypyrrole), delivered specific capaci-
tances of up to 500 F  g−1, energy densities reaching 
120 Wh  kg−1, and power densities of 70 kW  kg−1. 
Notably, they retained more than 95% capacitance 
after 10,000 charge–discharge cycles, underscoring 
excellent durability. These results confirm the scal-
ability and multifunctionality of this hybrid synthe-
sis route for next-generation energy storage devices.
Beyond performance metrics, a techno-economic 
analysis indicated a 40% cost reduction compared 
with conventional CVD while maintaining compat-
ibility with roll-to-roll manufacturing. This conver-
gence of material quality, cost-effectiveness, and 
scalability highlights the potential of the hybrid 
strategy for practical deployment in electric trans-
portation, grid-level storage, and flexible electronics.
Future research should prioritize improving the 
uniformity of microwave-assisted processing at 
industrial scales and incorporating real-time diag-
nostics—such as in situ Raman spectroscopy—for 
adaptive, closed-loop process control. The inte-
gration of solid-state or gel electrolytes with gra-
phene–polymer hybrids could further enable flexible 
and conformable energy storage systems. Extending 
the machine learning framework to include sustain-
ability metrics, such as environmental impact and 
life-cycle energy costs, will also enhance its indus-
trial applicability.
In summary, this work establishes a predictive and 
scalable synthesis strategy that unites machine learn-
ing, hybrid material engineering, and cost-effective 
processing. It lays the foundation for the advance-
ment and commercialization of high-efficiency, 
polymer-integrated graphene-based energy storage 
technologies.
