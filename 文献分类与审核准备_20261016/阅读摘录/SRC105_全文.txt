ABSTRACT
Uncontrolled bleeding under wet and deformable conditions remains a major challenge for conventional he -
mostatic materials, which often suffer from limited tissue adhesion and poor mechanical adaptability. Here, we 
developed an injectable sodium alginate hydrogel ionically cross-linked with Ca
2
⁺ and reinforced with hazelnut 
branch-derived biochar (HB) to improve structural stability and local hemostatic performance. The HB- 
containing hydrogel exhibited pronounced shear-thinning behavior, high injectability, enhanced wet tissue 
adhesion, and self-healing efficiency exceeding 80%, while achieving an adhesive strength of approximately 
420 kPa. Physicochemical characterization indicated homogeneous HB incorporation and Ca
2
⁺ retention by HB, 
which may influence the local ionic environment of the hydrogel network. In vitro studies demonstrated good 
cytocompatibility and hemocompatibility. In hemostatic assays, 6A7C-HB exhibited a blood clotting index (BCI) 
of 4.45%, markedly lower than that of the commercial oxidized cellulose hemostat Surgicel ® (26.98%), and 
shortened the clotting time to 4.4 min compared with 7.5 min for the untreated blood control and 7.2 min for 
Surgicel ® . In vivo evaluation in rat tail transection and liver injury models showed effective hemorrhage control, 
reducing blood loss by approximately 45 – 55% compared with untreated controls, while subcutaneous implan -
tation demonstrated acceptable biocompatibility without evidence of significant systemic toxicity or adverse 
histopathological responses. These findings demonstrate that HB reinforcement provides a simple and effective 
strategy for developing injectable alginate hydrogels with improved mechanical adaptability and localized he -
mostatic performance under wet and deformable conditions.
1. Introduction
Uncontrolled hemorrhage remains a major cause of mortality in 
trauma and surgical settings [ 1 , 2 ]. Therefore, effective hemostatic de -
vices are needed to promote rapid coagulation of blood, wound 
covering, and improved clinical outcomes. Among these, hemostatic 
hydrogels have attracted attention due to their good biocompatibility 
and ability to maintain a moist wound environment [ 3 , 4 ]. They are 
typically constructed from natural polysaccharides (e.g., chitosan and 
alginate) [ 5 , 6 ] and synthetic polymers such as polyvinyl alcohol, poly -
ethylene oxide and its derivative copolymers, and polyacrylate [ 7 ].
In addition to their hemostatic properties, features such as inject -
ability and self-healing are important in the design of multifunctional 
hydrogels, as they provide a significant advantage in medical practice

[ 2 , 8 ]. For example, their ability to conform to irregular geometries of 
wounds makes them suitable for use in minimally invasive treatments. 
However, when applied in a physiological environment, hydrogel 
structures are generally susceptible to deformation or mechanical 
damage caused by body movements or external pressure, which may 
reduce their functional performance [ 9 ]. To address these issues, 
injectable [ 10 ] and self-healing [ 11 ] hydrogels have been suggested. 
Self-healing hydrogels incorporate reversible dynamic interactions that 
enable the hydrogel network to repair itself upon damage, thereby 
enhancing durability and functionality in medical applications [ 12 ]. 
Nevertheless, maintaining structural integrity during injection and 
subsequent deformation remains an important challenge [ 13 ]. To 
address this limitation, reinforcing phases such as carbon-based nano -
materials [ 14 ], metal ions [ 15 ], and small molecules [ 16 ] can be 
incorporated into the hydrogel network. For locally applied hemostatic 
materials, sufficient contact with wet and mechanically dynamic tissue 
surfaces is also important for maintaining the material at the bleeding 
site [ 17 , 18 ].
Herein, we present a multifunctional hydrogel platform composed of 
99% natural components and designed to combine injectability, struc -
tural stability, self-healing, wet-surface retention, and localized hemo -
static performance. To enhance structural stability and recovery while 
preserving injectability, sustainably sourced hazelnut branch biochar 
(HB) was produced through controlled carbonization and activation 
processes and incorporated as a particulate reinforcing phase ( Fig. 1 a). 
Sodium alginate (SA), used as the polymer matrix, was stabilized with an 
ionic cross-linking strategy using Ca
2
⁺ ions. A small amount of poly -
vinylpyrrolidone (PVP) was added to increase the mechanical and 
chemical stability of the network by forming interchain hydrogen 
bonding interactions ( Fig. 1 b). The central question of this study was 
whether HB incorporation could improve resistance to mechanical 
disruption and structural recovery of the ionically cross-linked SA/PVP 
hydrogel while preserving the shear-thinning injectability required for 
application to irregular wound surfaces. Despite a water content of 97%, 
the resulting hydrogel exhibited high structural integrity and could be 
injected and conform to irregular surfaces ( Fig. 1 c). HB incorporation 
improved the self-healing behavior of the hydrogel, allowing it to 
maintain its functional integrity after mechanical damage ( Fig. 1 d). The 
system showed retention on wet tissue surfaces and hemostatic activity 
( Fig. 1 e). In a conceptual use case ( Fig. 1 f), the hydrogel was shown to 
conform to irregular wound surfaces following minimally invasive in -
jection, maintain local contact, and provide a physical barrier at the 
bleeding site. The incorporation of HB into the ionically cross-linked SA/ 
PVP hydrogel resulted in a multifunctional material that combines 
structural integrity, self-healing, tissue contact, and hemostatic perfor -
mance on a single platform. Owing to its simple fabrication process and 
predominantly natural composition, the hydrogel shows potential for 
biomedical applications that require injectable and locally acting he -
mostatic materials.

Detailed descriptions of the materials, instrumentation, experi -

2.1. Preparation of hazelnut branch biochar
Hazelnut branch-derived biochar (HB) was obtained from the 
hazelnut branch via carbonization and activation. The hazelnut branch 
was milled ( ≤ 250 μ m), carbonized in a horizontal tube furnace under 
nitrogen (10
◦
C/min to 900
◦
C, 1 h). For chemical activation, the carbon 
precursor was mixed with potassium hydroxide (KOH) at a 3:1 wt ratio 
and activated under the same furnace conditions at 800
◦
C. Residual 
KOH was removed by washing with 1 M HCl until the pH reached 
neutrality, followed by drying at 60
◦
C for 48 h ( Fig. 1 a).

2.3. Rheological and mechanical characterization
Rheological measurements were performed at 37
◦
C using a parallel- 
plate geometry to evaluate the viscoelastic properties of the hydrogels. 
The linear viscoelastic region was determined by amplitude sweeps, 
followed by frequency sweep measurements to evaluate storage 
modulus (G ′ ), loss modulus (G ″ ), and complex viscosity. Creep-recovery 
and cyclic step-strain tests were conducted to assess structural recovery 
and self-healing behavior. Injectability was qualitatively evaluated by 
syringe extrusion and quantitatively determined by measuring the 
extrusion force required to deliver 1.5 mL of hydrogel through a 21 G 
blunt-tip needle. Adhesive strength was measured by lap shear testing 
according to ASTM F2255 – 05, while wet tissue adhesion was further 
evaluated using freshly excised chicken skin and bovine liver. For the 
selected 6A7C and 6A7C-HB formulations, additional lap-shear mea -
surements were performed on PBS-prewetted and gelatin-coated tissue- 
mimicking model substrates.

2.4. In Vitro biological evaluation
Cytocompatibility was evaluated using human dermal fibroblasts 
cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/ 
streptomycin. Cells were exposed to hydrogel extracts for 24 h, and 
viability was determined using the MTT assay. Hemocompatibility was 
assessed by hemolysis analysis using bovine erythrocytes. The hemo -
static activity of the hydrogels was evaluated by determining the blood 
coagulation index and blood clotting time using recalcified whole blood.
2.5. In Vivo evaluation
Adult Wistar albino rats (250 – 300 g) were used for in vivo studies 
following approval by the KOBAY DHL A.S ¸ . Local Ethics Committee for 
Animal Experiments (Approval No. 779). Biocompatibility was evalu -
ated using a subcutaneous implantation model, with animals sacrificed 
at 7, 14, and 28 days for histopathological, hematological, and serum 
biochemical analyses. Hemostatic efficacy was assessed in independent 
animal cohorts using rat tail transection and liver injury models by 
measuring bleeding time and blood loss after hydrogel application.

3.2. Design and characterization of HB-integrated hydrogels
The hydrogel was designed to deliver hemostasis, injectability, self- 
healing, and wet adhesion within a predominantly natural composition. 
The network is stabilized by Ca
2
⁺ coordination to alginate carboxylates 
[ 26 ], while a 0.71% (w/v) PVP can provide additional hydrogen-bond 
bridges and enhances flexibility ( Fig. 3 a). HB was selected for its sus -
tainable origin, high surface area, and chemically active surface 
(-COOH, -OH, C ═ O,
–
– , -NH) capable of forming hydrogen bonds with 
alginate/PVP and π - π interactions with their π -rich domains [ 27 , 28 ]. 
The abundance of adsorption sites on HB can also partially sequester 
Ca
2
⁺ , locally modulating cross-link density [ 29 ]. This interaction may 
alter the local availability of Ca
2
⁺ involved in alginate cross-linking.
A 12-formulation screen (SA 4% vs 6%; CaCl ₂ 0.5/0.6/0.7%; ± HB) 
identified compositions balancing injectability, mechanical integrity, 
self-healing, and hemostasis. FTIR confirmed the retention of charac -
teristic SA/PVP bands, including the C ═ O stretching of PVP at ~1650 
cm ⁻
1
, the broad O-H stretching band of alginate around ~3400 cm ⁻
1
, the 
C-O-C stretching of alginate at ~1020 cm ⁻
1
, and the C-N stretching of 
PVP near ~1290 cm ⁻
1
, indicating the noncovalent incorporation of HB 
( Figure S2 ). Formulated gels are at near-neutral pH (6.68 ± 0.14), which 
is in the physiological pH range [ 30 ]. Contact angle measurements 
indicated a modest, consistent increase with HB incorporation 
( Figure S3 ). To evaluate colloidal stability and HB ’ s electrostatic impact, 
the ζ -potential was measured ( Figure S4 ). HB shifted the ζ -potential to 
more negative values across compositions; higher SA (4 – 6%) and Ca
2
⁺ 
(0.5 – 0.7%) amplified this effect, indicating enhanced electrostatic sta -
bilization. We attribute the shift to increased surface density of poly -
anionic carboxylates at higher SA, and partial Ca
2
⁺ binding at HB 
oxygenated functional groups [ 31 ], which could reduce Ca
2
⁺ -mediated 
charge screening of alginate carboxylates at the interface. Notably, the 
6A7C-HB formulation reached   37 mV, the most stable (| ζ | > 30 mV 
[ 32 ]) among the tested gels and therefore selected for subsequent per -
formance studies. The more negative ζ -potential indicates improved 
colloidal stability of hydrogel dispersion. This enhanced stability may 
facilitate homogeneous HB distribution within the hydrogel matrix and 
contribute to the reproducibility of its mechanical performance.
SEM imaging of cross-sections ( Figure S5 ) showed denser, more 
connected microstructures with HB and uniform particulate dispersion 
without aggregation. A notable feature in the Energy Dispersive Spec -
troscopy (EDS) analysis is the decreased intensity of Ca signals in the HB- 
containing sample, suggesting altered local Ca distribution associated 
with HB incorporation. ( Figure S6 ) This observation is further supported 
by Inductively Coupled Plasma (ICP) analysis. In a 0.7% CaCl ₂ solution 
containing 5 mg of HB, ICP analysis indicated that HB retained 
approximately 30% of the available calcium. This observation is 
consistent with previous reports showing that oxygen-containing surface 
groups on biochar can participate in Ca
2
⁺ binding through surface 
complexation and ion-exchange interactions [ 33 ]. Because Ca
2
⁺ avail -
ability strongly influences alginate junction-zone formation and mo -
lecular assembly [ 34 ], HB-mediated Ca
2
⁺ retention may locally affect the 
ionic environment and effective cross-linking of the alginate network.
3.3. Functional performance and mechanical adaptability of the hydrogel 
system
The viscoelastic behavior of the hydrogel was characterized by 
amplitude and frequency sweep tests. Amplitude sweep analysis showed 
that increasing SA concentration from 4% to 6% shifted the G ′ /G ″ 
crossover point to higher strain values, indicating enhanced resistance to 
network disruption ( Figure S7 ). This can be attributed to the higher 
density of ionic crosslinking and increased polymer chain entanglement. 
Notably, HB addition further delayed the crossover point for both SA 
concentrations, suggesting that interactions associated with HB rein -
force the network and increase its structural stability. Based on ampli -
tude sweep results, a strain value of 0.1% was selected for subsequent 
frequency sweep analyses. Frequency sweep analysis ( Figure S8 ) 
confirmed the hydrogel's pseudoplastic, shear-thinning behavior 
( Fig. 3 b), which is advantageous for injectability. The addition of HB

These values far exceed those reported for typical SA-carboxymethyl

particularly relevant for hemostatic applications, where maintaining 
intimate contact with wet tissue is essential for effective sealing of the

According to the data obtained from rheological and lap shear tests, 
increasing SA concentration from 4% to 6% and the addition of HB 
enhanced the resistance of the hydrogel network to mechanical 
disruption and its dynamic stability, resulting in a more cohesive, inte -
grated structure that can effectively distribute energy under deforma -
tion. In addition, among the samples containing 6% SA, the sample 
coded 6A7C-HB, which had a zeta potential of   37.2 mV, was more 
stable; therefore, subsequent studies were conducted using the 6A7C-HB 
sample. To further assess the behavior of the selected formulations 
under wet and tissue-mimicking conditions, lap-shear testing was 
repeated on PBS-prewetted and gelatin-coated model substrates. Under 
PBS-prewetted conditions, the lap-shear strength increased from 130.6 
± 14.4 kPa for 6A7C to 292.5 ± 42.0 kPa for 6A7C-HB ( Figure S10a ), 
while on gelatin-coated substrates it increased from 164.1 ± 10.2 kPa to 
421.5 ± 34.8 kPa ( Figure S10b ). These quantitative results were com -
plemented by ex vivo observations on wet biological tissues.
As shown in Figure S11 , the 6A7C-HB retained its interfacial integ -
rity under wet, tissue-compatible conditions, forming continuous ad -
hesive interfaces with both beef liver and chicken skin, unlike 6A7C. The 
incorporation of HB imparted the hydrogel with the ability to adhere 
strongly to soft, hydrated tissues, which were absent in the HB-free 
counterpart. Furthermore, when two pieces of chicken skin were 
placed with a small gap between the layers, the hydrogel successfully 
bridged the gap and bonded the tissues together. A similar result was 
observed for two separate pieces of beef liver, which were firmly joined 
through the hydrogel interface. Moreover, 6A7C-HB was shown to 
maintain interfacial contact during stretching, bending, and twisting on 
ex vivo chicken skin without prior drying ( Fig. 3 e). These observations 
indicate that HB incorporation improves the ability of the hydrogel to 
maintain stable contact with wet and deformable tissue surfaces, which 
is important for localized hemostatic applications. Maintaining adhesion 
under mechanical deformation is advantageous for applications in dy -
namic tissues that undergo continuous movement.
Self-healing properties of 6A7C-HB and 6A7C after deformation were 
evaluated by macroscopic observations and rheological measurements. 
Visual test ( Fig. 3 f) showed that after the hydrogel pieces, which were 
initially separated by cutting, a significant integration was observed 
starting from the second day. At this stage, an intermediate, orange- 
toned zone formed at the joint line of the pieces, which were red on 
one side and white on the other. After five days, the structural integrity 
was largely recovered. Self-healing capacity was also confirmed by 
stepped strain cycling tests ( Figure S12 ). At high strain, G ′ decreased 
rapidly with structural deterioration, while recovery was observed at 
low strain. Quantitative analysis indicated a statistically significant 
improvement in recovery (64% for 6A7C and > 80% with HB, Fig. 3 g). 
This value demonstrates that 6A7C-HB exhibits self-healing properties, 
exceeding the threshold accepted in the literature [ 37 , 38 ]. The increase 
in self-healing capacity complements the ease of injection and tissue 
adhesion performance. As shown in Fig. 3 h, the 6A7C-HB hydrogel 
displays a characteristic nonlinear strain response and remarkable 
self-recovery. This behavior arises from its intrinsic viscoelasticity [ 39 ], 
indicating enhanced elasticity yet limited deformability due to a 
strengthened network architecture. All these results support the role of 
HB contribution in network structure at both structural and functional 
levels. The improved rheological behavior and self-healing performance 
indicate that HB incorporation enhances the mechanical stability of the 
hydrogel while preserving its dynamic nature. ICP analysis further 
showed that HB adsorbs Ca
2
⁺ under the experimental conditions, sug -
gesting a possible influence on the local ionic environment. The mo -
lecular basis of these interactions, however, remains to be elucidated.
3.4. In Vitro hemostatic performance and biocompatibility
The 6A7C-HB was designed to address the demands of bleeding 
control and wound interface compatibility through a synergy of material 
chemistry, supramolecular interactions, and mechanical adaptability. In 
vitro tests targeting hemocompatibility, hemostatic activity, and cyto -
compatibility were conducted and interpreted in the context of the 
hydrogel ’ s physicochemical and structural features to assess its biolog -
ical functionality.
Fig. 4 a presents the percentage cell viability values obtained after 
24 h of incubation with the hydrogel film extracts, indicating the cyto -
toxicity of the samples compared to the control group. Cell viability in 
the 6A7C and 6A7C-HB groups was significantly reduced (P ≤ 0.05) 
compared to the control. The cell viability values were 100.03 ± 2.31% 
for the control, 86.11 ± 1.75% for sample 6A7C, and 87.81 ± 0.90% for 
6A7C-HB. Since cell viability remained above 70% in both samples, they 
are considered non-toxic [ 40 ]. To support these findings, cell mor -
phologies were imaged at the 24-hour mark before MTT application 
( Fig. 4 a). Morphological evaluations revealed cell densities similar to 
the control group, although slight detachment and rounding, indicative 
of cell stress, were occasionally observed.
The hemolysis values obtained following the incubation of red blood

3.39%, 0.01 ± 0.54%, 1.64 ± 0.76%, 1.38 ± 0.55%, and 48.37 ±
4.47%, respectively. Both 6A7C and 6A7C-HB exhibited hemolysis 
levels below 5%, with no significant difference compared with the 
negative control, confirming their favorable hemocompatibility. This 
property is particularly advantageous for hydrogels intended for direct 
contact with blood or damaged tissue. The combination of negligible 
hemolysis and rapid clot formation indicates that the hydrogel supports 
hemostatic function without causing detectable damage to erythrocytes. 
In contrast, Surgicel ® showed a significantly higher hemolysis value 
than both hydrogel formulations (p < 0.0001). The comparatively high 
hemolysis observed for Surgicel ® is consistent with the known physi -
cochemical behavior of oxidized regenerated cellulose (ORC). The car -
boxylic acid groups of ORC generate a low-pH microenvironment upon 
contact with aqueous media and blood, which can induce erythrocyte 
lysis and promote the conversion of released hemoglobin to acid he -
matin, contributing to the characteristic dark discoloration of ORC- 
based hemostats [ 41 ]. Importantly, Wu et al. reported a hemolysis 
ratio of 56.9% for ORC, which is comparable to the 48.37% observed for 
Surgicel ® in our study. The markedly lower hemolytic activity of 6A7C 
and 6A7C-HB therefore indicates that the developed hydrogels can 
support hemostatic function while maintaining substantially greater 
erythrocyte compatibility under the tested conditions.

3.5. Biocompatibility and systemic response
Subcutaneous implantation of the 6A7C-HB induced a time- 
dependent systemic response over a 28-day observation period. Hema -
tological analysis revealed a temporary increase in white blood cell 
counts, peaking at day 14 and trending toward baseline by day 28, 
consistent with implantation-associated inflammatory adaptation 
( Figure S13 ). Erythrocyte-related parameters (red blood cell count, he -
moglobin, and hematocrit) remained stable across time points, with no 
evidence of sustained erythrocyte compromise relative to controls.
Serum biochemistry showed transient elevations in transaminases, 
with ALT increasing on day 14 and partially declining by day 28, while 
AST increased progressively and reached significance on day 28 
( Fig. 5 a). Notably, these changes were not accompanied by histopath -
ological abnormalities in hepatic tissue ( Fig. 5 c), supporting preserved 
organ architecture over the study period. Renal markers were consistent 
with maintained renal function, with creatinine remaining stable and 
BUN fluctuations not indicative of overt impairment ( Fig. 5 a).

3.6. In Vivo hemostatic evaluation
In both the rat tail amputation and liver trauma models, 6A7C-HB 
application resulted in a significant reduction in total blood loss 
compared with the control group ( Fig. 5 d). Quantitatively, the hydrogel 
reduced blood loss by approximately 50 – 55% in the tail transection 
model and by ~45 – 50% in the liver injury model, demonstrating robust 
bleeding control across both superficial and highly vascularized tissues. 
Notably, blood loss was efficiently limited even in the highly vascular -
ized liver injury model, highlighting the capacity of 6A7C-HB to control 
hemorrhage under challenging conditions locally. Across all experi -
mental groups, total blood loss remained below 10% of estimated total 
blood volume, indicating that the observed effects were localized and 
non-life-threatening.
Taken together, these findings demonstrate that 6A7C-HB combines 
effective local bleeding control with an acceptable biocompatibility 
profile, characterized by controlled systemic responses and preserved 
hematological and biochemical parameters. Within this context, the 
observed reduction in blood loss supports the potential of 6A7C-HB as a 
locally acting hemostatic material. Importantly, this level of hemorrhage 
control was achieved without evidence of systemic toxicity or patho -
logical alterations in coagulation parameters, indicating a balanced 
profile of efficacy and safety.
4. Conclusion
An injectable alginate hydrogel reinforced with hazelnut branch- 
derived biochar was developed to address hemostasis under wet and 
mechanically dynamic conditions. Biochar incorporation improved the 
mechanical adaptability of the hydrogel while preserving its inject -
ability, leading to enhanced self-healing behavior, wet tissue adhesion, 
and structural stability. The optimized formulation also exhibited rapid 
blood clot formation, low hemolytic activity, and good cytocompati -
bility. Importantly, the hydrogels showed a markedly lower BCI and 
shorter clotting time than the commercial hemostatic material Surgicel ® 
under the tested in vitro conditions. In vivo evaluation further demon -
strated effective local hemorrhage control together with acceptable local 
and systemic biocompatibility. These improvements were achieved 
through a simple ionic cross-linking strategy combined with biochar 
reinforcement, without requiring additional chemical modification of 
the polymer network. This approach provides a practical means of 
improving the functional properties of alginate-based hydrogels while 
maintaining a predominantly natural composition. The combination of 
injectability, mechanical adaptability, wet tissue adhesion, and hemo -
static performance highlights the potential of this material for localized 
bleeding management and other biomedical applications requiring 
mechanically resilient hydrogel systems.
