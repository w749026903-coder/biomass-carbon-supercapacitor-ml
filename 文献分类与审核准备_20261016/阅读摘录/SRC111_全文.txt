Novel hierarchical porous carbon materials derived from bamboo shoot shells  
for high-performance symmetric supercapacitor 
 
Fanen Zeng Yaning Zhang Qi Lv Hao Xie Bing Xu* 
 
*Corresponding author 
xb@djtu.edu.cn 
 
School of Environmental and Chemical Engineering, Dalian Jiao Tong University, Dalian 116021, China 
 
 
Abstract 
Porous carbon material s were synthesized from bamboo shoot shells through carbonization and static air 
activation.  The physicochemical properties of bamboo shoot shell -based porous carbon (BBC) were observed 
by scanning electron microscopy, X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, X -ray photoelectron spectroscopy, 
and N 2 adsorption desorption analysis. BBC has an optim al hierarchical porous structure that displays a 
specific surface area of 546.04 m 2 g-1 and an average pore diameter of 3.28 nm.  The formation mechanism 
of the BBC, which has a hierarchical porous structure in static air activation, was illustrated systematically. 
To evaluate the suitability of BBC for practical implementations further, the constructed symmetric 
supercapacitor achieved an energy density of 11.11 Wh kg -1 at a power density of 252 W kg -1 and an  
outstanding cyclic retention rate of 100% over 7,000 cycles at a current density of 10 A g -1. 
 
Keywords Supercapacitor Bamboo shoot shells  Hierarchical porous structure  Static air activation  
 
1 Introduction 
With the progress of society and the rapid growth of the economy, environmental pollution caused by the 
increase in energy consumption has seriously threatened human health and environmental safety, giving 
more and more attention to green, environmental protection, and renewable energy. As a result , sustainable, 
eco-friendly, and renewable energies are becoming increasingly popular. Therefore, utilizing renewable 
energy sources and designing advanced energy storage systems are imperative to meet the rising demand 
for energy  [1]. Supercapacitors have  attracted c onsidera ble interest as one of the most promising new energy 

===== PDF PAGE 2 =====
2 
storage systems because of their fast charge and discharge rates  [2,3], considerable instantaneous power 
density, stable cycle reliability, excellent energy conversion efficiency, and extensive operating temperature 
range  [4-7]. Importantly, the electrode material has a substantial effect on the properties of supercapacitors. 
Thus, advancing high -performance electrode materials is crucial for optimizing the application of 
supercapacitors.  
Over the past decade, activated carbon materials have gained prominence in electrode materials 
research owing to their extensive specific surface area, controllable pore size, and remarkable stability. 
Unfortunately, the single microporous structure and po or electrical conductivity of activated carbon affect 
its electrochemical performance. Hence, infiltrating their inner pores fully by electrolyte is difficult, leading 
to lower specific capacitance and energy density  [8,9]. Ion diffusion and charge propaga tion would be 
facilitated in the porous carbon by the synergistic combination of micropores and mesopores  [10,11]. In 
addition, improving the electrical conductivity of carbon materials is conducive to the rapid transport of 
ions within the electrode. Thus, the preparation of carbon materials with high electrical conductivity and 
appropriate pore size structur e remains critical.  
Recent ly, researchers have explored several new carbon -based materials with better electrical 
conductivity and suitable pore size structures. In these materials, biomass carbon materials have elicited 
attention  for their sustainability and renewability and exhibited excellent electrochemical performance  
because of their natural porous structure and high electrical conductivity that  collectively contribute to their 
superior electrochemical properties  [12,13]. Examples of these materials include  barley straw  [14], lotus 
hemp [15], walnut shells  [16], and pleurotus eryngii  [17]. Among these materials, bamboo shoot shel ls, as 
a renewable biomass resource, possess a unique porous skeleton structure that contributes to the formation 
of porous carbon materials.  Furthermore , bamboo shoot shell s are not only environmentally friendly but 
also have a short growth cycle that ensures  efficient resource utilization . Consequently, porous carbon 
materials derived from bamboo shoot shells show considerable  potential for the fabrication of high -
performance double -layer capacitors  [18]. In previous studies, bamboo -based carbon materials  prepared by 
various activation methods used in supercapacitors have proven to be excellent electrode materials. Yang et 
al. used bamboo shoot shells as a precursor that underwent a series of treatments, high -temperature 
carbonization, and potassium hydrox ide (KOH) activation.  The obtained results showed that  the specific 
surface area of activated carbon was 1129.4 m 2 g-1, and its specific capacitance when the current density 
was 0.1 A g -1 was 173.2 F g -1 [19]. Abb as et al. prepared SiC/N double -doped bio-renewable carbon materials 

===== PDF PAGE 3 =====
3 
from bamboo via template method, and the energy density was 5.41 Wh kg -1 when the power density was 0.5 
kW kg -1 [20]. In contrast, although activated carbon with excellent electrochemical performance can be prepared 
via chemical activation and template methods , the consumption  of pickling and water washing to remove 
residual impurities is high, and it is also faced with complex processes, high equipment requirements, and 
difficult environmental governance. Considering these factors, developing a low -cost, simple, green, and 
high-performance activated carbon preparation method becomes particularly necessary.  Static air activation 
fulfills all of these requirements. The method only needs to react in an air atmosphere without introducing 
other gases or chemicals. It must also be si mple to operate and should not involve complex gas handling 
and operating steps.  
Herein , porous carbon materials were synthesized through  static air activation. The influence of varied 
activation temperatures and times on their electrochemical performance was examined , and the 
electrochemical performance of the electrode materials was tested in symmetric supercapacitors under 
optimal conditions.  The results show that  the carbon materials derived from bamboo shoot shells via static 
air activation possess a high specific surface area and a structure dominated by mesopore , which enhances 
the ion transport between the electrolyte and the carbon material , thereby improving the electrochemical 
performance. 
 
2 Experimental method 
2.1 Materials 
The bamboo shoot shells were produced in Sichuan Province. KOH, hydrochloric acid (HCl), ethanol, PTFE 
(60 wt%), and acetylene black conductive carbon were purchased from Aladdin Reagent Company. 
Deionized water was used in all experiments.  
 
2.2 Synthesis of the materials 
The bamboo shoot s shells were cleaned , pulverized, and then dried  in an oven at 80 ℃ for 12 h. Then, they 
were transferred in a tube furnace filled with N 2 protection at 500 ℃ (heating  rate of 5 ℃ min -1 ) for 2 h 
to obtain BBC -500. Then, BBC -500 was moved  to a muffle furnace and activated at different temperatures  
(700 ℃, 800 ℃, and 900 ℃ at a heating rate of 5 ℃ min -1) for a duration of 0 h or 1 h . To remove 
impurities,  the obtained products were soaked in 1 M HCl for 12 h. The PH of the product was washed to 

===== PDF PAGE 4 =====
4 
neutral and dried at 80 ℃ for 12 h. The resulting porous carbon material was called BBC -Y-Z, where Y 
denotes the reaction temperature, and Z denotes the reaction duration.  
 
2.3 Characterization of the prepared materials 
The morphology of the sample was examined using SUPRA 55 Scanning Electron Microscope.  X-ray 
diffractometry using the Empyrean X -ray diffractometer characterized the crystal structure of the samples 
between 10 ° and 80 °. The degree of graphitization and defects within the samples were assessed between 
800 cm -1 and 2200 cm -1 using the XplpRA PLUS Raman spectrometer. Adsorption/desorption isotherm was 
measured using the ASAP 2020 system. The surface functional groups and elemental compositions of the 
samples were examined  using the Krotos AXIS Ultra DLD X -ray photoelectron spectrometer.  
 
2.4 Electrochemical characterization of the prepared material 
Porous carbon material (80 wt%), acetylene black conductive carbon (10 wt%), and polytetrafluoroethylene 
(10 wt%) were mixed to form a slurry. Afterward, the slurry was coated on foam nickel and dried at 80 ℃ 
for 12 h, with the mass of the porous carbon material being approximately 3 mg. Subsequently, the electrode 
material was closely contacted with foam nickel to prepare the working electrode. Within the three -electrode 
system, the saturated mercuric glycol electrode (Hg/HgO) was used as the refe rence electrode, the platinum 
mesh acted as the opposite electrode, and the nickel foam coated with the BBC functioned as the working 
electrode. I n addition, within the two -electrode system, the symmetric supercapacitor was assembled using 
a CR2032 shell t hat packaged two slices of porous carbon material coated on foam nickel and a cellulose 
paper separator, with the mass of porous carbon material being approximately 3 mg. The cyclic voltammetry 
(CV, 10 –200 mV s -1), constant current charge/discharge (GCD, 0.5 –5 F g -1), and alternating current 
impedance (EIS, 1  Hz–100 kHz) of the porous carbon materials were measured on a CHI660E 
electrochemical workstation .

3 Results and Discussion 
3.1 Characterization 
The morphological changes in the bamboo shoot shell -based porous carbon material s and their precursor s 
were observed by SEM. After carbonization, the outer surface of the bamboo shoot shells is distributed with 
folds, but its inner surface remains to be very smooth (Fig. 1a). Due to the energy storage mechanism of the 
double electric layer capacitor, having enough time to complete the electrochemical reaction after ion 
adsorption becomes difficult.  Many regularly arranged  vascular bundles with a diameter of  3–5 µm can be 
observed in the sliced images of bamboo shoot shells, indicating that the carbonized bamboo shoot shell 
still retains the vascular bundle structure (Fig. 1 b and c).  The regular vascular bundles and ridged folds on 
the bamboo shoot shell surface s were preserved after activation (Fig. 1d and e).  Abundant micropores and 
mesoporous were embedded in the vascular bundle, constituting an orderly honeycomb layered porous 
carbon structure  [23].  The activation temperature increased with the specific surface area, total pore volume, 
and average pore diameter of carbon materials (Table 1), indicating that carbon materials have abundant 
pore structure and loose layered structure, and the densificatio n degree of pore structure increases with the 
activation temperature. These mesopores not only provide channels for ion transport but also decrease the 
resistance of ion transport. When the carbon material is in full contact with the electrolyte, the free charge 
in the pore will be adsorbed and migrated by ions, thereby improving the performance of the supercapacitor.  
 
Fig. 1 SEM images of bamboo shoot shell -based porous carbon  material s and their precursor  
 
Figure 2a illustrates an XRD patterns of the bamboo shoot shell -based porous carbon materials and 
their precursors. None of the porous carbon materials  displayed sharp peaks, indicating that they were 
amorphous carbon. The diffraction peaks at 2 θ = 23° and 43°exhibit the characteristics of amorphous carbon, 
corresponding to the (002) crystal faces and (100) crystal faces, respectively.  By comparison, the 
characteristic peak at 23° is gradually enhanced and widened after activation, indicating that the order of 
atomic arrangement is destroyed during activation, thus improving the degree of graphitization.  The 

===== PDF PAGE 6 =====
6 
characteristic peak at 43° is related to the conductivity of the porous carbon material  [24].  The peak intensity 
strengthened with the increase in activation temperature, which further confirmed that the electrochemical 
characteristics of the porous carbon materials  were enhanced after activation.  
Raman characterization further elucidates the degree of defect of the bamboo shoot shell -based porous 
carbon materials and their precursor s in Fig. 2b.  The characteristic D peaks at 1347 cm −1 and the G peaks 
at 1598 cm −1 are related to the lattice flaw and internal sp 2 hybrid stretching vibration of carbon atoms, 
respectively  [25].  The intensity ratio of the D -band and G -band (I D/IG) is an important index to estimate the 
degree of graphitization of carbon materials.  The ID/IG of BBC-500, BBC-700-0, BBC-800-0, BBC-900-0, and 
BBC-800-1 correspond to 0.73, 0.85, 0.89, 1.03, and 0.99, respectively. As the activation temperature 
increased, the intensity ratio also improved, indicating that the atomic arrangement is disordered. 
Furthermore, the D -band is shifted to the left, indicating an i ncrease in the degree of defects caused by 
activation strain  [26], and more defect points enhance the ion adsorption/desorption, implying that the 
electrochemical performance is improved.

deconvoluted into five peaks: C=C ( sp2, 284.63 eV, 36.26%), C -C (s p3, 284.98 eV, 28.71%), C -O (286.46 
eV, 14.09%), C=O (288.31 eV, 14.35%),  and O=C-O [28]. The O1s spectrum in Fig. 3c can be deconvoluted 
into three peaks: C=O (quinone type groups, 531.15  eV), C-O-C (ether groups, 532.89 eV), and O-H (phenol 
groups, 533.96 eV) [29]. The N1s spectrum in Fig. 3d can be deconvoluted into three peaks: Pyridin e N (N

(401.43 eV ). The appearance of the N1s peak indicates that the porous carbon material has successfully 
incorporated nitrogen, which means that the wettability of the electrode material in contact with the 
electrolyte has been improved, and the active contact area of the electrolyte has been expanded  [30]. The 
pseudo capacitance is generated by negatively charged pyrrole N and pyridine N, whereas the graphite N 
has electron transfer between carbon atoms, thus enhancing the electrical conductivity of the porous carbon  
materials . This instance contribut es to the operating  efficiency and cyclic stability of the supercapacitors

Structural characteristics and pore size are the critical factors that affect the electrical conductivity of 
carbon materials. Fig. 4 and Table 1 elucidate that N 2 adsorption and porosity characteristics and BJH pore 
size distribution of porous carbon materials and their precursors exhibited H4 hysteresis loops and type I/IV 
combined adsorption –desorption isotherms.  The adsorption curves in the low -frequency region are Near the 
Y axis, whereas the desorption curves in the middle -frequency region exhibit an inflection point, which is 
a unique characteristic of gas adsorpti on on microporous [32]. The existence of slit -shaped pores in the H4 
hysteresis loops indicates the existence of mesoporous. The pore structure of porous carbon  materials  are 
substantially influenced by activation temperature and time. As the activation temperature and time rise, the 
specific surface area of porous carbon material became 60 times larger than their precursor, and the average 
size expanded from 1.17 nm to 3.28 n m. This phenomenon indicates that the structure of porous carbon 
material was main ly mesopore.  Relative to other porous carbon materials, the adsorption capacity of BBC -
800-0 shows a larger proportion of mesopore, the pore size distribution mainly ranges from 2 nm to 4 nm, 
and an average pore size of 3.28 nm.  The results show that the coordinated interplay of pore formation, pore 
expansion, pore composition, and pore collapse contributes to the formation of BBC -800-0 porous structure  
[33].  Porous carbon materials with a high specific surface and a porous structure dominated by mesopores 
can provide numerous active sites, thereby enhancing ion transport and improving electrochemical 
properties [34].

3.2 Electrochemical characterization 
Figure 6 illustrates the electrochemical tests of bamboo shoot shell -based porous carbon  materials  and their 
precursors.  In Fig. 6a, the closed region of the CV curve reflects the energy storage capacity of the porous 
carbon materials . Notably, the closed region s of the CV curves for activation temperatures of 700 ℃ and 
900 ℃ are similar.  In contrast, porous carbons  materials  activated at 800 ℃ exhibit a larger closed CV 
curves area compared with other temperatures.  With the extension of activation time, the enclosed area 
gradually shrinks, indicating that BBC -800-0 exhibits optimal energy storage performance  [36].  Fig. 6b 
shows the symmetrical shape of the GCD curves with no significant IR drop, suggesting the excellent 
reversibility of the porous carbon. When the current density is 1 A g -1, the specific capacitance of BBC -800-
0 reaches a peak of 172.65 F g -1. The rate capability is shown in Fig. 6c, where data show that the specific 
capacitance of the porous carbon material decreases as the current density increases.  At a current density of 
0.5 A g-1, the specific capacitances for BBC-500, BBC-700-0, BBC-800-0, BBC-900-0, and  BBC-800-1 are 
52, 128.3 , 172.6 , and 138.6 F  g-1, respectively. When 5 A g-1 is added to their current densities, their specific 
capacitance changes to 27, 99, 139.5, 97.5, and 120.5 F g-1, with corresponding specific capacitance rates 
of 51.6% , 77.16% , 85.46% , 85.98 %, and 87% , respectively . The high specific capacitance and excellent

multiplication performance of BBC -800-0 are attributed to its increased specific surface area and porous 
structure dominated by mesoporous, which increase the effective contact area between the electrode and the 
electrolyte.  Refer to Fig. 6d to obtain a deeper understanding of the charge transport capacity of the porous 
carbon  materials . The linear slope in the low -frequency region is approaching  90°, indicating that the ion 
diffuses rapidly. The Nyquist semicirc le in the high -frequency region represents th e charge transfer process 
between the electrode/electrolyte, and the real X -axis intercept represents the equivalent series resistance 
value ( Rct) of the electrode.  By fitting calculation, the series resistance value of BBC -800-0(0.45 Ω) is the 
smallest, indicating that the combination of micropores/ mesoporous  reduces the ionic resistance in the 
electrolyte and facilitates the access of ions into the active site. The electrochemical test results show that 
BBC-800-0 has remarkable contact with the electrolyt e, and the ions can rapidly diffuse in the pores of the 
porous carbon  materials . This electrochemical performance is achieved because of the efficient utilization 
of the specific surface area of porous carbon materials.

Figure 7 illustrates  the electrochemical test of BBC -800-0 at different scanning rates.  In Fig. 7a, the 
CV curves begin to take on an ideal rectangular shape as the scanning rate increases, indicating that the 
BBC-800-0 has the optimal  double -layer capacitance characteristics.  Even at a scan rate of 200 mV s -1, the 
CV curve retains an approximately regular rectangular shape, which indicates that BBC -800-0 has 
satisfactory multiplicity rate capability and a double -layer capacitance energy storage mechanism  [37,38].  
In Fig. 7b, the GCD curve maintains a symmetrical triangle after a tenfold increase in current density, which 
indicates that the porous carbon materials has excellent charge –discharge efficiency and double -layer 
capacitance characteristics.

Although the low potential of the aqueous electrolyte limits the energy density of the double -layer 
supercapacitor, BBC-800-0 exhibits a superior energy density when contrasted with biomass -derived porous 
carbon materials (Table 2). Recent noteworthy findings are revealed in studies on biomass -derived carbon 
materials. For instance , porous carbon materials obtained from barley straw , activated using FeCl 3 and urea, 
demonstrates an energy density of 10.7 Wh kg -1 at a power density of 720 W kg -1 [14]. Similar ly, plantain -
based  porous carbon  materials , prepared through a static air activation, exhibits a specific capacitance of 8.6 
F g-1 at a current density of 4 A g -1 [15].  BBC-800-0 exhibits distinct advantages  in terms of electrochemical 
performance, pricing, and material procurement. The power density of this novel supercapacitor not only 
exceeds that of conventional batteries but also surpasses hybrid capacitors in delivering high -energy outputs. 
Notably, it d emonstrates a cycle retention rate of up to 100% amidst high -energy cycling.

4 Conclusions 
In summary, p orous carbon materials were synthesized from bamboo shoot shells through carbonization and 
static air activation.  BBC-800-0 exhibits a high specific surface area of 546.04  m2 g-1, a specific capacitance 
of 172.65  F g-1 at a current density of 0.5 A  g-1, and a specific capacitance rate of 85.46 %  upon increasing 
the current density tenfold. These values indicate its excellent multiplicative performance. Meanwhile,  the 
symmetric supercapacitor constructed with BBC -800-0 exhibits an energy density of 252 Wh  kg-1 at a 
power density energy of 11.11 W  kg-1 in 6 M KOH electrolyte and an outstanding cyclic retention rate of 
100% over 7,000 cycles at a current density of 10 A g-1. The excellent electrochemical performance of 
BBC-800-0 can be attributed to efficient ion transport and its predominantly mesoporous structure. These 
results show that the use of activated biomass carbon materials is promising for applications in 
supercapacitors.
