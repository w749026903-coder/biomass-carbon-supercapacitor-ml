Facile Molten Salt Synthesis of Sustainable Biomass-Derived Carbon 
for Electrochemical Energy Storage
Xiaosong Weia, Yanqing Caia,*, Yinyin Jina, Yun Jinga, Yalin Zhanga, and Xinggang Chena
a College of Materials Science and Engineering, North China University of Science and Technology, Tangshan, 063210 China
* e-mail: caiyanqing126@126.com
Received February 19, 2026; revised March 25, 2026; accepted March 27, 2026
Abstract—This research focuses on synthesizing biomass-derived porous carbon materials from soybean straw
and corn cob through a one-step molten salt carbonization -activation method, using three molten salt mix-
tures, namely NaCl–KCl, ZnCl 2–KCl, and ZnCl 2–NaCl, as both templates and activators. The synthesized
carbon materials were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD),
Raman spectroscopy, and Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) analyses. A series of electrochemical measure-
ment techniques were employed to investigate their three-electrode electrochemical performances and the
assembled symmetric supercapacitors. The results indicate that the soybean straw-derived carbon prepared
with the ZnCl
2–KCl mixture (denoted as ZK–S) exhibits a high specific capacitance of 197 F g –1  at a current
density of 0.1 A g –1  in a 6 M aqueous KOH electrolyte. The assembled symmetric supercapacitor device
exhibits a specific capacitance of 155 F g –1 , a power density of 50 W kg –1 , and an energy density of
12.84 W h kg–1  at 0.1 A g–1 . Moreover, the ZK–S carbon demonstrates remarkable cycling stability, retaining
over 105% of its capacity after 10 000 cycles, indicating a promising long cycle life. Therefore, this study
emphasizes the potential of soybean straw-derived carbon materials prepared by the molten-salt carboniza-
tion-activation method in promoting the development of high-performance supercapacitors.
Keywords: soybean straw, biomass-derived carbon, molten salt, supercapacitor, electrode materials
DOI: 10.1134/S0036024426701360
1. INTRODUCTION
With the excessive consumption of fossil fuels and
the increasing depletion of energy resources, carbon
dioxide emissions have been continuously rising,
making global warming increasingly severe. The
energy crisis and environmental crisis pose significant
threats to the survival and development of human
society. To address these issues, it is imperative to vig-
orously develop new technologies for energy conver-
sion and storage [1–5]. Energy storage devices such as
conventional capacitors [6], batteries [7], lithium-ion
batteries [8], fuel cells [9], and supercapacitors [10]
have garnered considerable attention from research-
ers. Among them, supercapacitors are regarded as one
of the most promising energy storage devices due to
their higher power density and superior cycle stability
compared to batteries, as well as higher energy density
than conventional capacitors [11]. Electrode materials
are key components of supercapacitors, and their per-
formance directly affects the overall performance and
efficiency of the energy storage devices [12]. Cur-
rently, carbon materials used in supercapacitors
include graphene [13], carbon nanotubes [14], carbon
nanofibers [15], activated carbon [16], and derived
porous carbon materials [17]. Notably, biomass-
derived porous carbon has gained prominence due to
its wide availability, low cost, and environmental
friendliness [18]. These characteristics enable it to
achieve excellent performance and meet the require-
ments of specific applications.
The energy storage performance of biomass-
derived porous carbon materials is primarily deter-
mined by their structural design. Key structural char-
acteristics include porosity, pore size, pore size distri-
bution, pore diameter, and pore length, all of which
affect the specific surface area, ion transport rate, of
the materials [19–21]. Biomass-derived activated car-
bon is typically prepared through chemical methods,
involving two steps: high-temperature carbonization
and activation. The carbonization process removes
volatile substances and hydrocarbons through high-
temperature pyrolysis, converting organic matter into
carbonaceous residues. The activation stage employs
activators (such as KOH) to construct a porous struc-
ture with a large specific surface area [22, 23]. There-
fore, the design of derived porous carbon structures
requires comprehensive consideration of multiple fac-
tors, such as the selection of precursor materials, car-
bonization conditions, preparation methods, template
types, and subsequent activation treatments. Adopting

tion of derived porous carbon materials, improving
their energy storage performance, and expanding their
application fields are the key objectives of porous car-

Molten salt has gained widespread use in the
preparation of porous carbon due to its dual role as a
template and activator, which simplifies the synthesis
process and improves efficiency [24–30]. Wang et al.
[31] employed chitosan as the carbon precursor (raw
material) and LiCl–ZnCl
2 molten salt as the template,
successfully fabricating hierarchically porous nitro-
gen-doped activated carbons via a simple one-step
carbonization method. Under a relatively low molten
salt/chitosan mass ratio (3 : 1) and a moderate calci-
nation temperature (1000 °C), the obtained porous
carbon material achieved a maximum specific surface
area of 1317 m
2 g–1  and a nitrogen content of 5.1 wt %.
Liu et al. [32] employed tobacco waste as the biomass
carbon precursor. The carbon material prepared by
carbonization in molten CaCl
2 at 850°C for 3 h exhib-
its a hierarchical porous structure and ideal capacitive
performance. In a three-electrode system with a 1 M
H
2SO4 aqueous electrolyte, the tobacco waste—
derived carbon delivers a specific capacitance of
196.5 F g –1  at 0.2 A g –1 . Moreover, the capacitance
retention rate remains 94% after 5000 charge-dis-
charge cycles at 0.1 A g –1 . Zhang et al. [33] using
ginkgo leaves as the raw material and CaCl 2–KCl
molten salt as the activator and template, N/S co-
doped porous carbon nanosheets were successfully
prepared. The material exhibits a specific surface area
of 1120 m2 g–1  and a specific capacitance of 318 F g –1
(1 A g–1 ) in a three-electrode system, along with excel-
lent rate capability (82% capacitance retention at
10 A g–1 ). Despite these advancements, most studies
have mainly focused on the structural regulation of a
single molten salt system. However, there is relatively
little research on the effects of molten salt carboniza-
tion and activation in different mixed molten salt sys-
tems and the underlying mechanisms. Therefore, it is
crucial to clarify the role of molten salt carbonization
in pore evolution and structural optimization of bio-
mass-derived carbon across different systems. Fur-
thermore, systematic comparative studies on one-step
molten salt carbonation—activation methods for spe-
cial cylindrical materials like soybean straw and corn
cob in various molten salt systems are still lacking, and
their potential structure-property correlations require
further investigation.
In this study, soybean straw and corn cob were
selected as biomass precursors, and porous carbon
materials for supercapacitor electrodes were prepared
via a one-step molten salt carbonization-activation
method. First, the biomass precursors underwent
impurity removal and washing. Subsequently, three
different molten salt systems, namely NaCl–KCl,
ZnCl
2–KCl, and ZnCl 2–NaCl, were mixed with the
pretreated biomass precursors by ball milling. Then,
the ball-milled mixtures underwent high-temperature
carbonization under an inert atmosphere. As a result,
corn cob-derived porous carbons and soybean straw-
derived porous carbons were obtained in NaCl–KCl,
ZnCl
2–KCl, and ZnCl 2–NaCl mixtures, respectively.
X-ray diffraction (XRD), Raman spectroscopy, scan-
ning electron microscopy (SEM), Brunauer–
Emmett–Teller (BET) specific surface area analysis,
and electrochemical measurement techniques were
employed to characterize the phase composition,
microstructure, pore size variation, and electrochem-
ical performance of the carbon materials. The pre-
pared corn cob-deriver porous carbons in ZnCl
2–KCl
melt exhibits excellent electrochemical performance
in 6 M KOH aqueous electrolyte, delivering a specific
capacitance of 197 F g –1  at 0.1 A g –1 . The assembled
symmetric supercapacitor achieves a specific capaci-
tance of 155 F g–1 , a power density of 55 W kg –1 , and
an energy density of 12.84 W h kg–1  at 0.1 A g–1 . Addi-
tionally, the influence mechanism of the biomass-
derived porous carbon materials on their electrochem-
ical performance and supercapacitor properties was
discussed.
2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTIONS
2.1. Preparation of Biomass-Derived Porous Carbon
Corn cob powder and soybean straw powder (par-
ticle size: 500 mesh) were used as biomass precursors,
while NaCl, KCl, and ZnCl
2 (analytical grade) were
employed as molten salt. The biomass precursors were
pretreated through sequential cleaning, impurity
removal, and drying steps. Three types of molten salt
mixtures (NaCl–KCl, ZnCl
2–KCl, ZnCl 2–NaCl)
were mixed and calcined at 300 °C for 2 h under N 2
atmosphere to remove moisture and volatile impuri-
ties. In a typical procedure, 30 g of pretreated corn cob
or soybean straw was separately mixed with three mol-
ten salt systems: 150 g of NaCl–KCl (75 g NaCl and
75 g KCl), 60 g of ZnCl
2–KCl (44 g ZnCl 2 and
16 g KCl), and 60 g of ZnCl 2–NaCl (42 g ZnCl 2 and
18 g NaCl). Each mixture was mechanically blended
in a ball mill for 1 h. Subsequently, the mixture was
transferred into a tube furnace and heated to 800°C at
a rate of 5 °C min –1  under a nitrogen flow of
120 mL min–1 . After reaching the target temperature,
it was held for 2 h to ensure complete reaction. The
furnace was then turned off and allowed to cool natu-
rally to room temperature. The resulting black powder
was collected and added to deionized water to remove
impurities. The mixture was stirred at 1200 rpm for 6 h
at room temperature, followed by centrifugation. The
precipitate was repeatedly washed with deionized
water and anhydrous ethanol until the pH reached 6.
The obtained black powder was then dried under vac-
uum at 70°C for 12 h. Finally, the corn cob and soy-
bean straw derived carbons prepared in NaCl–KCl,

2.3. Electrochemical Performances Testing
The electrode active materials prepared according
to Subsection 2.1 were used to fabricate the working
electrodes. Specifically, 0.1 g of the active material was
mixed with 0.018 g of acetylene black (as a conductive
agent) and 0.006 g of polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF,
as a binder) in an agate mortar. The mixture was thor-
oughly ground, followed by the addition of an appro-
priate amount of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP,
dispersant), and then further ground to achieve homo-
geneity.
The resulting mixture was uniformly coated on a
dried nickel foam current collector (3 × 4.5 cm) that
had been cleaned by ultrasonic treatment with anhy-
drous ethanol for 2 h. After the electrode film was vac-
uum-dried at 120°C for 12 h, it was pressed at a pres-
sure of 20 MPa for 1 min using a powder press. Finally,
the electrode film was punched with a hole-punching
machine to obtain the desired electrodes.
Identical working electrodes were assembled into
coin-type (CR2032) symmetric supercapacitors.
Electrochemical measurements were performed using
an electrochemical workstation (Chenhua, China),
including cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic
charge-discharge (GCD), and electrochemical
impedance spectroscopy (EIS). The EIS tests were
conducted over a frequency range of 10
5 to 10 –2  Hz
with an amplitude of 5 mV at open circuit voltage.
Electrochemical testing (cycling performance, rate
capability) was conducted with a LAND battery test-
ing system (China).
CV was performed at scan rates of 5, 10, 20, 40, 50,
100, and 200 mV s
–1 , and GCD tests were conducted
at current densities of 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 A g–1 .
In the three-electrode configuration, a Hg/HgO elec-
trode and a Pt electrode were used as the reference and
counter electrodes, respectively, with 6 M KOH aque-
ous solution as the electrolyte and a voltage window of
–0.8 to 0 V. For the two-electrode system, the poten-
tial window was set between 0 and 1 V.

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Characterization of Biomass-Derived 
Porous Carbon
In this study, different molten salt systems and bio-
mass precursors were employed to prepare biomass-
derived carbon materials with favorable specific
capacitance and high specific surface area, wherein
the molten salt exerts a dual function as both a tem-
plate and an activator. Figure 1 presents the schematic
diagram for the synthesis of biomass-derived carbon.
SEM analysis was employed to analyze the struc-
ture and morphology of biomass-derived carbons
(e.g., those derived from corn cob and soybean straw),
as depicted in Fig. 2. It was observed that the carbon-
ized products from different biomass sources displayed
distinct morphologies after activation via various mol-
ten salt systems. As illustrated in Figs. 2a, 2b, the pore
shapes of NK–S and NK–C obtained in the NaCl–
KCl system were irregular and larger than those of the
other two samples. In contrast, ZK–S and ZK–C
obtained in the ZnCl
2–KCl mixture presented honey-
comb—like porous carbon with a higher pore density
than other carbons, although their pore structures
were slightly smaller. This implies that these two types
of carbon have a larger specific surface area. The
structures of ZN–S and ZN–C obtained in the
ZnCl
2–NaCl system contained irregular pores, indi-
cating the presence of macropores, mesopores, and
even micropores. The hierarchical porous structure
formed by biomass-derived porous carbon exhibits
excellent stability, and the pore structure and specific
surface area play a crucial role in enhancing ion trans-
port and improving electrochemical performance.
In addition, further EDS analysis of ZK–S was
conducted and shown in Fig. 2g. It reveals that the
porous carbon contains not only carbon but also dop-
ing with oxygen and nitrogen atoms, along with trace
amounts of K
+, Zn2+, and Cl–  ions. The nitrogen dop-
ing can modulate the electron-donor behavior, which
is conducive to enhancing the electrochemical perfor-
mance. Moreover, oxygen-containing functional
groups on the carbon surface can contribute additional
pseudocapacitance by providing accessible reaction
sites [30–33].
To confirm the presence of tiny pores and their
impact on the electrochemical performance, the N
2
adsorption-desorption isotherms of the biomass-
derived carbons are conducted and presented in Fig. 3.
The isotherms of NK–S and NK–C correspond to
type I, suggesting that the samples predominantly
exhibit microporous. The isotherms of ZK–S,
ZK‒C, ZN–S, ZN–C combines type I and type IV,
suggesting that the samples predominantly exhibit
microporous and mesoporous structures. At low rela-
tive pressures ( P/P
0 < 0.05), the nitrogen adsorption
exhibits a sharp rise, which is attributed to the pres-
ence of abundant micropores. At high relative pres-
sures (P/P0 > 0.05), the adsorption capacity increases
gradually, indicating the existence of mesopores and
macropores.
The above results demonstrate that the prepared
carbon materials possess a three-dimensional porous
structure with the coexistence of micropores, meso-

3.2. Electrochemical Performance of Biomass-Derived

To further evaluate the practical application of bio-
mass-derived carbon in supercapacitor devices, the
biomass-derived carbon electrodes were assembled
into coin-type symmetric supercapacitors, and the
electrochemical performances of ZK–S//ZK–S,
ZK–C//ZK–C, ZN–S//ZN–S, ZN–C//ZN–C,
NK–S//NK–S, and NK–S//NK–S symmetric
devices were shown in Fig. 6. At a scan rate of 20 mV s
–1 ,
the CV curves of these porous carbon materials in

ogy, indicating excellent double-layer electrochemical
performance. The CV curve of the ZN–S//ZN–S
sample shows a slight distortion, indicating that the
specific capacitance value of the electrode material is
composed of double-layer capacitance and pseudoca-
pacitance, which may be caused by the embedding of
molten salt ions into the pores during the carboniza-

that the areas under the CV curves of the ZK–S, ZK–C,
ZN–C, ZN–S, NK–C, and NK–S electrodes

in the specific capacitance of the supercapacitors. This
finding is consistent with the analysis results of the CV
curves in the three-electrode system. Furthermore,
Fig. 6b illustrates the variation of specific capacitance
of the six porous carbon devices at different scan rates.
It can be seen that the ZK–S//ZK–S symmetric
device exhibit good capacitive performance, featuring
high reversibility and low internal resistance. At the
scan rate of 5, 10, 20, 40, 50, 100, and 200 mV s
–1 , the

and triangular symmetry. This indicates that all six
symmetric supercapacitors possess good reversibility

4. CONCLUSIONS
This study aims to compare the electrochemical
performances of electrode materials derived from two
different types of biomasses (corn cob, soybean straw)
in three molten salt mixtures (NaCl–KCl, ZnCl
2–
KCl, ZnCl2–NaCl), so as to evaluate their potential
for supercapacitor applications.
Material characterization reveals that the ZnCl 2–
KCl system, particularly when using soybean straw
(ZK–S), yields a carbon with the highest microporous
specific surface area (up to 2280 m 2 g –1 ) among the
series. This well-developed microporous structure
facilitates efficient electrolyte ion transport and pro-
vides abundant active sites for charge storage. As a
result, the ZK–S electrode delivers the highest spe-
cific capacitance of 197 F g
–1  at 0.1 A g–1 , the assem-
bled symmetric supercapacitor achieves a specific
capacitance of 155 F g–1 , a power density of 55 W kg–1 ,
and an energy density of 12.84 W h kg –1  at 0.1 A g –1 ,
outperforming the other five materials.
All six obtained carbons (i.e., ZK–S, ZK–C, ZN–
S, ZN–C, NK–S, NK–C) demonstrate outstanding
long-term cycling stability, retaining their specific
capacitance without significant decay over
10000 cycles, which may due to the doping of impurity
atoms within the biomass, such as N and O atoms and
the incorporation of uncleaned ions (K +, Na+, Zn2+)
in the molten salt.
In conclusion, the ZnCl2–KCl molten-salt system
is identified as an effective route for synthesizing bio-
mass-derived porous carbons with optimized microp-
orosity, which is crucial for achieving high capacitance
and excellent rate performance. These findings high-
light the significance of selecting both the appropriate
biomass precursor and molten-salt composition to tai-
lor the pore structure and enhance the electrochemi-
cal properties of carbon-based supercapacitor elec-
trodes.
