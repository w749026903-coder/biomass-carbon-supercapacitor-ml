Stable electrode material for use in supercapacitor with iodine doping 
after sulfonation of mesoporous activated carbon particles based 
on microalgae biomass
Abdulkadir Levent1 · Cafer Saka2 
Received: 31 October 2024 / Revised: 15 February 2025 / Accepted: 19 February 2025 / Published online: 28 February 2025 
© The Author(s) 2025
Abstract
Developing appropriate electrode materials is crucial for achieving supercapacitors with high power output and extended 
cycle life. Among these, biomass-derived carbon electrodes hold a prominent position in supercapacitor applications. How-
ever, these electrodes need to be modified by appropriate methods to increase their performance. In this study, sulphur and 
iodine heteroatom functionalization was performed using a hydrothermal method on activated carbon (SPAC) derived from a 
microalgae precursor through potassium hydroxide activation, resulting in iodine- and sulphur-doped SPAC (iodine@S-doped 
SPAC). Characterization was carried out with Scanning electron microscope (SEM), Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy 
(FTIR), X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), and nitrogen adsorption/desorption 
analyses. The electrochemical properties of the resulting composite material as a supercapacitor were examined. A specific 
capacitance of 208.95 F/g was achieved with the iodine@S-doped SPAC supercapacitor. The maximum energy density and 
power density of the supercapacitor, derived from the iodine@S-doped SPAC sample, were calculated as 5.11 Wh/kg and 
2000 W/kg, respectively. Additionally, the coulombic efficiency of this supercapacitor was calculated to be 87.57% at a cur-
rent density of 0.06 A/g. It also exhibited a capacity retention of up to 89.72% over 1000 cycles.
Keywords Microalgae · Pyrolysis · Iodine and sulphur doping · Hydrothermal heating · Supercapacitor
1 Introduction
Energy, which is vital for world life, seriously affects the 
economies and the environment of countries around the 
world due to the current dependence on non-renewable fos-
sil fuels [1]. Interest in renewable, environmentally friendly, 
high-performance energy storage devices such as batteries, 
supercapacitors and fuel cells for electrochemical energy, 
which is an important part of clean energy, tends to increase 
[2, 3]. Among these, supercapacitors, in particular, have gar-
nered significant interest from researchers [4–6].
The excellent microstructure and reasonable pore size dis-
tribution of biomass-derived carbon materials enable their 
use in various applications. In particular, the outstanding 
charge storage/release capacity and high cycling stability of 
carbon-based supercapacitors promote their applications in 
energy storage and conversion [7]. The development of low-
cost and renewable biomass-based carbon materials for use 
in energy storage devices is an important issue. As typical 
electrode material for supercapacitors, biomass-based car -
bon precursors are attracting intense interest as a reliable and 
readily available source [8–12]. However, further enhancing 
the performance of such carbon-based materials when used 
as electrodes is an important research topic. Increasing the 
specific surface area and incorporating heteroatom doping 
are widely preferred strategic approaches to improving the 
performance of carbon-based electrode materials. The elec-
trochemical activity of supercapacitors can be significantly 
improved by appropriately doping biomass-derived carbon 
materials with heteroatoms. The incorporation of heter -
oatoms with different electronegativities and atomic radii 
than carbon alters the electron distribution of the material. 
As a result, heteroatom functional groups enhance properties 
such as wettability, redox activity, and electrical conduc-
tivity, thereby promoting pseudocapacitance formation and

Heteroatoms such as sulphur (S), nitrogen (N), phos-

naceous materials as a heteroatom, disrupting the electron 
balance density of the material and resulting in different 
bond lengths and bond angles [1 , 24]. Sulphur-doped car -
bon materials also have significant interest in supercapacitor 
applications.
Another important element among heteroatoms is iodine. 
The iodine doping has begun to be considered especially 
in some applications [ 25–27]. There is still little research 
on iodine doping into porous carbon structure. With iodine 
atoms having a relatively high electronegativity (2.66) and 
a larger radius (140 pm) compared to carbon, both the elec-
tronic structure of carbon materials can be optimized and 
the d-spacing can be increased [28]. Thus, we believe that 
investigating how iodine can be integrated into carbon mate-
rials to enhance energy storage performance in supercapaci-
tor applications is of great importance. Doping iodine as a 
heteroatom can significantly increase the electrochemically 
active sites of carbon-based materials [29, 30].
Biomass-based carbon materials are widely used in vari-
ous application areas [31– 33]. Among biomass-based car -
bon materials, algae have attracted great attention in differ-
ent application areas with their important features such as 
fast growth rates and high photosynthetic efficiency [ 34]. 
Spirulina platensis, which has an important place among 
algae and is characterized by rapid growth and wide distribu-
tion, consists mainly of polysaccharides, proteins, and lipids. 
One of the significant properties of Spirulina platensis  is 
its natural nitrogen content. Therefore, it can be utilized in 
various applications without requiring an additional nitrogen 
source for doping. With its low cost and these advantageous 
characteristics, Spirulina platensis emerges as a promising 
candidate for biomass-derived carbon materials as electrodes 
in supercapacitor applications. This study focuses on the 
synthesis of iodine@S-doped SPAC through a series of 
pyrolysis and hydrothermal processes, utilizing Spirulina 
platensis microalgae as the carbon precursor, with S and I 
atoms as heteroatoms for electrode material in supercapaci-
tor applications. The synthesized material was character -
ized using XRD, EDS, SEM, FTIR, and nitrogen adsorption/
desorption analyses. Its electrochemical properties were 
evaluated through CV, GCD, and EIS measurements.
2  Experimental section
2.1  Sample preparation
This experimental study consists of three stages. The first 
stage is the production of activated carbon. The procedure 
for producing activated carbon from Spirulina platensis  
microalgae as a carbon source was reported in another study 
[35]. In summary, microalgae as a carbon precursor (20 g) 
and KOH activator (20 g) as a chemical activator were added 
to an Erlen flask at a ratio of 1:1. The pure water (100 mL) 
was added to this mixture and mixed on a magnetic stirrer at 
room temperature for approximately 1 h. Then, this mixture 
was placed in an oven and impregnated at 80 °C overnight. 
After this process, the samples taken into porcelain cruci-
bles were pyrolysed in a muffle furnace at 600 °C under a 
carbon dioxide atmosphere for 30 min. After the pyrolysis 
process, this sample was left to cool to room temperature, 
washed with hot and cold deionized water, and dried in an 
oven at 105 °C. The sample obtained by this process was 
called SPAC.
In the second stage, sulphur doping was performed with 
sulfuric acid. A certain amount of SPAC (3 g) and 30 mL of 
sulfuric acid diluted with pure water at a 1:3 ratio was added 
to a conical flask [ 35]. This mixture was hydrothermally 
processed in an oven at 105 °C for 24 h. After this process, 
the obtained sample was repeatedly washed with hot and 
cold pure water and was named S-doped SPAC.
The third stage involves adding iodine to the S-doped 
SPAC sample by hydrothermal heating method. To achieve 
iodine doping, S-doped SPAC was mixed with iodine at a 
1:2 ratio in a 30 mL aqueous solution. Heating and mixing 
were carried out via hydrothermal processing on a magnetic 
stirrer at 50 °C for 6 h. Subsequently, the obtained sample 
was washed multiple times with pure water and dried in an 
oven at 80 °C for 2 h. This sample was named iodine@S-
doped SPAC.

SEM was used for the surface morphologies of S-doped

The elemental compositions of the iodine@S-doped

Fig.  3g. The presence of C, N, O, S, and I atoms on the

doped into the S-doped SPAC sample. In addition, the pres-
ence of functional groups containing up to 30% oxygen rich-
ness gives the sample structure a hydrophilicity feature. A 
large number of active redox sites can be provided on this 
hydrophilic surface with a large number of surface defects 
[41, 42]. As a result of the inclusion of iodine (2.66) and sul-
phur (2.58), which have different electronegativities, into the 
 sp2 carbon (2.55) framework, the properties of the electron 
donor change through the redistribution of charge on the 
sample. As a result, neighbouring carbon atoms with lower 
electronegativity are likely to become positively charged 
[43].

3.2  Electrochemical performance
Current–potential distribution was investigated for the 
iodine@S-doped SPAC capacitor with the CV technique. 
Experiments were carried out using the CV technique in 
the electrolyte environment of 1 M  H2SO4, 1 M NaOH, and 
1 M  Na2SO4 in the voltage range from − 0.3 to + 0.9 V. CV 
voltammograms with 10 mV/s scanning in these electrolyte 
environments are given in Fig.  6. The capacitance values 
obtained in 1 M  H2SO4, 1 M NaOH, and 1 M  Na2SO4 elec-
trolyte environment were 110.92 F/g, 64.58 F/g, and 17.86 
F/g, respectively. Compared to NaOH and  Na2SO4 electro-
lyte environments, there is a higher specific capacitance 
value in the  H2SO4 environment. In particular, broad redox 
peaks were observed in the CV curves in the  H2SO4 envi-
ronment, and discharge data from GCD curves with current 
densities below 0.16 A/g also exhibited similar trends. These 
observations are generally attributed to the redox reactions 
or Faradaic processes in the electrode materials of super -
capacitors. This behaviour may depend on several factors, 
including the surface chemistry of the electrode material, 
ion transport or diffusion, the structural characteristics of the 
material (such as porosity, layered structure, or defects in the 
crystal structure), and the coexistence of both double-layer 
capacitance and Faradaic processes in supercapacitors. Elec-
trolytes, which significantly affect the electrolyte–electrode 
interface and the internal structure of the active material, 
are of great importance in transferring the charge between 
two electrodes in supercapacitors [55, 56]. Similar to the 
results obtained in this study, in a previous study [57], spe-
cific capacitance values of 130 F/g, 110 F/g, and 79 F/g were 
obtained for a carbon-based supercapacitor in  H2SO4, KOH, 
and  Na2SO4 electrolyte environments, respectively. Com-
pared to other electrolyte solutions, the  H2SO4 electrolyte 
has a smaller ion size, making it easier for ions to penetrate 
the electrode pores. Compared to the equivalent conductiv -
ity of the  H2SO4 electrolyte of 1300  cm2  ohm−1    eq−1 , the 
equivalent conductivities of NaOH and  Na2SO4 electrolytes 
are 248.1 and 150  cm2  ohm−1    eq−1 , respectively.  H+ ions 
in  H2SO4 are much smaller and have significantly higher 
mobility compared to other ions such as  Na+ or  SO4
2-. 
This allows for rapid access to the electrode surface and 
enhances the efficiency of redox reactions.  H2SO4 exhibits 
very high ionic conductivity, especially in aqueous solution 
environments, resulting in low internal resistance and faster 
charge/discharge kinetics. Compared to NaOH and  Na2SO4, 
the small and highly mobile  H+ ions enable rapid charge 
transfer, whereas  Na+ ions can sometimes face volumetric 
constraints when diffusing into the pores of carbon-based 
electrodes. As a result,  Na+ ions do not contribute to pseu-
docapacitive reactions, leading to reduced capacitance. In 
terms of charge transfer effects,  H2SO4 demonstrates lower 
charge transfer resistance since protons can easily react on 
the electrode surface. In contrast, the reaction of  OH- ions in 
NaOH are kinetically more challenging, leading to increased 
charge transfer resistance [ 58, 59]. Thus, a better capaci -
tive behaviour was achieved in the  H2SO4 electrolyte with a 
higher conductivity.

mizing surface interactions. However, as the scan rate or 
current density increases, there is insufficient time for ions 
to fully interact with the electrode surface. This results in a 
limited capacity, confined to charge storage on the surface 
or in larger and more easily accessible pores.
Moreover, the CV curves for the iodine@S-doped SPAC 
maintained their semi-rectangular shape even at a high scan 
rate, indicating that it responds quickly to reverse voltage 
current and has low ion transport resistance.

charge–discharge process. CV measurements gave similar

the lower electron current density due to the participation 
of a large number of electrolyte ions in the charging and 
discharging process. However, at large current densities, nar-
rower curves occur due to the small number of electrolyte 
ions and electrons participating in the charging and discharg-
ing process [62]. The maximum specific capacitance value 
of 208.95 F/g was reached at a current density of 0.06 A/g. 
At the same time, the coulombic efficiency for this superca-

A/g. Table  1 gives a comparison of the specific capacitance

electrolyte solutions prepared from various biomass-based

4  Conclusions
Heteroatom doping typically includes elements such as N, 
P, and O, which are commonly used in carbon-based elec-
trode systems for supercapacitors. In this study, as an alter-
native to frequently used heteroatoms in the literature, S and 
iodine functionalization was performed on activated carbon 
derived from a microalgal precursor. This functionalization 
was achieved using a hydrothermal method following acti-
vation with KOH agent. Characterization was carried out 
through FTIR, EDS, SEM, XRD, and nitrogen adsorption/
desorption analyses. After the treatment of S-doped SPAC 
under hydrothermal heating conditions with iodine, highly 
ordered spherical particles were observed on the surface 
by SEM analysis. The particle size of the iodine@S-doped 
SPAC sample was found to be 31.22 nm. The elemental 
compositions of C, N, O, S, and I atoms were determined to 
be 43.46%, 9.02%, 28.72%, 2.82%, and 6.95%, respectively. 
The electrochemical properties of the obtained composite 
material as supercapacitor were investigated. When S atom 
is added as a heteroatom to carbon materials, it results in a 
larger radius and higher electrochemical activity compared 
to the N atom, disrupting the electron density and creat-
ing different bond lengths and angles. With iodine atoms 
having relatively high electronegativity and a larger radius 
compared to carbon, both the electronic structure of the 
carbon materials can be optimized, and the d-spacing can 
be increased. The iodine@S-doped SPAC supercapacitor 
achieved a specific capacitance value of 208.95 F/g at a 
current density of 0.06 A/g. The maximum energy density 
and power density calculated for the supercapacitor designed 
from the iodine@S-doped SPAC sample were 5.11 Wh/kg 
and 2000 W/kg, respectively. Furthermore, the coulombic 
efficiency of this supercapacitor was calculated to be 87.57% 
at a current density of 0.06 A/g, showing a capacity reten-
tion performance of up to 89.72% over 1000 cycles. This 
study demonstrates that electrodes obtained through iodine 
and S heteroatom doping can be effectively used in super -
capacitor applications. Moreover, future studies on the use 
of iodine alone or in conjunction with other heteroatoms in 
carbon-based materials may lead to the development of more 
advanced supercapacitor electrodes.
