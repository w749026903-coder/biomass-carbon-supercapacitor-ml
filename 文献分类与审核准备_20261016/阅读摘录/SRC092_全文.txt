ABSTRACT: As urgent demands increase in energy-grid systems, supercapacitors have been
further documented as highly promising energy-storage technologies across a range of
applications owing to their high-power density, rapid charge−discharge capabilities, and
prolonged endurance life. Porous carbon-based materials, particularly those derived from
metal−organic frameworks (MOFs), are prominent candidates for supercapacitor electrode
materials owing to their customizable inner-pore structures and superior electrochemical
properties. In this work, we systematically manipulate the carbonization temperature (700−900
°C) of Al-NDC-MOF to tailor the hierarchical pore structure, defect density, and surface
chemistry of the resulting porous carbons (AC-Al-NDC-x), aiming to unravel the structure−
performance−device correlations required for stable dual-ion storage. Systematic character-
ization reveals that carbonization temperature plays a crucial role in regulating pore structure
hierarchy, defect density, and surface functional groups. As a result, the optimized sample (AC-
Al-NDC-800) exhibits balanced electrochemical performance, enabling its application as both
positive and negative electrodes in symmetric and asymmetric two-electrode devices. This behavior is attributed to the synergistic
effect of micropores for charge storage, mesopores for ion transport, and appropriate surface functionalities for interfacial stability.
Although the achieved energy density and cycling stability are comparable to previously reported porous carbon systems, this work
highlights a rational design strategy for balancing pore structure and surface chemistry to achieve stable dual-ion storage under
practical two-electrode conditions.
KEYWORDS: metal−organic frameworks (MOFs), porous carbon materials, dual-ion storage, carbonization-regulated structure,
supercapacitors, two-electrode devices
1. INTRODUCTION
As advancements in electrochemical energy storage technology
continue, supercapacitors (SCs) have demonstrated distinct
advantages in various applications owing to their high-power
density, rapid charge−discharge capabilities,
1,2
and exceptional
cycling stability.
3,4
They have become indispensable compo-
nents in sectors such as industry,
5,6
energy,
7−9
and trans-
portation.
10,11
However, their relatively low energy density
presents a significant challenge for widespread adoption. This
limitation is primarily determined by the energy storage
mechanism, structural stability, and efficiency of the interfacial
reactions of the electrode materials within the different
operating potential windows.
Porous carbon-based materials, recognized for their high
specific surface area, customizable inner-porous net structure,
and excellent electrical conductivity, are regarded as prime
candidates for active SC electrode materials.
12,13
Among these,
carbon-based materials derived from metal−organic frame-
works (MOFs) have emerged as a focal point in contemporary
research owing to their significant advantages, such as a
designable and tunable precursor structure and a controllable
thermal conversion process.
14−17
By meticulously adjusting
process parameters, such as carbonization temperature and
activation intensity, it is possible to synergistically optimize the
key properties of MOF-derived carbon materials, such as the
specific surface area, pore structure distribution, defect density,
and surface chemical composition, thereby enhancing their
electrochemical energy-storage performance.
18−20
Numerous
studies have demonstrated that these materials can achieve a
high specific capacitance in three-electrode systems and exhibit
good rate capability in two-electrode devices.
21−24
Aluminum-based MOFs constructed from conjugated
aromatic ligands represent a promising yet relatively underex-
plored category of precursors for porous carbon electrodes,
although several recent studies have begun to explore Al-MOF-
derived carbon materials for supercapacitor applications,

conjugated framework and a robust coordination structure.
During carbonization and activation, aluminum species can be
effectively removed, enabling the formation of high-purity
carbon materials while retaining framework-derived pore
characteristics. These features provide a favorable structural
basis for generating porous carbons capable of supporting
charge storage in both negative and positive potential regions.
Nevertheless, systematic investigations on Al-NDC-MOF-
derived carbon materials remain limited compared with those
on ZIF-type or transition-metal-based MOFs.
More importantly, the relationship between the structural
characteristics of Al-NDC-MOF-derived porous carbons and
their electrochemical behavior across different potential
windows has not yet been fully clarified. In practical
supercapacitor operation, electrode materials are required to
function under both negative and positive polarization
conditions, where the charge storage behavior and interfacial
responses can differ significantly. How the pore structure
hierarchy, carbon framework characteristics, and surface
chemical composition collectively influence electrochemical
performance remains insufficiently understood. This issue
limits the rational design of Al-MOF-derived carbon electrodes
for practical device applications.
To address these issues, this study synthesized a series of
MOF-derived porous carbon materials (denoted as AC-Al-
NDC-x) using Al-NDC-MOF as the precursor through
controlled carbonization and chemical activation processes.
Rather than pursuing solely a new material design, this work
focuses on elucidating how the incremental structural changes
induced by carbonization temperature influence the balance of
charge-storage behavior in negative and positive potential
windows. We aim to establish a clear structure−performance−
device correlation that links the hierarchical pore architecture,
defect chemistry, and surface functional groups to the dual-
potential electrochemical behavior in practical two-electrode
supercapacitors.
2. EXPERIMENTAL METHODS
2.1. Experimental Materials
1,4-Naphthalenedicarboxylic acid (1,4-NDC), aluminum chloride
hexahydrate, polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), N,N-dimethylformamide
(DMF), deionized water, absolute ethanol, sodium hydroxide
(NaOH), potassium hydroxide (KOH), nickel hydroxide (Ni(OH)
2
),
and commercial activated carbon (AC) were used.
2.2. Synthesis Methods
2.2.1. Synthesis of Al-NDC-MOF. Al-NDC-MOF was synthe-
sized with minor modifications based on the previously reported
literature.
27
Specifically, 1.081 g of 1,4-naphthalenedicarboxylic acid
(1,4-NDC, 5 mmol) was dissolved in 20 mL of N,N-dimethylforma-
mide (DMF). Meanwhile, 1.207 g of aluminum chloride hexahydrate
(5 mmol) and 0.100 g of PVP were dissolved in 20 mL of deionized
water. The two solutions were mixed and stirred for 15 min, then
transferred into a 100 mL stainless-steel autoclave lined with Teflon.
The autoclave was placed in a blast drying oven and maintained at
120 °C for 12 h for the reaction.
After the reaction completed, the system was cooled down to room
temperature. The resulting white solid product was collected by
centrifugation, washed twice with absolute ethanol and deionized
water, respectively, and then freeze-dried for 12 h. The final white
powder was designated as an Al-NDC-MOF.
2.2.2. Carbonization and Alkali Washing. A certain mass of Al-
NDC-MOF was weighed and placed in a porcelain boat, which was
then transferred into a tube furnace. Carbonization was carried out at
700 °C for 2 h under a nitrogen atmosphere, with a heating rate of 4
°C/min. The obtained black powder was added into 20 mL of 10
mol/L NaOH solution, heated to boiling and kept boiling for 30 min.
After cooling to room temperature, the mixture was stirred
continuously for 12 h. The solid product was separated by
centrifugation, washed with deionized water until the filtrate reached
neutral pH, and freeze-dried for 12 h to obtain C−Al−NDC-700.
2.2.3. Activation. C−Al−NDC-700 and KOH were mixed
uniformly at a mass ratio of 1:3. A small amount of deionized water
and absolute ethanol (approximately 1 mL in total) was added into
the mixture, which was then soaked for 12 h. The resulting mixture
was transferred into a porcelain boat and placed in a blast drying oven
at 75 °C to evaporate the solvents, yielding a black viscous slurry. The
slurry was transferred into a tube furnace and activated at 600 °C for 2
h under a nitrogen atmosphere, with a heating rate of 4 °C/min. The
activated product was washed repeatedly with deionized water until
neutral pH, separated by centrifugation, and freeze-dried for 12 h to
obtain the activated porous carbon, designated as AC-Al-NDC-700.
AC-Al-NDC-800 and AC-Al-NDC-900 were synthesized following
the identical procedure, with only the carbonization temperature
altered. The material synthesized at a carbonization temperature of
800 °C was named AC-Al-NDC-800, while that synthesized at 900 °C
was named AC-Al-NDC-900, with all other experimental conditions
kept unchanged.

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A series of porous carbon materials, designated AC-Al-NDC-x
were synthesized using Al-NDC-MOF as the precursor. This
was accomplished by systematically varying the carbonization-
activation temperatures between 700 and 900 °C. This study
focuses on establishing the relationship between structural
characteristics of MOF-derived carbon materials and their
electrochemical behavior. The effects of carbonization−
activation temperature on the carbon framework configuration,
pore structure evolution, and surface chemical composition
were systematically analyzed, with particular attention to their
influence on charge-storage responses under negative and
positive polarization conditions. The results indicate that AC-
Al-NDC-800 exhibits the most balanced electrochemical
response in both negative and positive potential windows,
enabling its effective application as both positive and negative
electrodes in symmetric and asymmetric supercapacitor
devices. These findings establish a clear correlation between
the structure, ion behavior, and device performance, offering
valuable insights for the strategic design of MOF-derived
carbon electrodes with dual-ion storage capabilities.
Initially, the Al-NDC-MOF precursor functions as a reliable
foundation for the subsequent carbonization-activation proc-
ess, ensuring both structural integrity and thermal stability.
The XRD pattern shown in Figure S1 confirms the successful
synthesis of the Al-NDC-MOF precursor. The TGA results are
shown in Figure S2, the MOF undergoes a three-phase weight
loss during heating: a gradual weight reduction from room
temperature to 248 °C, attributed to the elimination of pore-
adsorbed water and residual solvents;
28
a stable mass phase
from 248 to 520 °C, indicating the framework’s robust
medium-temperature stability; and a rapid weight decrease
above 520 °C, resulting from the thermal breakdown of
organic ligands and the collapse of the MOF framework.
28
The
mass stabilized after 728 °C, leaving behind inorganic
aluminum-based residues. This distinct thermal decomposition
pattern provides a crucial thermal foundation for the accurate
control of the high-temperature carbonization process. SEM
images (Figure 1(a−c)) reveal that Al-NDC-MOF consists of
micron-sized blocky or flower-like aggregates formed by flaky
structures with clear interlayer gaps and some surface
roughness. This morphology facilitates pore formation and
development during carbonization. EDS analysis (as shown in
Figure S3 and Table S1) identified only three elements: C, O,
and Al, with mass fractions of 29.01%, 50.93%, and 20.05%,
and atomic fractions of 38.09%, 50.19%, and 11.72%,
respectively. This confirms the high purity of the material,
featuring abundant organic carbon sources and removable
metal nodes, establishing a compositional basis for producing
high-purity porous carbon materials.
Following carbonization, alkali washing, and KOH activa-
tion, as illustrated in Figure 1(d), the materials undergo
substantial modifications in their crystal and pore structures.
SEM images of AC-Al-NDC-700, AC-Al-NDC-800, and AC-
Al-NDC-900 at different magnifications are provided in the
newly added Figure S4. AC-Al-NDC-700 retains large blocky
morphologies with relatively dense structure and limited pore
development, AC-Al-NDC-800 exhibits a well-developed
three-dimensional hierarchical porous network with intercon-
nected micro-, meso-, and macropores, and AC-Al-NDC-900
shows partial structural collapse and particle agglomeration.
TEM and HRTEM images of AC-Al-NDC-800 are newly
included in Figure S5, revealing a short-range ordered, long-
range disordered carbon framework. Dark-field TEM and EDS
mapping (Figure S6) show a uniform distribution of C, N, and
O elements across the carbon framework, confirming defect-
rich graphitic carbon and uniform heteroatom doping. These
images provide direct visual evidence supporting structural
analysis and the observed electrochemical performance.
The XRD patterns presented in Figure 1(e) demonstrate
that none of the activated samples exhibit the characteristic
diffraction peaks of the MOF. Instead, they displayed broad
and diffuse peaks at 2θ ≈ 23° and 43°, corresponding to the
(002) and (100) crystal planes of graphitoid carbon. This
transformation signifies that the materials were effectively
converted into porous carbon primarily composed of
amorphous carbon and structures with a low degree of
graphitization. As the carbonization temperature increased, the
(002) peak became less pronounced and slightly shifted,
indicating an enhancement in the local ordering of the carbon
framework. However, owing to the combined effects of KOH’s
strong chemical etching of KOH and the rapid decomposition
of organic ligands, a highly graphitized structure was not
formed, resulting in a porous carbon network with a high
defect density. Notably, no characteristic peaks of metallic Al
or its oxides were observed in any of the activated samples,
thereby ensuring the high purity of the carbon materials.

Although AC-Al-NDC-900 demonstrated an increased specific
surface area, its micropore volume experienced a notable
reduction, and the pore size distribution shifted toward larger
pores. This suggests that some ultra micropores either merged
or collapsed during high-temperature activation. Such
structural changes may restrict the effective accommodation
of electrolyte ions within the confined pores, thereby
diminishing their practical contribution to dual-ion charge
storage under device-level operating conditions. These “non-
effective pores” are unlikely to fully participate in charge
storage under high current densities or in practical devices,
thus limiting the effective translation of their structural
advantages into device performance.

The XPS survey spectra illustrated in Figure 1(h) exhibit
distinct characteristic signals for C 1s, O 1s, and N 1s in AC-
Al-NDC-700 and AC-Al-NDC-800, whereas the N 1s signal is
almost absent in AC-Al-NDC-900. As depicted in Figure 1(i),
the high-resolution C 1s spectra reveal that all three samples
possess a prominent peak associated with sp
2
-hybridized C−
C/C=C bonds at a binding energy of 284.7−284.8 eV range.
With increasing temperature, the peak intensities of the
functional groups at 286.0−286.4 eV (linked to C−O/C−N
bonds) and 287.6−289.0 eV (related to C=O/O−C=O
bonds) gradually decreased. The O 1s spectra (Figure 1(j))
can be deconvoluted into three components, corresponding to
C=O groups (531.9−532.3 eV), C−O/hydroxyl groups
(533.5−533.6 eV), and weakly bonded oxygen species. AC-
Al-NDC-800 retains a moderate level of oxygen-containing
functional groups, which facilitates the enhancement of the
material’s interfacial wettability. The N 1s spectra (Figure S8)
indicate that AC-Al-NDC-800 contains pyridinic N (398.7 eV)
and pyrrolic/graphitic N (400.2−400.4 eV); these heteroatom-
containing functional groups can contribute to additional
surface pseudo capacitance.
34,35
In contrast, AC-Al-NDC-900
exhibited a very low nitrogen species content, and its
electrochemical performance primarily depended on the
carbon framework and pore structure.
To further verify the surface chemical structure beyond XPS
analysis, Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was
conducted (Figure S9). As shown, all AC-Al-NDC-x samples
exhibit relatively weak and broad absorption bands, indicating
that most organic functional groups were removed after high-
temperature carbonization and KOH activation, leaving a
carbon-dominated framework. A broad peak around ∼3438
cm
−1
can be attributed to the stretching vibration of −OH
groups or adsorbed water, suggesting the presence of residual
oxygen-containing functional groups on the surface. Weak
absorption bands observed in the range of 1650−1580 cm
−1
are assigned to C=C skeletal vibrations of aromatic structures
and/or C=O groups, indicating the retention of partially
conjugated carbon frameworks. In addition, weak signals
around 1200−1250 cm
−1

absorption bands gradually decreases, especially those
associated with oxygen-containing groups, indicating pro-
gressive removal of surface functionalities and increased
carbonization degree. This trend is consistent with the XPS
results and agrees well with previous reports on MOF-derived
carbon materials.

electrolyte affinity of the materials, static water contact angle
measurements were performed on AC-Al-NDC-700, AC-Al-

surface roughness at higher temperatures. Notably, the highly

predominantly used to evaluate the intrinsic electrochemical
properties of materials under ideal conditions. In contrast, real-
world two-electrode supercapacitors require electrode materi-
als to withstand more complex potential distributions,
polarization effects, and interfacial reaction environments.
44
Consequently, materials exhibiting high specific capacitance in
a three-electrode configuration may not ensure balanced ion
storage and stable polarization behavior at the device level,
particularly when both cation- and anion-driven charge storage
processes are involved in the operation.

From a broader perspective, the present results suggest that
optimizing porous carbon materials for supercapacitors should
not be limited to maximizing the specific surface area or energy
density alone. Instead, achieving a coordinated balance among
pore structure hierarchy, defect chemistry, and surface
functional groups is essential for enabling stable dual-ion
storage under realistic device conditions. In this regard, the
AC-Al-NDC-800 sample represents a model system in which
micropores provide sufficient charge storage sites, mesopores
facilitate ion transport, and appropriate surface functionalities
contribute to interfacial stability. This synergistic effect is
particularly important for maintaining consistent electro-
chemical behavior across both negative and positive potential
windows, which is a key requirement for practical two-
electrode devices but has been insufficiently addressed in many
previous studies.
4. CONCLUSION
This study elucidates the collaborative impact of carbonization
temperature on the structural and functional properties of
porous carbon materials derived from Al-NDC-MOF.
Importantly, although carbonization-temperature adjustment
is a conventional strategy, its systematic application within the
Al-NDC-MOF system clarifies how a balanced combination of
micropore-dominated charge storage and mesopore-facilitated
ion transport can be achieved. Owing to its hierarchical pore
structure, balanced defect graphitization (I
D
/I
G
≈ 1), and
appropriate residual heteroatom-containing functional groups,
AC-Al-NDC-800 exhibits exceptional electrochemical perform-
ance: 94.30% capacitance retention after 3000 cycles in three-
electrode tests and stable operation in symmetric and
asymmetric two-electrode devices (e.g., 25.9 Wh/kg in
Ni(OH)
2
//AC-Al-NDC-800). In addition, the combined
kinetic analysis, interfacial wettability evaluation, and impe-
dance interpretation provide a more explicit mechanistic basis
for understanding ion-storage and charge-transfer behavior in
this system. This balance is crucial for achieving stable dual-ion
storage across both negative and positive potential windows.
Our findings establish a structure-performance-device correla-
tion for Al-NDC-MOF-derived carbons, moving beyond the
conventional pursuit of maximum specific surface area or
single-window capacitance toward a more holistic design
paradigm.
