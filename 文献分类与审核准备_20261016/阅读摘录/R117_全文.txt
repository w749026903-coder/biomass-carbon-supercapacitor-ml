Abstract
Activated carbon derived from biomasses with different oxygen-bearing chemi-
cal constituents has been explored as supercapacitor electrode. The rice straw
(RS) and eucalyptus wood biomasses were selected owing to their contrasting
composition of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin contents. The activated car-
bons of these biomasses are obtained by pyrolysis at 550
/C14 C followed by thermal
activation (700 /C14 C) after treatment with optimized KOH. The specific capaci-
tance of RS-based activated carbon (ACRS-700), measured from galvanostatic
charging-discharging studies at 0.5 A g /C0 1, is much higher (eg, 365 F g /C0 1) than
that of ACEW-700 (168 F g /C0 1). The higher specific capacitance of ACRS-700 is
attributable to: (i) significantly higher surface area (3345 m 2 g/C0 1) comprising
of interconnected micro- and mesoporous structure; (ii) porosity of about
2.0 cm
3/g; and (iii) oxygen-rich hemicellulose contents. The possible charge
storage mechanism in ACRS-700 has been discussed. The real-time application
of ACRS-700 has been demonstrated by lighting up red LEDs using ACRS-
700//ACRS-700 symmetric supercapacitor device, operated with aqueous and
freshly synthesized [Bu 4N+][NO3
/C0 ] ionic liquid as electrolytes. The maximum
energy density (42.7 Wh kg /C0 1) and the power density (1.9 kW kg /C0 1) for ionic
liquid electrolyte are favourable for supercapacitor applications.

biomass, charge storage mechanism, porousactivated carbon, supercapacitor electrode,

1 | INTRODUCTION
About 50 billion tonnes of biomasses are generated annu-
ally and these are mostly derived from agriculture and
forestry sources. Management of such huge quantities of
waste biomasses has been a challenging task from envi-
ronment and climate change perspective. There has been
a continuous effort for finding sustainable technology to
utilize this organic carbon-rich waste for generating
wealth, including valuable technological products.
1 In
this regard, conversion of plant-derived waste biomasses
intoactivated carbon has been a lucrative option. This has
been well-studied as adsorbent for environmental
applications.2,3 Lately, porousactivated carbon from
waste biomasses has revealed very promising result as
carbon-based supercapacitors (SCs) to meet the drastic
demand in the energy sector.
4-6 The carbon-based SCs
offer promising futuristic prospect as efficient energy-
storage device(s) due to their fast charging-discharging,
durable cycle life, high-power delivery capabilities, and

materials as SCs correspond to electrical double-layer
capacitors (EDLCs) and store charge via adsorption/

interface and offer high power density.
10 The favorable
features of activated carbon (AC) as SCs are associated
with relatively higher electrical conductivity, large spe-

11 The carbon materials, operated at higher

metal borides (CoB, NiB, NiCoB). 12-15 Moreover, the scal-
able manufacturing of porousactivated carbon from bio-
mass waste is a feasible option that is lucrative for
production of largescale SCs to meet the requirement of
energy production. In this scheme, one can avoid the
commonly employed strategy of managing large quanti-
ties of agro and forestry wastes via open field burning,
which leads to environmental pollution.
Some of the common biomasses used for developing
supercapacitor electrodes include eucalyptus wood, bam-
boo, rice husk, rice straw, tea leaves, coconut husk,
bagasse from sugarcane, and eucalyptus bark.
16-20 Among
them, rice straw contains some traces of silica along with
cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin. The silica is expected
to combine with lignocellulose to produce matrix of high
chemical and thermal stability.
21 While on the other
hand, eucalyptus wood-derived AC with hierarchical
porous structure has also been explored for preparation
for supercapacitor application. It is one of the most com-
mon and widely planted species found in 90 countries of
our world due to its rapid growth rate and renewable
availability.
22 Though several methods including
microwave-based heating are available for the prepara-
tion of AC, but direct heating of biomasses for carboniza-
tion at higher temperature has been a preferred one
owing to its ability to process large quantities of bio-
mass.
23 The electrochemical studies of these ACs are pri-
marily focussed on the effect of pore dimensions and
consequential increase in specific surface area as salient
factors for EDLC-type SCs. However, the chemical com-
position of the AC tends to vary due to differences in the
constituents of their source biomasses. This may impact
on the structural and morphological features of the AC,
which consequently alter their electrochemical proper-
ties. For example, lignin-rich biomasses are rich in oxy-
gen functionalities and may act as natural oxygen
dopants in the corresponding AC and might improve
electronic conductivity, surface wettability, and electro-
chemical reactivity at the carbon electrode/electrolyte
interface for accessing electrolyte ion and favor the
charge accumulation capacity.
24 This is consistent with
higher specific capacitances exhibited by carbonaceous
materials doped with heteroatoms, owing to higher
charge densities in dopants of higher electronegativity
than carbon.
25 These studies are mostly based on
nitrogen-doped carbon materials. While supercapacitor
electrodes made with oxygen-functionalized carbona-
ceous materials are less studied. Moreover, the oxygen
functionalities might offer additional redox property-
based pseudocapacitive characteristics for further
enhancing the charge storage process and could also
improve charge migration due to more polar electrode
surface and enhanced wettability of the electrolytes.
26
Looking at these advantages, biomasses with different
chemical composition were chosen for synthesizing
oxygen-rich AC as supercapacitor electrode materials.
The porous and oxygen-enriched AC of these biomasses
were obtained by carbonization method via direct heating
in inert condition. It is followed by alkali-treated thermal
activation. KOH activation is chosen for laboratory scale
due to its low activation temperature and it produces AC
with well-defined micropore distribution, large micro-
pore volume, and a very high surface area. Moreover,
metallic potassium in the carbon matrix expands the
space between carbon atomic layers, and leads to the pro-
duction of more porosity.
27 Several studies on porous AC
obtained from rice straw show high specific capacitance
and the results are attributed to favourable EDLC-based
charge storage owing to formation of very high surface
area. It is however not very clear why rice straw-based
AC exhibits such high surface area while AC derived
from other biomass sources does not. It could be due to
variation in the chemical composition of the biomasses,
particularly in terms of their cellulose, hemicellulose, lig-
nin, ash, and extractives. It is worth studying the correla-
tion of AC derived from strikingly different compositions
of biomasses with their charge storage properties. In view
of this, we have selected two biomasses, that is, rice straw
and eucalyptus wood of strikingly different composition
with respect to lignin, cellulose, and hemicellulose to pre-
pare AC for understanding the correlation between com-
position of biomasses and their effect on charge storage
property.
The electrochemical properties of AC obtained from
rice straw and eucalyptus wood biomasses are studied in
a three-electrode set-up. The neutral aqueous electrolyte
(2 M NaCl) has been used, as the acidic and alkaline elec-
trolytes are associated with evolution of H
2 or O 2 gases
when operated at larger potential window and may affect
the stability of the electrode materials in device opera-
tion.28 Besides, ionic liquid-based non-aqueous electro-
lyte has been explored here to achieve higher energy
density requirement by virtue of their ability to operate

2 | EXPERIMENTAL METHOD
2.1 | Preparation of porous activated
carbon (AC) from biomasses
Batch of dried and grinded rice straw biomass is taken in
a pre-cleaned silica boat and kept in a tube furnace
(Nabertherm) at 550
/C14 C for 2 h in the presence of N 2 gas
for carbonization. The heating rate is optimized to 10 /C14 C
min/C0 1. The as-obtained carbonized sample is further
thermally activated after treatment with KOH at 1:3
weight ratio. The temperature for thermal treatment is
kept at 600, 700, and at 800 /C14 C. The heating is continued
for 2 h in a tube furnace with 100 mL min /C0 1 flow rate of
N2 gas rate. The respective samples are further grinded
using agate mortar and pestle and then soaked in HCl
solution (0.5 N) for 12 h. The separated AC is then
cleaned with de-ionized (DI) water for several times until
the pH of the washed solution becomes neutral. The
finally cleaned AC is oven-dried at 120
/C14 C for overnight.
Based on the activation temperature, the AC samples
obtained from rice straw are named as ACRS-600, ACRS-
700, and ACRS-800. Similarly, the AC sample obtained
from eucalyptus wood is named as ACEW-600, ACEW-
700, and ACEW-800. The production yield of these sam-
ples is determined, for example, the yield of ACRS-700
and ACEW-700 were 5.6-5.8 wt% and 8.6-8.9 wt%, respec-
tively. A schematic representation of the methodology for
the preparation of porous AC from rice straw and euca-
lyptus wood biomass is presented in Scheme
1A. The
chemical reagents used for the preparation of respective
porous AC and the techniques used for their
characterization are given in Section S1, as Supporting
Information.
2.2 | Electrochemical analysis
All electrochemical studies (eg, CV, GCD, and EIS) of the
respective AC samples are carried out in a three-electrode
system using a Multi Autolab/M204 electrochemical
workstation (Metrohm Autolab B.V., The Netherlands).
The respective AC samples are used as working electrode.
A saturated calomel electrode is taken as a reference elec-
trode. A platinum electrode has been used as an auxiliary
electrode. The working electrode is prepared as follows:
80 wt% of the AC (ie, ACRS or ACEW) is thoroughly
mixed with 10 wt% carbon black and 10 wt% Polyvinyli-
dene fluoride binder. The mixture is then magnetically
stirred in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone solvent for 12 h until
a sticky slurry is formed. The slurry is casted on a cleaned
flexible graphite sheet of dimension 1 cm /C2 1 cm and
then dried at 80
/C14 C overnight to form the respective work-
ing electrode. The weight of the active material in the
working electrode is about 1 mg, which is accurately
measured in a microbalance. The thickness of working
electrodes is 1 mg cm
/C0 2. The EIS measurements are per-
formed at a frequency range between 10 /C0 2 and
2 /C2 104 Hz with an amplitude of 5 mV. The cyclic studies

and ACEW-700 are performed at 10.0 A g
/C0 1 for

3 | RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1 | Optimization of porous activated
carbon materials and its characterization
The CV and GCD plots of the batches of AC derived from
the biomass of rice straw, for example, ACRS-600, ACRS-
700, and ACRS-800 as supercapacitor electrodes are given
in Figure
S1, (Section S2, given as Supporting Informa-
tion). The specific capacitances of these batches activated
at different temperatures are obtained from their respec-
tive GCD measurements recorded at an arbitrary current
density of 1.0 A g
/C0 1 is found in the order of ACRS-700
(258 F g /C0 1) > ACRS-600 (205 F g /C0 1) > ACRS-800
(166 F g /C0 1), as determined from Equation ( 1). Notably,
the batch of ACRS-700 exhibited maximum specific
capacitance, which is higher than various types of ACs
prepared from different sources. 30,31 The role of texture,
morphology, and concentration of major constituents
might play a role for the variation in their charge storage
properties. In view of this, a comprehensive study has
been performed on AC derived from the rice straw
(ACRS-700) and eucalyptus wood (ACEW-700) bio-
masses, both activated at 700
/C14 C under nitrogen gas flow
after treating with 3 M KOH.
The CV curves of ACRS-700 and ACEW-700 recorded
after running initial 50 cycles at variable scan rates are
given in Figure 1A, B , respectively. The respective GCD
plots of ACRS-700 and ACEW-700 are given in
Figure 1C, D . The CV depicted a rectangular-shaped plot
for both the batches at all the scan rates and current den-
sities, and the GCD appeared a triangle-shaped plot.
These profiles are typically observed for SCs,
4,5 and corre-
spond to capacitive behavior in carbon-based SCs with
fast charging-discharging capability. 32 The coulomb effi-
ciency of ACRS-700 is more than 100% at lower current
density, for example, at 0.5 A g /C0 1, which has been
discussed in Section S3, Supporting Information. With
increase in scan rate, the area of CV curve increases and
it is consistent with the Randles-Sevcik equation. The
specific capacitance ( Csp) of ACRS-700 and ACEW-700
are determined using Equation ( 1). The specific capaci-
tance of ACRS-700 determined from the GCD measure-
ments is maximum at 0.5 A g /C0 1 (365 F g /C0 1), which is
about 1.5 times greater than that of ACEW-700
(168 F g /C0 1 at 0.5 A g /C0 1, Table 1). The corresponding volu-
metric capacitances are also given in Table 1. The specific
capacitance decreased with increase in current density.
This is attributable to the time constraint for the electro-
lyte ions to access all the active sites of the electrode. The
potential drop at the beginning of the discharge stage in
GCD profile is negligible at lower current densities (ie, at
0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 A g
/C0 1) and it increased gradually
at higher current densities up to 10.0 A g /C0 1. This might
be attributable to the limited electrolyte diffusion
kinetics.
It is worthwhile to investigate the structural, mor-
phological, textural, and compositional parameters of
the two materials to account for the variation in their
specific capacitances. The structural property of ACRS-
700 and ACEW-700 has been studied from XRD and
Raman spectroscopy. Both the batches exhibit two
broad diffraction peaks in the XRD spectra, correspond-
ing to (002) and (101) lattice plane reflections of gra-
phitic structure, respectively (Figure
2A). The more
intense peak corresponding to (002) plane reveals
graphitization and carbonization of the sample, while
the minor peak corresponding to the (101) facet is
indicative of a partial crystalline nature in the AC
formed due to KOH activation process.
33 These broad-
ened XRD peaks indicate partial amorphous and disor-
dered structure in both the batches of AC. The (002)
plane of ACRS-700 is observed at 23.8 /C14 , which appeared
at a lesser 2 θ value than that of ACEW-700
(2θ = 25.1/C14 ). This indicates larger interlayer distance in
the ACRS-700 than ACEW-700. It is attributable to
higher concentration of non-planar hemicellulose com-
ponent in ACRS-700 than that in ACEW-700 (men-
tioned later in proximate analysis discussion). Our

The porous structure in AC is mainly achieved by
thermal activation of KOH-treated pyrolyzed biomasses.
During the KOH activation process, the pyrolyzed bio-
mass reacts with KOH and tends to liberate a series of
CO (g), H
2 (g), CO2 (g), and H 2O (g) leading to the forma-
tion of pores and the intermediate products such as
K
2CO3 and K 2O. The produced K 2CO3 and CO 2 react

also responsible for the formation of additional pores.
The as-obtained potassium intercalates into the carbon
matrix, which is removed during washing with dilute
HCl and DI water producing large fraction of micropo-
rous structure. The tentative chemical reactions involved
in the formation of pores have already been discussed in
our earlier published work.
