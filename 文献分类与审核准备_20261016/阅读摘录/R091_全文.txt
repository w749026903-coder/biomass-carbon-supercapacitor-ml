Abstract
The graphitization and porous structure of carbon materials have been the focus of research on the electrochemical properties 
of supercapacitors. In this work, highly graphitized porous carbon (HPCS) is fabricated from biowaste sunflower shells using 
potassium hydroxide (KOH) as an activator. The effects of the mass ratio of KOH to raw material and activation temperature 
on the electrochemical properties are studied in detail. The results show that highly graphitized porous carbon after activation 
at 800°C for 2 h (HPCS-800–2) exhibits obvious graphitized stripes with a spacing of ca. 0.34 nm and a lower I D/IG value 
(0.797) than that of pristine carbon with non-activation (0.917). The HPCS-800–2 reaches a high specific capacitance of 
403 F   g−1  at a current density of 0.5 A   g−1 . In addition, the HPCS-800–2//HPCS-800–2 symmetric supercapacitor device 
displays outstanding higher energy density of 20.84 Wh   kg−1  than that of the YP50//YP50 device (8.18 Wh   kg−1 ).
Keywords Biomass · graphitization · sunflower shells · supercapacitors
Introduction
Supercapacitors, as a new type of energy storage device, 
mainly store energy through charge accumulation or 
reversible surface redox reaction. 1–4 Supercapacitors have 
received increasing attention owing to their fast charging 
and discharging ability, high power density, and long cycle 
life,5–7 and have been widely applied in many fields, such 
as transportation, portable electronics, medical electronics, 
and military equipment. 8,9 According to the energy storage 
mechanism, supercapacitors can be divided into two cat-
egories, namely electric double-layer capacitors (EDLCs) 
and pseudocapacitors. 10–12 Pseudocapacitors arise from 
fast and reversible oxidation–reduction (redox) or faradaic 
charge reaction of electrode materials, including conductive 
polymers such as polyaniline and  polypyrrole13 and metal 
oxides such as  MnO2  and14  Co3O4.15 EDLCs arise from elec-
trostatic charges accumulated at the electrode–electrolyte 
interface, such as carbon materials including carbon nano -
tubes (CNT), 16 ordered mesoporous carbon, 17 porous car -
bon,18,19 graphene, 20,21 and biomass carbon. 22–24 Among 
these, biomass carbon electrode materials have garnered 
increasing attention because of their abundant sources, low 
cost, and environmentally friendly nature.25–28
Many biomass waste materials are used to produce carbon 
electrodes applied in supercapacitors, such as straw, peel, 
fruit, and animal hair. 29 Kempasiddaiah et al. 30 reported 
mesoporous biomass carbon which was prepared from rad-
ish leaf waste, showing specific capacitance of 129 F   g−1  
at current density of 0.25 A   g−1  with specific surface area 
of about 704   m2   g−1 . Sankar et al. 26 prepared ultrathin 
mesoporous graphite carbon using biomass green tea waste 
as a raw material through a KOH activation process com -
bined with water or hydrochloric acid treatment, exhibit-
ing specific capacitance of 162 F   g−1  at current density of 
0.5 A   g−1 . Sun et al. 31 selected kelp as a carbon source and 
used its natural salt content (Ca, Na, etc.) as a template to 
obtain mesoporous carbon. Consequently, multichannel 
pores were obtained through chemical activation to enrich 
the microporous structure, thereby increasing the specific 
surface area and providing a wealth of effective adsorp-
tion sites. Mesoporous materials not only enhance the ionic 
conductivity of materials, but also improve the wettability 
of materials, which is critical to electrochemical-related

For carbon materials, the concerned problems involve

Experimental
Reagents and Materials
Sunflower shells were obtained from local supermarkets 
(Yinchuan City, Ningxia Province, China), KOH and HCl 
were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., 
Ltd., and YP50 was purchased from Kuraray Co., Ltd. The 
reagents were used as received.
Construction of Highly Graphitized Porous Biomass 
Carbon from Sunflower Shells (HPCS)
Pretreatment of Sunflower Shells
The sunflower shells were cleaned using water under stirring 
and ultrasound-assisting to remove the dirt and impurities, 
and then dried at 80°C over 12 h.
Fabrication of HPCS
First, a certain mass of sunflower shells was carbonized at 
300°C for 2 h to prepare the black precursor, denoted as 
SS-300–2. Next, 1 g precursor was soaked in different mass 
amounts of KOH (1, 2, 3, 4 g) in 30 mL water for 12 h 
to absorb all the ions in the solution, and the solvent was 
evaporated completely overnight. Subsequently, the obtained 
powders were carbonized and activated at different tempera-
tures (700°C, 800°C, and 900°C) at a rate of 2°C/min for 2 h, 
respectively. The powders were then immersed in 1 M HCl 
over 12 h and washed with water more than three times until 
the pH reached 7, followed by drying at 75°C for 12 h. The 
obtained samples were abbreviated as HPCS-X–Y (X = 700, 
800 and 900, where Y  = 1, 2, 3, and 4 represent the mass 
ratios of KOH to SS-300–2). In addition, pristine sunflower 
shells were carbonized at 800°C in a tubular furnace for 2 h 
at a similar rate to obtain carbon without pre-carbonation 
and KOH activation, which was labeled CS-800–0.
Material Characterization
The crystal structure of the samples was determined using 
x-ray diffraction (SmartLab, Cu  Kα). The morphology 
information was obtained by scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM, Hitachi SU-5000) and transmission electron micros-
copy (TEM, Talos 200 S). The specific surface area and pore 
size distribution were studied based on  N2 adsorption and 
desorption isotherms (JW-BK200C). Raman spectra were 
measured on a DXR Raman spectrometer. The elemental 
composition and chemical states of the samples were charac-
terized by x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo 
Scientific K-Alpha).
Preparation of Working Electrode
The prepared active material, acetylene black, conducting 
graphite, and polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) were mixed 
in a mass ratio of 80:7.5:7.5:5 to form a uniform slurry. 
Subsequently, it was coated on nickel foam of geometric 
surface area of ca. 1  cm2 as current collector (active material 
mass loading of 4 mg   cm−2 ). Then, the working electrode 
was obtained after drying at 80°C for 12 h and pressing at 
10 M Pa for ca. 15 s.
Electrochemical Performance Evaluation
The electrochemical properties were measured by a three-
electrode system comprising a reference electrode (saturated 
calomel electrode [SCE]), a counter electrode (platinum 
foil), and a working electrode. Cyclic voltammetry (CV), 
galvanostatic charge/discharge (GCD), and electrochemi-
cal impedance spectroscopy (EIS) were assessed using a

Results and Discussion
The preparation process for HPCS is as shown in Scheme 1. 
Firstly, the sunflower shells are cleaned and carbonized at 
300 °C for 2 h in order to make some small organic molecu-
lar materials decompose. Subsequently, the obtained powder 
is soaked in different concentrations of KOH solution for 
12 h to adsorb the ions completely. Then, the final powder 
is carbonized and activated at high temperature simultane-
ously to obtain 3D honeycombed highly graphitized porous 
carbon, which was denoted as HPCS.
The morphology of the prepared HPCS and SS-300–2 
is characterized using SEM and TEM. Figure S1 shows the 
SEM images of SS-300–2, exhibiting an irregular sheet-like 
structure with thickness of several microns. After heat treat-
ment at 800°C, the CS-800–0 still retains a sheet-like struc-
ture with length of about tens of microns and an irregular 
surface with no pores, as shown in Fig.  1a and Figure S2. 
However, after KOH activation, the HPCS800–2 exhibits

abundant pore structures with different sizes (Fig.  1b and 
Figure S3), which is caused by activation and pyrolysis of 
potassium hydroxide. Firstly, potassium hydroxide is con-
verted into  K2CO3, and then  K2CO3 is decomposed into  K2O 
and  CO2 with the increase in temperature. The generated 
 K2CO3,  K2O, and  CO2 can all react with carbon to produce 
CO. With the elimination of gases (CO and  CO2) and potas-
sium vapor (K), abundant pores form in the carbon matrix, 
resulting in the formation of porous carbon materials. 38,39 
This hierarchical porous structure is conducive to the infil-
tration of electrolyte ions. The TEM image of HPCS-800–2 
shows a thin sheet structure (Fig.  1c) and partial microcrys-
tal and fewer-layer carbon are found in Fig.  1d. The lattice 
fringe distance of 0.34 nm belongs to the (002) plane of 
graphite, which can improve the conductivity and electro-
chemical performance.
In order to further characterize the structure of carbon, 
the XRD patterns, Raman spectra, and porous structure 
of CS-800–0 and HPCS samples are examined at differ -
ent temperature (HPCS-700–2, HPCS-800–2, and HPCS-
900–2), as shown in Fig.  2. Figure  2a shows the XRD pat-
terns of the four samples. Two small weak peaks appear at 
approximately 28° and 40°, corresponding to the (200) and 
(220) crystal planes of KCl (PDF#41-1476) for CS-800–0 
because of the large number of salts present in the biomass 
carbon that cannot be removed completely. At the same time, 
all samples show two distinct broad peaks at 22–24° and 
43.5°, corresponding to the (002) and (100) crystal planes of 
graphite, respectively. The ideal angle for ordered graphitic 
structures is reported at 26°; however, the lower diffraction 
angles at 22–24° for all samples suggest an amorphous car-
bon structure. 40 Meanwhile, among the activated samples 
prepared at different temperatures, the (002) peak intensity 
of HPCS-800–2 is the smallest, indicating that the activation 
effect of HPCS-800–2 is the best.41 From the Raman spectra 
(Fig.  2b), the four samples all reflect an obvious D peak 
at ~ 1358   cm−1 , and G peak at  ~ 1585   cm−1 , corresponding 
to defect/disordered structure and graphite structure, respec-
tively. Similarly, the SS-300–2 shows the same two peaks 
as seen in Figure S4. The intensity ratio of the D peak and 
G peak (I D/IG) presents the degree of graphitization. 42,43 
The values of I D/IG are 0.834, 0.797 and 0.828 for HPCS-
700–2, HPCS-800–2, and HPCS-900–2, respectively. Those 
data are all lower than that of CS-800–0 (0.917), indicating

provide active sites for charge storage and redox reaction. 
The mesopores and macropores facilitate the migration of

pores of all HPCS samples are in the range of 2 ~ 5 nm. The

CS-800–0, larger than that of the HPCS samples. It is worth 
noting that HPCS-800–2 shows the smallest average pore 
size among all samples. The lower ID/IG (0.797) and smaller 
average pore size (2.47 nm) for HPCS-800–2 predict better 
electrochemical performance than the others.

76.59%, 21%, and 2.41%, respectively. The content of N and

Figure  3b shows the deconvolution of the C 1s spectrum

C–O (~  285.7 eV), and C=O (~  288.1 eV), respectively. 
Two types of oxygen appear in the high-resolution spec-
trum of O 1s, attributed to C=O (~ 531.8 eV) and C–O 
(~ 533 eV), respectively (Fig.  3c). In Fig.  3d, N 1s spectra 
reveal the presence of two kinds of nitrogen, pyrrole nitrogen 
(~ 400.5 eV) and graphite nitrogen (~ 401.7 eV). The double 
co-doping of oxygen/nitrogen enhances hydrophilicity and 
provide pseudocapacitance, implying good electrochemical 
performance.52

Conclusion
Highly graphitized porous carbon was fabricated from cheap 
sunflower shells. The influence of the mass ratio of KOH 
to raw material and the activation temperature on the elec-
trochemical performance were investigated. With increas-
ing temperature, the specific capacitance first increased and 
then decreased. And as the ratio of KOH to raw materi-
als increased at 800°C, the specific capacitance exhibited a 
similar trend to temperature change. HPCS-800–2 showed 
excellent electrochemical performance, with a specific 
capacitance of 403 F   g−1  at 0.5 A   g−1  based on high gra-
phitization (I D/IG = 0.797), high specific surface area and 
proper pore size. Additionally, the HPCS-800–2//HPCS-
800–2 device exhibited outstanding higher energy density 
of 20.84 Wh   kg−1  in comparison with that of the YP50//
YP50 device (8.18 Wh   kg−1 ).
