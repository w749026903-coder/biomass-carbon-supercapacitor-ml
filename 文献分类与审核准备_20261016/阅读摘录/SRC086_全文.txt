Abstract
This complete investigation presents a combined experimental and computational 
Approach to emergent high-performance nickel cobalt sulfide (NiCo 2S4) 
nanostructures for Supercapacitor applications. NiCo 2S4 samples were synthesised 
via a two-step solvothermal Method at temperatures of 120–180 °C (designated 
NCS1-NCS4) and scientifically characterised using XRD, BET, SEM, XPS and FTIR. 
The optimised NCS4 electrode exhibited a notable specific capacitance of 920.04 
Fg− 1 from cyclic voltammetry and 375 Fg − 1 from galvanostatic Charge-discharge 
measurements. DFT calculations revealed that the (111) surface has the lowest 
surface energy (0.85 Jm − 2) and the highest electrochemical activity, while nickel 
vacancies have the lowest formation energy (1.8 eV) under Ni-poor conditions. ML 
models achieved R 2 = 0.97 in predicting capacitance, with synthesis temperature 
identified as the most critical parameter (importance: 0.28). A Comparative analysis 
of simulated and experimental results showed excellent agreement (average 
deviation < 5%). The symmetric supercapacitor device achieved an exceptional 
specific capacitance of 1234.89 Fg − 1, an energy density of 42.9 Whkg − 1, a power 
density of 735 Wkg − 1, and 82.82% capacitance retention after 5000 cycles. Pre-
fabrication analysis indicated optimal mass loading of 1.5 mg cm − 2 and electrode 
thickness of 200 nm for device development. This integrated methodology provides 
a comprehensive framework for the rational design of transition metal sulfide 
electrodes, with applications in electric automobiles, portable semiconductor 
technology, renewable energy storage, and industrial power systems.
Keywords Nickel cobalt sulfide, Solvothermal synthesis, Supercapacitor, DFT 
simulation, Machine learning, Energy storage, Device fabrication

  1 Introduction
We need better ways to store energy because we use a lot of portable devices, renewable 
energy and electric cars these days. Even though we have many renewable energy sys -
tems, they still have some problems that stop them from being used widely. That’s why 
we urgently need to find better ways to save energy when we have it and use it when we 
need it. Many things we use every day, like cell phones, walkie-talkies and electric cars, 
rely on devices that store electrical energy such as batteries, supercapacitors and fuel 
cells. Supercapacitors, which work like fast-charging batteries are getting a lot of atten -
tion because they can charge and discharge quickly, just like lithium-ion batteries [ 1, 2]. 
The exponential growth in global energy consumption, combined with the urgent tran -
sition toward sustainable energy systems has catalysed intensive research in advanced 
energy storage technologies [ 3]. Supercapacitors have emerged as promising candidates 
due to their unique combination of high-power density, rapid charge-discharge capabil -
ity and exceptional cycling stability [ 4]. Unlike batteries that store energy through bulk 
Faradaic reactions, enabling superior power delivery [5].

capacitors. A new group of ternary metal systems of semiconductors, such as NiFe 2O4, 
NiCuS4, NiCo 2S4, CuCo 2S4, MnCo 2O4, etc. hold great potential for achieving high 
energy density with good power density and stability [ 10] Among ternary semiconduct -
ing materials, sulphur-containing compounds play a significant role. Various compounds

are under investigation for supercapacitor applications. By integrating these materials 
with other conductive additives, such as metals or carbon-based materials, their per -
formance can be further enhanced. The conductive large surface areas and excellent 
electrical conductivity are in gerent characteristics of metal sulfide nanostructures. it

2 Experimental section
2.1 Materials and synthesis
NiCo2S4 was synthesised via a two-step solvothermal method. Nickel nitrate hexahy -
drate (Ni(NO 3)2.6H2O 0.12mM) and cobalt nitrate hexahydrate (Co(NO 3)2.6H2O 0.24 
mM) were dissolved in an isopropanol /ethylene glycol mixture (1:1 v/v). The Ni: Co 
molar ratio was kept 1:2 The solution was subjected to hydrothermal treatment at 120–
180 °C for 6 h.The resulting Ni-Co precursor was mixed with thioacetamide (TAA, 1:3 
molar ratio and sulfurized at 160 °C for 6 h. Sample prepared at 120 °C,140 °C,160 °C 
and 180 °C were labelled NCS1, NCS2, NCS3 and NCS4 respectively.

2.3 Computational methods
First-principles calculations were performed using the Vienna an initio Simulation Pack-
age (VASP) [25] with the projector augmented wave method. The exchange-correlation 
functional was treated within GGA-PBE [ 26], with DFT + U correction (U = 6.0  eV for 
Ni, 5.0 eV for Co) to properly describe strongly correlated d electrons [27]. A plane-wave 
cutoff of 735 eV and k-point meshes of 6 × 6 × 6 (bulk) and 4 × 4 × 1 (surfaces) was used. 
Machine learning models were developed using Python with scikit-learn and XG-Boost 
Libraries, with 5-fold cross-validation and SHAP analysis for feature importance [28].

3.1 Structural and morphological analysis
Figure 1 present the XRD patterns and FTIR spectra of NiCo 2S4 samples. XRD analysis 
confirmed the formation of the cubic spinel NiCo 2S4 phase (Fd-3 m space group) with 
characteristic peaks at 2θ = 31.5o, 38.4o, 44.6o, 56.9o, 59.3o, 65.5o and 78.1o. The crystallite 
size decreased from 30.2 nm (NCS1) to 23.5 nm (NCS4) with increasing temperature. 
The crystallinity and phase purity of the powder were studied by using an XRD graphs 
show that NiCo 2S4 is crystalline. The diffraction peaks observed at 2θ values of 38.4 o 
belongs to the (400) plane whereas peaks at 44.6o, 65.5o and 78.1o are due to the Ni. XRD 
studied confirm the crystalline nature of the NiCo 2S4 powder. The peaks are assigned to

NiCo2S4 is a compound that crystallises in a spinel structure, which if characterised 
by a cubic lattice. This material has garnered attention due to its unique properties, par -
ticularly in applications such as catalysis, energy storage and as an electrode material. In 
its X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern, NiCo 2S4 typically exhibits distinct diffraction peaks 
corresponding to various Miller indices (hk1). The most prominent peaks are observed 
at 2θ values of 18.4 o (111), 31.5 o (220), 38.4 o (311), 44.6 o (400), 56.9 o (331), 59.3 o (422) 
and 65.5o (511), which are characteristic of the spinel structure of the material. These 
peaks are indexed based of the standard cubic spinel lattice. The JCPDS card 00-020-
0782 is commonly used to identify NiCo 2S4 and it provides the reference for the specific 
diffraction pattern expected for this compound.
As the temperature of the material varies, especially under high-temperature condi -
tions, subtle but noticeable changes can occur in its XRD pattern. The diffraction peaks 
may shift to lower 2θ values due to thermal expansion of the crystal lattice. This shift 
occurs because, at higher temperatures, atoms in the lattice vibrate more intensely, caus-
ing the lattice to expand slightly, which in turn decreases the diffraction angle according 
to Bragg’s law. The extent of these shifts can be used to determine the material’s thermal

dominated by Ni 3d and Co 3d states hybridized with S 3P states, while the conduction 
band consists primarily of metal d States. The presence of d states near the Fermi level is 
responsible for the electrochemical activity.

6 Conclusions
This comprehensive study demonstrates the successful development of high-perfor -
mance NiCo 2S4 nanostructures through an integrated experimental-computational 
approach. The key findings are; The optimized NCS4 electrode exhibited specific capaci-
tance of 920.04 F g − 1 (CV) and 375 F g − 1(GCD), with the symmetric device achieving 
1234.89 F g-1, energy density of 42.9 Wh kg − 1 and 82.82% retention after 5000 cycles. 
DFT simulations revealed that the (111) surface has the lowest surface formation energy 
(0.85  J m− 2) and the highest electrochemical activity. Nickel vacancies have the low -
est formation energy (1.8  eV). Machin learning models achieved R 2 = 0.97 in predict-
ing capacitance, with synthesis temperature identified as the most critical parameter 
(importance: 0.28). A comparative analysis showed excellent agreement between simu -
lated and experimental results (average deviation < 5%), thereby validating the integrated 
approach. Pre-fabrication analysis indicated optimal mass loading of 1.5 mg cm − 1 and 
thickness of 200 nm for device development.

research explores the relationship between the structural, morphological and physi -

ity based on synthesis conditions and material characteristics. DFT simulations are also 
utilised to gain deeper insights into NiCo 2S4 electronic structure, change distribution 
and redox activity at the atomic level. This approach helps to better understand how it 
stores charge.
