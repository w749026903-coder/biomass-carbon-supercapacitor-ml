for high-performance supercapacitors: 
panoramic insights across dimensional spectra

1 Introduction
Electricity has emerged as the principal currency of energy exchange in the modern 
world. The recent increase in the acceptance of electrically operated automobiles such 
as e-bicycles, e-mopeds, electric cars, hybrid buses, and e-rickshaws has amplified the 
demand and dependency on electrical power [ 1– 5]. The global rise in living standards 
can be largely accredited to increasing reliance on electrical power. The development of 
various electrochemical energy storage devices-such as conventional capacitors, super -
capacitors, batteries, and fuel cells has been crucial in addressing energy management

Abstract
The design architecture of electrode materials plays a pivotal role in shaping the 
electrochemical performance of supercapacitor. The dimensionality of electrode 
material proposes trade-off between specific surface area, pore architecture, 
mechanical strength and flexibility. This review undertakes a comprehensive 
classification of electrode materials into zero-dimensional, one-dimensional, two-
dimensional, and three-dimensional architectures, further correlating their structural 
characteristics with surface area, electrical conductivity, and ion transport dynamics. 
Various classes of materials, including carbon-based nanostructures, transition metal 
oxides, transition metal dichalcogenides, MXenes, and metal–organic frameworks, 
are analysed with respect to morphology-dependent performance characteristics. 
The design of heterostructures and multi-dimensional electrodes, as well as various 
synthesis methods, and the crucial elements of sustainability and scalability, are also 
taken into account. Furthermore, viewpoints for future avenues that pave the way 
for the development of supercapacitors are presented. Overall, this review aims to 
bridge the understanding between dimensional architecture, synthesis approach 
and performance, offering acuity in rational material design strategies for sustainable, 
high-performance supercapacitor systems.
Keywords Energy storage, Electrode, Dimensionality, High-performance 
supercapacitor

Supercapacitors (SCs) have attracted considerable attention in technological innova -
tions due to their distinctive properties. They serve as an effective intermediary between 
batteries and traditional capacitors, providing a unique combination of rapid charging 
and discharging rates, high specific power, and enhanced specific energy [ 9– 15]. With 
a wide operating temperature range, exceptional cyclability, and high coulombic effi -
ciency, SCs stand out as sustainable and efficient energy storage devices, potentially 
surpassing current battery technologies [ 16– 20]. Contemporarily, SCs are in use for 
automated external defibrillators, automated robots, emergency doors of aircraft, regen -
erative braking system of automobiles, electric bus, armed force services, and defence 
utilities [21– 26].
Various studies conducted in recent years have demonstrated the growing importance 
of SCs in the future of energy storage systems. Within its broad landscape of research, 
the development of SC electrode is receiving extensive attention due to its paramount 
role in energy storage. Focused research efforts are directed toward the development 
and optimization of advanced electrode materials for commercial supercapacitor [ 27–
30]. In light of these widespread experimental studies, there is a critical need for review 
articles to consolidate and summarize the existing scientific knowledge. A comprehen -
sive overview of advancements, challenges pertaining development of electrode materi -
als are essential to propel future investigations for development of next-generation SCs.
A recent review comprehended summary of advancements in multi-dimensional 
transition metal oxides, focusing on the relationship between structure and properties 
to develop and synthesize high-performance SC electrodes [ 31]. Analogously, a differ -
ent study emphasized on range of zero-dimensional to three-dimensional architectures 
for SCs, with primary focus on carbon-based materials and their synergetic combina -
tions with various promising alternatives [ 32]. A group of researchers presented a study 
conferring the SC electrode materials from the standpoint of zero to three-dimensional 
nanostructures [ 33]. A far wider variety of electrode materials were discussed in this 
review, and attention to the performance of SCs in relation to the dimensionality of the 
electrode material was emphasized. This unconventional way of assessment of electro -
chemical performance and dimensional structure demands additional exploration.

Most available reviews tend to focus on specific classes of materials, which limits a 
broader understanding of how different material families affect electrochemical perfor -
mance. To address this subject, our effort aims to provide critical analysis of the latest 
progress concerning the electrode materials, with a particular focus on their morphol -
ogy, dimensional characteristics, and their influence on supercapacitor performance. 
A revelation from the literature survey comprehends a gap in review articles that sys -
tematically explore recent advancements across a broad spectrum of electrode materials 
for SC applications. Absence of systematic evaluation of newer material families, such 
as MXenes and transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), metal organic frameworks 
(MOFs) built the foundational motivation for this exercise. The distinctiveness of this 
review lies in the consideration of wide variety of materials and assessment from the 
standpoint of dimensionality, synthesis practises, design of heterostructure, scalability 
as well as sustainability of SC electrode materials. This assessment exclusively consid -
ered only the aqueous electrolyte based SCs; neglecting other categories- organic, gel, 
and ionic liquid. This careful selection minimizes variances from different electrolytes, 
allowing a focused analysis of effect of dimensionality of electrode material on superca -
pacitor’s performance.
Nanostructured electrodes are transforming the research of energy storage by improv-
ing the charge transport, energy density, and cycling stability [ 34– 36]. Based on their 
spatial characteristics, these are categorized into zero-dimensional (0D), one-dimen -
sional (1D), two-dimensional (2D), and three-dimensional (3D) structures (refer to 
Fig. 2). The structural architecture of these materials plays a pivotal role in governing key 
electrochemical properties such as ion transport, charge storage mechanisms, and elec -
trical conductivity. Each of these category offers distinctive features and morphological 
characteristics to deliver meritorious performance in SC related applications [ 37– 40]. 
Table 1 and Fig.  3 emphasize the significance of material dimensionality and their cor -
responding characteristics that are recently explored.

Different charge storage principles can be witnessed in a SC (as depicted in Fig.  5). 
For the purpose of energy storage, it can opt for faradaic, non-faradaic, or amalgama -
tion of both schemes. Accordingly, there are three different types of SCs—pseudocapaci -
tors (PSC), electric double layer capacitors (EDLC), and hybrid supercapacitors (HSC), 
respectively [46– 49]. EDLCs operate on purely non-faradaic and reversible adsorption–
desorption mechanism, in which energy is stored electrostatically through the formation 
of an electric double layer at the interface between the electrode surface and the elec -
trolyte. This process enables rapid charging, as ions from the electrolyte adhere to the 
electrode surface during the charging phase and are subsequently released during dis -
charging. The absence of chemical transformations in this mechanism provides EDLCs 
with exceptional cyclic stability, an extended cycle life, cost-effectiveness, and ease of

fabrication. These attributes position EDLCs as a leading option among energy storage 
devices, particularly with respect to cyclic stability [ 48, 50].
PSCs function through a faradaic reduction–oxidation (redox) process, wherein 
charge storage is achieved by the reversible transfer of electrons between the electrode 
and the electrolyte, occurring at or near the electrode surface. In contrast to batteries, 
which rely on diffusion-controlled charge storage, PSCs undertake a surface-controlled 
mechanism. This distinction enables PSCs to attain higher energy densities compared to 
traditional EDLCs. The pseudocapacitive phenomenon is primarily observed in various 
materials, including metal oxides such as ruthenium oxide and cobalt oxide, as well as 
conductive polymers, including polyaniline and polypyrrole [50, 51].
HSCs integrate the charge storage mechanisms of both faradaic (pseudocapacitors) 
and non-faradaic (EDLCs) processes.  By combining these two approaches, HSCs effec -
tively balance performance parameters, offering a coordinated enhancement in cyclic 
stability, power density, and energy density.  Such hybrid systems frequently utilize 
composite materials, often comprising carbon-based materials like graphene or carbon 
nanotubes intermixed with either conducting polymers or metal oxides, to optimize the 
electrochemical properties [48, 52].
The benchmark parameters for assessment of SC are- specific capacitance, cyclic sta -
bility, equivalent series resistance (ESR), energy density (ED), and power density (PD) 
[53– 56]. Various electroanalytical techniques such as Electro Impedance Spectroscopy 
(EIS), Cyclic Voltammetry (CV), and Galvanostatic Charge Discharge (GCD) method 
are accepted for quantification of SC’s performance. The capacitance expressed as Far -
ads, is the total charge stored corresponding to the applied voltage. Based on the design 
of the SC, areal capacitance and gravimetric capacitance are often evaluated. The energy 
stored in a SC is relative to charge stored and applied voltage and mentioned in terms 
of Wh/kg. Energy density-interchangeably known as specific energy is the amount of 
energy that can be stored in the energy storage device. It is directly related to the square 
of the operating voltage. Consequently, utilizing a broad potential window can enhance 
energy density, which is further influenced by the type of electrolyte employed in SC’s 
design. Power density is the rate at which the stored energy can be delivered per unit 
time. It is commonly expressed as W/kg. Aqueous electrolytes provide lower resistance, 
resulting in higher power density; whereas the higher resistance of organic electrolytes 
alleviates its power output [ 14, 57, 58]. The resistance originating from individual com -
ponents (such as current collector, electrode, electrolyte, separator membrane) needs to 
be taken into consideration to evaluate the net resistance of SC cell, this is known as 
Equivalent Series Resistance (ESR). The EIS technique is utilized to calculate ESR, and 
is quantified in term of Ohm (Ω) and influenced by the type of electrolyte used. Cyclic 
stability is used to quantify SC’s performance after a certain number of cycles. It is mea-
sured as difference between initial capacitance and the capacitance after n th consecu -
tive cycle. It is commonly stated as % retention which is the degree of capacitance that 
is preserved. The capacitance decrement originates from several degradation processes 
such as electrode corrosion, redox byproduct buildup, active material loss, pore modi -
fications, and increasing internal resistance. Cyclic stability reflects the extent to which 
an electrode material preserves its structural integrity and electrochemical functionality 
over repeated charge–discharge cycles.

3 Significance of dimensionality on electrochemical performance of SC
The optimization of electrochemical performance relies on a well-balanced combination 
of surface area, pore size, and electrical conductivity, all of which are strongly influenced 
by the dimensionality of the electrode material [ 59– 61]. The nanoscale structure and 
diverse geometry of materials embark exceptional electrochemical properties, thus play -
ing a crucial role in advancing high-performance SCs. The performance matrix of a SC, 
such as specific capacitance, energy density, power density, cyclic stability, equivalent 
series resistance are highly influenced from the nano-architecture. The specific surface 
area, pore volume, pore size, thermal and mechanical strength, electrical conductivity 
are the significant pillars that controls the charge transfer principles. Therefore, optimi -
zation of these parameters can not be neglected to develop the high-performance SC 
electrode materials. The dimensionality spectrum covers span from 0 to 3D, and each of 
the dimension exhibits unique ways to contribute towards enhanced energy storage. The 
dimensionality approach is non-conventional and therefore needs further exploration 
for the development of new-generation SCs.
4 Carbon-based nanostructured electrodes for SC
Carbon-based materials are extremely popular choice as an energy storage electrode 
due to abundant availability, less cost, ease of synthesis [ 62– 65]. They have forever 
been a front-runner among all electrodes due to their notable electrochemical proper -
ties such as high electrical conductivity, exceptional surface area, chemical tunability at 
the nanoscale, low weight, and mechanical flexibility. The discovery of numerous car -
bon derivatives such as graphene, graphene oxide (GO), reduced graphene oxide (rGO), 
carbon nanotubes (CNTs), fullerenes, carbon quantum dots (CQDs), activated carbon 
(AC) has tremendously enhanced the energy storage capabilities of SCs [ 66– 68]. Their 
versatility in structure and significant physicochemical properties contributes towards 
boosted electrochemical performance of supercapacitors. This section will further 
emphasize the recent developments in carbon electrode materials across the spatial 
dimensions.
4.1 0D carbon-based materials
0D carbon materials are small spherical structures usually under 100  nm. Fullerenes, 
made entirely of carbon, often resemble icosahedral architecture, with C60 or "buckyball" 
being the most popular. Carbon nano-onions share a similar structure with fullerenes, 
featuring concentric layers of graphitic carbon. Quantum dots, in contrast, are semi -
conductor nanostructures that create quantized energy levels by confining electrons 
or holes in three dimensions, consisting of a mix of sp 3 and sp 2 carbon atoms [ 69– 71]. 
Such architectures exhibit large electrical conductivity, high degree of surface develop -
ment that allow them to function as effective electron acceptors. This leads to enhanced 
charge transfer, further enabling efficient charge storage at the nanoscale [72– 74].

T. Seesan et al. evaluated the electrochemical performance of hydrothermally synthe -
sized reduced graphene quantum dots (rGO/QD) with an average size of 4.14 nm and a 
graphitic interlayer spacing of 3.41 Å [ 75]. This 0D material disclosed quantum confine-

observed with KOH/rGO-QDs electrolyte was marginally elevated compared to KOH

dots (GQDs) for supercapacitor was reported by H. Ahmad and colleagues [ 76]. This 
study conducted electrochemical investigation through cyclic voltammetry and dem -
onstrated intra comparison between the carbon-based nanomaterials. The monolayer 
GQDs showed expansive surface area, uniform size distribution, and great porosity 
resulting boosted capacitance vis-`a-vis the distinct carbon-based electrodes. A capaci -
tance of 113 F/g with potential span of −0.7 to 0.3 V was obtained from GQD electrode 
material.
Another team of researchers investigated mesoporous fullerene (C 60) as flexible nega -
tive electrode for SC [ 77]. The EDLC type electrode achieved a specific capacitance of 
440 F/g at a current density of 2 A/g, utilizing a 6 M LiCl aqueous electrolyte. Remark -
able cyclic stability (retaining 98.67% capacitance over 10,000 cycles), and a rate capabil -
ity of 84.85% was witnessed. EIS analysis revealed low equivalent series resistance and 
charge transfer resistance, which indicated efficient charge transport originating from 
the material's low dimensionality.
The versatile family of carbon material is studied with much interest due to their abun-
dance and cost effectiveness [79– 81]. P . Kadyan et al. explored an eco-friendly approach 
to yield activated carbon nano-onions (CNOs) extracted from waste flaxseed oil [ 78]. 
CNOs with a size of 20–50  nm and a high BET surface area of 181 m 2/g successfully 
delivered a specific capacitance of 745.9 F/g at a current density of 2 A/g (refer to Fig.  6 
(a, b and c). The symmetric cell arrangement delivered an energy density of 22.5 Wh/
kg with a power density of 2102 W/kg.  It retained ~ 89% of its capacitance post 5000 
charge–discharge cycles. The nano-onion morphology is found to augment the charge 
storage by enabling efficient ion incorporation and transport, highlighting the potential 
opportunity of biomass-derived 0D nanostructures for energy storage.

4.3 2D carbon-based materials
Carbon 2D materials, notably graphene and alternatives, have grown in popularity 
due to their inherent benefits. The energy storage capacity is greatly increased by their 
remarkable electrical conductivity, thermal stability, as well as large and open flat sur -
faces that provides substantial electrode–electrolyte interfaces [ 99– 101]. As graphene 
can be manipulated chemically, functional groups may be added to further enhance elec-
trode–electrolyte interaction and boost capacitance even further. Researchers are also 
investigating composite materials that combine graphene or reduced graphene oxide 
(rGO) with other class of materials to capitalize on the advantages while preserving their 
high conductivity [102– 104].

As a brief summary of this section-several studies have reported limitations associ -
ated with 0D carbon-based nanomaterials such as the requirement of energy-intensive 
resources, complex synthesis methods, low material yield, solvent-precursor compatibil-
ity. Also, the spatial constraints in zero-dimensional nanomaterial limits their ability to 
form efficient conductive networks, which can adversely affect electrode performance. 
In contrast, one-dimensional nanomaterials demonstrate higher aspect ratio, better elec-
tron and ion transport as well as enhanced mechanical stability [ 121, 122]. Such charac-
teristics promotes the expediency of 1D nanomaterials for application as SC electrode. 
The 1D carbon-based nanomaterials like CNTs exhibit exceptional axial electron mobil-
ity, their linear structure imposes intrinsic trade-offs in SC’s design. The tubular geom -
etry restricts ion diffusion to radial pathways, leading to sluggish kinetics in high-power 
scenarios. Additionally, their surface area limits charge storage capacity compared to 2D 
nanostructures [123, 124]. As the dimensionality advances from a 1D to a 2D structure, 
the exposure of accessible electrochemically active facets increases, thereby enhancing 
their effectiveness in energy storage applications. While 2D carbon-based materials like 
graphene have garnered significant attention due to short ion diffusion lengths; however, 
their tendency to restack obstruct efficient ion transport [125]. This may later result into 
loss of active sites and structural instability of electrode material. Such factors cannot 
circumvent poor performance pertaining energy density and cyclic stability. 3D carbon 
nanomaterials with well-connected pores not only offer a larger accessible surface area 
but also facilitate continuous ion and electron transport, hence supposedly elevating 
electrochemical performance.
The synergic effect commonly observed in composite electrode such as rGO/CNT 
is due to possible interaction between multi-dimensional (2D/1D) architecture of elec -
trode material [ 126]. 1D CNT exhibits enormous surface area, excellent mechanical 
strength and high electrical conductivity as well. On the other hand, two-dimensional 
rGO with functional groups offers advantages for energy storage purpose. The interfa -
cial contact between these structures minimizes charge-transfer resistance and enables 
fast ion diffusion, thereby enhancing both rate capability and overall capacitance. The 
TMO/carbon composite refers to the complementary interaction between (i) the high 
electrical conductivity and large accessible surface area of the carbon-based nano-struc -
ture (such as rGO, CNT), which promotes efficient charge transport and double-layer 
capacitance formation, and (ii) the rich redox-active sites provided by the transition 
metal oxide component, which contributes pseudocapacitance via faradaic redox reac -
tions [127]. The individual contribution of non-faradaic and faradaic charge storage is 
evaluated based on Dunn’s method [128].

5 Transition metal oxides nanostructured electrodes for SC
Since the charge storage methods of carbon-based materials rely on ion adsorption at 
the electrode/electrolyte interface, they suffer from poor specific capacitance. Like -
wise, conducting polymers are also not as completely successful as electrode materi -
als, due to their mechanical deterioration [ 135]. Instead, the most promising materials 
for high-performance supercapacitors are transition metal oxides (TMOs) because of 
their large availability, high theoretical specific capacitance, and strong redox reaction 
potential [ 136– 139]. Notably, metal oxides exhibiting pseudocapacitance demonstrate 
specific capacitance often several order of magnitude higher than those of carbon-based 
EDLC electrodes. This attribute further emphasizes their potential for next-generation 
SC technologies. Multiple oxidation states are shown by TMOs, including Manganese 
Dioxide (MnO2), Cobalt Oxide (Co 3O4), Nickel Oxide (NiO) and different mixed metal 
oxides [140– 142]. This allows for quick and reversible electron transfer processes, which 
are crucial for energy storage. Incorporating nanostructured oxides enhances superca -
pacitor output by increasing surface area and improving ion transport [ 143, 144]. Pos-
sible dimensional configurations are being explored from family of TMO materials; each 
offering unique benefits that support improved SC performance. These materials pos -
sess unique structural and electrochemical properties that could lead to the advance -
ment of high-performance energy storage devices, which are essential for meeting the 
increasing energy demands across various industries.
5.1 0D transition metal oxide electrode materials
0D TMOs typically have sizes between 1 and 100 nm, which is attributed to their dis -
tinct size-dependent electronic characteristics. They yield high surface-to-volume ratio, 
which significantly increases electron availability. Compared to bulk materials, their 
quantum confinement effects provide an interesting route to higher energy storage effi -
ciency, which makes it a viable option for cutting-edge supercapacitor designs [ 31]. The 
circumstances of synthesis method evidently influence the applications of nanomaterials 
in several domains [ 145, 146]. In order to achieve optimal performance in applications 
that demand large surface areas, like energy storage applications, bottom-up approaches 
are considered as ideal. On the other hand, some top-down methods might be preferred 
in production due to their ease of use or affordability [ 147, 148]. The understanding of 
the correlation between synthesis conditions and nanomaterial’s characteristics is cru -
cial in adaptation of materials for specified energy storage applications. Ruthenium 
oxide (RuO2) is widely adopted as an electrode for high-performance pseudocapacitor 
applications owing to multiple redox states, fast redox kinetics, wide potential window.

7 MXene nanostructured electrodes for SC
The transition metal carbide-nitride materials shortly referred as MXenes, have show -
cased enormous potential as new class of electrode materials for supercapacitors. The 
empirical relation M n+1XnTx (n = 1 to 3) is provided as best description of this com -
pound. Here, M symbolizes transition metal, and X designates carbon and/or nitro -
gen, and T denotes functional groups attached to it (as depicted in Fig.  20(a). MXene 
materials standout because of their extraordinary properties, such as their surface area, 
fast charge transfer and ion diffusion, high electrical conductivity, and customized sur -
face chemistry [ 175, 176, 193]. MXenes exhibiting 2D nano-architecture are the most 
extensively researched. Their exquisite ultrathin structure offers higher surface area and 
larger active site, which speeds up redox processes and improves capacitance values. The 
exceptional energy storage capabilities of 2D MXenes are a result of their capacity to 
sustain different oxidation states, which improves their pseudocapacitive behaviour and 
electrical double-layer capacitance. Tantalum carbide additionally contains features that 
improve electrochemical performance, like low electrical resistivity, and a few stoichio -
metric compositions that have been reported to be superconductors. In addition, their 
Seebeck coefficients are low, and they exhibit resistance to thermal shocks, and corro -
sion. Tantalum carbide has superior qualities to its comparable metal counterparts, and 
is an excellent heat conductor. On this basis, R. Syamsai directed efforts to synthesize 
tantalum carbide by removing aluminum from its parent phase, or tantalum aluminium 
carbide [ 186]. SEM illustrations of the synthesized TA 4C3 clarified successful etching 
and exfoliation of the layers (depicted in Fig. 21(a and b). The surface area obtained from 
BET analysis was observed to be 6.599 m 2/g. The cyclic voltammograms were carried 
out between −0.2 to 1 Volt and it highlighted hybrid EDLC-pseudocapacitive behaviour 
with 0.1 M H 2SO4. Furthermore, it quantified highest specific capacitance of 481 F/g at 
5 mV/s with a decrease of 11% after 2000 cycles as shown in Fig. 21(c).
Several intrinsic problems including the tendency for self-oxidation and layer restack -
ing prevents a widespread use of MXenes. A study led by Sandra Mathew addressed this 
issue with MXene composite material (Ti 3C2/CeO2) electrode, by gaining almost two 
and a half times more capacitance than pristine MXene [187]. The dual functionality not 
only highlighted the versatility of the material but also its potential for integration into 
hybrid energy systems that require both efficient energy conversion and storage. Sev -
eral studies have introduced Cerium oxide as new electrode material for electrochemical 
applications due to its unique qualities, which include its favourable redox characteris -
tics, stability, cost-effectiveness, and eco-friendliness. Notably, the fast interconversion 
between Ce3+ and Ce4+ causes negatively charged oxygen atoms to be removed from its 
oxide lattice, creating oxygen vacancies. Redox processes can be promoted by these oxy -
gen vacancies, which improves the kinetics of the reaction. A three-electrode arrange -
ment with 3 M KOH as the electrolyte was preferred for electrochemical analysis. The 
composite electrode produced an ultimate capacitance of 1908.5 F/g at 1 A/g of current 
supplied. Later on, 91% of its capacitance was successfully retained even after 4,000 sub -
sequent cycles.

8 Metal–organic frameworks nanostructured electrodes for SC
Metal Organic Framework (MOF) constitutes of network of organic ligands allied with 
metal ions or clusters (as depicted in Fig.  22). The small network of MOF when joined 
together, shapes a new arrangement with characteristic integration of porous and stable 
structural framework featuring augmented surface area [ 196]. In addition, MOFs exhib-
its high thermal and chemical stability owing to their stronger molecular bonding as 
compared to other class of material-viz. metal oxides, and metal sulfides. Collectively, 
these characteristics renders promoted effectiveness [ 197, 198]. The synergy of organic 
ligand and functional groups is sought to boost electrochemical performance through 
strengthened redox potential. MOFs are intrinsically extended crystalline structure, in 
which metal nodes and organic linkers propagate into periodic networks. With such 
configuration, the 0D MOF having all three spatial restrictions cannot exists. However, 
1D, 2D, 3D MOFs have been widely synthesized and well investigated for supercapacitor 
applications [199– 201]. Therefore, in this discussion the 0D pristine MOF are exempted. 
This section shall further share insights regarding role and significance of dimensionality 
of MOFs.

8.2 2D metal organic framework electrode materials
The two-dimensional construction of MOFs offers reduced ion diffusion lengths and 
enhanced electronic conductivity due to the extended planar conjugation and acces -
sible surface area, thereby significantly improving electrochemical performance [ 211]. 
To address the poor structural stability and limited electrical conductivity of metal 
oxide-based materials, significant attention has been given towards the development 
of MOF-derived metal oxide materials. In order to achieve an improved performance, 
the self-supported material in-situ grown on current collector/substrate is an effec -
tive approach. A recent study has described derivation of Vanadium Oxide (VO 2) from 
Vanadium-based MOF [ 212]. A facile hydrothermal route was testified for preparation 
of VO 2 nanosheets over flexible carbon substrate. The electrochemical analysis with 
three-electrode configuration in an aqueous 2 M Na 2SO4 electrolyte revealed significant 
redox peaks confirming its pseudocapacitive characteristics. The specific capacitance as 
much as 470 F/g was estimated at current density of 1 A/g. Furthermore, an excellent 
capacitance retention of 96% was witnessed after 5000 cycles. Also, 80% of rate capabil -
ity was achieved with tenfold increase in applied current.
Generally, MOFs with 1D nanostructures often exhibit anisotropic transport behav -
iour and limited charge carrier mobility, which attenuates electrochemical performance. 
Xu Zhang demonstrated that the modulation of precursor ratio can induce a structural 
evolution from 1D nanowire to 2D nanosheet structure [ 214]. The 2D Ni-MOF was 
observed to provide a high density of electrochemically accessible redox-active sites, 
which markedly enhanced the faradaic reaction kinetics. The large surface-to-volume 
ratio, quick ion transport, and enhanced mechanical robustness ratifies the 2D MOF 
as meritorious over 1D MOF nanostructured electrode materials. Another prospective 
electrode is Nickel Oxide, popular due to its theoretical capacitance of 2584 F/g. Zhipeng 
Sun and group reported 2D Nickel-based MOF achieving specific capacitance of 527.1 
F/g at 1 A/g [ 215]. The NiO-MOF was transformed into nanosheets through an anneal -
ing process, without majorly disrupting its structural features. The electrochemical 
analysis indicated a pseudocapacitive pattern. Also, the columbic efficiency was found 
to be 99% for every cycle, showcasing its future revelation. J. Ma et al. accomplished suc-
cessful growth of Cobalt-based MOF onto flexible carbon cloth. The nano-flakes (with 
sheet thickness of 140 nm) adhered on carbon fibers exhibited rapid ion diffusion due to 
shorter length contributing towards high surface-controlled charge storage [ 213]. Upon 
electrochemical investigation (refer to Fig.  24(d)) with 2  M KOH aqueous electrolyte, 
it delivered an areal capacitance of 1756 mF/cm 2 (equivalent to specific capacitance of 
829 F/g) at 2 mA/cm 2 of current applied. With 50-fold increase in current, not only did 
it retained 76% of its initial capacitance, but also delivered 103% cyclic stability perfor -
mance after 15,000 cycles of charging and discharging.

exceptional surface area and inherent porous structure are key attributes of high dimen -
sional MOFs. Owing to this, they are considered as appropriate SC electrode materi -
als [198]. Cerium Oxide is a cost-effective rare earth metal oxide and is known for its 
dynamic redox properties and environmentally friendly characteristics. Although, it still

9 Heterostructure/Multi-dimensional electrodes for SC
The electrochemical performance is directly linked with the electrode’s architecture, 
synthesis route employed, nature of ion and electron transport. The structure and geom-
etry of (0D, 1D, 2D, 3D) nanomaterials are the major factors that determines the charge 
transport principles [227, 228]. In 0D architecture, the charge transport primarily occurs 
through the interstitial voids between discrete particles. Due to the substantial resis -
tance at particle surfaces, it lacks a continuous path and therefore turn out to be sluggish 
and complicated. On the contrary, continuous pathways in 1D structure allows them to 
move directionally through narrow channels. This charge transport is anisotropic and 
often governed by interactions with the channel walls, surface charge effects, resulting in 
selective and efficient conduction [ 229]. In case of 2D layered structures, charge trans -
port occurs through interlayers. The interlayer spacing and presence of defects/vacan -
cies are critical for smooth ion mobility [ 230]. The 3D continuous and interconnected 
network of pores/channels allows ions to move in all directions. This isotropic and volu -
metric transport enables rapid and efficient ion diffusion and better electrolyte access. 
However, the pore size distribution and the surface charge of the pore walls, chemical 
composition are the governing factors for ion movement [231].
SCs performance is often limited by the intrinsic drawbacks such as discrete network 
structure, limited pathways in 0D, lower surface area in 1D, and restacking issues in 
2D materials. In order to guarantee a high electrochemical activity, and to enhance the 
interfacial reaction kinetics, higher-dimensional designs are typically beneficial. By com-
bining the benefits of each dimension and creating the hierarchical architecture of multi-
dimensional materials, the ‘dimensionality-integration’ technique may offer a practical 
solution. Figure  27 illustrates the possible interactions between different dimensionali -
ties. The 2D graphene building blocks can create a 3D nanostructure with fundamentally 
novel properties, such as interconnected graphene monolayers that enhances ion trans -
port and electron transfer due to their continuous network. Designing multi-dimen -
sional nanocarbons for use as electrodes in electrochemical energy storage systems 
reveals multiple benefits. The combination of 1D and 2D nanocarbons can significantly 
increase the specific surface area of the active electrode materials. Also, when using 2D 
carbon-based materials (such as graphene), other nanocarbons (such carbon nanotubes) 
can be employed as pillars or spacers to prevent the aggregation of 2D nanocarbons. 
The spacers may offer effective pathway for the electrolyte ions to improve the charge–
discharge properties. The 1D and 2D units are suitable building blocks for a 3D frame -
work. Engineering 3D architectures from assembly of low-dimensional units is a proven 
strategy to overcome these challenges and unlock superior electrochemical performance 
[232– 234].
Dimensionality transition is a process involving transition of high-dimensional materi-
als to low-dimensional ones and the low-dimensional materials to the high-dimensional 
ones. The possible interactions between lower-to-higher dimensional materials are illus-
trated in Fig.  26. The unique 2D-2D layered nano-junctions have a significantly large 
contact surface between the adjacent sheets compared to the other two types of hybrids.

Two-dimensional materials like graphene and MXenes are distinguished for their high 
surface area, which is beneficial for energy storage purpose. However, these materials 
often exhibit  unsatisfactory volumetric performance  due to low packing density and 
limited ion-accessible volume [ 236, 237]. Defect and doping engineering, assembly into 
3D networks, hybridization with conductive networks are few suggested strategies for 
improvement in energy storage functionalities of 2D materials. S. Yang et al. demon -
strated the 3D hierarchical graphene-carbon nanotube architecture as effective to over -
come the stacking of individual graphene sheets [ 238]. The specific capacitance of 3D 
hierarchical structure (326.5 F/g) was observed to be significantly higher than that of 2D 
graphene (83 F/g). A recent innovative method developed for energy storage applica -
tions is the development of MOF-on-MOF architecture. A thin coating of a conductive 
MOF can be deposited on top of a thicker (base) layer of a supporting MOF to create 
the hierarchical heterostructure. Such MOFs provide high surface area, lower diffusion 
restrictions, and enhance ionic conductivity for optimal electrochemical performance. 
R. Deka et al. demonstrated in situ growth of ZIF-67 on Ni-BTC [ 239]. The hybrid elec-
trode exhibited specific capacitance of 1063 F/g at 4 A/g current density. The cycling 
performance retention of 98% after 5500 cycles validates its robust stability.
In case of MXenes, tendency of oxidation and restacking are major bottlenecks lim -
iting their performance in energy storage and related applications. Several strategies 
such as constructing three-dimensional (3D) porous structures, regulating surface ter -
mini, and introducing spacers have been developed to mitigate these issues [ 240]. A 
study reported a unique approach to overcome the difficulties regarding Ti 3C2 MXene 
[241]. Through hydrothermal synthesis, MoS2 was integrated into Ti3C2, while Cu2O was 
embedded through an in-situ chemical reduction. The resulting Ti 3C2-MoS2-Cu2O ter-
nary composite exhibited enhanced conductivity and charge transfer. The MoS2 contrib-
ute in expansion of interlayer spacing and protected against oxidation, whereas Cu 2O 
stabilized the lamellar structure. As a result, specific capacitance of 1459 F/g at current 
density of 1 A/g was obtained.
The dimensionality also plays a critical role in defining a supercapacitor’s mechani -
cal flexibility during bending and its long-term cycling stability. Higher-dimensional (2D 
and 3D) and hybrid architectures in supercapacitors significantly improve both flexibility 
during bending and resistance to long-term degradation. These structures enable high 
capacitance retention and mechanical robustness, making them ideal for flexible and 
wearable energy storage applications. Combining 1D, 2D, and 3D structures enhances 
both mechanical and electrochemical properties, resulting in high flexibility and stable 
performance during deformation. Conducting polymers (such as polyvinyl alcohol, poly-
aniline) as SC electrode suffers from disadvantage of shrinking and swelling, which com-
promises their cyclic performance. Also, popypyrrole has limited mechanical strength. 
To address this issue, a flexible, all-solid-state hydrogel electrode was synthesized by 
integrating 1D polypyrrole (Ppy) nanofibers and 2D reduced graphene oxide into a 3D 
polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) matrix via multi-dimensional material assembly [ 242]. The PVA 
matrix stabilized the structure, enhancing overall flexibility and conductivity. Notewor -
thy performance with specific capacitance 483.3 mF/cm2 at 1 mA/cm2, along with 91.2% 
capacitance retention after 5000 charge–discharge cycles was observed.

11 Future Prospective and Challenges
Over the past several decades, extensive research has significantly advanced the energy 
storage technology through the integration of nanomaterials. This progress has led to the 
identification of a variety of new and highly effective materials. The prospect of spatial 
dimensions categorises them into 4 groups. A few of the prevalent morphologies across 
the spectrum of material dimensionality are illustrated in Fig. 30.  The electrochemical 
performance of a SC is majorly influenced by an electrode’s surface area, morphology, 
size and distribution of pores. An ideal electrode material is desired to have high surface 
area, enhanced electrical conductivity, structural rigidity, mechanical and thermal stabil-
ity, durability against corrosion, surface wettability with electrolyte used.  .
Among the families of electrode materials, carbon-based materials remain com -
mercially dominant owing to their superior electrical conductivity, large surface area, 
and exceptional cycle stability. The non-faradaic charge storage mechanism allows for 
rapid charge transfer due to high electrical conductivity. Besides, the discovery of gra -
phene has transformed the energy storage technology showcasing synergistic electrical, 
mechanical, and thermal properties. Despite its versatility, the absence of an inherent 
electronic bandgap has encouraged an interest in alternative 2D materials with semi -
conducting properties. Alternative carbon material such as CNT, fullerene have been 
extensively investigated to enhance the charge storage contribution. However, their low 
energy density issue is yet to be solved, generating a scope for supplementary research

also be achieved [ 296]. Despite their considerable potential, MXenes also disclose few 
limitations. The serious concern associated with them is their health impact- can lead 
to detrimental effects on the skin, digestive tract, and respiratory system. Another issue 
includes restacking tendency that hinder ion accessibility and susceptibility to oxidation 
under ambient conditions. Addressing these limitations necessitates innovative fabrica -
tion techniques and environment friendly strategies to ensure long-term stability and 
performance [233, 297, 298].

MOFs show strong reversibility; they are disadvantaged by weak reversibility and lower 
capacitance, respectively. Developing heterojunctions using bimetallic MOF containing 
mixed-metal ions is another promising stratagem. Several studies have shown that bime-
tallic MOF-based supercapacitors benefit from synergies such as reduced particle size, 
increased surface area, improved porosity, and enhanced conductivity. Developing a bet-
ter understanding of the degradation mechanisms of current MOF-based SC that occur 
at higher voltages is crucial to tackle this challenge [200].

ful matching of electrode potentials, material stability, and electrolyte compatibility.

An interdisciplinary research approach, combining theoretical modelling, compu -

12 Conclusion
In supercapacitors, the contest to achieve high-performance output focuses on boosting 
the potential window and energy density to match batteries' capacity, while simultane -
ously minimizing the production costs. Irrespective of class of material, morphological 
features of electrode material (such as porosity, specific surface area, and pore volume) 
are key factors that lay foundation for electrochemical performances of a supercapaci -
tors. The electrical conductivity, pore size distribution, and available electroactive sites 
are the several significant aspects which influence charge storage capacity. Several 
researchers have demonstrated polydimensionality/heterostructures as alternative of 
mono-dimensional materials for development of supercapacitor electrodes. Hybrid 
composite electrodes featuring heterostructure architectures are found to have dem -
onstrated remarkable potential, offering synergistic improvements in charge storage 
efficiency, electrical conductivity and cycling stability. Future research should focus on 
overcoming existing material limitations, optimizing electrolyte–electrode interactions, 
and leveraging computational tools for material design. With the sustained efforts and 
an interdisciplinary collaboration, next-generation supercapacitor with enhanced energy 
density, sustainability, and cost-effectiveness can be developed.
