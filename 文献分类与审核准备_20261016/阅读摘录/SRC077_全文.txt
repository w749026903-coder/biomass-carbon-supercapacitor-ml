of structurally diverse nanoporous carbon materials 
and their capacitive behavior in dilute ionic liquid

ABSTRACT
The growing global demand for efficient energy storage has highlighted the need 
to discover and establish relationships and rules that link the electric double-layer 
(EDL) capacitance to the structural properties of the materials used in electrodes. 
Among these materials, nanoporous carbon stands out for its high microporos-
ity and precisely adjustable pore size distribution, both of which play a crucial 
role in influencing EDL performance. At the same time, machine learning (ML) 
has emerged as a toolbox in materials science, enabling the prediction and opti-
mization of various application-related properties based on experiment-derived 
structure and surface characteristics. In this study, the ML method was applied 
to 67 nanoporous carbon materials (carbide-derived carbons (CDCs)), with dif-
ferent structures and textures. Their EDL capacitance was then modeled under 
both positive and negative polarization in an electrolyte whose ions had an asym-
metric, i.e., extremely non-spherical geometric structure. The structural and tex-
tural properties of the materials in the dataset were described using experimental 
descriptors of carbon materials derived from nitrogen and carbon dioxide adsorp-
tion measurements. These descriptors were used as model inputs, while the target 
property, EDL capacitance, was measured in three-electrode cells using 1.9 M 
EMIm-TFSI in ACN as electrolyte. The ML modeling results demonstrate that 
combining experimentally derived structural descriptors, such as specific surface 
area, ion size-related volume fraction of pore size distribution, and bulk den-
sity of CDC electrodes, in one quantitative nanostructure–property relationship 
(QnSPR), enables accurate prediction of specific volumetric capacitance for both 
volumetric cathodic capacitance  (R2 = 0.93) and volumetric anodic capacitance 
 ( R2 = 0.94) using multiple linear regression. The textural descriptors in the models 
indicate that the most effective pore size range for electrosorption is consistent

GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT
Introduction
The global push for high-performance energy stor-
age has driven the development of a variety of elec-
trochemical solutions, including electrostatic, redox, 
and hybrid storage. Electric double-layer capacitors 
(EDLCs), or ultracapacitors, known for their long lifes-
pan and environmental friendliness, use large surface 
area carbon electrodes and can withstand thousands 
of charge–discharge cycles [1 ]. Ultracapacitors store 
energy by electrosorption of ions onto the carbon sur-
face, making nanoporous carbon materials particu-
larly attractive due to their large specific surface area 
(SSA) and high bulk density. However, the large SSA 
does not necessarily mean high capacitance [2 , 3]. It 
is essential to have the proper pore size distribution 
(PSD) in the carbon material, because the PSD signifi-
cantly affects ion adsorption ability, or in other words, 
the specific capacitance of the carbon electrodes [4–7].
Previous studies [4 –7] have revealed that the spe-
cific capacitance of carbon can be quantitatively esti-
mated using porosity data obtained from gas adsorp-
tion measurements. It has been shown that as pore 
size increases, the capacitive performance gradually 
becomes independent of both pore size and elec-
trode potential, with larger pores exhibiting better 
charging dynamics [5 ]. However, subnanometer-
sized pores, which better match the size of electrolyte 
ions, significantly increase the specific capacitance 
and energy density of ultracapacitors [6 –11].
Among the electrolytes used for supercapaci-
tors, ionic liquids (ILs) are considered a promising 
choice due to their environmental friendliness and 
good electrochemical stability over a wide voltage 
range exceeding 3.5–4 V [12]. The major disadvan-
tage of ILs is their low ionic conductivity, especially 
below room temperature [13, 14]. The conductivity 
of IL ions can be improved by diluting them with 
an organic solvent, albeit at the cost of a somewhat 
poorer electrochemical stability. Nonaqueous elec-
trolyte solutions containing organic salts commonly 
exhibit an applicable voltage window of 2.6–2.9 V 
[1, 13, 15, 16].
The effect of carbon pore size on the perfor-
mance of diluted IL, 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium 
bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide/acetonitrile 
(EMIm-TFSI/ACN) electrolyte was highlighted in 
a study where ultracapacitors with microporous, 
mesoporous, and micro-mesoporous carbon elec-
trodes were compared [17]. This study concluded 
that smaller pores are advantageous for the EMIm-
TFSI/ACN ultracapacitor, whereas a wide pore size

Machine learning (ML) has become an important 
toolbox in the advancement of materials science, 
including energy materials, helping to optimize 
and improve the properties and design methods of 
(carbon) materials, as well as the quality of products 
based on (carbon) materials, such as the performance 
and efficiency of ultracapacitors [18– 21], fuel cells 
[22, 23], and solar absorbers [24, 25]. The ML meth-
ods are used to develop quantitative structure–prop-
erty relationships (QSPR) [26, 27], which establish 
mathematical links between electrochemical process 
parameters and the structural properties of materi-
als. Data-driven machine learning techniques can 
be used to predict the specific capacitance of carbon 
materials [6 , 7, 28–34], cycling stability [35, 36], and 
power density of EDLC [37], as well as the produc-
tion parameters of carbon materials used in energy 
storage. In machine learning applications, typical 
descriptors characterizing the material and process 
conditions of ultracapacitors are textural proper-
ties (SSA, pore volumes, pore sizes,  Id/Ig ratio from 
Raman spectra, etc.), synthesis conditions of material 
(temperature, activation, doping, etc. ), and electro-
chemical operational properties (such as potential 
window, current density, electrolyte type, and elec-
trolyte concentration) [18, 19, 38].
Main attention in data-driven modeling has been 
given to predicting and describing capacitance, e.g., 
ML algorithms have been used to determine the 
effects of carbon material properties and test condi-
tions on EDLC capacitance. For example, in a study 
based on datasets of 167 biomass-derived carbon 
materials collected from different research papers, 
it was found that the multilayer perceptron (MLP) 
algorithm yielded the best determination correla-
tion coefficient of 0.817 based on the surface area 
and pore volume distribution [34]. Another work 
derived ML models from the capacitance data of 121 
different carbon materials in aqueous electrolytes, 
also collected from different research papers. In this 
case, the best results were obtained with the regres-
sion trees (RT) and MLP algorithm, with a model 
root-mean-square error (RMSE) of ~ 68 for either 
method. In both cases, the most important variables 
were SSA, pore volume (PV), and average pore size 
(PS), while potential window (PW) and  Id/Ig ratio 
contributed to lower weight [28].
Artificial neural networks (ANNs) have been shown 
to be effective in identifying dependencies between 
EDLC capacitance and parameters describing such 
systems. For example, data from 178 carbon samples 
collected from published papers were used to build a 
model using the ANN method with five properties of 
material (i.e., SSA, PS,  Id/Ig ratio, N-doping level, and 
voltage window). When tested with real capacitance 
data, the square of the correlation coefficient  (R2) of 
the ANN model reached 0.91 [30]. Also, Zhou et al., in 
a comparative study of several ML methods, demon-
strated on a dataset comprising 70 data points that the 
ANN model quite accurately replicates the relation-
ship between capacitance and the SSA of micropores 
and mesopores across various scan rates [37]. This ena-
bled the creation of a Ragone plot, which predicts the 
optimal characteristics of activated carbons for achiev-
ing maximum energy and power density.
Decision tree-based consensus methods have also 
shown good results in identifying dependencies 
between EDLC capacitance and parameters describ-
ing such systems and material structure, compared 
to other ML methods. For example, Asinari and cow-
orkers [31] assembled a representative dataset with 
4899 data records for carbon-based materials. They 
found that of the five regression models applied, the 
capacitive performance was best correlated with the 
XGBoost model, where the most important descriptors 
were specific surface area, the presence of nitrogen 
doping, and the potential window. Another example 
is given for the XGBoost method, which resulted in a 
model with a good predictive specific capacitance for 
80 porous carbon materials and was validated on 20 
materials [39]. For this model, porous structure char-
acteristics such as  SSAµ/SSA, SSA, and PS contributed 
most to the performance of capacity among all porous 
structure properties. Similarly, in a comparative study, 
Yang et al. showed that XGBoost provides a reliable 
method for predicting the electrochemical capacitance 
of diverse biochars. In this study, it was found that, 
besides activation conditions and SSA, biomass prop-
erties like C/N and C/O ratios play a crucial role in 
capacitance prediction [40].
Another comparative study that is worth noting is 
the work of Abdi et al., in which four ML techniques to 
forecast the capacitance of carbon-based ultracapaci-
tors were tested [41]. Out of these, the Super Learner 
(SL) model emerged as the most effective, achieving 
an  R2 of 0.9768 and an RMSE of 18.099. In the afore-
mentioned study, it was also found that the specific

As shown above, comparing machine learning 
methods and searching for the best method are main-
stream research in current scientific literature. The per-
formance of models is usually evaluated using various 
statistical metrics. However, a significant limitation of 
many machine learning methods is the difficulty of 
interpreting the results, especially when using com -
plex models.
The previous overview also shows that almost all 
works, regardless of the ML method, confirm the 
importance of SSA in describing capacitance. How-
ever, using SSA alone does not provide sufficient pre-
dictive accuracy. It is important to note that in all the 
examples above, the datasets required for modeling 
were compiled from different literature sources. Data-
sets assembled from multiple literature sources can 
be valuable for machine learning studies. However, 
differences in experimental procedures and report-
ing practices can introduce additional variability into 
the data. Estimating and predicting energy storage 
properties with ML methods always requires reliable 
data series, as well as large and systematic variability 
of (carbon) samples in these series. However, when 
aggregating results from different research groups 
into a single dataset, one must account for the signifi-
cant variability in the data, which mainly stems from 
different interpretations of incorrect formulas and key 
terminology [42].
A vast majority of ML studies considering relation-
ships between structure and capacitive performance of 
electrode materials are based on full-cell (2-electrode 
cell) data, which limits the ability to investigate spe-
cific interactions between electrolyte ions and elec-
trode materials. The main reason for using full-cell 
data is the abundance and availability of suitable data 
from literature sources. Correct modeling of half-cell 
data requires the availability of reliable and compa-
rable (same experimental conditions, same method-
ologies: potential ranges, measurement algorithms, 
correctness of experimental performance, etc.) meas-
urement results, which are usually not provided by 
literature sources.
Previous studies by the authors of this paper in 
applying ML algorithms to describe the electrical dou-
ble-layer (EDL) capacitance of carbon materials in dif-
ferent electrolytes separately under anodic or cathodic 
polarization have shown that systematic datasets 
based on structure and computational models built on 
them allow mechanistic insight into the physicochemi-
cal processes of capacitance [6 , 7]. In these studies, it 
was demonstrated that experiment-derived structural 
descriptors derived from gas adsorption data can be 
effectively used in multiple linear regression models 
to accurately predict the EDL capacitance of nanopo-
rous carbons in nonaqueous electrolytes under differ-
ent polarization conditions. For example, using 100 
carbide-derived carbon (CDC) samples, the predictive 
QnSPR models were derived for EDL capacitance in 
triethylmethylammonium tetrafluoroborate/propyl-
ene carbonate (TEMA/PC) electrolyte [6 ]. In another 
study, a three-parameter QnSPR model was developed 
to estimate volumetric cathodic capacitance in spiro-
(1,10)-bipyrrolidinium tetrafluoroborate/acetonitrile 
(SBP-BF4/ACN) electrolyte [7]. Both studies emphasize 
the importance of matching carbon pore size with elec-
trolyte ion size to maximize capacitance performance 
(see discussion of ion size comparisons).
Previous QnSPR studies have not sufficiently 
addressed the performance of the capacitance of 
nanoporous carbon materials in IL-based electrolytes, 
which mainly consist of large but asymmetric ions. 
The aim of the current study was to address this gap 
and demonstrate the role of subnanometric pores of 
carbon material in IL electrolyte (1.9 M EMIm-TFSI/
ACN) using machine learning model derivation meth-
odology within the QnSPR approach. In addition, the 
aim was to shed light on the electrosorption of asym-
metrical ions in nanopores. Special attention was paid 
to the reproducibility and consistency of experimen-
tal measurements of the capacitive properties and 
texture/porosity characteristics of carbon materials, 
and based on this, to a reliable and physicochemi-
cally interpretable model was developed that reveals 
meaningful relationships between experimentally 
determined material descriptors and EDL capacitance.

Carbon materials

different structures and textures were selected, based 
on prior knowledge of the relationships between the 
structure of CDC materials and synthesis conditions. 
CDCs, which are known as highly nanoporous mate-
rials, were synthesized by chlorination under differ-

Electrochemical characterization
Electrochemical characterization of carbon materials 
was performed in three-electrode test cells. Working 
electrodes with a diameter of 7 mm and a thickness 
of 100 ± 10 µm were prepared by pressing a slurry of 
90% CDC and 10% PTFE (Aldrich, 60% dispersion in 
water) into a carbon sheet. All electrodes were made 
under identical and carefully controlled conditions, 
minimizing processing-related variability in carbon 
structure descriptors and allowing the transforma-
tion of carbon powder into an electrode to be con-
sidered a uniform change across the entire material 
set. The 1.9 M solution of 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazo-
lium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (EMIm-TFSI, 
Sigma-Aldrich, ≥ 98%) in acetonitrile (ACN, Sigma-
Aldrich, anhydrous, 99.5%) was used as electrolyte. 
The density of the electrolyte solution was 1.18 g  cm−3, 
and the water content measured by Karl Fisher titra-
tion was < 130 ppm. Carbon sheets with a high specific 
surface area, coated with a 2 µm Al layer (PVD) to 
improve electrical contact, were used as reference and 
counter electrodes. Electrodes were separated by two 
~ 1 mm SWP glass mat layers (Anpei). All components 
were vacuum-dried (1 mbar, 110 °C) for one week, and 
the assembled cell was further vacuumed overnight at 
110 °C. The test cell was soaked with electrolyte for 24 
h before measurement.
The cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves were recorded 
first in the negative potential region of 0 V to − 1.2 
V, and then, the maximum voltage was gradually 
increased to − 1.8 V by using 0.2 V steps and 2 cycles 
in each range. The low voltage scan rate (v ) of 1 mV 
 s−1 was used to avoid the effect of differences in dif -
fusion rates due to different types of carbon and their 
particle sizes used. A similar protocol was followed in 
the positive potential region (up to + 1.6 V). All meas-
urements were performed at room temperature. The 
potentiostat–galvanostat 1286 Solartron was used for 
all electrochemical measurements.

Quantitative nanostructure–property 
relationships
The QnSPR study of the cathodic and anodic capaci-
tances of carbon materials was conducted using 
regression analysis, which is one of the simplest, 
yet most widely used, machine learning algorithms. 
Multi-linear regression (MLR) is particularly valued 
for its excellent interpretability [43]. MLR performs 
best with systematic datasets and high-quality input 
data and assumes a linear relationship between input 
and output variables [18, 27, 44], all of which are valid 
for the present dataset. Therefore, there is no need to 
implement more complex ML methods, which can 
describe complex nonlinear relationships and interac-
tions and potentially improve predictive performance, 
but often have limited interpretability.
In this study, we used experimentally measured 
structural descriptors, such as various pore size frac-
tions VCO2[x−y ] (where x and y are the lowest and high-
est pore sizes of the fraction), SSA by DFT (S dft), total 
pore volume (V t), micropore volumes by DFT (V µ), 
mesopores volume by DFT (Vme), and the average pore 
size (APS  = 2V t ∕S dft). In addition, the bulk density 
of the electrode (D el) was included. This electrode-

Descriptors were selected stepwise by adding or 
removing descriptors one by one based on the Akaike 
information criterion (AIC). AIC evaluates model 
performance by balancing goodness of fit and com-
plexity. It considers two key factors: (1) the model’s 
fit, measured by the maximized log-likelihood, and 
(2) the number of parameters, which penalizes overly 
complex models. A lower AIC value indicates a better 
trade-off between accuracy and simplicity [48].
Also, because several descriptors were strongly cor-
related, the variance inflation factor (VIF) was calcu-
lated and any descriptors with VIFs exceeding 5 were 
omitted. In simpler terms, VIF measures how much 
the variability in one descriptor is explained by all the 
other descriptors in the model. When a descriptor’s 
VIF is high, it indicates that its behavior is largely pre-
dictable from the other descriptors, meaning it pro-
vides little unique information. This redundancy can 
adversely affect the model, so those descriptors were 
removed to maintain a more stable and interpretable 
model [49].

Results and discussion
General parameters of the carbon materials 
dataset
A diverse selection of carbon materials with different 
structures and pore sizes is required for systematic, 
descriptive, and predictive QnSPR analysis. In this 
study, a dataset comprising 67 CDC materials was 
utilized (Table 1). The materials are ranked based 
on increasing specific surface area and are labeled 
sequentially as CDC-1 through CDC-67. The proper-
ties of these materials are influenced by the different 
chemical and structural compositions of the precur-
sors—metal carbides—as well as by different synthesis 
and post-processing conditions. CDC materials with a 
narrow micropore size distribution were mainly syn-
thesized from silicon or titanium carbide, while those 
with a wider PSD were produced from molybdenum, 
boron, niobium, or zirconium carbide. To increase the 
variability of materials in the dataset, 14 of the CDCs 
were treated with water vapor at high temperature, 
which effectively edged the nanopores and expanded 
the PSD [52].
The general variability of the carbon samples is 
characterized by the range of Sdft values from 303 to 
1947  m2g−1, the total pore volume (V tot) from 0.29 to 
1.76  cm3g−1, and the ratio of micropore volume to total 
pore volume (V µ/Vtot) from 0.09 to 0.87. The average 
pore size (APS) ranges from 0.86 to 3.62 nm. Figure 1 
illustrates the variations in the carbon materials in this 
study, with an example of the pore size distribution 
calculated from  CO2 and  N2 adsorption measurements 
for the three CDC materials (CDC-2, CDC-17, and 
CDC-64). The adsorption isotherms and cumulative 
pore size distributions of the three materials are pre-
sented in Figs. S1 and S2 in the Supplementary Infor-
mation. CDC-2 is a low-porosity, mainly mesoporous 
carbon material synthesized from aluminum carbide, 
with a micropore to total pore volumes ratio of 0.09. 
Most of the carbon samples in this study are highly 
microporous, such as CDC-17 (synthesized from SiC), 
which contains pores around 0.5 and 0.8 nm, with a 
micropore volume-to-total pore volume ratio of 0.87. 
CDC-64 (synthesized from  Mo2C) represents a micro-
mesoporous carbon that, in addition to subnanometric 
pores, contains a significant amount of pores of about 
1.7 and 3 nm in size and has a micropore volume to 
total pore volume ratio of 0.40.
EDL capacitance of studied materials
The nanoporous carbon materials selected for this 
study include a wide range of CDC materials, includ-
ing those with a majority pore size below 1 nm and 
those with a wide pore size distribution in both the 
micropore (< 2 nm) and mesopore (2–50 nm) ranges. 
Thus, it was necessary to establish a universal method 
and identify a potential window for accurately meas-
uring the specific capacitance values across all 67 car-
bon materials. Figure 2 shows CV curves scanned over 
the positive and negative electrode potential ranges 
for three CDC materials (CDC-2, CDC-17, and CDC-
64) with substantially different pore size distributions. 
To illustrate the electrochemical stability of the mate-
rials over a wide potential range, comparative CV

Texture descriptors of carbon materials
The values of the descriptors used were determined 
experimentally within the same research framework 
and under comparable conditions, ensuring a high 
level of data consistency. Numerous texture descrip-
tors (Sdft, Vtot, Vµ, Vme (Vtot-Vµ), and APS) were derived 
from nitrogen adsorption measurements of 67 carbon 
materials. Pore size-related descriptors for character-
izing subnanometer pores were calculated from cumu-
lative PSD data using  CO2 adsorption. It is relevant to 
note here that for a slit-shaped pore geometry, which 
was used in this study, the surface area in the pore (S) 
is proportional to the pore volume (V  = d/2 × S, where 
d is the distance between the pore walls). Therefore, 
both surface-related or volume-related pore size 
distribution data could be used equally as a meas -
ure of the quantity of pores in predefined pore size 
intervals. In this study, the volumes of subnanometer 
pore size fractions with 0.1 nm increments from 0.4 to 
1.2 nm were derived from  CO2 cumulative PSD pat-
terns. Respective texture descriptors were labeled as 
VCO2[x− y] , where x and y represent the smallest and 
largest pore sizes in the fraction. In the case of volu-
metric capacitance, the electrode density (D el) was 
also included in the descriptors. Del characterizes the 
compressed state of carbon particles in the carbon elec-
trode (i.e., self-standing carbon film) and accounts for 
potential differences in particle sizes across different 
samples.

Conclusions
The relationships between the structure of nanoporous 
carbon materials and their EDL capacitances under posi-
tive and negative polarization in an ionic liquid electro-
lyte with asymmetric ions were investigated using the 
QnSPR approach based on the multiple linear regres-
sion (MLR) method, applying experimentally derived 
descriptors. The MLR was specifically used to capture 
the key structure–property relationships in a transpar-
ent and physically interpretable manner. A compre-
hensive analysis was performed on 67 carbide-derived 
carbon materials with varying textures and structures. 
The CDC materials were chosen fit-for-purpose to mini-
mize the potential impact of non-carbon atoms and sur-
face functional groups commonly present in pyrolyti-
cally prepared activated carbon materials on the EDL 
capacitance. The structural and textural properties of 
the materials in the dataset were described using exper-
imentally determined descriptors of carbon materials 
obtained from nitrogen and carbon dioxide adsorption 
measurements. These descriptors were used as model 
inputs, while the target property, EDL capacitance, was 
measured in three-electrode cells using 1.9 M EMIm-
TFSI in ACN as electrolyte.
The results show that the cathodic capacitance in 
the EMIm-TFSI electrolyte is generally higher than the 
anodic capacitance, despite expectations based on ion 
dimensions. However, considering the mechanisms of 
ion interaction with the carbon surface, the volume, and 
the Bader charge of ions, the ratio of anodic and cathodic 
capacitance can be explained in favor of the cathodic 
one.
Two QnSPR models were developed to predict volu-
metric EDL capacitance under positive and negative 
polarization. The study demonstrates that the prediction 
accuracy for specific volumetric capacitance is signifi-
cantly improved by including texture descriptors such 
as specific surface area, ion-accessible pore volume, 
and electrode bulk density in the model. Both models 
contain three descriptors: the volume of a desolvated 
ion size-related pore fraction, the carbon SSA, and the 
bulk density of CDC electrodes. The model for cathodic 
volumetric capacitance has a coefficient of determina-
tion  R2 = 0.93 and an estimated model scale parameter 
/uni0302.s1/u1D70E = 7.88. The anodic volumetric capacitance model has a 
slightly higher accuracy: a coefficient of determination 
 R2 = 0.94 and an estimated model scale parameter /uni0302.s1/u1D70E = 
5.92.
The present study demonstrates that the effective 
pore size range for the electrosorption of the EMIm-
TFSI electrolyte follows the smallest dimension of the 
desolvated ion. For EMIm⁺ cation, the preferred pores 
are 0.4–0.5 nm. In contrast, the slightly larger but highly 
asymmetric TFSI⁻ anions prefer even smaller pores 
below 0.4 nm. In conclusion, carbon texture parameters 
and the MLR methodology enabled the development of 
robust QnSPR models that can reliably predict the volu-
metric capacitance of large and structurally asymmetric 
electrolyte ions based solely on material texture. A pos-
sible development direction to improve the descriptive 
and predictive capabilities of QnSPR models would be 
to include additional texture descriptors to characterize 
the properties of the carbon surface electronic structure, 
and the nature and quantity of functional groups.
