Computation & theory
Machine learning enabled property prediction 
of carbon‑based electrodes for supercapacitors
Rajat Kushwaha1, Mayank K. Singh1, Sarathkumar Krishnan1, and Dhirendra K. Rai1,* 
1 Sustainable Energy and Environmental Materials (SEEM) Lab, Department of Metallurgical Engineering and Materials Science, 
Indian Institute of Technology, Indore, Simrol, Indore 453552, India
ABSTRACT
For supercapacitor electrodes, carbon is one of the most sought materials. Many 
synthetic strategies have been reported for carbon materials with a wide range 
of features in terms of surface area, porosity, the extent of the disorder, and 
the presence of various doping elements. Though there are many reports on the 
independent impact of these properties on supercapacitive outcomes of carbon 
materials, the collective effect of these features on the supercapacitive behavior 
is still not well understood. Therefore, it is imperative to explore new methods 
to predict the supercapacitive performance of carbon material without attempt-
ing experimentation. In this regard, this work reports on the comparative effi-
cacy of five machine-learning (ML) techniques for estimating the capacitance of 
carbon-based supercapacitors, including Linear Regression, Lasso Regression, 
XGBoost, Random Forest Regressor, and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). We 
gathered data from various research papers and articles to prepare the dataset, 
followed by Data Cleaning and Feature Selection to select the best parameters 
for training our Machine Learning model. Additionally, five factors were cho-
sen to determine their effects on capacitance: voltage window, specific surface 
area  (m2/g), pore size (nm),  ID/IG ratio, and extent of N-doping. Out of five ML 
approaches, XGBoost, Random Forest Regressor, and Artificial Neural Networks 
(ANN) produced respectable prediction results when tested against actual data. 
More significantly, this project demonstrates the promise of Machine-Learning 
in selecting suitable materials for energy storage applications.
Introduction
Compared to batteries, supercapacitors possess the 
unique advantages of fast charging and discharg-
ing, high power density, and longer cyclic life [1 , 2]. 
However, the energy they can store is much less than 
the batteries. Such difference in behavior is mainly 
because of different charge storage mechanisms oper-
ating in batteries and supercapacitors. While charge 
storage in a battery is achieved by electron transfer

across the electrode–electrolyte interface [3 ], there is 
simple ion accumulation at or near the electrode in 
the case of supercapacitors [4]. Because of high power 
density and rapid charging, supercapacitors are 
increasingly appealing to various sectors, including 
transportation, aircraft, and communications [5 –7]. 
Electrodes are the most crucial part of supercapaci-
tors. Carbon-based materials have been extensively 
employed as electrode materials for supercapacitors 
due to their outstanding chemical and physical quali-
ties, low cost, easy availability, significant capacitance, 
and high electrical conductivity associated with their 
large specific surface area [8–10].
Researchers are keen to boost the specific capaci-
tance of carbon-based electrodes to improve super-
capacitors’ performance. For instance, numerous 
attempts have been made to alter the pore structure, 
add surface functional groups, and increase specific 
surface area to enhance the capacitance [11– 15]. Nev-
ertheless, presently there is no accepted guiding prin-
ciple that can adequately explain ways to enhance 
capacity; hence, the work of modification on capaci-
tor electrodes only depends on a few empirical ideas 
or hazy theoretical models [16]. The workable solu-
tion to this issue is to derive conclusions from a lot 
of data. In this connection, Machine Learning (ML), 
one of several data science technologies, is useful for 
establishing a predicted link between input and out-
put variables [17].
Since ML techniques can effectively and accurately 
anticipate property connections by utilizing datasets 
that are already accessible, this can be a perfect tech-
nique to predict the materials properties without actu-
ally attempting the experiment [18– 26]. Hence, with 
appropriate data set feed, ML can be implemented to 
obtain an advanced evaluation method for predicting 
the capacitance of carbon-based electrodes for super-
capacitors. Most of the time, data sets to feed ML for 
materials property predictions (primarily electrical, 
optical, and mechanical) are derived from theoretical 
calculations [27] or large-scale projects such as Materi-
als Genomic Initiative [28]. However, such approaches 
are not viable for carbonaceous materials as their com-
position is constant, which is carbon. Therefore, the 
possible dataset input for carbon materials should be 
based on their microstructures, degree of the disor-
der, and extent of different dopings. Moreover, for 
supercapacitive property prediction, experimental 
conditions, such as potential window and type of 
electrolyte, can also be used as the data set. Therefore, 
simply extracting data from the available publications 
and establishing a relationship between multivari-
ate information and performance is the most logical 
course of action.
More than 1000 publications concerning carbon-
based supercapacitors have been compiled by Zhu 
et al. [29]. It has been the source for the extraction of 
more than 10,000 data points, which include physi-
cal and chemical characteristics of the carbon mate-
rial (specific surface area, pore size, the ratio of  ID/IG, 
and doping elements) and the experimental conditions 
(such as electrolyte, test voltage window, and corre-
sponding specific capacity). Using these data points 
and theoretically derived input values, there have been 
reports on ML-assisted supercapacitive prediction of 
carbon-based materials [ 29]. For example, Zhao and 
co-workers have reported on the use of Linear regres-
sion [30], Lasso regression [31], and Artificial Neural 
Networks (ANN) [32] models of ML to predict super-
capacitive features of carbonaceous electrodes. During 
the application of ML, the ANN model has been used 
in most cases [ 33, 34], which has been able to fetch 
respectable predictions upon comparison with the test 
data. However, ANN is costly in terms of time and 
computer power, requiring hectic training [35]. Moreo-
ver, ANN is known to overfit the simple dataset [36]. 
To the best of our knowledge, the efficacy of superca-
pacitive behavior prediction of carbon materials using 
XGBoost, Random Forest Regressor models of ML has 
not been attempted and compared with other reported 
models, such as Linear regression, Lasso regression, 
and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN).
Here, we introduce ML to forecast how well carbon 
materials will perform in terms of capacitance. Although 
ML encompasses a vast array of various techniques, 
this report focuses on the efficacy comparison of Linear 
regression, Lasso regression, XGBoost, Random Forest 
Regressor, and ANN in predicting the carbonaceous 
material supercapacitive behavior. A least-squares 
function known as a linear regression equation is used 
to analyze the connection between one or more inde-
pendent variables and dependent variables in statistics 
[30]. Lasso, in contrast, is a regression analysis technique 
that carries out both variable selection and regularisa-
tion in order to improve the statistical models’ capacity 
to predict outcomes [31]. Variable selection and regu-
larisation when fitting a generalized linear model are 
characteristics of lasso regression. Therefore, utilizing 
Lasso regression, the dependent/response variable may 
be modeled and then predicted regardless of whether

direction for appropriate modification of carbon materi-
als to derive the best supercapacitive performance, but 
also helps in choosing suitable ML models.
Methods and models
Data extraction
We gathered data regarding Carbon-based superca-
pacitors from more than 1000 published papers, mostly 
inherited from Zhu et al. [29] dataset, along with some 
new data sets from recent publications. Out of these 
papers, more than 600 rows of data were collected, 
which included information like the test voltage win-
dow and specific capacity of the test system, as well as 
the physical and chemical characteristics of the carbon 
material such as the specific surface area, pore size, the 
ratio of  ID/IG, and extent of nitrogen doping.
Data cleaning
Since the data collected was extracted from various 
sources, hence, to maintain the uniformity of data, we 
performed various data cleaning procedures like
• Dealing with NULL & NAN values by eliminating 
their associated rows or replacing them with the 
mean of other values in the same row.
• Outlier Detection Outliers are observations that 
stand out (have unique characteristics) from the 
rest of the data in a population sample. Removal of 
data corresponding to such outliers is essential as 
they may negatively influence the trained model.

Feature selection
Further to data extraction and cleaning, appropriate 
variables have been selected because our database has 
a wide variety of them. The independent features were 
selected considering they have a physical significance 
and impact on the property, which means they should 
be able to explain the dependent variable adequately. 
The ideal collection of variables for the model can be 
chosen in a variety of ways. In this work, we have 
employed two methods:
1. The first method is to select features according to 
the basic domain and scientific knowledge.
2. The second way is by plotting a correlation matrix 
of various features
Feature selection according to domain 
knowledge
The following features were finalized through domain 
knowledge:
(a) Specific surface area (m2/g) As the charge storage 
in a supercapacitor occurs through the adsorp-
tion of ions on the electrode surface, the specific 
surface area is the most crucial characteristic of 
carbon materials-based electrodes. The higher 
specific surface area offers a larger platform for 
ion adsorption and, thus, offers high specific 
capacitance.
(b) Voltage window (V) Though for a perfect electrical 
double layer (EDL) supercapacitor, theoretically, 
the applied potential window should not affect 
the specific capacitance; however, its energy den-
sity is proportional to the square of the applied 
voltage  (Ed = 1/2CV 2). The choice of the opera-
tional potential window depends upon the type 
of electrolyte used. In this way, the voltage win-
dow is a key indicator of how well the device 
works overall.
(c) ID/IG Besides the microstructures, carbon has 
other inherent characteristics that may be studied 
using Raman spectroscopy, such as the degree of 
crystallization and the pattern of hybridization. 
Generally, the degree of crystallization of carbon 
compounds is evaluated using the intensity ratio 
of the D peak (peak arising due to disorder in 
the sheet) to the G peak (peak arising due to  sp2 
C–C bond vibration) (I D/IG). The ID/IG ratio is a 
straightforward measure of the extent of disorder 
in the carbonaceous material, which eventually 
significantly affects the surface area and porosity. 
Moreover, the conductivity of carbon materials 
is also strongly dependent on their crystalline or 
disordered structure.
(d) Pore size (nm) The distribution of carbon pore sizes 
has an impact on the accessibility of electrolyte 
ions as well. For example, a significant increase 
in capacitance has been observed with nonaque-
ous solvents when electrode pore size lies below 
1 nm [40]. The pore size can be calculated using 
the methods described below:
(e) Heteroatom (N) In addition to optimized struc-
tures, carbonaceous materials for electrochemi-
cal energy storage applications also significantly 
depend on the appropriate components. Among 
several other elements, Nitrogen is one of the 
most commonly used heteroatoms in the carbon 
matrix. Owing to its lone pair electrons, the pres-
ence of N in the carbon matrix facilitates effective 
ion adsorption and enhances the wettability of the 
hydrophobic carbon surface.

An easy way to describe a correlation matrix is as a 
table that shows the correlation coefficients for dif-
ferent features. The matrix displays the relationships 
between every possible pair of values in a table. It is a 
useful tool for finding patterns in the data and show-
ing them visually, as well as for summarizing a very 
large set of data. The variables are shown in the rows 
and columns of a correlation matrix. There is a correla-
tion coefficient in each cell of a table. The range of the 
correlation coefficient lies from −1 to + 1. The correla-

1. Linear Regression Linear Regression can be stated 
as an ML algorithm based on supervised learning 
[30], which performs a regression task. Using inde-
pendent variables as input, regression models a 
target prediction value. Finding out how variables 
and predictions relate to one another is its primary 
purpose. The optimal line for predicting the value 
of “y” for a given value of “x” is fitted while train-
ing this model. By locating the best values of coef-
ficients θ 1 and θ 2, the model produces the best 
regression fit line where θ 1 represents intercept 
and θ2 represents the coefficient of x.
2. Lasso Regression Lasso regression is one of the regu-
larization techniques. It is preferred over regres-
sion techniques for a more accurate prediction. 
This model uses the concept of shrinkage[31]. 
When data values shrink toward the mean, it is 
referred to as shrinkage. The lasso regression 
method involves simple and sparse models (i.e., 
models having fewer parameters). The Lasso 
regression penalizes the coefficient value of each 
feature to decrease or shrink toward zero. There-
fore, it is acceptable for the less important variable 
to have a coefficient of zero or almost zero. It is 
usually used for an overfitting model.
3. XGBoost XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) is 
a decision-tree-based ensemble Machine Learn -
ing algorithm [37], which uses a gradient boost-
ing method. Decision trees are generated sequen-
tially in this approach. Weights are significant in 
XGBoost. All independent variables are given a 
weight, and the decision tree that forecasts out -
comes uses these weights to predict the outcomes. 
Variables that the tree incorrectly predicted are 
given more weight before being placed into the 
next decision tree. These distinct classifiers/predic-
tors are then combined to produce a robust and 
accurate model.
4. Random Forest Regressor Random Forest is an 
ensemble approach capable of completing both 
regression and classification tasks [38]. It does 
this by using several decision trees in conjunction 
with a technique called Bootstrap and Aggrega-
tion, which is more frequently referred to as bag-
ging. Simply put, this method takes the results of 
multiple decision trees and averages them into a 
single result, which can then be used to make more 
accurate predictions or classifications than would 
be possible using a single decision tree alone.
5. Artificial Neural Network The phrase "Artificial 
Neural Network" comes from biological neural 
networks, which create the structure of the human 
brain. Neurons in artificial neural networks are 
connected to one another in the same way that 
neurons in the human brain. These networks have 
multiple layers, each containing a different number 
of neurons, referred to as nodes. It is a branch of 
artificial intelligence that tries to copy the network 
of neurons in the human brain so that computers 
can understand and make decisions in a way that 
is similar to how humans do. The artificial neu-
ral network is made by programming computers 
to act like brain cells that are linked together. The 
ANN model used in our work is made up of 6 
dense layers (Fig. 3). A Dense layer represents a 
fully connected layer, where each neuron is con-
nected to all neurons in the previous layer. The 
network starts with a smaller number of neurons 
i.e., 5 neurons (units) which represent the 5 input 
parameters we have considered in our work and 
gradually increases the number of neurons in sub-
sequent layers, potentially allowing the network 
to learn complex patterns from the input data. We 
have used Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation 
function in each of the layers which is a popular 
choice for neural networks due to its ability to 
introduce non-linearity while being computation-
ally efficient.
In our study, we fitted our data to all five ML 
Algorithms. The best performance was achieved by 
ANN followed up by Random Forest Regressor and 
XGBoost. However, observed performances of Linear 
regression and Lasso regression models were below 
the acceptance level. To compare the performance 
of all 5 models, we used the following three metrics:
1. Root Mean Squared Error  The RMSE calculates the 
squared root of the average squared difference 
between the predicted and actual values.
2. Mean Absolute Error The term "mean absolute error" 
refers to the arithmetic mean of all absolute errors 
for a given collection of measurements of the same 
quantity.

Conclusion
In order to forecast the capacitance performance of car-
bon materials in supercapacitors, this work used five 
ML-based models (Linear regression, Lasso, XG-Boost, 
Random Forest Regressor and ANN using the data 
extracted from more than 1000 research papers. For 
the training of these ML models, five parameters (Spe-
cific surface area  (m2/g), Voltage window (V),  ID/IG 
ratio, Pore Size (nm), & N (at.%)) were used as features 
based on domain knowledge and correlation matrix 
selection methods. When evaluated with actual data, 
the ANN model showed the best prediction outcomes, 
with the least RMSE and MAE and highest  R2-Score 
values. XGBoost and Random Forest Regressor also 
performed very close to the ANN model despite tak-
ing less training time. This work provides the correct 
direction for suitable modification of carbon materials 
to derive the best supercapacitive features. Moreover, 
it also helps researchers choose suitable ML models 
for accurate performance prediction in the fields of 
Material Science.
