ABSTRACT
Biomass-based activated carbon materials provide a new approach for the
development of high-performance electrodes for supercapacitor cells. They are
inexpensive and sustainable for the large-scale production of electrode materi-
als. Therefore, this study aims to produce carbon electrodes from date seeds
with a hierarchical-nanoﬁber architecture activated using KOH and CO
2. The
results showed that the carbon electrode had a framework of interconnected
pores in the presence of nanoﬁbers and exhibited excellent electrochemical
performance. The pore size distribution can be adjusted with the increased CO
2
activation temperature. Furthermore, the hierarchical-nanoﬁber architecture
contained a gradient distribution from nanopores to micropores which has
optimal connectivity and increased the capacitance of the electrical bilayer as
well as abundant oxygen on the surface or edges of the carbon matrix. The
highest speciﬁc capacitance was obtained at 258.88 Fg
-1 with a current density
of 1 Ag -1 in a symmetrical two-electrode system. The symmetrical superca-
pacitor cell circuit had an energy density of 7.11 Wkg -1 with a power density of
125.46 Wkg-1 in an H2SO4 electrolyte. These results offer an efﬁcient strategy for
the preparation of high-performance carbon electrodes based on date seeds
biomass.
1 Introduction
Advances in industry and modern technology have
led to a high energy demand for daily operation and
further development [ 1, 2]. This energy demand is
mostly met by conventional and natural exploitation
of fossil fuels such as petroleum and its derivatives
with high costs [ 3, 4]. The never-ending demand for
energy and the uncontrolled exploitation of nature
have culminated in the entry of new pollutants into
the environment, leading to further environmental
pollution [ 5]. To overcome these problems, there is a
need to develop energy-efﬁcient storage devices
which can store large amounts of energy from
renewable sources and release it when needed. This
includes electrochemical energy storage devices such

Supercapacitors are energy storage systems that
have received great attention due to their high power
density, long cycle life, and fast charge–discharge
rate [ 8]. They are classiﬁed according to their charge
storage mechanism into pseudocapacitors, capaci-
tances derived from the reversible Faraday reaction
of electroactive species, electric double layer capacitor
(EDLC), capacitances from the reversible electrostatic
of the ion adsorption at the electrode–electrolyte
interface, and a hybrid with a double layer reaction
occurring on one side and a faraday reaction on the
other [ 9]. EDLC have a higher power density and
longer cycle life than hybrid and PC supercapacitors.
The component consists of two symmetrical elec-
trodes, an electrolyte, a current collector, and a sep-
arator[10]. Electrodes are the core of EDLC and play
an important role in energy storage mechanisms,
hence, the design and development of electrode
materials is a way to improve the performance of
supercapacitors [ 11]. Meanwhile, electrode materials
commonly used in EDLC are carbon-based such as
biomass-based activated carbon, nanoﬁbers, nan-
otubes, aerogels, nanobelts, graphene, and others
[12].
Activated carbon is a form of carbon that has been
processed into carbon that is highly porous so that it
has a very large surface area available for adsorption
and chemical reactions. Activated carbon is environ-
mentally friendly and has a low cost which has
become an ideal choice as an electrode material,
compared to other carbon materials. For the most
part, activated carbon is conventionally produced
from coal and petroleum and their by-products [ 13].
However, due to environmental pollution and lim-
ited fossil fuel reserves, researchers are exploring
activated carbon from waste biomass as a potential
source for activated carbon preparation [ 14]. By-
products and residues with high lignocellulosic con-
tent, obtained from agricultural and industrial wastes
can be converted into activated carbon [ 15]. Signiﬁ-
cant research has been carried out recently to prepare
activated carbon from different biological products
such as rice husk [ 16], bamboo [ 17], mango peel [ 18],
datura stramonium seed [19], ﬂixweed seeds [ 20], lotus
seed pods [ 21], avocado seed [ 22], rubber seed shells
[5], Wisteria sinensis seed [23], Eucalyptus globulus seed
[13], datura metel seed [ 24], polyalthia longifolia seed
[25], Xanthoceras sorbifolia seed [ 26], oak seed [ 27],
coffee beans [ 28], and others, in this study, waste
biomass from date seeds was used.
Date palms ( Phoenix dactylifera ) are a major food
source in the Middle East and North Africa with a
sweet taste and popularly loved by everyone. The
content contained includes carbohydrates with a
percentage of 77.34–84.45% as well as various
important minerals such as magnesium, manganese,
iron, potassium, calcium, sodium, copper and zinc.
Date palms are also rich in antioxidants which help to
reduce the risk of cancer and boost the immune
system [ 29]. The processing industry produces waste
in the form of seeds ranging from 6.10 to 11.47% of
the fruit. The date seeds waste can also be used for
additional food ingredients in livestock, manufacture
of caffeine-free coffee drinks, and antioxidants [ 30].
Moreover, they are potentially applied as activated
carbon due to their rich carbon composition identi-
ﬁed from the content of cellulose, hemicellulose, and
lignin with percentages of 42%, 18%, and 11%,
respectively.
Physical activation is one of the methods used for
the manufacture of carbon materials in oxidizing
environments such as air, oxygen, steam, carbon
dioxide or their mixtures, at temperatures of
450–900 /C176C[
7]. Carbon dioxide gas has clean char-
acteristics and tends to maintain the carbon electrode
at high temperatures. The main purpose of the acti-
vation process is to increase the pore volume, diam-
eter, and improve the structure, as well as porosity of
the carbon electrode. It causes the micropores formed
in the previous process to widen due to the erosion of
the walls between the micropores and the formation
of new pores, thereby increasing the speciﬁc surface
area. These properties determine the porous structure
of the carbon electrodes ranging from high to low
porosity. In general, the higher the activation tem-
perature and time, the larger the pore formation, but
the development of high porosity causes a widening
of the pore size leading to macroporosity [ 5, 22]. This
study focused on the manufacture of porous carbon
material derived from the waste of date palm plan-
tations, which has low cost and is renewable. The
optimization of the CO
2 activation temperature was
selected for the formation of the hierarchical-nanoﬁ-
ber structure framework with a high surface area and
electrical conductivity to reach high electrochemical
performance as an electrode material for superca-
pacitors. The surface morphology, speciﬁc surface
area, pore size distribution, and functional groups of

2 Experimental section
2.1 Preparation of carbon electrode
Date seeds biomass (DS) was cleaned and dried using
sunlight and a drying oven at a temperature of
110 /C176C. The sample with a constant mass was pre-
carbonized for 30 min at 200 /C176C under vacuum, fol-
lowed by ball-milling to produce a powder with a
homogeneous size that is /C20 53 lm, which was
chemically activated using 0.3 M KOH for 2 h and
dried at 110 /C176C. Subsequently, the dried sample was
molded using a hydraulic press to produce a coin-
shaped carbon electrode. The next process was car-
bonization-physical activation in an integrated man-
ner by inserting pellets into a horizontal tube furnace
and heating at a rate of 3 /C176C min
-1 to 600 /C176C, then the
sample temperature was maintained for 60 min
under a nitrogen atmosphere at a ﬂow rate of 1.5
Lmin
-1. Heating was continued at a rate of
10 /C176Cm i n-1 to 800 /C176C, during which the sample
temperature was maintained for 60 min under a
carbon dioxide atmosphere at a ﬂow rate of 1 Lmin
-1.
In the second stage heating process, the temperature
ranged from 700 /C176C, 750 /C176C, and 800 /C176C, each was
coded with DS-700, DS-750, and DS-800. Afterward,
the sample was cooled to room temperature under a
nitrogen atmosphere, then neutralized using distilled
water (aquadest) to a pH of * 7 and dried in the
oven at 110 /C176C. The carbon electrodes produced were
polished with a diameter of 7–8 mm and a thickness
of 0.2–0.3 mm. The carbon electrode preparation
scheme is demonstrated in Fig. 1.

3 Results and discussion
3.1 Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy
analysis
Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy Analysis
was carried out to determine the functional group of
the DS carbon electrode at a wave number of
450–4500 cm
-1 as shown in Fig. 2a. The characteristic
of peak at 3425 cm -1 is related to the stretching of the
hydroxyl O–H vibrations or the adsorbed water,
while the wave number located at 2924 cm
-1 refers to
aliphatic C–H vibrations. C–H band or alkane com-
pounds are caused by lignocellulosic degradation,
while the peak associated with the C–O vibration is
located at 1627 cm
-1 referring to the lactonic, car-
boxyl or anhydride groups. The absorption peak at
wave number 2325 cm
-1 can be attributed to the
C:C functional group caused by the stretching
mode of the aldehyde. This functional group indi-
cates the purity of the carbon in the framework which
can increase the conductivity of the material. Fur-
thermore, oxygen-containing functional groups such
as O–H and C–O can effectively improve the wetta-
bility, inﬁltration of the electrode material, and
reduce the charge transport resistance which posi-
tively affects the capacitive performance of the car-
bon electrode [ 34, 35].

3.3 Spectroscopy Raman analysis
The ﬁne structure or graphitization of the DS carbon
electrode obtained from the Raman spectra is pre-
sented in Fig. 3 showing the D and G peaks at 1345
and 1595 cm
-1, respectively. This phenomenon is a
characteristic of amorphous and ideal graphite layers
of carbon material which corresponds to the results of
XRD analysis. The D-band corresponds to local
defects and disordered carbons with sp
3 bonds, while
the G-band indicates the E2g phonon of the sp 2
hybridized carbon. The ratio of D-band and G-band
(ID/IG) intensity is an important indicator to assess
the degree of graphitization and structural distur-
bance of the DS carbon electrode. The DS-700, DS-750
and DS-800 electrodes have ID/IG ratios of 1.007,
0.968 and 0.998, respectively. A low ID/IG value
indicates that the carbon matrix has a high degree of
graphitization which translates to better conductivity
[40].

Figure 4a, b shows the surface morphology of the
DS-700 electrode which has a hierarchical structure
with the presence of multiple mesopores and a pre-
dominance of macropores without nanoﬁbers. The
low activation temperature causes the carbon elec-
trode to react slowly with CO
2, leading to the meso-
pores and macropores having the lowest average
diameter of 16 nm and 91.42 nm, respectively. Con-
sequently, the ﬁber splits into a pore structure due to
the mismatch of the given temperature. Figure 4c, d
shows the surface morphology of the DS-750 elec-
trode which has a hierarchical-nanoﬁber structure
caused by an increase in the physical activation
temperature. At 750 /C176C, the carbon electrode reacts
again with CO
2 gas in a complex manner after being
chemically activated by KOH to form the initial
morphological structure. The increase in activation
temperature causes the reaction to produce a
widening of the pores, leading to mesopores and
macrospores with an average diameter of 34 nm and
98 nm, respectively. Also, the nanoﬁber structure
previously formed in the chemical activation process
with an average diameter of 22.8 nm which is
advantageous in the reservoir and facilitates ion
transfer within the carbon matrix. The hierarchical-
nanoﬁber structure can increase the speciﬁc capaci-
tance, because the junction between the ﬁbers can
shorten the charging route in the pores of the carbon
electrode, increase the charge transfer efﬁciency, as
well as reduce the internal resistance of the electrode.
Moreover, the hierarchical structure as an ion buffer
effectively minimized the diffusion distance of the
ion electrolyte to the interior surface of the electrode.
Figure 4e, f shows the surface morphology of DS-
800 with a hierarchical structure consisting of
macropores and mesopores having an average
diameter of 123 nm and 45 nm without the presence
of nanoﬁbers. This occurred because the increase in
the physical activation temperature above 750 /C176C
caused an excessive reaction of carbon and CO
2
electrodes. Consequently, the nanoﬁbers formed in
the chemical activation process cannot be maintained
to form a pore structure with a widening of the
diameter. The reaction also caused the pores of the
carbon electrode to collapse, the inner wall channel
becomes thinner, and adjacent pores are intercon-
nected [ 44, 45]. The pore dimensions and surface
morphology of the carbon electrodes affect the
migration and transfer of electrolyte ions. In general,
macropores function as channels for easy access to
electrolytes but have minimal contribution to capac-
itance, while mesopores can increase velocities by
facilitating the diffusion and transport of electrolyte
ions. Micropores play a role in ion storage at the
electrodes, therefore, signiﬁcant improvements can
be obtained by increasing mesopores and micropores.

4 Conclusion
This study was conducted to synthesize carbon-
derived material from date palm seed biomass (DS)
by activation using KOH and CO
2 as electrodes of
symmetric supercapacitor cells. The activation of CO 2
was optimized by varying the temperature at 700 /C176C,
750 /C176C, and 800 /C176C to regulate the distribution of
pores. The results showed that all DS carbon elec-
trodes had absorption at a wave number of
1627 cm
-1 which indicated the C–O functional
group. Furthermore, microstructure analysis showed
that the amorphous structure with the lowest Lc and
the highest La values was owned by DS-750 with
values of 10 nm and 39.53 nm, respectively. This
sample also exhibited a hierarchical-nanoﬁber mor-
phological structure with the highest speciﬁc surface
area of 943.629 m
2g-1. The electrochemical proper-
ties’ measurement showed that the highest speciﬁc
capacitance, power density and energy density val-
ues were 258.88 Fg
-1, 7.11 Whkg -1 and 125.46
Wkg-1, respectively. Based on the results, the acti-
vation of KOH and CO 2 at 750 /C176C is the optimum
temperature for the manufacture of activated carbon
electrodes derived from date seeds biomass to
increase the performance of supercapacitor cells.
