ABSTRACT
By offering a green and sustainable option to reusing biomass, the creation of energy harvesting/storage sys-
tems from biomass/waste materials has signi ﬁcant scienti ﬁc and technological implications. To achieve the
required applications in the ﬁelds of supercapacitor and conversion, microstructure management is crucial
for carbon-based materials. Here, a facile production of heteroatom-doped porous carbon (HAPC) from
Enhydra ﬂuctuant leaves as electrode material for supercapacitor is demonstrated. Large speci ﬁc-surface area
and good pore-size distribution were achieved. An exceptional speci ﬁc-capacitance ( C
sp) 428 F/g at 1 A/g in
an acidic electrolyte was achieved. The solid-state symmetric-supercapacitor device (SSSD) produces the high-
est C
sp of 113 F/g and 73.88 Wh/kg energy –density at 1 A/g, whereas 5.11 kW/kg power-density was achieved
at 10 A/g. The SSSD exhibits outstanding durability, where it holds 94% of its Csp after 10,000 charge/dis-
charge series.
1. Introduction
A global goal is now to produce and store energy ef ﬁciently, eco-
friendly, and sustainably. Numerous rechargeable devices have been
created, and electrochemical-related technology has garnered a lot of
attention as an ef ﬁcient method for electrical energy storage. Because
of their large power capacity, outstanding coulombic reversibility
(98% or more), exceptional cyclability, and environmentally benign
characteristics, supercapacitors play a signi ﬁcant role in energy har-
vesting/storage. The pseudocapacitors and double-layer capacitors
are the two primary groups of supercapacitors based on the charge-
storage mechanism [1–4]. So, in the recent decade, the main chal-
lenges are to explore biocompatible sources which are renewable,
eco-friendly, cost-effective, and high-adsorption electrode materials,
having fast ion and/or electrode transport, and also ﬁlled with tunable
microstructure/surface chemistry [5]. Biomass is a renewable, crucial
precursor to produce carbonaceous materials for energy harvesting/
storage device applications. Therefore, nowadays researchers put more
attention into processing biomass-derived materials for their attract-
able storage properties including their extensive accessibility, being
renewable, and also cost-effective starting ingredients. As a result, var-
ious types of applications are exploited for these kinds of properties
including carbon-dioxide capture [6], hydrogen storage [7], dye-sensi-
tized solar cells [8], water treatment [9], and most importantly energy
storage [10,11]. More importantly, these are useful for their attract-
able integral homogeneous, also speci ﬁc biological properties that
are applied as patterns for constructing active electrode materials con-
necting well-deﬁned geometries. Besides this, products containing car-
bon precursor as a major quantity and also the well-balanced networks
are signi ﬁcant for the metabolic process of making biowaste-based
electrode materials. Because of this, they are appropriate for applica-
tions involving electrochemical charge/discharge that require rapid
ion movement. Recently, diverse types of biomass or biowaste-derived
carbon materials are indispensable in the fabrication of self-doped
hierarchical porous carbon-based electrodes and this gets increasing
interest as a warming theme for energy harvesting/storage and conver-
sion-related ﬁelds [12].
Biomass-derived porous graphite carbon materials (BPGCs) are
renowned as new justi ﬁable carbon materials for their well-develop-
ment of porous structure, interesting graphitic structure, doping with

storage and conversion, there are three different systems that have
been classi ﬁed and investigated to utilize the BPGCs including fuel
cells, lithium-ion batteries, and supercapacitors. Although, after
detailed study, the porous carbon materials have some limitations like
(i) a low degree of graphitization with a poor graphitic structure,
resulting a less conducting and transmission of ions and electrons,
(ii) low speci ﬁc-surface areas, (iii) lack of heteroatoms for doping,
etc. The above-mentioned limitations have imperfect the proper
“green” applications in energy harvesting/storage and conversion as
well as environmental pollution protection [14].
BPGCs materials contain a broad range of “green” applications
owing to their outstanding electrical conductivity, upgraded crys-
talline orientation, and also exceptional physicochemical possessions
which could facilitate ion diffusion [15]. However, graphitic carbon
materials have some dif ﬁculties including their costly nature and poor
porous structure. To be stunned by these applications, excellent porous
graphitic structures of carbon materials with appropriate heteroatoms
doping are developed. Therefore, heteroatoms-self-doped porous bio-
mass carbon materials have been fabricated as ef ﬁcient electrodes
for symmetric electrochemical supercapacitors as they feature extre-
mely large energy –density in both aqueous and organic-based elec-
trolytes. As a result, they exhibit signi ﬁcant potential for energy
harvesting/storage and conversion-related applications [16,17].
Supercapacitors (SCs) are the most proper devices as they have
energy storage as they have large power-density and excellent cyclic
electrochemical stability [18]. Although in the past decades, activated
carbon materials have been utilized as electrode materials in superca-
pacitor applications but in recent decades biomass-derived activated
carbons (ACs) attract much more attention. When heteroatoms like
B, S, O, N, and P are introduced into the carbon skeleton for construct-
ing the porous structure of carbon elements, the electrochemical per-
formance of SCs increased. Doping with N and O in the
supercapacitors results in a better comprehensive activity instead of
using other heteroatoms like B or S [16,19]. For synthesizing ACs,
the most used methods are chemical, physical, or chemical-physical
activations [20]. These ACs have some useful properties such as a high
surface area with larger pore volume and unique electrochemical
behavior for choosing as supercapacitors. However, these have some
disadvantages e.g., a large number of chemical reagents are needed
in the chemical activation path. In these reactions, the using excess
candidates are not recycled easily and cause environmental pollution.
Sometimes, the dead and fallen leaves were successfully used for pro-
cessing the ACs by the same method of activation using abundant alka-
lis or acids but these are so common [21]. To resolve the above
problems, unique nano-structures of biomass-based materials are cho-
sen for the ACs production only through facile one-step carbonization
in the absence of any other agents. Nowadays, to construct functional
carbon materials, molecular modi ﬁcation-based gel precursors are the
most favorable preparation method. And for this, various types of gels
are used.
In this study, we applied a straightforward production of HAPC
skeleton from leaves of Enhydra ﬂuctuant, and the produced active car-
bon matrics were utilized as active materials for energy harvesting/
storage purposes. The synthesized carbon materials had a good pore
size and distribution with a large speci ﬁc-surface area. Synthetic por-
ous carbon has exceptional speci ﬁc-capacitance ( C
sp), measuring
428 F/g at a current density of 1 A/g under H 2SO4 electrolyte. This
is owing to the porous carbon's high active surface and pore volume.
Active electrode materials' electrokinetic research reveals the largest
capacitive input to total charge storage. Produced HAPC matrices uti-
lized in the construction of the solid-state symmetric-supercapacitor
device (SSSD) exhibit the highest Csp of 113 F/g at 1 A/g with a wide
voltage frame of 2 V. The as-fabricated SSSD exhibits maximum 73.88
Wh/kg energy –density at 1 A/g current density and 5.11 kW/kg
power-density at 10 A/g current density. Even in an acidic electrolyte,
the manufactured SSSD exhibits outstanding rate capability and dura-
bility. Subsequently 10,000 nonstop charge/discharge cycles, the gad-
get still retains 94% of its Csp. The synthesized HAPC skeletons have a
substantial possibility to bridge the electrochemical performance gap
between conventional electrolytes because of their extremely interest-
ing electrochemical performance than formerly described carbona-
ceous electrodes using a simple carbonization process.
2. Experimental
2.1. Chemicals
All chemicals are of the AR grades and were all used without fur-
ther puri ﬁcation. Ammonium chloride (NH 4Cl, 99.9%), concentrated
hydrochloric acid (HCl, 99.9%), and potassium hydroxide (KOH,
99.9%) were all furnished by Sigma Aldrich. De-ionized water was uti-
lized to prepare the aqueous solutions.
2.2. Synthesis of heteroatom-doped porous carbon (HAPC) skeleton
The raw green leaves of Enhydra ﬂuctuant were collected, and the
extra impurities, that were adhered to the composed leaves, were
removed by washing them in double distilled water. After that, the
leaves were chopped into little slices and dried in the sun for a week.
The dried raw green leaves were crushed in a high-speed mixing-grin-
der to produce dust from the leaves. Before the materials were car-
bonized in the subsequent procedures, the as-produced dust was
activated by NH 4Cl and KOH. NH 4Cl and green leaves powder were
combined. First, a 1:1 stoichiometric mass ratio of ammonium chloride
and dust of green and raw leaves were mixed together in a mortar. The
mixture was then maintained in a drying oven under vacuum and pure
Argon (Ar) atmosphere at ∼80 °C for 24 h. Later, KOH was added to
the mixture in the stoichiometric mass ratio of 1:1 and thoroughly
mixed to create a homogenous mixture. The mixture was then main-
tained in the vacuum oven under the same conditions. The pre-acti-
vated leaves were carbonized at various temperatures; 800, 900, and
1000 °C in a continuous Ar gas ﬂow applying a constant heating tem-
perature rate of 5 °C/min; they were then held at the annealing tem-
perature for two hours. Later, under a continuous Ar gas ﬂow, the
product was left cooling naturally to reach room-temperature. To get
rid of any unreacted components, the resulting ingredients were
washed for numerous periods applying 10% HCl and distilled water.
After that, distilled water was used to wash away the black substance
in order to neutralize it. The produced sample was ultimately desic-
cated in a desiccator. Also known as H-PC EF-800, EF-900, and EF-
1000, these synthetic HAPC materials are carbonized at 800, 900,
and 1000 °C, respectively. Supplementary Materials contain the mate-
rial characterization and other electrochemical measurements.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Formation of HAPC materials from Enhydra ﬂuctuant leaves
Different chemical components can be found in the raw, green
leaves of E. Fluctuant . It has various medicinal properties. It is a plant
with great therapeutic potential and numerous positive properties,
including anticancer, antioxidant, and antidiabetic, antibacterial,
anti-diarrheal. The existence of phytochemicals such as ﬂavonoids,
alkaloids, phenol, tannins, saponins, and carbohydrates can be cred-
ited to these activities [29,30]. Raw green leaves include a variety of
nutrients, including pectin, crude ﬁber, carotenoids, moisture, fat,
and polyphenols [29,30]. In the synthesis, carbohydrates are the main
source of carbon precursors.

Using the KOH activator, solid –solid reactions were involved
accompanied by solid –liquid ones. During the course of reactions,
the KOH is also reduced and changed into metallic K. The oxidization
of carbon precursors is producing carbonates and CO 2/CO. The cross-
reaction similarly happens concurrently [31–34].K 2CO3 is produced
during the activation phase at temperatures between 400 and 600 °
C. At a temperature greater than 600 °C, the K 2CO3 breaks down into
K2O and CO 3. Over 800 °C will be the reaction's completion tempera-
ture. Furthermore, the CO/CO 2 and K chemicals produced interact
with carbon precursor at temperatures higher than 700 °C. As a result,
the carbon increases in its lattice structure throughout the irreversible
chemical activation processes. The obtained carbon-based materials
have a high degree of microporosity. The involved mechanism is illus-
trated by the following equations:

The reaction temperature and KOH account for the heteroatom
content, the surface area of the produced carbon materials, and its
porosity. Here, we used 1:1 (carbon precursor:KOH) ratios of H-PC
materials that were synthesized at various pyrolyzed temperatures.
3.2. Morphological and structural analysis
The crystallinity of the synthesized HAPC framework made from E.
Fluctuant leaves was analyzed utilizing the P-XRD pattern at various
carbonization temperatures. Because the synthesized HAPC materials
are amorphous, no identi ﬁable or strong peaks could be seen in the
XRD investigation of HAPC matrices made from leaves of E. Fluctuant.
Two peaks, which corresponded to the re ﬂection of the graphitized
carbon framework (0 0 2) and (1 0 0) crystalline planes were present
in the prepared HAPC materials EF-800, EF-900, and EF-1000 at about
2θ = 24° and 44°, respectively, as shown in Fig. 1a [35]. Due to the H-
PC EF-800 material ’s smaller pyrolyzed temperature, which was insuf-
ﬁcient to activate biomass in the presence of the KOH activator, a
minor shift in the P-XRD pattern was seen for these materials.
The diffraction patterns (0 0 2) of prepared HAPC framework EF-
900 and EF-1000 are higher compared to the pattern of EF-800, show-
ing the graphitization degree of the HAPC framework somewhat
increased as the activation temperature increased. These two peaks
imply that the synthetic HAPC framework produced at varied pyro-
lyzed temperatures had a higher disordered carbon framework
[36,37]. The XRD peaks of HAPC framework EF-900 are broader than
EF-800 and EF-1000 respectively, re ﬂecting that the HAPC framework
EF-900 had enhanced disordered carbon matrices compared to others.
No other peaks were disordered in the diffraction pattern excluding
the peaks described above. The pre-activated leaves of E. Fluctuant
were favorable at 900 °C exhibiting the maximum degree of distur-
bance in the carbon matrices [38]. The activated HAPC framework
made from E. Fluctuant leaves is shown in Fig. 1 b with their Raman
spectra compared at the three different carbonization temperatures.
The result indicates the existence of a defect (D) and a graphitic (G)
bands as two distinct peaks at ∼1352 and ∼1581 cm −1, respectively
[39]. The D band which resulted from the κ-point-phonons breath-
ing-mode A 1g mode’s symmetry, revealed the presence of structural
defects and contaminants in the activated H-PC materials as they were
initially created by carbonization with an activator. The sp 2 hybridized
carbon in-plane E 2g symmetry mode produced another peak in the G
band. A calculation of the intensity ratio of the D and G peaks was
done in order to assess the disorder degree in the carbon-based matri-
ces [39]. The synthesized HAPC frameworks EF-800, EF-900, and EF-
1000 have (ID/IG) ratios of 0.93, 0.99, and 0.93, respectively. The EF-
900 materials showed the greatest level of graphitization or the most
disordered carbon framework when compared to other synthetic
HAPC frameworks [12]. To improve the performance and long-term
durability of supercapacitors, a speci ﬁc amount of graphitization
would reduce the electron communication resistance during the charg-
ing and discharging event [40]. It is interesting to clear here that the
(ID/IG) ratio changes to some extent as the temperature increases.
The lattices are lengthened with the help of intercalation of K during
the KOH stimulation step, and the pores are formed through the etch-
ing process of the carbon [41,42]. Consequently, the activation
increases the number of defects and disarrays in the carbon material
structure. On the other hand, disorder and structural alignment in
the carbon framework can be encouraged by high-temperature pyrol-
ysis. The tradeoff between high-temperature carbonization and KOH
chemical activation leads to the graphitic degree. Another peak corre-
sponding to the 2D band of the active carbon matrix was detected at
∼2800 cm −1, suggesting that typical graphitization occurs through
the activation process. To see the morphology and microstructure of
the produced active HAPC frameworks, scanning electron microscopic
measurements were carried out.
The Fig. 2 a-c displays the FE-SEM image of HAPC framework at
various activation temperatures. Following carbonization, the HAPC
framework exhibits a rather well-developed porous framework with
nanoﬂakes. The HAPC materials ’ nanoﬂakes architectures were attrib-
uted to both the leaves derived from E. Fluctuant biomass materials'
highly developed nutrition and metabolic channels as well as the pore
channels created by moisture and small-molecule gases that were
released after a thermal breakdown in the presence of KOH activator
[43–45]. The KOH activator can pass through these channels and into
the interior of the carbon materials where it interacts with the material
to widen the pore sizes [46,47]. Fewer pores and nano ﬂakes are seen
on the EF-800 at lower temperatures. As the temperature increases,
nanoﬂakes become more regular and the porosity structure becomes
more systematic, as seen in Fig. 2 b. The internal structure of the pro-
duct breakdown beyond 900 °C, the channel ’s inner walls become thin,
and fewer nanoﬂakes are visible as seen in Fig. 2c. When the KOH acti-
vator is added, the surface of the HAPC gets rougher, and the amount
of pores considerably rises, suggesting that the KOH activator reacts
successfully with the carbon particles in the biowaste. This caused
the pores to open and grow due to the carbon matrices in the biowaste
[25,26]. The EDX investigation of HAPC framework EF-900 discloses
the existence of heteroatoms in HAPC framework ( Fig. S1 ). Transmis-

microstructural properties of the prepared HAPC framework EF-900.
The interconnected thin nano- ﬂake structure of the prepared HAPC

3.3. Electrochemical analysis
3.3.1. Three-electrode performance
The produced HAPC framework will be a good option for excellent
supercapacitor (SC) electrode materials because of their larger active
surface area, porous nature along heteroatoms, and consistent ﬂake-
like structure. With the application of a predictable three-electrode
system using a Pt, Ag/AgCl, and manufactured graphite sheet as coun-
ter, reference, and working electrode, respectively, measurements of
various electrochemical performances, such as cyclic voltammetry
(CV), galvanostatic charge/discharge (G-CD), and electrochemical
impedance spectroscopic (EIS) studies were made. The system utilized
three different electrolytes: neutral (Na
2SO4), acidic (0.5 M H 2SO4),
and alkaline (1 M KOH). The prepared EF-900 materials have Csp of
113, 540, and 89 F/g in the above electrolytes, respectively, with a
wide potential window ranging from −0.2 to 1 V vs. Ag/AgCl refer-
ence electrode at 5 mV/ sec scan rate ( Fig. S3 ).
When compared to other HAPC frameworks created at different
annealing temperatures, the EF-900 exhibits a higher Csp at a smaller
scan rate while utilizing H 2SO4 electrolyte. So, we choose the acidic
electrolyte as a supporting medium for all electrochemical applica-
tions. At a small scan rate of 5 mV/ sec, the HAPC materials EF-800,
EF-900, and EF-1000 have a Csp of 234, 540, and 305 F/g in acidic
electrolytes ( Fig. 5 a). When related to additional synthetic carbon
materials, the EF-900 may have a maximal capacitance in various scan
rates compared to EF-800 and EF-1000 owing to the large porous
structure, active surface area, and existence of heteroatoms in their
interrelated carbon framework. Comparative CV plot of graphite
sheets and EF-900 measured in acidic electrolyte is shown in Fig. S4 .
In addition, XRD, Raman, SEM, and BET studies all point to a temper-
ature of 900 °C as being the optimal temperature for the carbonization
of pre-activated carbon precursor. In addition to this, research has
shown that a decrease in the C
sp of electrode materials occurs simulta-
neously with increasing the value of the scan rate. When the scan rates
were relatively modest, the electrochemical reaction was carried out
by the vast maximum amount of the inner part of the active electrode.
The lowest scan rates have given rise to the recording of the highest
Csp. This is because the lower rate of period time which permits the
inner charges to grow and enter intensely to the hierarchical porous
carbon matrices of the active electrode materials results in the highest
Csp. Because the majority of the electrochemical-active materials' inner
active portion does not participate in electrochemical reactions, the Csp
of the electrochemical-active materials decreases with applying higher
scan rates [22–28]. The produced HAPC framework also possesses the
aforementioned electrochemical characteristics. At higher scan rates,
all of the prepared HAPC frameworks have lower Csp than lower scan
rates. In H 2SO4 electrolyte, the prepared HAPC frameworks EF-800,
900, and 1000 had, respectively, Csp of 210, 475, and 265 F/g at
20 mV/ sec scan rate ( Fig. S5 a, S6a, S7a). Under the same circum-
stances, the prepared HAPC frameworks EF-800, EF-900, and EF-
1000 showed, respectively, Csp of 169, 359, 205 F/g at 60 mV/ sec scan

800, EF-900, and EF-1000 displayed Csp of 182, 428, and 234 F/g,
respectively, as shown in Fig. 5 b. As we know surface area, pores,
and channels, which can produce multiple electrical layers within
the charging/discharging processes, are the most important properties
of carbon materials. High coulombic ef ﬁciency and electrochemical
reversibility were demonstrated by the triangular and almost linear-
shaped charge/discharge pro ﬁles that were seen for the produced H-
PC materials with a low IR drop. With nitrogen/sulfur-doped porous
carbon sheets, Y Chen et al. reported a maximum Csp of 132F/g at
an applied 1 A/g current density in an acidic electrolyte [58]. The
aforementioned factor, particularly the inaccessibility of the inner
active region of the electrode materials, is the main cause of the Csp
of the produced H-PC materials degrading at high current densities.
The H-PC EF-800, EF-900, and EF-1000 materials have Csp of 113,
279, and 150 F/g, respectively, in acidic electrolytes, when 5 A/g cur-
rent density was applied ( Fig. S5 b, S6b, S7b). Similarly, the H-PC EF-
800, EF-900, and EF-1000 materials have Csp of 67, 167, and 83 F/g,
respectively, at 20 A/g current density ( Fig. S5 b, S6b, S7b). Due to
its large surface area, large pore volume, and the presence of heteroa-
toms in the linked carbon skeleton, the EF-900 displays excellent Csp
and rate capabilities when compared to other prepared HAPC materi-
als EF-800 or EF-1000 ( Fig. 5 c and d). The pseudocapacitive property
of the electrode materials was accelerated by the heteroatom's (N)
optimum presence, which in turn increased the capacitive properties
of the prepared HAPC materials.

3.3.2. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopic (EIS) analysis
To investigate the electrode –electrolyte interface, the resistance,
and charge transport capability properties of produced HAPC frame-
works during electrochemical reactions, a three-electrode setup was
used. Nyquist plots of the prepared HAPC framework in acidic elec-
trolytes are presented in Fig. 7. As revealed in high frequencies region,
a distorted semicircle indicates the charge-transfer resistance (R ct)o f
electroactive electrode materials could be detected while a straight
line was observed in low frequencies region indicating the diffusion
of ions into active electrode materials. At a high carbonization temper-
ature, the prepared HAPC framework seems to have better charge stor-
age performance, as indicated by a roughly vertical line formed in the
low-frequency region [60]. It appears that the synthesized HAPC
framework EF-800 line is more perpendicular to the hypothetical axis.
Notice that the Warburg impedance appears as a straight line with a
slope of 45°. So, the prepared HAPC-900 framework shows a straight
line with a slope of 45° whereas other prepared HAPC materials show
a straight line with a higher slope. So, the prepared HAPC-900 shows
better diffusion of ions in the electrode and electrolyte interface in
acidic electrolyte compared to other synthesized nanomaterials. That
is why the prepared HAPC-900 displays higher stability and speci ﬁc
capacitance in acidic electrolyte.
The synthesized HAPC frameworks EF-800, EF-900, and EF-1000
show the R ct values 41.52, 29.2, and 45.52 Ohm, respectively, in an
acidic electrolyte. As noticed, the R ct of HAPC framework EF-900
demonstrates improved electron carrier ef ﬁcacy at the electrode/ elec-
trolyte interface compared to other synthesized nanomaterials. Also, in
comparison to EF-800 and EF-1000 materials, the EF-900 has a lower
Rct owing to its increased pore volume, surface area, and HAPC frame-
work. The greater electron transfer ef ﬁciency of the HAPC framework
EF-900 aids in its improved electrochemical performance.

4. Conclusions
The biomass of E. Fluctuant was successfully converted into het-
eroatom-doped porous carbon (HAPC) nano ﬂakes by annealing it at
various temperatures. Synthetic porous carbon materials have an ade-
quate surface area, outstanding capacitive performance, and great
durability even in electrolytes with a high level of acidity. Interest-
ingly, utilizing the constructed solid-state symmetric device, the pre-
pared carbon materials achieve a maximum energy –density of 73.88
Wh/kg and a power-density of 5.11 kW/kg which is similar to the
commercially existing carbon-based devices. Owing to their large sur-
face area, porosity, decreased R ct, and interconnected carbon frame-
work, the HAPC framework nano ﬂakes exhibit exceptional
endurance up to 10,000 charge/discharge cycles (94% Csp retention).
The current study offers a sound strategy for the use of biomass mate-
rials for different applications in addition to demonstrating the effec-
tive procedure of the HAPC framework for energy harvesting/
storage application.
