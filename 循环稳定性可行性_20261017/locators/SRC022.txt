Introduction The rapid expansion of renewable energy systems, electric vehicles, and large -scale artificial intelligence data centers has increased the demand for efficient energy storage systems [1–3]. The active material must be combined with a mechanically stable and electrically conductive current collector , while a complete device requires positive and negative electrodes with complementary electrochemical characteristics and an ion-conducting electrolyte [22, 23]. Numerous biomass-recycling studies have focused mainly on converting a single organic fraction into an electrode material while continuing to rely on commercially available current collectors [24–26]. Such approach es demonstrate the applicability of biomass -derived carbon but do not fully utilize the functional components already present in heterogeneous waste products. Therefore, a broader recycling strategy should separate the physically and chemically distinct waste fractions of the original product and assign each fraction a suitable role within the energy storage device. Despite its suitability as a carbon electrode, biomass -derived carbon stores charge through a predominantly non-Faradaic mechanism, which can limit capacitance and energy density [27]. In this study, a recycling strategy was developed to recover both tobacco residues and metallic heating elements (MHEs) from used heated tobacco and integrate them into a single asymmetric supercapacitor device. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to assign these two waste-derived fractions complementary roles as a carbonaceous active material and a current collector, respectively, in an energy -storage device. Conclusion In summary, an integrated strategy was developed to recycle the organic and metallic components of used heated tobacco as functional parts of an asymmetric supercapacitor. The MHEs maintained continuous and flat morphologies, comparable surface wettability, and nearly identical four-probe resistance values before
