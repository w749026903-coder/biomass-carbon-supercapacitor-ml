BWC-3-800 demonstrated the highest capacitance retention rate of all birch carbon samples, with only 0.2% degradation in capacitance after 60 000 cycles, comparable to Kuraray YP80F, which exhibited a 0.17% capacitance reduction. Degradation for BWC-2-800 was also minimal – 0.66%. For samples produced at a lower activation temperature a somewhat more rapid, albeit still relatively small reduction in capacitance was observed – with BWC-2-700 and BWC-3-700 exhibiting 2.75% and 1.25% degradation, respectively. The measured samples exhibited an increase in capacitance during the first 5 000-20 000 cycles. This could be attributed to additional electrolyte wetting and pore activation during the cycling process. Cycle value at which peak capacitance was reached varies substantially between the samples: BWC-2-700 at 5000 cycles, BWC-3-700 and BWC-2-800 at approximately 20000 cycles, and BWC-3-800 at 49000 cycles. It should be noted that BWC-3-800 additionally had the highest pore volume of all the carbon samples, but due to BWC-3-700 and BWC-2-800 having identical peak capacitance cycle values while their corresponding pore volumes differ considerably, a clear correlation between these two datasets cannot be proven. Conclusion In this research, birchwood charcoal was converted into high surface area activated carbons that are suitable for aqueous supercapacitor electrodes. A tunable pore architecture was obtained by varying the NaOH-to-precursor ratio and the activation temperature, ranging from predominately microporous structures (at 700 °C) to mixed micro-mesoporous carbons (at 800 °C).
