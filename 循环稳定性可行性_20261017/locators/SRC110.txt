Conclusion In this study, a sustainable and high-performance electrode material was successfully developed from bamboo stem biomass using NaOH chemical activation. The resulting carbon featured a hierarchical pore str

PDF p18 | numeric=True
s, and partial graphitic ordering. These features enabled a hybrid charge storage mechanism, combining electric double-layer capacitance and pseudocapacitance. Electrochemical evaluation demonstrated a remarkably high specific capacitance of 833 F/g at 0.5 A/g, excellent rate capability, and 94% capacitance retention after 5000 cycles, highlighting the material’s robust stability and energy storage potential. The low equivalent series resistance (0.2 Ω) further validated the material’s high conductivity and fast ion diffusion pathways. Compared with other recently reported biomass-derived carbons, the bamboo-derived electrode material outperforms in terms of both capacitance and sustainability. This work demonstrates that bamboo stem, an abundant and renewable biomass resource, can serve as an effective prec ursor for producing activated carbon with tunable porosity and functional chemistry. The green synthesis route, combined with scalable processing, positions this material as a promising candidate for next-generation eco-friendly supercapacitor devices. Although the bamboo-derived activated carbon demonstrated high specific capacitance and excellent short-term cycling stability, the study is limited by the narrow aqueous voltage window and absence of long-term or large-scale evaluation. Future work will focu s on optimizing the activation process and expanding the voltage window through alternative electrolytes.
