Figure 6g shows the cycling performance of the activated car- bon materials at 500 mA g –1. The capacity of all samples increases slightly during the initial cycling. After stabilization, the capacities of YP50, AC600, AC650, and AC700 are 40.1, 42.7, 44.2, and 50.3 mAh g –1, respectively. After 3000 cycles, the capacities remain at 37.9, 34.8, 37.4, and 43.1 mAh g –1, with capacity retention rates of 94.51, 81.50, 84.61, and 85.69%, respectively. All samples deliver a high coulombic e ﬃ ciency of more than 99% during cycling. As evidenced by post-cycling SEM and TEM images ( Figures S7 and S8 ), the lower cycling stability of AC700 compared with YP50 is attributable to the fact that its rich porous architecture induces more by-product accumulation and pore blockage during cycling at high current densities. Although YP50 presents the best cycling stability, AC700 combines a higher initial capacity with good cycling sta- bility, and its capacity after 3000 cycles is still 13.72% above that of YP50. As shown in Figure 6h, AC700 delivers linear voltage – time curves during the long-term cycling, with a gradual increase in IR drop. The underlying reasons for the outstanding electrochemical performance of AC700 can be summarized as follows. The higher spray pyrolysis temperature (700 °C) leads to a precursor containing more alkali etching agents and a better-developed carbon matrix. After activation, AC700 exhibits a high surface area, improved structural order, and sulfur-derived pseudo capacitance/wettability. These properties collectively enhance both the reversible capacity and the long- term cycling stability. 4. CONCLUSIONS In summary, we developed a facile spray pyrolysis-activation cou- pling strategy for the e ﬃ cient valorization of papermaking black liquor into spherical hierarchical porous activated carbon (SHPAC). In this process, lignin and hemicellulose in the black liquor serve directly as carbon sources, and the Na/K compounds act as both etching agents and sacriﬁcial templates.
