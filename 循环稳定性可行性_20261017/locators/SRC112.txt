These results highlight the importance of balancing micropore-based charge storage and mesopore-assisted ion transport in biomass-derived carbon electrodes. 1 Introduction The increasing demand for efficient energy conversion and storage, together with growing environmental concerns, has stimulated extensive research into sustainable electrochemical energy-storage technologies. Among various energy-storage sys- tems, rechargeable batteries generally provide relatively high energy density, whereas electrochemical capacitors, particularly supercapacitors, have attracted considerable attention because of their high power density, rapid charge–discharge capability, long cycling life, and operational safety [ 1–8 ]. Recent advances in graphene-based and flexible electrochemical energy-storage systems have further demonstrated the importance of elec- trode architecture, surface chemistry, and electrode–electrolyte interactions in determining energy-storage performance [ 9–11 ]. Carbon materials have been widely investigated as supercapac- itor electrodes because of their physicochemical stability, large specific surface area, favorable charge-transport characteristics, and tunable pore architecture [ 12–18 ]. Cycling stability is a crucial parameter for evaluating the practical applicability of supercapacitors. As shown in Figure 7f , the device exhibits a capacitance retention of approximately 106% after 10,000 continuous charge–discharge cycles at 10 A g− 1 [ 49 ].
