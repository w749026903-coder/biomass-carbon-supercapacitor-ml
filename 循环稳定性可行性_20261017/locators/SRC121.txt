Figure 6a shows the stability testing of the DSAC electrode via a 2000-cycle GCD test at 10 A/g in 1 M KOH. It shows that the DSAC electrode retains 83.3% of its initial capacity after 2000 discharge cycles. The 10-A/g current density was chosen because lower currents would take a few days to charge/discharge < 2000 cycles, which proves the high capacity to store electric charges. The 17% loss of capacitive performance after 2000 cycles is relatively high compared to conventional > 90% capacity retention after 5000–10,000 GCD cycles, which calls for deeper investigation. Although the exact reasons for the capacity decay cannot be accurately identified, several insights could be drawn. First, the pseudocapacitive and the diffusion-controlled EDLC (inside the microporous channels) contributions are significant, as shown in Fig. 6. The pseudocapacitive contribution stems from the surface oxygen-functional groups (Fig. 2E, F). Unfortunately, the ion uptake into the electrode material during oxidation causes swelling and strain, generating internal stresses and forming cracks. Furthermore, the surface oxygen groups might experience degradation with repeated redox reactions. Such changes could reduce the available surface area and ion accessibility for further charge/discharge cycles34. Alternatively, the applied PVDF binder strength of adhesion with the carbon matrix might have been weakened with the repeated GCD cycles. Other DSAC papers have found similar decays in the capacitive performance of the DSAC supercapacitor 7. In another study 28 that used banana peel activated with H 3PO4 to develop a Zn-ion hybrid supercapacitor, also 83% (initial capacity is 70 F/g) of the capacity was retained after 50,000 cycles at 1 A/g. To study the effect of increasing the KOH electrolyte concentration on the DSAC electrode performance, CV (Fig. 6b) and GCD tests in 3 M KOH were performed. Figure 6c exhibits the GCD of DSAC in KOH 1 M and 3 M at 4 A/g, which demonstrates an increase in the capacity overall in the case of 3 M compared to 1 M KOH. The GCD results indicate that 3 M KOH exhibits longer charge–discharge times, suggesting higher capacitance due to increased ionic conductivity and higher utilized surface area in the KOH 3 M case.
