The capacitance values reduce as the current densities increase, as the ions do not get sufficient time to access the entire surface area under total resistance. Good capacitance retention of 79% was achieved after 4500 cycles at a current density of 5 A/g and a coulombic efficiency of 99% was obtained. From the literature it was noted that microstructures at times restrict ion transport. The microporous structures contribute to high capacitance initially but as the charge- discharge cycles increase ion trapping may take place and on repeated cycles this may clog the porous structure causing capacitance degradation [ 32]. The capacitance retention could be further increased if operated at a much lower current density as the material may experience capacitive fading due to rapid ion diffusions at a relatively higher current density of 5 A/g. However, a high coulombic efficiency suggests highly reversible charge storage with stable porous carbon structures.
