An indication that supports the efforts required to achieve enhanced cyclic stability by post-modifications of AC employing advanced techniques. While the current work shows the potential of agro-food industrial biowaste-derived AC for electrochemical energy storage and CO 2 capture applications, some elements of practical implementation deserve additional exploration. The synthesis procedure uses easily accessible agro-industrial bio- waste feedstocks and well-established, intrinsically scalable carbonization and activation techniques, yet a thorough techno- economic analysis is outside the domain of this work and has to be investigated in future studies. Long-term electrochemical cycling stability, in particular, is a significant criterion for evaluating electrode durability and capacitance retention over extended periods of operation, and efforts should be made to achieve and sustain it. At the same time, dynamic breakthrough experiments based on mixed-gas adsorption measurements has to be carried out to determine selectivity in real-world scenarios. Future research should systematically assess improving per- formance and cycling stability, cost-effectiveness, process scal- ability, and energy requirements to provide a more thorough assessment of the long-term applicability of these materials by implementing advanced modification strategies that closely adhere to sustainable options. 4. The combination of micropores and mesopores yielding a hi- erarchical pore morphology, as well as the presence of functional groups at the pores had a significant influence on CO 2 ad- sorption and selectivity. AC2 exhibited the highest capture capacity of 2.55 mmol/g at 298 K and 1 bar along with cyclic stability, as well as an experimental CO 2 /N 2 selectivity of 46.5 at 50 mbar, indicating that selective CO 2 adsorption is primarily controlled by the availability of energetically favorable ad- sorption sites, whereas the pressure-dependent selectivity be- havior indicates progressive active sites occupation. The dominance of physisorption-induced CO 2 affinity is indicated by an increase in adsorption capacity of 2.82 mmol/g for AC2 at 273 K, with low Q st values in the 13.8−7 kJ/mol range and stability over multiple cycles. Electrochemical measurements reveal that the high graphitic carbon content and resulting electrical conductivity, accessible hierarchical pore structure with high microporosity, and oxygen-containing surface func- tionalities of the AC samples are the primary contributors for the high capacitive response across the investigated scan rate range, which is consistent with enhanced ion transport and stable electric double-layer formation. At the same time, symmetric full-cell system-based GCD profiles exhibited almost symmetric charge−discharge curves with a reasonable rate capability, retaining ∼70% of initial ca- pacitance despite a 4-fold increase in current density and ∼78% retention of capacitance after 1000 cycles. AC produced from agro-industrial waste streams is therefore promising in selective CO 2 capture and supercapacitors coupling potential in- environmental and energy applications with waste upcycling. In summary, the dual performance of these AC samples is de- termined by the interplay of graphitization, surface functionality, and pore structure. A hierarchical pore structure with surface functional groups promotes CO 2 affinity, while a hierarchical pore network with increased graphitic ordering promotes ion transport and hence electrochemical charge storage. Balancing these structural elements is thus critical for optimal performance in CO 2 capture and supercapacitor applications.
