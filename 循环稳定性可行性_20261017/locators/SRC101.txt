N The global demand for high-performance, environmentally friendly, and efficient energy storage systems continues to rise in line with the rapid advancement of modern technologies. Among various options, supercapacitors have attracted significant attention due to their density, rapid charge− discharge capability, and excellent cycling stability compared to conventional batteries. 1−5 However, their relatively low energy density remains a limitation. 6 Consequently, the development of advanced electrode materials capable of simultaneously delivering high capacitance and long-term stability has become a research focus. In this context, transition metal oxides and porous carbon materials are emerging as promising candidates, as they offer complementary charge- storage pseudocapacitance and electric double-layer capaci- tance (EDLC) mechanisms, respectively. 7−9 Supercapacitors generally store energy through two primary mechanisms, EDLC and pseudocapacitance. In EDLC systems, charge storage occurs through the electrostatic accumulation of electrolyte ions at the electrode/electrolyte interface without involving faradaic reactions, enabling excellent power capability and long-term cycling stability. 10,11 Porous carbon materials, including activated carbon, graphene, and biomass-derived carbon, are widely utilized as EDLC electrodes due to their high electrical conductivity, large accessible surface area, and hierarchical pore structures. In contrast, pseudocapacitive materials store charge through rapid and reversible faradaic reactions involving surface redox processes, providing higher specific capacitance than conventional carbon-based electro- des. Transition metal oxides have attracted considerable attention as pseudocapacitive materials because of their multiple oxidation states and rich redox activity. The intimate interfacial coupling, as evidenced by the seamless phase boundaries observed in the HRTEM analysis (Figure 4), also establishes efficient electron- transfer pathways between the Fe redox centers and the carbon framework, preserving electrical contact and facilitating rapid charge transport throughout prolonged cycling. 50−52 Con- sequently, the strengthened Fe−C interfacial coupling enhances redox reversibility, maintains the structural integrity of the electrode, and improves capacitance retention during long-term operation. This interpretation is consistent with previous studies demonstrating that Fe−C and Fe−N−C interfacial bonding plays a crucial role in stabilizing electrode microstructures, promoting charge-transfer kinetics, and preventing the degradation of active sites during extended electrochemical cycling. 56−58 After the durability test, the ESR increased substantially to 11 Ω, while the Rct slightly decreased to approximately 9 Ω (Figure 9c). Second, continuous ion insertion and extraction associated with faradaic redox reactions generate repeated volumetric expansion and contraction of the Fe-doped ZnO nanoparticles. The accumulated mechanical stress over thousands of cycles can induce structural fatigue, including partial pulverization of the nanoparticles and weakening of their attachment to the activated carbon matrix. Consequently, the deterioration of interparticle contact and conductive pathways contributes to the increased ESR observed after cycling. In contrast, the relatively stable and slightly decreased R ct after prolonged cycling highlights the importance of the Fe−C interfacial interaction in maintaining charge-transfer kinetics. The strong covalent Fe−C anchoring bonds effectively preserve intimate contact between the Fe-containing active species and the conductive carbon framework, preventing severe interfacial separation and maintaining accessible electron-transfer pathways during repeated cycling. Therefore, although some structural degradation occurs within the pseudocapacitive component, the stabilized interface helps prevent catastrophic electrode failure and preserves electro- chemical reversibility. Similar roles of metal−carbon interfacial interactions in improving structural stability and charge- transfer behavior have been reported for advanced composite electrodes. 53 The capacitance retention of 72% after long-term cycling (Figure 9e) therefore results from the combined effects of gradual degradation of Fe-doped ZnO, partial loss of electrochemically active sites, and increased internal resist- ance. 59 The FESEM images after the durability test further support this interpretation (Figure S3). Despite the noticeable capacitance decay, the Coulombic efficiency remained remarkably high at 99.9%, indicating a highly reversible and stable charge−discharge process through- out the cycling test. Under the 45° bending condition, the electrochemical durability exhibited faster performance degra- dation compared to the initial state. The capacitance retention decreased to 56.5% after 1500 cycles and further dropped to 15.2% after 3000 cycles, while the Coulombic efficiency remained at 100% (Figure 11f). These results suggest that mechanical deformation induced by bending adversely affected the electrochemical stability of the flexible ASC device, leading to accelerated capacitance fading during prolonged cycling. Compared with recently reported flexible supercapacitors, the electrochemical performance of the present flexible ASC is within the range reported for biomass- and ZnO/carbon-based flexible energy storage devices, although its cycling stability remains relatively lower. Huang et al. reported a flexible NCNF/BC@ZnO/NiCo-LDH asymmetric supercapacitor with an energy density of 21 Wh kg −1 at a power density of 757.1 W kg −1 and 80% capacitance retention after 8000 cycles. 64 A coffee waste-derived activated carbon-based flexible supercapacitor delivered an energy density of 12.5 Wh kg −1 at a power density of 202 W kg −1 , while maintaining 90.5% capacitance retention after 5000 cycles, 65 a flexible Co- (OH) 2 //porous carbon asymmetric supercapacitor also exhibited a high energy density of 76.7 Wh kg −1 at 1416.9 W kg −1 together with 91% capacitance retention after 5000 cycles, 66 and a RuO 2 /CNOs flexible supercapacitor delivered an energy density of 10.6 Wh kg −1 at a power density of 4456 kW kg −1 while maintaining 94.47% capacitance retention after 4000 cycles. 67 In comparison, the present Fe-ZnO/AC-based flexible ASC achieved a comparable energy density of 20.5 Wh kg −1 at 240.4 W kg −1 but showed lower cycling stability, retaining 55.7% of its initial capacitance after 5000 cycles. The relatively faster capacitance decay may be attributed to gradual electrochemical degradation of the active material, possible structural instability, and interfacial deterioration between the electrode, electrolyte, and current collector during prolonged cycling 64 rather than mechanical deformation, since the cycling test was conducted in the initial state before bending. EIS analysis revealed an overall increase in impedance after bending, associated with higher charge-transfer resistance and hindered ion transport due to mechanical strain. Interestingly, the ESR in the after-bending state was slightly lower than that in the initial state (Figure 11i), possibly due to improved interfacial contact induced by mechanical compression during bending.
