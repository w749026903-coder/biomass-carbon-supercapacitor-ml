Keywords Microalgae · Pyrolysis · Iodine and sulphur doping · Hydrothermal heating · Supercapacitor 1 Introduction Energy, which is vital for world life, seriously affects the economies and the environment of countries around the world due to the current dependence on non-renewable fos- sil fuels [1]. Interest in renewable, environmentally friendly, high-performance energy storage devices such as batteries, supercapacitors and fuel cells for electrochemical energy, which is an important part of clean energy, tends to increase [2, 3]. Among these, supercapacitors, in particular, have gar- nered significant interest from researchers [4–6]. The excellent microstructure and reasonable pore size dis- tribution of biomass-derived carbon materials enable their use in various applications. In particular, the outstanding charge storage/release capacity and high cycling stability of carbon-based supercapacitors promote their applications in energy storage and conversion [7]. The development of low- cost and renewable biomass-based carbon materials for use in energy storage devices is an important issue. As typical electrode material for supercapacitors, biomass-based car - bon precursors are attracting intense interest as a reliable and readily available source [8–12]. Furthermore, the coulombic efficiency of this supercapacitor was calculated to be 87.57% at a current density of 0.06 A/g, showing a capacity reten- tion performance of up to 89.72% over 1000 cycles. This study demonstrates that electrodes obtained through iodine and S heteroatom doping can be effectively used in super - capacitor applications. Moreover, future studies on the use of iodine alone or in conjunction with other heteroatoms in carbon-based materials may lead to the development of more advanced supercapacitor electrodes.
