ABSTRACT  
The main aim of this research work is to make activated carbon (AC) materials based on biochar obtained from 
date seeds via pyrolysis and activated with H
2
SO
4
. The study aimed to assess the potential of these biochar-based 
materials for energy storage in supercapacitor by investigating the impact of different activation temperatures on 
their electrochemical performance in an alkaline electrolyte. The focus was on identifying the most efficient 
material. Results from Raman Spectroscopy and XRD analysis revealed that sample DSAC-700 exhibited more 
ordered domains and suitable storage sites compared to the other biochar-based materials. Measured value of the 
ratio of intensity of D & G band (I
D
/I
G
) for DSBC-600, DSAC-700, DSAC-800, and DSAC-900 were 1.13, 0.83, 
1.06, and 1.04, respectively. The lowest value of (I
D
/I
G
) for DSAC-700 indicated a higher ordered graphitic plane, 
resulting improved conductivity and diffusion of ions within the aligned graphitic domains, which positively 
impacted the electrochemical performance. Samples DSAC-700 showed 487.5 F/g specific capacitance at 1 A/g 
current density. Galvanic charge and discharge trends also showed that DSAC-700 had higher charge storage 
ability with less discharge. The biochar-based materials (BC and ACs) derived from DS showed promise as 
materials for energy storage applications, and they represent a step towards clean energy.   
1. Introduction 
Energy consumption worldwide has been steadily increasing, with 
levels reaching 575 quadrillion (BTU) in 2015 and projected to reach 
736 quadrillion BTU by 2040. But unfortunately, current energy re -
sources are insufficient to meet this demand and may result in signifi -
cant energy crises due to issues such as non-renewable nature of natural 
resources, environmental effects, and higher energy requirements [1,2] . 
It is imperative and pressing to prioritize the development of energy 
solutions that are efficient, inexpensive, simple to produce, and eco- 
friendly, including energy storage devices such as fuel cells, batteries, 
capacitors, and supercapacitors [3 – 6] . 
Amongst the energy storage devices, supercapacitors have several 
exceptional characteristics. These are fast charging and discharging, 
long and stable cycles, superior power and energy density that distin -
guish them from others. Due to these features, supercapacitors are seen 
as a complementary storage device to capacitors, fuel cells, and con -
ventional rechargeable batteries. The excellent performance of 
supercapacitors makes them useful in many applications, such as 
portable and consumer electronics, transportation, aerospace, and sen -
sors [7 – 9] . 
Based on energy storage mechanism, supercapacitors are categorized 
into two types which are electrical double layer capacitors (EDLCs) and 
pseudo-capacitors [10] . The performance of supercapacitors is highly 
influenced by the properties of the active electrode materials [11] . 
Carbon-nanotubes (CNTs), carbon-nanofibers (CNFs) and biomass 
derived graphene like carbon are some promising active materials for 
electrode that can be employed for electrical double layer capacitors 
(EDLCs). In contrast, pseudo-capacitive behavior mainly relies on 
carbon-based materials like metal oxides, metal hydroxides and con -
ducting polymers. Due to these material redox reactions occur during 
the charging and discharging processes [12 – 17] . 
Various carbon enriched materials are being used for energy storage 
in supercapacitor applications. Conventionally biomass derived gra -
phene like materials owe the characteristics like high conductive nature, 
high surface area and mechanical flexibility [18,19] . However, the

Layered double hydroxides (LDHs) are known for their structural flexi -

However, when LDHs aggregate, it can result in reduced energy density

Amongst the carbon-based materials ACs are the most widely used 
materials for energy storage in supercapacitor applications for a long 
time. Owing to the characteristics like abundant availability, renew -
ability, large specific surface area, stable electrochemical performance, 
eco-friendliness ACs are in high demand due to their ability to provide 
outstanding electrochemical efficiency [23,24] . Utilizing the natural 
structure of biomass, which offers a continuous, interconnected, and 
hierarchical arrangement, has become increasingly popular in recent 
years. Previously mollusk shell was used to produce microporous carbon 
having honeycomb like structure through a cheap and environment 
friendly process. Inherited and interconnected hexagonal channels of 
these shells provide a promising electric conductivity to enhance elec -
tron diffusion and efficient electrolyte penetration. Owing to these 
properties it has a significant potential for improving capacitive per -
formance [25] . Due renewable nature of biomass, it can be converted 
into many useful products like bio oil, gases and biochar via different 
thermochemical conversion techniques like torrefaction [26] , pyrolysis, 
gasification and hydrothermal conversion [27] . When produced under 
controlled and optimal conditions, biochar generated via pyrolysis can 
result in high-quality material in different fields. According to studies to 
produce biochar some methods are used, the torrefaction process yields 
the highest percentage of biochar, exceeding 35%, pyrolysis up to 34% 
and gasification with approximate percentage of 10%, respectively [28] . 
This bio-char based material produced via pyrolysis can be modified by 
various surface modification techniques which are discussed in detail in 
[29] . These surface modified biochar based materials produced from 
biomass feedstocks can be utilized in variety of ways for potential 
application [30,31] . 
.Researchers are trying to produce activated carbon (AC) from 
different lignocellulosic biomasses including sugarcane bagasse, rice 
husk, bamboo, walnut shell, coconut shell, empty banana fruit bunch 
[32] . Potential of biomass for bioenergy prospective has been elaborated 
in a previous study which provides a comprehensive knowledge of 
biomass utilization for value added products [33] . 
The production of activated carbon involves using different carbon- 
rich sources as precursors, including but not limited to woody and 
nonwoody, coal, and petroleum pitch [34] . ACs can be produced by a 
two-stage process. Formerly, the carbon-rich material undergoes low- 
temperature carbonization, and in the second stage, it undergoes acti -
vation at a higher temperature. Activated carbon can be created using 
physical or chemical activation methods. Surface modification by a 
physical activation method uses steam, air, or oxygen at high tempera -
tures to activate biochar. In contrast, a surface modification via chemical 
activation technique involves the treatment of the biomass precursor 
feedstocks with an activating agent such as an acid, a base, or a salt in an 
inert atmosphere [35] . Various biomass waste materials, including 
fruits, husks, shells, vegetable waste, animal waste, and microorgan -
isms, have been utilized to produce activated carbon. These materials 
have been used in various applications related to energy and the envi -
ronment [36,37] . 
Many biomass feedstocks including shells, stalks, have been used to 
make ACs. The produced ACs have shown potential as electrode mate -
rials for supercapacitors [38 – 42] . Previously using a fixed bed pyro -
lyzer, the pyrolysis of Saudi Arabian mixed date palm seeds was 
performed. The study aimed to explore the effects of different pyrolysis 
temperatures (ranging from 200 to 1000 
◦

hydrogen-rich syngas and on the physicochemical properties of the DS 
biochar [43] . So far there is currently no study on the production of ACs 
from date seeds that can be used as an energy storage material. 
In Pakistan, after citrus and mango, Date Palm is third most impor -
tant crop. It is cultivated on 90,000 ha with the production of 600,000 
mt/year. Pakistan is the seventh highest producer and exporter of dates 
in the world [44] . Waste being generated from date agro industry and 
date processing industry include immature date fruit fell from tree, (DS), 
spoiled dates, lost dates and date press cake [45] . This waste can cause a 
serious environmental burden as well as the loss of raw materials. DS is a 
rich source of carbon and also containing cellulose and lignin content 
higher than other biomass feedstocks [46,47] . Therefore, based on the 
abundant availability of DS and inherent higher carbon content with less 
ash content DS were selected as a precursor for biochar (BC) production. 
The novelty of this research lies in the production of activated carbon 
from date seeds using chemical activation specifically for supercapacitor 
application. This is an innovative approach as date seeds are an abun -
dant waste and rarely used material for supercapacitors or any other 
application (energy storage) and converting them into a useful product 
for energy storage can contribute towards sustainability and resource 
efficiency. Therefore,

2. Experimentation 
2.1. Preparation and characterization of activated carbon

(PVDF), carbon black, KOH, ethanol, HCl, and H

from Sigma Aldrich. Fig. 1 shows the work scheme to produce biochar- 
based materials and its application for supercapacitors. Ajwa dates were 
purchased from the market and its seeds were collected, washed, and 
dried in the oven. Then ground into fine powder up to 1 mm in size by 
heavy duty blender. 
Fixed bed reactor used to carry out slow pyrolysis of dried powder 
from (DS). Previously a study reveals that the pyrolysis process was 
conducted at 600 ℃ for a stay time of 90 min at a 20 ℃ /min ramp rate, 
subsequent to purging with a nitrogen with a flow rate of 50 ml/min, the 
material produced, executed excellent electrochemical properties [48] 
therefore experiment was performed only at 600 ℃ . BC samples ob -
tained at 600 ℃ named DSBC-600. An ultrasound assisted treatment was 
performed to activate the obtained biochar. 2 gm sample of DSBC-600 
was sonicated for 2 h in 1 M solution of H
2
SO
4 
in a sonication bath. 
Then the treated biochar samples were dried and calcined in a tube 
furnace under nitrogen flow and heated at 700 ℃ , 800 ℃ and 900 ℃ for 
1 h at a heating rate of 20 ℃ /min to produce activated carbon (AC) 
named DSAC-700, DSAC-800, and DSAC-900. 
To examine how chemical activation affects surface modification and 
functional characteristics, XRD, SEM-EDX, Raman Spectroscopy, and 
FTIR techniques were used to analyze both activated and non-activated 
carbon. 
In accordance with the ASTM D1762-84 standards, a proximate 
analysis of the feedstock was conducted to determine its moisture con -
tent, ash content, volatile content, and fixed carbon content [49] . C, H,

been done using a C/H/N Elemental analyzer (model 5E-CHN200). 
Oxygen content was calculated by the difference. Biochar-based mate -
rials were characterized through XRD analysis using D2 Phaser, SEM 
imaging using JSM 6490A, and using Energy Dispersive X-ray Analysis 
(EDX) with an EDAX Z2-i7 analyzer to determine its composition.

2.2. Preparation of electrode for supercapacitor 
The Ni foam substrates, each measuring 1 cm × 1 cm, were first 
treated with ethanol and 0.1 M solution of hydrochloric acid, and sub -
sequently washed and dried at 70 
◦
C overnight. Then a mixture of AC, 
carbon, and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in a weight ratio of 
80:10:10 was prepared in 150 μ l of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP) to 
load on the surface of treated nickel foam. The coated Ni-foam dried 
overnight at 100 
◦
C in an oven. Bare Ni-foam and coated Ni-foam were 
weighed, and sample loading was calculated by the difference. 
2.3. Electrochemical testing 
The capacitive performance of the materials was measured from 
cyclic voltammetry (CV) and galvanostatic charge – discharge (CD) 
techniques using a CH instrument electrochemical workstation. A three- 
electrode setup was established, in which the AC coated nickel foam as a 
working electrode, platinum electrode as a counter electrode while Ag/ 
AgCl was utilized as a reference electrode. 1.0 M KOH solution was used 
as an electrolyte. Furthermore, to check the resistance of materials the 
electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was carried out in a 
range of 0.1 – 105 Hz frequency. The CV analysis and GCD experiments 
were conducted for all the materials at 0 – 0.5 mV with varying scan rates 
of 10, 50 100 mV/s and current densities. From the GCD profile using 
Eq. (1) the specific capacitance of active materials was calculated [50] .
