ABSTRACT
This research investigated the electrochemical properties and cycling stability of coffee grounds-derived biomass 
carbon (CGBC) and Ni(OH)
2
-CGBC composite materials as supercapacitor electrodes. These materials were 
produced by transforming coffee grounds into biomass carbon through thermal annealing and then growing Ni 
(OH)
2 
nanosheets onto the CGBC using a microwave-assisted hydrothermal method. Optimal properties were 
achieved with 10 mM Ni(OH)
2 
precursors for enhancing electrochemical performance. Various analyses, 
including FESEM, XRD, FETEM, and XPS, confirmed the uniform incorporation and beneficial properties of Ni 
(OH)
2
-CGBC composite materials. Electrochemical tests under the 1 M KOH solution revealed significant im -
provements in specific capacitance and coulombic efficiency, maintaining good reversibility across different 
current densities. After 1000 charge-discharge cycles at 1 A/g, the capacitance retention rates were 64.8 % 
(CGBC) and 55.9 % (Ni(OH)
2
-CGBC), with stable coulombic efficiencies of 97.5 % and 96.8 %, respectively. 
These results suggest that Ni(OH)
2
-CGBC composite materials hold significant promise for long-term super -
capacitor applications.
1. Introduction
The finite resources of fossil fuels, continuous population growth, 
and technological advancements pose energy shortage challenges for 
humanity [ 1 , 2 ]. Therefore, renewable energy development has become 
the current energy trend [ 3 , 4 ]. However, the regional limitations of 
renewable energy and the issue of power storage present significant 
challenges in their application [ 5 ]. Hence, until a breakthrough in power 
generation technology occurs, energy storage devices play a critical role 
[ 6 ]. Supercapacitors combine the rapid charging and discharging ca -
pabilities of capacitors with the high energy storage capacity of batte -
ries. This allows them to deliver quick energy bursts and store significant 
power, making them suitable for various applications, from consumer 
electronics to industrial equipment. Their capacity to endure frequent 
charge cycles without considerable degradation makes them highly 
efficient and reliable in demanding situations [ 7 , 8 ]. It integrates the fast 
charge and discharge capabilities found in capacitors with the robust 
energy storage capacity of batteries, offering a unique solution that 
harnesses both technologies ’ strengths to deliver both rapid energy 
bursts and long-term power storage [ 9 , 10 ]. Supercapacitors offer several 
benefits compared to traditional options, including higher power 
density, fast charge and discharge rates, extended lifespan, excellent 
cycle stability, and low sensitivity to temperature changes [ 11 ]. These 
advantages make their application range extremely wide, encompassing 
energy supply, vehicle transportation, mobile communications, artificial 
intelligence, biosensing, medical equipment, and wearable devices 
[ 12 – 14 ].
Biomass carbon, a carbon-rich material similar to charcoal, is pro -
duced by thermally decomposing biological waste (such as food and 
agricultural residues) in a limited oxygen environment [ 15 – 19 ]. Owing 
to its physicochemical properties, including a large surface area, sig -
nificant pore volume, and unique functional groups, it holds great 
promise as an exceptional alternative to activated carbon for use as an 
adsorbent [ 20 – 24 ]. The effectiveness of biomass carbon production is 
determined by various carbonization and activation factors, such as the 
type of biomass, temperature, duration, and choice of activating agent 
[ 21 , 25 ]. The largest quantities of biomass, which are abundant and 
renewable globally, typically come from agricultural waste, forest resi -
dues, wood processing by-products, and energy crops [ 26 , 27 ].
Coffee is the second largest agricultural product after tea, making 
coffee grounds a noteworthy subject of study in food waste [ 28 – 30 ]. 
However, the electric double-layer capacitance provided by carbon

2

suitable for supercapacitor electrodes. Electrochemical tests demon -

2

reversibility, and high coulombic efficiency, indicating potential for 
long-term supercapacitor applications.
2. Experimental
2.1. Fabrication of coffee grounds-derived biomass carbon (CGBC)
The material is transferred into a tube furnace after grinding the 
coffee grounds into a fine powder. Under a nitrogen atmosphere, the 
powder is subjected to high-temperature carbonization at 650 
◦
C for 2 h. 
Once the process is complete and the product has cooled, coffee ground- 
derived biomass carbon (CGBC) is obtained.
2.2. Fabrication of Ni(OH)
2
-CGBC composite
The 0.01 g prepared CGBC (coffee ground-derived biomass carbon) is 
added to 40 mL of deionized water containing various concentrations of 
Ni(OH)
2 
precursors (nickel nitrate: urea = 1: 2). After thorough stirring 
to ensure a uniform mixture, the solution is placed into a Teflon-lined 
autoclave and transferred to a microwave reactor. The reaction condi -
tions are set at a temperature of 150 
◦
C, involving a heating period of 20 
min and a subsequent holding period of 30 min. Following the micro -
wave reaction, the resulting product is extracted through centrifugation, 
thoroughly washed, and dried. This meticulous process produces the Ni 
(OH)
2
-CGBC materials specifically tailored for electrode applications.
2.3. Electrochemical experiment
The synthesized Ni(OH)
2
-CGBC composite materials are blended 
with carbon black and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) in a ratio of 8:1:1 
[ 46 ]. An appropriate 1-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) is added to create 
an electrode slurry. After ultrasonic treatment for 30 min, 75 μ L of the 
slurry is deposited onto a nickel foam that has been cleaned with 
acetone, ethanol, hydrochloric acid, and deionized water. The coated 
nickel foam is then placed in an oven and dried overnight at 50 
◦
C. 
Electrochemical testing is conducted using a Zahner Zennium electro -
chemical workstation. In this setup, each electrode is analyzed within a 
three-electrode electrochemical system: the sample is attached to the 
working electrode, a saturated calomel electrode (SCE) acts as the 
reference electrode, and a platinum sheet functions as the counter 
electrode. Various concentrations of potassium hydroxide (KOH) serve 
as the electrolyte.
2.4. Characterization
The synthesized Ni(OH)
2
-CGBC composite materials underwent a 
thorough analysis of their morphology, crystal structure, and physical 
properties utilizing various advanced characterization techniques. Sur -
face morphology was examined using field emission scanning electron 
microscopy (FESEM, Hitachi S-4800), which provides detailed visuali -
zation of the material surface. Field-emission transmission electron 
microscopy (JEOL 2100F) was used to observe the microstructure, 
crystal structure, and composition in depth. The crystal structure was 
elucidated with X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker D2 Phaser system), 
which identified the material ’ s crystallographic phases. The composite ’ s 
elemental composition and chemical states were analyzed using X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, PHI 5000 VersaProbe). Furthermore, 
the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) method (Micromeritics ASAP 2020) 
was employed to quantify the surface area and porosity, offering crucial 
insights into the material ’ s adsorption properties.

2

2

negatively impact electrochemical performance. The nanosheets

increased to 10 mM ( Fig. 2 d). However, at a concentration of 20 mM Ni 
(OH)
2

stack together, reducing the specific surface area and active sites, 
thereby diminishing electrochemical performance. At 30 mM Ni(OH)
2 
precursors ( Fig. 2 f), the nanosheets are large and begin to grow densely 
again. These observations indicate that the microwave-assisted hydro -
thermal synthesis of Ni(OH)
2 
on CGBC with a Ni(OH)
2 
precursor con -
centration of 10 mM produces a surface structure that closely matches 
the ideal properties of a supercapacitor electrode active material. This 
optimized structure may effectively enhance the electrochemical per -
formance of the active material.
