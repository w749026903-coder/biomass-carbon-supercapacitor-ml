ABSTRACT
The US Environmental Protection Agency recently released strict regulations limiting per- and polyfluoroalkyl 
substances (PFAS) in drinking water. In response, this study used activated carbons derived from biomass waste 
components for effective water treatment. By varying the composition of feedstock mixtures, perfluorobutane 
sulfonic acid adsorption efficiencies ranged from 31 to 98 %, indicating that biomass structure influences PFAS 
sorption. Activated carbon from a feedstock mixture of 50 % lignin, 35 % cellulose nanocrystals, and 15 % 
cellulose nanofibers achieved over 98 % removal of three regulated and four unregulated PFAS compounds 
without requiring additives or surface functionalization.
1. Introduction
The US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) recently introduced 
regulations aimed at reducing exposure to six per- and polyfluoroalkyl 
substances (PFAS), commonly known as “ forever chemicals, ” in drinking 
water [1] . These guidelines are expected to benefit 100 million people, 
preventing thousands of deaths and many PFAS-related illnesses. To 
meet these regulations, utilities are exploring granular activated carbon 
(AC) to remove PFAS from water sources.
Agricultural residues, which accumulate and contribute to environ -
mental and health hazards, offer a sustainable and abundant source for 
producing AC [2,3] . This biomass is mainly comprised of lignin and 
cellulose, both of which have been shown to contribute to the formation 
of the AC structure [4] . However, the relationship between biomass 
components on AC structure and their effectiveness as PFAS sorbents has 
yet to be fully understood. This research aims to fill this gap by inves -
tigating how the structure of AC derived from lignin (LIG) and cellulose 
influences their PFAS adsorption efficiency. The investigation includes 
an initial screening experiment to identify the most effective AC derived 
from LIG, cellulose nanocrystals (CNCs), and cellulose nanofibers (CNFs) 
for adsorbing short-chain PFAS. Once the best-performing AC was 
identified, experiments were repeated to understand the material ’ s 
adsorption behavior on seven common PFAS.
2. Materials and methods
PFAS compounds were selected to include anionic (PFBA, PFBS, 
PFOA, and PFOS), zwitterionic (6:2 FTAB), and cationic (PFHxSAmS 
and PFOSAmS) species with varying chain lengths to understand PFAS 
structure-dependent adsorption by AC. Further details of each model 
PFAS are presented in Table S1. Feedstock biomass characterization 
results using previously established methods [4] are provided in 
Table S2.
Eight mixtures of biomass components were chosen to create a range 
of surface areas (SSA) and microporosity. AC were prepared as described 
previously [4] and named using the system listed in Table 1 AC-5 was 
duplicated in this work to understand batch-to-batch variations. In 
addition to the nine AC samples described, a common wood biochar was 
activated using the same protocol. This compares the adsorption effi -
ciency of tailored AC to AC produced from currently available biochar. 
The SSA and micropore analysis were measured on a Micromeritics 
TriStar II 3020 using BET analysis, surface charge of the best AC was 
determined using zeta potential, and elemental analysis was conducted 
using x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). More details on BET 
sample preparation and analysis techniques can be found in Supple -
mentary Material.

3. Results and discussion
BET analysis results ( Table 1 ) reveal that all AC exhibit high SSAs 
with varying micropore fractions. The reported range of SSAs and 
microporosity in Table 1 underscores the importance of feedstock ma -
terial selection, as specific properties can be tailored based on relative 
ratios of LIG, CNC, and CNF. AC composed of a blend of biomass com -
ponents exhibit greater SSAs and micropore fractions, highlighting the 
synergistic effects of biomass components [4] . AC-Bio exhibits a similar 
SSA to other AC, containing microporosity 17 % higher than any other 
sample. An unpaired t -test indicates a non-significant difference (p- 
value = 0.054) between AC 5-1 and AC 5-2, suggesting minimal varia -
tion between sample batches.
The effectiveness of various biomass-derived AC for the adsorption of 
two short-chain compounds, PFBA and PFBS, and the adsorption ca -
pacity at equilibrium (Q

respectively. PFBS adsorption efficiencies were over 30 % for all AC and 
as high as 98 %, exceeding the results of commercial activated carbons

trends between SSA, CNF, and PFAS adsorption efficiency, so analysis of 
variance was used to verify if these relationships were significant. There

shown in Fig. 2 a and Table S4. Notably, AC-7 and AC-8, which present 
the highest SSA, exhibited exceptional PFBS adsorption efficiencies of 
95 % and 98 %, respectively. These findings surpass previous biochar 
studies by 6 – 80 % due to the high SSA achieved during the activation 
process [8 – 10] .
The adsorption efficiency of PFBA was below 40 % across all mix -
tures due to its greater hydrophilicity compared to PFBS and the 
generally lower adsorption affinity of carbonaceous materials towards 
carboxylate versus sulfonate PFAS. As shown in Fig. 2 b, PFBA removal 
efficiency generally increased with SSA, but an R
2 
of 0.45 highlights that 
additional factors are also influential. AC-8 achieved a notable PFBA 
adsorption efficiency of 33 %, a significant achievement for an experi -
ment conducted at room temperature. In contrast, previous studies using 
powdered or granular AC often required acidic conditions or surface 
functionalization of the carbons to achieve PFBA removal levels. For 
example, Huang et al. [11] achieved a maximum PFBA removal effi -
ciency of approximately 10 % using silicon modified AC, and Zhang 
et al. [12] reported a PFBA removal capacity of 20 % using commercial 
granular AC, which is 13 % lower than the removal capacity reported 
here under similar conditions. These findings advance short-chain PFAS 
adsorption, demonstrating that AC derived from a mixture of biomass 
components offer desired properties for efficient water treatment. 
Interestingly, as CNF concentration increases, PFBS and PFBA adsorp -
tion efficiency decreases ( Fig. 2 , Table S5). CNF contains fewer crys -
talline regions than CNC, and it has been shown that the combination of 
amorphous units and the lower decomposition temperature of CNF leads 
to a lower SSA with a higher degree of micropores [4] . No correlation 
was seen between micropore fraction, LIG/CNC content, and removal 
efficiency.

adsorption capability of AC-8 was tested with five additional compounds 
( Fig. 1 b). AC-8 demonstrated exceptional adsorption efficiencies, 
exceeding 99 % for PFOA, PFOS, 6:2FTAB, PFHxSAmS, and PFOSAmS.

4. Conclusion
Through tailored AC, this work achieved over 99 % adsorption for 
PFOA, PFOS, and PFHxS, in line with new EPA guidelines, along with 
excellent adsorption efficiency of emerging PFAS species. By optimizing 
AC with tunable properties, we enhanced PFAS removal through hy -
drophobic and electrostatic interactions, surpassing previous biochar 
studies. This approach offers a greener method for removing PFAS from 
water while valorizing agricultural waste, benefiting human and envi -
ronmental health.
