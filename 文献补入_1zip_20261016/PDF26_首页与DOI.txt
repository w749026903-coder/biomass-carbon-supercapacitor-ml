ABSTRACT
To address the optimization issue of experimental parameters during the experimental preparation of biomass 
char supercapacitors, this study applied six different regression models (four machine learning models and two 
deep learning models) to predict the specific capacitance of biomass char supercapacitors. The results indicate 
that the Transformer model performs best in terms of prediction accuracy, with an R
2 
(coefficient of determi -
nation) of 0.9609, an MAE (mean absolute error) of 16.8599, and an RMSE (root mean square error) of 19.4569. 
Furthermore, analysis indicates that among the experimental parameters, the activation temperature exerts the 
most significant influence on the specific capacitance. The carbonization temperature and carbonization time 
exhibit a strong coupled relationship with the specific capacitance: at low carbonization temperatures, 
carbonization time shows a positive correlation with specific capacitance, whereas at high carbonization tem -
peratures, carbonization time correlates negatively with specific capacitance. A high heating rate demonstrates a 
strong negative correlation with specific capacitance. This study employs regression models to analyze experi -
mental parameter data, thereby providing novel approaches for optimizing experiments aimed at designing 
superior supercapacitors.
1. Introduction
As developed and developing nations progressively implement their 
strategic objectives of carbon peaking and carbon neutrality, countries 
are gradually reducing their reliance on fossil fuel energy sources, 
decreasing the share of thermal power capacity, and pursuing greener, 
lower-carbon methods of electricity generation [ 1 ], which has fostered 
the continuous advancement of renewable energy, with particular 
emphasis on enhancing the utilization of wind and solar power [ 2 ]. 
However, unlike thermal and nuclear power these renewable energy 
sources lack the stable power output and easy-to-implement power 
regulation capabilities of generation methods. Their electricity output is 
thus subject to fluctuations due to temporal and spatial factors [ 3 ]. 
Consequently, the integration of green yet intermittent power genera -
tion methods such as wind and solar energy into the grid gives rise to a 
series of new grid challenges, including voltage fluctuations, frequency 
instability, and diminished grid frequency regulation and peak load 
management capabilities [ 4 ]. The construction of energy storage power 
plants (such as supercapacitor energy storage, electrochemical energy 
storage, pumped hydro storage, thermal energy storage, and.) is one of 
the most effective methods for addressing these issues [ 5 ].Among these, 
supercapacitor energy storage stands out as the most promising and 
sought-after energy storage device for grid frequency regulation due to 
its high power density, rapid charge-discharge capabilities, and 
extended cycle life [ 6 ]. The preparation of supercapacitors typically 
employs char-based materials. Biomass char, as a low-carbon-footprint, 
environmentally friendly, and cost-effective carbon-rich material, ex -
hibits favorable capacitive physicochemical properties, together with 
the potential of abundant functional groups, and a tunable pore struc -
ture. Consequently, it has been extensively explored for the fabrication 
of high performance supercapacitors [ 7 ].
However, biomass char derived from simple carbonization processes 
often suffers from underdeveloped pore structures, limited functional 
groups, and suboptimal surface morphologies. As a result, super -
capacitors fabricated using such char materials show suboptimal energy 
storage performance, which hinders their practical application in grid

mental parameters, the substantial workload involved, and the coupled 
effects between these parameters, it is challenging to investigate their 
interrelationships through individual experiments alone. Consequently, 
researchers have begun employing alternative methodologies to opti -
mise the production process of biomass char based supercapacitors. 
Machine learning regression algorithms represent one of the most 
widely adopted approaches [ 17 ]. These algorithms can process large 
volumes of complex data, analyse non-linear relationships between data 
points, and generate models that reflect the intricate relationships be -
tween input and output data. This enables us to analyse the coupled 
relationships between factors influencing supercapacitor performance 
through these models, thereby establishing more optimal experimental 
methods for fabricating higher-performance supercapacitors. Ahmed 
Emad-Eldeen et al. utilized a biomass char based supercapacitor dataset 
comprising 130 samples (encompassing material physicochemical 
properties and test conditions) to compare the training efficacy of seven 
machine learning algorithms and eight deep learning algorithms on a 
test set. The results indicated that machine learning algorithms are more 
effective in the field of supercapacitor performance prediction [ 18 ]. 
Peng Liu et al. compiled experimental data from 669 biomass char based 
EDLCs published between 2017 and 2023, encompassing 14 input fea -
tures including experimental parameters, pore structure, chemical 
composition, and measurement conditions. They employed eight ma -
chine learning models, including CatBoost, to predict specific capaci -
tance, with a particular focus on exploring the influence of biomass 
porous char microstructure on specific capacitance [ 19 ]. Yuxuan Sun 
et al. collected data on 157 biomass porous char samples prepared using 
potassium salt activators and nitrogen doping (including experimental 
conditions, physicochemical properties, and testing conditions). 
Employing three machine learning models for training, they revealed 
that heating rate is the most critical factor influencing the specific 
capacitance of biomass char, optimized the preparation process for 
biomass porous char and designed experiments to experimentally vali -
date the models [ 20 ]. Although these studies employing machine 
learning regression algorithms to optimize supercapacitor performance 
have achieved some progress, the input features in these studies include 
both experimental variables and pore structure parameters. These two 
features are not mutually independent(the experimental variables can 
affect the pore structure); the coupling effects among parameters 
inevitably influence the final model prediction outcomes, making it 
challenging to independently reflect the impact of individual experi -
mental parameters on supercapacitor performance [ 21 ].
Therefore, in order to independently explore the influence of 
experimental parameters on the performance of biomass char based 
supercapacitors, this study selected only seven experimental parameters 
derived from the carbonization and activation processes as input vari -
ables. This limited set of input variables poses a significant challenge for 
training a high-performance regression prediction model [ 19 ]. Never -
theless, the specific capacitance prediction model trained on such a 
dataset — with high feature independence — can more accurately reflect 
the influence of each experimental parameter on supercapacitor per -
formance. This facilitates analysis of the coupling relationships among 
parameters and identification of optimal parameter. Finally, experi -
ments were designed based on the optimized experimental parameters 
obtained from the model to validate the model ’ s outputs and the con -
clusions of the model analysis. Collectively, this work holds a great 
significance for the fabrication of high-performance biomass char based 
supercapacitors.
2. Materials and methods
The research content of this paper encompasses data collection, 
database establishment, model selection, as well as model training, 
analysis, and interpretation. This study selected six machine learning 
regression models, covering two major technical directions: deep 
learning and ensemble learning. Among them, deep learning models 
included two neural network architectures: Transformer and Gate 
Recurrent Unit (GRU). Ensemble learning-based models comprised four 
regression models: XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), CatBoost, and 
LightGBM. Three evaluation metrics were employed to assess the pre -
dictive efficacy of these models for the specific capacitance of biomass 
char based supercapacitors. The predictive performance of the six 
models was compared to identify the optimal model. Finally, the optimal 
model was analyzed via three model interpretability methods, exploring 
the key factors influencing the specific capacitance of biomass char 
based supercapacitors and their trends of influence. This provides 
theoretical guidance for the experimental parameter optimisation of 
higher-performance biomass char based supercapacitors.
2.1. Data collection and processing
Machine learning regression tasks require a dataset with sufficient 
sample size and high-quality samples. A sufficiently reliable dataset can 
significantly enhance the predictive performance and reliability of the 
model [ 22 ]. In this study, all data were collected from relevant literature 
on biomass char based supercapacitors published between 2021 and 
2025, Keywords such as ‘pre-carbonization ’ , ‘KOH activation ’ , ‘super -
capacitor ’ , and ‘porous char ’ were used to search relevant papers in 
various literature search engines(e.g., Web of Science, X-MOL, ELSEV -
IER). The specific capacitance of biomass char based supercapacitors is 
influenced not only by the experimental preparation process but also by 
factors such as the type of activator employed during activation and the 
testing conditions applied during testing. Therefore, to ensure consis -
tency and eliminate confounding variables, all selected samples were 
porous char materials prepared using KOH activation. Testing was 
conducted under a three-electrode configuration with 6 M KOH solution 
as the test electrolyte. The selected data points were all specific capac -
itances measured at a current density of 1 A/g. As most data related to 
supercapacitor specific capacitance in the literature were presented as 
images, the data were digitized and extracted using GetData Graph 
Digitizer 2.22 software and the impact of Graphic digitization noise was 
analyzed in S1. A total of 144 sample data points were collected from 36 
papers concerning biomass char based supercapacitors(Table.S7). 
Additionally, factors potentially affecting the specific capacitance of 
biomass char based supercapacitors during preparation and testing(such

Each sample in the dataset comprises eight features: seven input 
features and one output feature. The input features include three pa -
rameters from per-carbonization process — pre-carbonization temper -
ature (PerCarT), carbonization heating rate (CarHS) and carbonization

three-electrode system, 6 M KOH electrolyte solution, and a current

2.2. ML model training
Machine learning and deep learning models have progressively 
gained greater influence in the field of supercapacitor performance 
prediction in recent years. This study selected two deep learning algo -
rithms (Transformer and GRU) alongside four ensemble learning algo -
rithms (XGBoost, RF, CatBoost, and LightGBM) to establish relationship 
models between various experimental parameters during the super -
capacitor fabrication process and the resulting specific capacitance. For 
the four ensemble learning models, the Particle Swarm Optimization 
(PSO) algorithm was employed to optimize the model hyperparameters 
[ 23 ]. For both neural network model, the Adam optimizer was used to 
adjust the neuron parameter weights, enabling the neural network al -
gorithms to gradually converge towards a high-performance state [ 24 ].
Transformer is a deep learning model based on the self-attention 
mechanism, proposed by Vaswani et al. in 2017 [ 25 ]. In regression 
tasks, the Transformer model employs the self-attention mechanism 
within its encoder to capture global dependencies among input features. 
It extracts deep features and maps them to continuous target values, 
optimizing parameters via a regression loss function (RMSE) and back -
propagation. This ultimately yields the mapping rules between input 
and continuous output. This approach entirely eliminates the reliance on 
recurrent structures inherent in traditional sequence models (such as 
Recurrent Neural Networks), demonstrating exceptional parallel pro -
cessing capabilities and strong ability to capture global dependencies. 
Consequently, it is frequently employed in tasks spanning natural lan -
guage processing, machine translation, and computer vision [ 26 ]. Its 
ability to model complex inter-parameter relationships makes it suitable 
for optimizing experimental parameters.
GRU is an enhanced variant of the recurrent neural network (RNN) 
model, proposed by Cho et al. in 2014 [ 27 ] to address the issue of 
gradient explosion in traditional RNN models. GRU efficiently captures 
long-range dependencies within sequential data by dynamically 
capturing key temporal information through two gating units (update 
gate and reset gate). This generates hidden states containing global 
temporal information, which are then processed through an output layer 
to produce continuous predictions. Error is calculated via a regression 
loss function, and neural parameters are updated through back -
propagation of gradients using Backpropagation Through Time(BPTT), 
enabling predictions to progressively approximate true values. This 
model is particularly well-suited for sequence regression tasks involving 
limited sample sizes [ 28 ].
The four selected ensemble learning models are all based on decision 
trees as their fundamental components. By integrating the predictions 
from multiple trees, these models enhance their generalization capa -
bilities and reduce overfitting. Among them, the RF model is an 
ensemble method based on Bagging. This approach reduces variance by 
training multiple independent trees in parallel, representing an 
ensemble technique characterized by ‘unbiased estimation coupled with 
high variance’ [ 29 ]. The other three are all gradient-boosted decision 
tree (GBDT) ensemble algorithms, which sequentially train multiple 
trees to progressively reduce prediction error. They belong to the cate -
gory of ensemble methods that employ ‘iterative error correction’ [ 30 ].
The RF regression model comprises multiple independent decision 
trees. Each individual tree is trained using a subsample generated via 
bootstrap sampling from the original dataset. Training ceases when each 
tree reaches to a predefined depth or achieves optimal predictive per -
formance, the final prediction of the RF model is the average of all de -
cision tree predictions. This training method renders the predictions of 
the RF model more stable and improves its generalization performance.
XGBoost, CatBoost, and LightGBM are all optimized variants of the 
Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT) algorithm. During training, 
they construct multiple regression trees through serial iteration, opti -
mising the loss function via splitting nodes. Each tree uses the residuals 
from the previous tree's predictions as its training data. Upon comple -
tion, each tree is assigned a weight, with the final prediction being the 
weighted output of all trees. The primary distinction among these three 
GBDT models lies in their differing decision tree splitting strategies. 
XGBoost employs an exact greedy algorithm [ 31 ], which selects the 
optimal split by traversing all possible split points. CatBoost employs an 
ordered splitting strategy [ 32 ], After sorting the feature values, select 
the splitting point to avoid the issue of model overfitting;LightGBM 
employs a histogram algorithm [ 33 ], discretising continuous features 
into histograms (reducing the number of split points), combined with 
gradient one-sided sampling and feature bundling to reduce dimen -
sionality, can significantly enhance the splitting efficiency of decision 
trees.
2.3. Hyperparameter optimization
In the training of neural network models, optimizers serve as indis -
pensable component. Their function lies in adjusting parameters such as 
the weights and biases of neurons within the neural network, thereby 
minimizing the model's loss function. This process ensures the model's 
predicted outcomes approximate the sample values as closely as 
possible. Traditional optimizers include Stochastic Gradient Descent 
(SGD), momentum-based methods, and Root Mean Square Propagation 
(RMSprop). These optimizers require manual adjustment of the learning 
rate and suffer from issues such as slow convergence and poor optimi -
zation performance. This study selected the Adaptive Moment Estima -
tion (Adam) algorithm as the optimizer for both the Transformer and 
GRU models. This optimizer combines the advantages of momentum and 
RMSprop algorithms, offering benefits such as rapid convergence, strong 
stability, and adaptive learning rates without requiring extensive 
parameter tuning. It stands as one of the most widely employed opti -
mizers in neural network model training. Additionally, parameters such 
as the number of layers, heads, positional encoding, learning rate, batch 
size, which pertain to model architecture and training, are difficult to 
optimize using a single hyperparameter optimization algorithm to find 
the optimal combination. Therefore, hyperparameter tuning for both the 
Transformer and GRU neural network models is performed manually to 
achieve a high level of fitting performance for these two models.
During the training process of machine learning models, adjusting 
hyperparameters significantly enhances predictive performance. This 
study employs the Particle Swarm Optimization algorithm as the 
hyperparameter optimization method for this task. PSO is an algorithm 
originating from the collaborative behaviors of animal groups, such as 
bird feeding and fish migration. Compared to the high computational 
cost of traditional grid search, the PSO algorithm efficiently locates the 
optimal combination of hyperparameters within the parameter space by 
simulating group collaboration during the optimization process.
Furthermore, to mitigate overfitting and ensure robust performance 
evaluation, a five-fold cross-validation method was adopted to access 
models performance [ 34 ].
