ABSTRACT
Uncontrolled bleeding under wet and deformable conditions remains a major challenge for conventional he -
mostatic materials, which often suffer from limited tissue adhesion and poor mechanical adaptability. Here, we 
developed an injectable sodium alginate hydrogel ionically cross-linked with Ca
2
⁺ and reinforced with hazelnut 
branch-derived biochar (HB) to improve structural stability and local hemostatic performance. The HB- 
containing hydrogel exhibited pronounced shear-thinning behavior, high injectability, enhanced wet tissue 
adhesion, and self-healing efficiency exceeding 80%, while achieving an adhesive strength of approximately 
420 kPa. Physicochemical characterization indicated homogeneous HB incorporation and Ca
2
⁺ retention by HB, 
which may influence the local ionic environment of the hydrogel network. In vitro studies demonstrated good 
cytocompatibility and hemocompatibility. In hemostatic assays, 6A7C-HB exhibited a blood clotting index (BCI) 
of 4.45%, markedly lower than that of the commercial oxidized cellulose hemostat Surgicel ® (26.98%), and 
shortened the clotting time to 4.4 min compared with 7.5 min for the untreated blood control and 7.2 min for 
Surgicel ® . In vivo evaluation in rat tail transection and liver injury models showed effective hemorrhage control, 
reducing blood loss by approximately 45 – 55% compared with untreated controls, while subcutaneous implan -
tation demonstrated acceptable biocompatibility without evidence of significant systemic toxicity or adverse 
histopathological responses. These findings demonstrate that HB reinforcement provides a simple and effective 
strategy for developing injectable alginate hydrogels with improved mechanical adaptability and localized he -
mostatic performance under wet and deformable conditions.
1. Introduction
Uncontrolled hemorrhage remains a major cause of mortality in 
trauma and surgical settings [ 1 , 2 ]. Therefore, effective hemostatic de -
vices are needed to promote rapid coagulation of blood, wound 
covering, and improved clinical outcomes. Among these, hemostatic 
hydrogels have attracted attention due to their good biocompatibility 
and ability to maintain a moist wound environment [ 3 , 4 ]. They are 
typically constructed from natural polysaccharides (e.g., chitosan and 
alginate) [ 5 , 6 ] and synthetic polymers such as polyvinyl alcohol, poly -
ethylene oxide and its derivative copolymers, and polyacrylate [ 7 ].
In addition to their hemostatic properties, features such as inject -
ability and self-healing are important in the design of multifunctional 
hydrogels, as they provide a significant advantage in medical practice

[ 2 , 8 ]. For example, their ability to conform to irregular geometries of 
wounds makes them suitable for use in minimally invasive treatments. 
However, when applied in a physiological environment, hydrogel 
structures are generally susceptible to deformation or mechanical 
damage caused by body movements or external pressure, which may 
reduce their functional performance [ 9 ]. To address these issues, 
injectable [ 10 ] and self-healing [ 11 ] hydrogels have been suggested. 
Self-healing hydrogels incorporate reversible dynamic interactions that 
enable the hydrogel network to repair itself upon damage, thereby 
enhancing durability and functionality in medical applications [ 12 ]. 
Nevertheless, maintaining structural integrity during injection and 
subsequent deformation remains an important challenge [ 13 ]. To 
address this limitation, reinforcing phases such as carbon-based nano -
materials [ 14 ], metal ions [ 15 ], and small molecules [ 16 ] can be 
incorporated into the hydrogel network. For locally applied hemostatic 
materials, sufficient contact with wet and mechanically dynamic tissue 
surfaces is also important for maintaining the material at the bleeding 
site [ 17 , 18 ].
Herein, we present a multifunctional hydrogel platform composed of 
99% natural components and designed to combine injectability, struc -
tural stability, self-healing, wet-surface retention, and localized hemo -
static performance. To enhance structural stability and recovery while 
preserving injectability, sustainably sourced hazelnut branch biochar 
(HB) was produced through controlled carbonization and activation 
processes and incorporated as a particulate reinforcing phase ( Fig. 1 a). 
Sodium alginate (SA), used as the polymer matrix, was stabilized with an 
ionic cross-linking strategy using Ca
2
⁺ ions. A small amount of poly -
vinylpyrrolidone (PVP) was added to increase the mechanical and 
chemical stability of the network by forming interchain hydrogen 
bonding interactions ( Fig. 1 b). The central question of this study was 
whether HB incorporation could improve resistance to mechanical 
disruption and structural recovery of the ionically cross-linked SA/PVP 
hydrogel while preserving the shear-thinning injectability required for 
application to irregular wound surfaces. Despite a water content of 97%, 
the resulting hydrogel exhibited high structural integrity and could be 
injected and conform to irregular surfaces ( Fig. 1 c). HB incorporation 
improved the self-healing behavior of the hydrogel, allowing it to 
maintain its functional integrity after mechanical damage ( Fig. 1 d). The 
system showed retention on wet tissue surfaces and hemostatic activity 
( Fig. 1 e). In a conceptual use case ( Fig. 1 f), the hydrogel was shown to 
conform to irregular wound surfaces following minimally invasive in -
jection, maintain local contact, and provide a physical barrier at the 
bleeding site. The incorporation of HB into the ionically cross-linked SA/ 
PVP hydrogel resulted in a multifunctional material that combines 
structural integrity, self-healing, tissue contact, and hemostatic perfor -
mance on a single platform. Owing to its simple fabrication process and 
predominantly natural composition, the hydrogel shows potential for 
biomedical applications that require injectable and locally acting he -
mostatic materials.
2. Materials and methods
Detailed descriptions of the materials, instrumentation, experi -
mental protocols, and statistical analyses are provided in the Supple -
mentary Information where appropriate.
2.1. Preparation of hazelnut branch biochar
Hazelnut branch-derived biochar (HB) was obtained from the 
hazelnut branch via carbonization and activation. The hazelnut branch 
was milled ( ≤ 250 μ m), carbonized in a horizontal tube furnace under 
nitrogen (10
◦
C/min to 900
◦
C, 1 h). For chemical activation, the carbon 
precursor was mixed with potassium hydroxide (KOH) at a 3:1 wt ratio 
and activated under the same furnace conditions at 800
◦
C. Residual 
KOH was removed by washing with 1 M HCl until the pH reached 
neutrality, followed by drying at 60
◦
C for 48 h ( Fig. 1 a).

2.3. Rheological and mechanical characterization
Rheological measurements were performed at 37
◦
C using a parallel- 
plate geometry to evaluate the viscoelastic properties of the hydrogels. 
The linear viscoelastic region was determined by amplitude sweeps, 
followed by frequency sweep measurements to evaluate storage 
modulus (G ′ ), loss modulus (G ″ ), and complex viscosity. Creep-recovery 
and cyclic step-strain tests were conducted to assess structural recovery 
and self-healing behavior. Injectability was qualitatively evaluated by 
syringe extrusion and quantitatively determined by measuring the 
extrusion force required to deliver 1.5 mL of hydrogel through a 21 G 
blunt-tip needle. Adhesive strength was measured by lap shear testing 
according to ASTM F2255 – 05, while wet tissue adhesion was further 
evaluated using freshly excised chicken skin and bovine liver. For the 
selected 6A7C and 6A7C-HB formulations, additional lap-shear mea -
surements were performed on PBS-prewetted and gelatin-coated tissue- 
mimicking model substrates.
