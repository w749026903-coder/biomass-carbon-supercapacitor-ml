Integrated Experimental and Machine Learning for Optimization and Performance 
Prediction of Cobalt-Based Metal-Organic Framework-Coated Nickel Foam Electrodes 
for Symmetric Supercapacitors
Radhakrishnan K1, Rohit Kumar Shaw1, D V Siva Krishna Rao K1, Aditya Kumar1,*
Department of Energy and Environment, National Institute of Technology, Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu, India
*Corresponding Author: adityakumar@nitt.edu
Abstract:
Key challenges in designing high-performance metal–organic framework (MOF)-based 
supercapacitor electrodes include optimizing precursor concentration and properly predicting 
electrochemical performance. In this article, we provide an integrated experimental and 
machine learning (ML) framework to optimize Co-MOF-coated nickel foam electrodes 
manufactured via a simple and scalable dip-coating process. Concentration-dependent 
structural and morphological evolution was validated by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM), with the 3 mmol Co precursor electrode (NFC3) showing the most 
uniform coating and superior electrochemical performance. The improved NFC3 electrode was 
built into an NFC3//NFC3 symmetric supercapacitor with a specific capacitance of 19.97 F 
g−1 at 1 A g−1 and 72% capacitance retention after 1000 charge–discharge cycles. 
Electrochemical studies, such as cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge–discharge 
(GCD), electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), Ragone analysis, b-value and Dunn's 
analysis, demonstrated that the charge storage process was mostly surface-controlled 
capacitive. Five ML regression models were trained using experimentally generated CV data 
to predict the electrochemical current response. Among these, the Random Forest model had 
the highest prediction accuracy (R² = 0.999825, RMSE = 1.28 × 10⁻⁴ A, MAE = 3.10 × 10⁻⁵ 
A). The proposed experimental-ML framework provides an effective technique for optimizing 
MOF-based electrodes and accelerates the data-driven creation of high-performance symmetric 
supercapacitors.
Keywords: Co-MOF; Nickel foam electrode; Symmetric Supercapacitor; Machine Learning; 
Random Forest Regression; Electrochemical Energy Storage; Current Response Prediction.

1. INTRODUCTION:
The rapid expansion of renewable energy systems, electric vehicles (EVs), portable devices 
and smart power grids has created an urgent demand for energy storage technologies with high 
power density, fast charge–discharge rate and long operating lifetime [1–3]. Renewable energy 
sources such as solar, wind and hydropower are sustainable alternatives to fossil fuels. 
However, the intermittent nature of these sources needs the use of efficient energy storage 
systems to ensure continuous power delivery and increase grid stability [4–7]. Supercapacitors 
are remarkable for their high-power density, quick charge/discharge ability, excellent cycling 
stability, and long service life, and several electrical energy storage technologies have garnered 
considerable attention [8–10]. These advantages make them particularly desirable for 
applications where the power delivery is instantaneous such as electric vehicles, portable 
gadgets, wearable devices and hybrid energy storage systems [2,11,12]. However, further 
research of improved electrode materials and optimized device architectures are required to 
increase the energy and power performance without affecting the long-term cycle stability to 
sustain efficient electrochemical performance at high current densities  [13–15].
Supercapacitors are often categorized as electric double-layer capacitors (EDLCs), 
pseudocapacitors and hybrid supercapacitors according to charge-storage mechanism [16–20]. 
EDLCs store charge via a non-faradaic process of ion adsorption and possess great power 
density and cycling stability, but have a restricted energy density. Pseudocapacitors, in contrast, 
use rapid and reversible faradaic reactions to obtain larger capacitance, whereas hybrid 
supercapacitors combine capacitive and battery-type electrodes to balance energy and power 
densities [18–20].  However, nonlinear electrochemical behaviour arising from the intricate 
interaction between electrode structure, electrolyte and operating circumstances makes 
performance optimization difficult [9,21]. Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have attracted 
considerable attention as supercapacitor electrode materials because of their high surface area, 
tuneable pore structures, and abundant electroactive sites, which facilitate rapid ion transport

Nickel foam serves as an effective three-dimensional current collector by providing excellent

ML algorithms have surfaced as a formidable instrument for forecasting electrochemical 
performance, identify optimal operating conditions, and reduce trial-and-error experiments 
[8,26,27]. Among various ML approaches, several regression algorithms have been 
successfully applied to predict supercapacitor performance, including LR, DT, RF, GBR, and 
KNN [14,28–30]. These models can predict electrochemical current response and specific 
capacitance using experimental parameters such as scan rate, potential, discharge time, 
equivalent series resistance (ESR), and IR drop [9,31]. For instance, GBR and RF have 
demonstrated high predictive accuracy for specific capacitance by learning from experimental 
CV and GCD data [14,30]. Machine learning models enable the identification of complex 
nonlinear relationships between electrochemical parameters and charge-storage behaviour, 
resulting in supporting rapid performance prediction and electrode optimization [32–34]. 
However, many existing models remain limited by small datasets, overfitting, poor 
interpretability, and insufficient validation using practical symmetric supercapacitor devices 
[8,10,16,31,34].Despite significant progress in machine learning (ML)-driven supercapacitor 
research, several critical gaps remain unaddressed. First, only a limited number of studies have 
combined cobalt based metal-organic framework (MOF) coated nickel foam electrodes with 
machine-learning-based prediction [24,30]. The systematic investigation of how precursor 
concentration influences electrochemical performance in MOF-coated electrodes using data-
driven approaches remains absent from the literature [4,30]. Recently, efforts have sought to 
integrate electrochemical kinetics analysis with ML predictions and validate the models using 
practical symmetric device performance [30,34,35]. Scan-rate-resolved electrochemical 
datasets (2-200 mV/s) remain limited, restricting the development of robust machine-learning 
models for capturing nonlinear relationships between scan rate, charge-storage kinetics, and 
electrochemical performance [1,16]. Furthermore, most studies focus on capacitance prediction 
using three-electrode systems, with limited integration of comprehensive electrochemical 
kinetics (CV, GCD, EIS, b-value, and Dunn’s analysis) and validation using practical 
symmetric devices [2,15,33,35]. To address these gaps, this study develops an integrated 
experimental-machine learning framework for concentration-optimization of Co-MOF-coated 
nickel foam electrodes.
