ABSTRACT: The rapid demand for sustainable, high-performance energy
storage has accelerated the development of biomass-derived electrode materials
for next-generation supercapacitors. However, the intrinsically low electrical
conductivity and limited electrochemical activity of many metal oxide electrodes
remain major challenges that restrict their practical application. In this work, a
novel Fe-doped ZnO/activated carbon composite derived from oil palm empty
fruit bunches (Fe-ZnO/AC) was successfully synthesized via a hydrothermal
method and employed as an electrode material for asymmetric supercapacitors.
ZnO was selected for its low-cost, abundance, nontoxicity, and facile synthesis,
while Fe doping was introduced to enhance electrical conductivity, charge-
transfer kinetics, and redox activity. Simultaneously, activated carbon derived
from empty fruit bunch (EFB) waste provides a conductive, porous carbon
matrix that facilitates ion diffusion and electron transport. This study highlights
the synergistic integration of Fe-doped ZnO with activated carbon, enabling simultaneous enhancement of conductivity,
electrochemical performance, structural stability, and sustainability within a single-electrode system, a strategy that remains rarely
explored for asymmetric supercapacitor applications. The Fe-ZnO/AC electrode exhibited a high specific capacitance of 458.2 F g
−1
at 0.1 A g
−1
in a three-electrode configuration. In an asymmetric two-electrode configuration, the device delivered specific
capacitances of 179.7 and 102.3 F g
−1
for the coin cell and flexible cell, respectively. The corresponding energy densities reached 35.9
and 20.5 W h kg
−1
with power densities of 239.7 and 240.4 W kg
−1
, respectively. These findings demonstrate the excellent
electrochemical performance, stability, and energy storage capability of the Fe-ZnO/AC composite, highlighting its strong potential
as a low-cost, sustainable, and high-performance electrode material for next-generation asymmetric supercapacitors.
1. INTRODUCTION
The global demand for high-performance, environmentally
friendly, and efficient energy storage systems continues to rise
in line with the rapid advancement of modern technologies.
Among various options, supercapacitors have attracted
significant attention due to their density, rapid charge−
discharge capability, and excellent cycling stability compared
to conventional batteries.
1−5
However, their relatively low
energy density remains a limitation.
6
Consequently, the
development of advanced electrode materials capable of
simultaneously delivering high capacitance and long-term
stability has become a research focus. In this context, transition
metal oxides and porous carbon materials are emerging as
promising candidates, as they offer complementary charge-
storage pseudocapacitance and electric double-layer capaci-
tance (EDLC) mechanisms, respectively.
7−9
Supercapacitors generally store energy through two primary
mechanisms, EDLC and pseudocapacitance. In EDLC systems,
charge storage occurs through the electrostatic accumulation of
electrolyte ions at the electrode/electrolyte interface without
involving faradaic reactions, enabling excellent power capability
and long-term cycling stability.
10,11
Porous carbon materials,
including activated carbon, graphene, and biomass-derived
carbon, are widely utilized as EDLC electrodes due to their
high electrical conductivity, large accessible surface area, and
hierarchical pore structures. In contrast, pseudocapacitive
materials store charge through rapid and reversible faradaic
reactions involving surface redox processes, providing higher
specific capacitance than conventional carbon-based electro-
des. Transition metal oxides have attracted considerable
attention as pseudocapacitive materials because of their
multiple oxidation states and rich redox activity. However,
their practical applications are often limited by poor electrical
conductivity and structural instability during repeated charge−
discharge cycles.
12
Therefore, integrating transition metal
oxides with porous carbon materials represents an effective
strategy to combine the high capacitance contribution of

Zinc oxide (ZnO) is considered one of the most promising
metal oxides due to its natural abundance, low cost, non-
toxicity, and notable redox activity. However, its poor electrical
conductivity and limited electrochemical stability due to
structural degradation and residual impurities significantly
restrict its electrochemical performance.
14−24
To overcome
these limitations, two widely adopted approaches include metal
doping and composite formation with a conductive carbon
matrix. Among various dopants, iron (Fe) has been shown to
effectively enhance the electrical conductivity and charge
carrier concentration of ZnO. Kumar et al. (2023) reported
that Fe-doped ZnO exhibited a specific capacitance of 286 F
g
−1
in a three-electrode configuration, demonstrating the
positive effect of Fe incorporation on charge transfer kinetics.
25
In another study, integration of ZnO with activated carbon
(AC) was widely investigated to enhance surface area and
facilitate ion transport.
26

27
reported a
ZnO/AC nanocomposite with a specific capacitance of 160 F
g
−1
, while Yadav et al. (2022) obtained 77.7 F g
−1
from a ZnO-
AC composite derived from walnut shell waste.
28
These
highlight the synergistic contribution of the EDLC behavior of
carbon and the pseudocapacitive properties of ZnO, although
the overall performance remains moderate.
In recent work, Saka and Levent (2024) demonstrated that
nitrogen- and ZnO-doped carbon particles derived from waste
biomass can improve electrochemical performance, yielding
high-capacitance electrodes suitable for energy storage
applications.
29
Recent studies have demonstrated that oil
palm empty fruit bunch (EFB)-derived activated carbon is a
highly promising electrode material for supercapacitors owing
to its naturally abundant carbon content and well-developed
porous structure. Yulianti et al. reported hierarchically porous
EFB-derived activated carbon with a specific surface area of
1215 m
2
g
−1
and an outstanding specific capacitance of 459.28
F g
−1
, where the presence of naturally occurring SiO
2
enhanced
electrode wettability.
30
More recently, biomass-derived acti-
vated carbon prepared from EFB achieved a specific surface
area of up to 1375 m
2
g
−1
, delivering a specific capacitance of
346 F g
−1
together with 82.9% capacitance retention after 5000
charge−discharge cycles, further confirming the excellent
potential of EFB-derived carbon for sustainable electro-
chemical energy storage.
31
Despite these encouraging advances, most reported studies
have focused on optimizing the porous carbon framework itself
or introducing heteroatom functionalities to improve the
capacitive performance. The combination of EFB-derived
activated carbon with Fe-doped ZnO to simultaneously exploit
the high electrical conductivity and large surface area of
biomass-derived carbon together with the enhanced pseudo-
capacitive activity of Fe-modified ZnO has rarely been
investigated. This represents an important opportunity for
the development of low-cost, sustainable, and high-perform-
ance electrode materials.
In this study, Fe-ZnO/AC composites were synthesized via a
hydrothermal method and comprehensively evaluated in both
three-electrode and asymmetric two-electrode configurations.
The results demonstrate that the synergistic effects of Fe
doping and ZnO-carbon hybridization with a locally abundant,
waste-derived carbon matrix significantly enhance the redox
activity, electrical conductivity, and cycling durability. Distinct
from previous studies, this work integrates Fe doping and
ZnO-carbon hybridization within an EFB-based activated
carbon matrix, achieving improved electrochemical perform-
ance through a fully biomass-supported material architecture.
This integrated approach offers an effective and sustainable
material design approach for the development of high-
performance materials with enhanced electrochemical proper-
ties for advanced energy-storage applications.

2.4. Synthesis of Fe-ZnO/AC Composite
The composite of Fe-ZnO/AC powders was prepared by dispersing
Fe-doped ZnO and AC in a solution containing CTAB, isopropanol,
and urea, maintaining the same ratio of CTAB and isopropanol as
used in the Fe-doped ZnO synthesis. The resulting suspension was
then transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave and
subjected to hydrothermal treatment at 150 °C for 2 h. After
completion of the process, the obtained composite was thoroughly
rinsed several times with DI water and subsequently dried at 105 °C
for 24 h to yield the final Fe-ZnO/AC composite (Figure 1). To
investigate the effect of Fe-doped ZnO loading, composites containing
4%, 8%, 15%, and 25% relative to the mass of EFB were synthesized.
The resulting samples were designated as 4%-Fe-ZnO/AC, 8%-Fe-
ZnO/AC, 15%-Fe-ZnO/AC, and 25%-Fe-ZnO/AC, respectively.

3.3. Fabrication and Two-Electrode Measurement of a
Supercapacitor
The active material slurry for both electrodes was formulated
by dispersing the active material, polyvinylidene fluoride
(PVDF) binder, and acetylene black (AB) conductive additive
in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) to obtain a uniform and
stable mixture with a weight composition ratio of 8:1:1,
respectively. The prepared slurry was then uniformly deposited
onto carbon paper, which functioned as a current collector,
followed by drying before the cell assembly.

sembled in a CR2032 coin cell configuration, using the Fe-
ZnO/AC composite as the positive electrode and activated
carbon (AC) as the negative electrode. A Whatman microfiber
glass membrane presoaked in 1 M H
2
SO
4
electrolyte was
placed between the two electrodes to serve as the separator
and ion-transport medium.
