ABSTRACT: Metallic 1T phase molybdenum disulfide (MoS
2
) is
among the most promising electrode materials for supercapacitors, but
its capacitance and cyclability remain to be improved to meet the
constantly increasing energy storage needs in portable electronics. In this
study, we present a strategy, covalent functionalization, which achieves
the improvement of capacitance of metallic 1T phase MoS
2
. Covalently
functionalized by the modifier 4-bromobenzenediazonium tetrafluor-
oborate, the metallic MoS
2
membrane exhibits increased interlayer
spacing, slightly curled layered architecture, enhanced charge transfer,
and improved adsorption capabilities toward electrolyte molecules and
ions. Thanks to these boosted properties, the functionalized metallic
MoS
2
membrane exhibited excellent supercapacitor performances in a 0.5
M TBABF
4
(acetonitrile as the solvent) electrolyte (with a specific
capacitance of 135.67 F/cm
3
at 1 A/g, more than three times that of the unfunctionalized metallic MoS
2
membrane) and good
stability, which can maintain a capacitance retention of 76.0% after 10 000 charge−discharge cycles.
KEYWORDS: covalent functionalization, metallic 1T phase MoS
2
, membrane, supercapacitors, high adsorption capacitance
1. INTRODUCTION
Supercapacitors make up for the shortcomings of batteries in
the realm of fast charge and discharge devices
1−4
and have
garnered substantial attention due to their advantages in power
density, cycle stability, maintenance cost, and safety.
5,6
Numerous two-dimensional materials have been investigated
and harnessed for use in supercapacitors (SCs), such as
graphene,
7,8
transition metal carbide/nitride (MXene),
9,10
and
transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs).
11
Among them,
metallic 1T phase molybdenum disulfide (MoS
2
) is one of the
most promising electrode materials for supercapacitors
12−17
owing to its well-defined layered structure, which provides
large surface area and thereby results in more electric double-
layer storage capacitance per unit voltage.
18,19
Moreover,
metallic 1T phase MoS
2
holds higher electrical conductivity
than semiconducting 2H phase MoS
2
,
20
which makes its
application in supercapacitors more promising.
18,19
Still, its
electrochemical storage performance (in terms of capacitance
and cyclability) remains to be improved to meet the constantly
increasing energy storage needs in portable electronics.
According to the ionic adsorption mechanism of electric
double-layer capacitors (EDLCs),
8
the electrochemical storage
performances are influenced by the relative size of solvated
ions and the interlayer spacing of electrode materials. The
adsorption process will slow down if the interlayer spacing is
smaller than the diameter of solvated ions since the solvated
ions can only enter the interlayer space after desolvation.
21,22
As we all know, the pristine MoS
2
only shows a channel width
of around 0.3 nm,
23−25
which is too narrow to facilitate the
adsorption and transportation of ions. Hence, it is important to
increase the interlayer spacing of MoS
2
for enhancing its
electrochemical storage capacity. Exfoliating bulk MoS
2
into
nanosheets can effectively meet the requirement, but the width
of the channel may decrease again due to the filtration process
during electrode preparation. In this case, functionalizing
nanosheets by introducing functional groups on the surface of
the materials can further enlarge the interlayer distance and
impart additional capacity to the materials. A variety of
modifiers (organohalide reagents,
26,27
thiol reagents,
28,29
and
aryl diazonium salts
30,31
) have been utilized to functionalize
TMD materials previously as the introduced molecules
between the nanosheets will be conjugated on the surface of
chemically exfoliated nanosheets and effectively enlarge the
ionic transport path. However, the electrochemical perform-
ance of functionalized 1T MoS
2
has been rarely reported, and

Herein, we present a strategy�covalent functionaliza-
tion
32−35

1T phase MoS
2

functionalization by the modifier 4-bromobenzenediazonium
tetrafluoroborate (BBD), the interlayer spacing of the MoS
2
membrane expanded from 0.62 to 1.22 nm, which had a
profound positive impact on the electrochemical performance
and stability of supercapacitors. By filtering the modified
nanosheets onto a gold-sprayed PVDF substrate, the
volumetric specific capacitance of the MoS

most reported TMD-based supercapacitor materials. Mean-
while, through density functional theory (DFT) calculations,
we unraveled that the surface functional groups introduced by
BBD play a critical role in improving charge transfer between
Mo and S, leading to charge redistributions. These optimized
electronic structures resulted in an increased work function
and reduced energy costs for the adsorption of cations, anions,
and electrolyte molecules, which enlarges the capacitance and
suppresses the self-discharge to achieve remarkable perform-
ances of supercapacitors.
2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Preparation of MoS
2
and Other TMD Membranes. The
transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) nanosheets (MoS
2
, WS
2
,
TiS
2
, and TaS
2
) were synthesized by our previously reported
electrochemical lithium intercalation-based exfoliation method.
36,37
Typically, TMD powders, carbon black, and PVDF (mass ratio 8:1:1)
were mixed with NMP and coated on the copper foil as a cathode,
which was assembled into the coin cell using lithium foil as an anode.
Then these cells were slowly discharged (0.1C) to 0.7 V (for WS
2
and
TaS
2
)/0.9 V (for MoS
2
and TiS
2
) to realize the Li
+
intercalation
process (see Figures S18 and S19 for the corresponding discharging
curves). The number of inserted Li ions x was precisely controlled by
the cutoff voltage. When discharging MoS
2
to 0.9 V, x was
approximated to be 1, leading to the formation of a uniform single-
layer nanosheet structure. Conversely, the insufficient (x < 1) or
excessive (x > 1) intercalation caused the production of few-layer
nanosheets or pulverized nanoparticles during exfoliation (see Figure
S18). After discharging, the lithiated cathodes were taken out and
ultrasonicated in DI water for about 1 h to achieve the TMD
suspension. Then the obtained suspension was purified via
centrifugation (10 000 rpm, 15 min) and redispersion in water
several times. By weighing the mass after drying the solution, the
concentration of the prepared MoS
2
dispersion was identified as 0.1
mg/mL. Finally, all the TMD nanosheet solutions were filtered over a
golden-coated PVDF membrane (200 nm-diameter pore size) and
washed with DI water to form the TMD membrane.
2.2. Preparation of Functionalized MoS
2
and Other
Membranes. The preliminary synthesis process of functionalized
TMD membranes was the same as that of the TMD membranes.
Different volumes of the dispersion (10, 20, 30, and 40 mL) were
used to fabricate membranes with different thicknesses. After filtering
the TMD nanosheet solution into gold-coated membranes, 10 mL of
1 mM BBD in water was slowly dripped onto the upper surface of the
TMDs under vacuum filtration to functionalize the materials. The
reason for not mixing MoS
2
and BBD solutions directly is to prevent
TMD nanosheets’ aggregation. Afterward, the TMD-BBD membranes
were prepared after being washed with DI water several times and
dried at 100 °C in a vacuum oven. For other functionalized MoS
2
nanosheets, after the sonication process, the solution with the
modifiers (0.1 mL of iodoethanol, 10 mL of 1 mM iodoacetamide,
and 10 mL of 1 mM iodoacetic acid) was added into the suspension
and sonicated for 10 min again to make the functionalization process
complete. Then, the suspension was also purified via centrifugation
and redispersion several times and finally filtered on the golden-
coated PVDF membrane.
2.3. Characterization. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
images and energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) mapping of the cross
section were obtained on TESCAN MIRA LMS equipment. X-ray
diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected on a Rigaku SmartLab 9 kV
X-ray diffractometer (Cu Kα radiation source, λ = 1.5406 Å). A
PerkinElmer Spectrum II FT-IR spectrometer was used to perform
Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy, and WITec
RAMAN alpha 300R equipment was used to get the Raman spectra.
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Fisher ESCALAB
XI
+
X-ray photoelectron spectrometer) was executed for the valence
state of materials. Atomic force microscopy (AFM, Bruker “Multi-
Mode 8” AFM system) and transition electron microscopy (TEM,
FEI Talos F200x) were used to observe the layered structure. Zeta
potentials of MoS
2
and MoS
2
−BBD nanosheet dispersion were tested
by a Malvern Zetasizer Particle Analyzer. Contact angles of
membranes with electrolyte were tested by Data Physics Contact
Angle Tester.
2.4. Electrochemical Measurement. Electrochemical measure-
ments were conducted using a standard three-electrode configuration
on an electrochemical workstation (IVIUMnSTAT). A 0.5 M portion
of tetrabutylammonium tetrafluoroborate (TBABF
4
) in acetonitrile
was used as an electrolyte, and TMDs on the golden-coated PVDF
membranes were directly used as the working electrode. Cyclic
voltammetry (CV) was conducted at a scan rate of 100 to 2000 mV/s,
and the galvanostatic charge−discharge (GCD) tests were measured
at a current density of 1 to 5 A/g. All these electrochemical tests were
performed under the voltage window of −0.1 to 0.7 V vs Ag/AgCl.
2.5. Computational Method. To investigate the improved
supercapacitor performances induced by the MoS
2
−BBD, we have
applied the density functional theory (DFT) through the CASTEP
packages.
38
To supply accurate descriptions of the exchange−
correlation interactions, we have applied the generalized gradient
approximation (GGA) and Perdew−Burke−Ernzerhof (PBE) func-
tionals.
39−41
The cutoff energy was set to 370 eV within the selection
of ultrafine quality and ultrasoft pseudopotentials. To achieve efficient
energy minimizations, the k-point setting was set to coarse quality and
the Broyden−Fletcher−Goldfarb−Shannon (BFGS) algorithm was
applied.
42
In this work, we selected the single-layer 1T-MoS
2
in a 6 ×
6 × 1 supercell and introduced 2 BBD functional groups on the MoS
2
surface as MoS
2
−BBD to calculate the adsorption energies of
electrolyte molecules CH
3
CN, cation TBA
+
, and anion BF
4
−
. We
applied the following convergency criteria for the geometry
optimizations including the following: (1) Hellmann−Feynman
forces should not exceed 0.001 eV/Å, and (2) the total energy
difference should not be over 5 × 10
−5
eV/atom. For the
investigations of the electrolyte distributions, we introduced −0.1
and +0.7 V/Å in the (100) directions to the interlayer of MoS
2
−BBD
and pristine MoS
2
during the molecular dynamic (MD) simulations.
For all the MD simulations, we applied the NVT mode under 400 K,
where each simulation step was set to 1.5 fs.
